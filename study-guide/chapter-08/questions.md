# Chapter 8: Capital Gains — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** Which of the following is a capital asset?
A) Stock-in-trade  B) A personal car  C) A painting held for personal use  D) Rural agricultural land in India
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) A painting** — Paintings, jewellery, sculptures and works of art are capital assets even if held for personal use. Stock-in-trade, personal cars (personal effects) and rural agri-land are excluded.
</details>

**Q2.** Under Section 2(14), agricultural land in which location IS a capital asset?
A) Rural India  B) Urban India  C) A village far from any municipality  D) None
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Urban India** — Urban agri-land in India and foreign agri-land are capital assets; rural agri-land in India is excluded.
</details>

**Q3.** Listed equity shares held for 11 months and sold on/after 23 July 2024 give rise to:
A) Long-term capital gain  B) Short-term capital gain  C) Exempt income  D) Business income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) STCG** — Listed equity becomes long-term only after more than 12 months. 11 months < 12 months → short-term.
</details>

**Q4.** Units of a Specified Mutual Fund (65%+ in debt) acquired after 1 April 2023 are always:
A) Long-term  B) Short-term  C) Exempt  D) 12-month assets
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Short-term** — Under Section 50AA, SMFs (and MLDs and depreciable/unlisted bonds) are always short-term irrespective of holding period.
</details>

**Q5.** Period of holding of demat securities is determined by:
A) LIFO  B) Weighted average  C) FIFO  D) Date of last purchase
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) FIFO** — First-in-first-out: the security that first entered the demat account is deemed sold first.
</details>

**Q6.** The cost of acquisition of bonus shares allotted after 01-04-2001 is:
A) Market price on allotment  B) Issue price  C) Nil  D) FMV on 01-04-2001
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Nil** — Bonus shares cost nothing, so cost of acquisition is Nil (FMV on 01-04-2001 applies only if issued on/before that date). Their holding period runs from the date of allotment.
</details>

**Q7.** Mr. X inherits a house his father bought in 2005. For Mr. X's holding period and cost:
A) Holding starts at inheritance; cost is current market value  B) Previous owner's holding and cost are used  C) Cost is Nil  D) Always short-term
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Previous owner's holding and cost are used** — Under s.49(1), the father's holding period is included (making it long-term) and his cost is taken as cost of acquisition.
</details>

**Q8.** Which is NOT regarded as a transfer for capital gains?
A) Sale of shares  B) Exchange of one house for another  C) Redemption of Sovereign Gold Bonds by an individual  D) Relinquishment of a partner's interest
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Redemption of SGB by an individual** — Specifically excluded. The other three are transfers.
</details>

**Q9.** Which expense is NOT deductible while computing capital gains?
A) Brokerage  B) Stamp duty  C) Securities Transaction Tax (STT)  D) Legal fees for the transfer
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) STT** — STT and CTT are not deductible. Brokerage, stamp duty and transfer-related legal fees are deductible.
</details>

**Q10.** The short-term capital gains rate under Section 111A (STT-paid equity) is:
A) 10%  B) 12.5%  C) 15%  D) 20%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D) 20%** — Post-23 July 2024, Section 111A STCG is 20%. (12.5% is the LTCG rate; 15% was the old 111A rate.)
</details>

**Q11.** The general long-term capital gains rate under the new provisions is:
A) 10%  B) 12.5%  C) 20%  D) Slab rate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) 12.5%** — LTCG on all categories is 12.5% (without indexation).
</details>

**Q12.** Under Section 112A, the annual exemption on LTCG from STT-paid listed equity is:
A) ₹1,00,000  B) ₹1,25,000  C) ₹1,50,000  D) ₹2,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) ₹1,25,000** — The first ₹1,25,000 of such LTCG each year is exempt; the excess is taxed at 12.5%.
</details>

**Q13.** A house was bought in 2010 and sold in September 2024. The tax on its LTCG is:
A) Always 20% with indexation  B) Always 12.5% without indexation  C) Lower of 20%-with-indexation or 12.5%-without  D) Slab rate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Lower of the two** — For immovable property bought before but sold after 23 July 2024, a parallel calculation is done and the lower tax is payable.
</details>

**Q14.** Mr. P sells listed equity (STT-paid, held 3 years) with LTCG of ₹3,00,000. His LTCG tax is:
A) ₹37,500  B) ₹21,875  C) ₹60,000  D) Nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) ₹21,875** — Exempt ₹1,25,000; taxable = 3,00,000 − 1,25,000 = 1,75,000 × 12.5% = **₹21,875**.
</details>

**Q15.** Section 54EC requires reinvestment in NHAI/REC bonds within:
A) 3 months  B) 6 months  C) 1 year  D) 2 years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) 6 months** — Maximum exemption lower of ₹50,00,000 / amount invested.
</details>

**Q16.** Section 54F is available when the original asset is:
A) A residential house  B) Agricultural land  C) Any long-term capital asset other than a residential house  D) Stock-in-trade
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Any LTCA other than a residential house** — e.g. shares or gold; exemption is proportionate to reinvestment of net consideration in one house. Section 54 covers a residential house as the original asset.
</details>

**Q17.** Under Section 54, the gains can be reinvested in TWO residential houses if the capital gains do not exceed:
A) ₹1 crore  B) ₹2 crore  C) ₹5 crore  D) ₹10 crore
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) ₹2 crore** — If gains are within ₹2 crore, the new asset can be two houses instead of one. The overall cap on exemption is ₹10 crore.
</details>

**Q18.** Indexed cost of acquisition: cost ₹20,00,000, CII (purchase) 200, CII (transfer) 363. The indexed cost is:
A) ₹20,00,000  B) ₹36,30,000  C) ₹11,01,928  D) ₹33,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) ₹36,30,000** — 20,00,000 × 363 / 200 = ₹36,30,000.
</details>

**Q19.** Capital gains on "other" assets earned in foreign currency are converted into INR at the TT buying rate on:
A) Date of transfer  B) Last day of the month before the month of transfer  C) Last day of the financial year  D) Date of acquisition
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Last day of the month preceding the month of transfer** — e.g. transfer on 15 May → use 30 April rate.
</details>

**Q20.** The Capital Gains Account Scheme (CAGS) lets a taxpayer:
A) Avoid all capital gains tax permanently  B) Park unutilised gains/consideration pending reinvestment to claim exemptions  C) Get a 20% rebate  D) Convert STCG into LTCG
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Park unutilised gains pending reinvestment** — CAGS holds the money until the new asset is bought/constructed within the time limit, preserving exemptions like 54/54F.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Mrs. Mehta's transactions (all transfers in December 2024, i.e. after 23 July 2024).**
1. She bought a **residential house** on 14 April 2011 for **₹1,00,00,000** and sold it in December 2024 for **₹3,00,00,000**. CII 2011-12 = 184; CII 2024-25 = 363. She plans to buy a new residential house for **₹1,50,00,000**.
2. She sold **listed equity shares** (STT-paid, held 4 years) for a gain of **₹4,00,000**.
3. She sold **listed equity shares held 8 months** (STT-paid) for a gain of **₹2,00,000**.
4. She sold **physical gold** (held 30 months) bought for ₹5,00,000 at ₹9,00,000.

**Q1.** Compute the LTCG on the house under both old and new provisions, and identify the gain figure used.
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

- **Old (indexed):** Indexed cost = 1,00,00,000 × 363/184 = **₹1,97,28,260**. LTCG = 3,00,00,000 − 1,97,28,260 = **₹1,02,71,740**.
- **New (no indexation):** LTCG = 3,00,00,000 − 1,00,00,000 = **₹2,00,00,000**.

Because the property was bought before but sold after 23 July 2024, a parallel calculation applies — both figures are carried forward to compare tax.
</details>

**Q2.** After claiming the Section 54 exemption of ₹1,50,00,000, what is the tax on the house gain (pick the lower)?
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

- **Old:** 1,02,71,740 − 1,50,00,000 = gain fully covered → **Nil** taxable → tax **Nil**.
- **New:** 2,00,00,000 − 1,50,00,000 = 50,00,000 × 12.5% = **₹6,25,000**.

Pay the **lower = Nil**. The s.54 exemption (buy within 1 year before to 2 years after, or construct in 3 years) fully shelters the indexed gain.
</details>

**Q3.** Compute the tax on the listed-equity LTCG of ₹4,00,000 (item 2).
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

Held 4 years (> 12 months) and STT-paid → **Section 112A LTCG**. First ₹1,25,000 exempt. Taxable = 4,00,000 − 1,25,000 = ₹2,75,000 × 12.5% = **₹34,375**.
</details>

**Q4.** Compute the tax on the 8-month listed-equity gain of ₹2,00,000 (item 3).
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

Held 8 months (≤ 12 months) → **STCG**. STT-paid equity → **Section 111A @ 20%**. Tax = 2,00,000 × 20% = **₹40,000**. (No ₹1,25,000 exemption applies — that is only for 112A LTCG.)
</details>

**Q5.** Compute the gain and tax on the physical gold (item 4).
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

Physical gold held 30 months (> 24 months) → **LTCG** (gold is "any other asset", 24-month threshold). Indexation is not available (sold after 23 July 2024 and not land/building). LTCG = 9,00,000 − 5,00,000 = ₹4,00,000 × 12.5% = **₹50,000**. (No ₹1,25,000 exemption — that's only for STT-paid equity under 112A.)
</details>
