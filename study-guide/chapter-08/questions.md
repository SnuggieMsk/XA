## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Under which head of income is any profit arising from the transfer of a capital asset taxed?
A) Income from Other Sources  B) Profits and Gains of Business  C) Capital Gains  D) Income from House Property
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The Income Tax Act says any income arising from the **transfer of a capital asset** is chargeable under the head "Capital Gains".
- A) Other Sources catches residual income like interest/dividends, not asset-transfer profit.
- B) Business profits cover stock-in-trade dealings, not capital assets.
- D) House Property taxes rental income, not the gain on selling the house.
</details>

**Q2.** Capital asset is defined under which section of the Income Tax Act?
A) Section 2(47)  B) Section 2(14)  C) Section 54  D) Section 111A
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Section 2(14)** defines "capital asset" as property of any kind held by an assessee.
- A) Section 2(47) defines "transfer", not capital asset.
- C) Section 54 is an exemption (house-to-house reinvestment).
- D) Section 111A sets the short-term tax rate on STT-paid equity.
</details>

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT treated as a capital asset?
A) Jewellery  B) Stock-in-trade held for business  C) A painting  D) Goodwill of a business
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Stock-in-trade**, consumable stores and raw material held for business or profession are expressly excluded from the definition of capital asset.
- A) Jewellery is explicitly a capital asset even though personal.
- C) Paintings are listed as capital assets (works of art), not personal effects.
- D) Goodwill is a specific inclusion as a capital asset.
</details>

**Q4.** A personal car used by an individual and his family is best described as:
A) A long-term capital asset  B) A short-term capital asset  C) A personal effect (not a capital asset)  D) Stock-in-trade
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A car held for personal/family use is a **personal effect**, which is excluded from the definition of capital asset, so no capital gains arise on its sale.
- A) and B) classify it as a capital asset, which it is not.
- D) Stock-in-trade applies to goods held for sale in business, not a personal car.
</details>

**Q5.** Which of the following personal-use items is STILL treated as a capital asset (so its sale is taxable)?
A) Wearing apparel  B) Refrigerator  C) Archaeological collections  D) Television
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Even if meant for personal use, **jewellery, precious/semi-precious stones, archaeological collections, drawings, paintings, sculptures and any work of art** are NOT personal effects — they remain capital assets.
- A), B), D) Clothes, fridge and TV are ordinary personal effects and are excluded.
</details>

**Q6.** Agricultural land of which type is NOT a capital asset?
A) Urban agricultural land in India  B) Rural agricultural land in India  C) Agricultural land in a foreign country  D) Land within prescribed municipal limits
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Rural agricultural land situated in India** is excluded from the definition of capital asset.
- A) Urban agricultural land in India IS a capital asset.
- C) Agricultural land in a foreign country IS a capital asset.
- D) Land within prescribed limits of municipalities IS a capital asset.
</details>

**Q7.** As a general rule (for transfers on/after 23 July 2024), a capital asset is "long-term" if held for:
A) More than 12 months  B) More than 24 months  C) More than 36 months  D) More than 48 months
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Generally an asset is long-term if held for **more than 24 months** immediately preceding transfer (and short-term if held for not more than 24 months).
- A) 12 months is the exception threshold for listed equity-type assets, not the general rule.
- C) and D) 36/48 months are not thresholds under the current law.
</details>

**Q8.** For listed equity shares, the holding period to qualify as a long-term capital asset is:
A) More than 12 months  B) More than 24 months  C) More than 36 months  D) Always short-term
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Listed shares (equity or preference) are long-term if held for **more than 12 months** (Exception 1).
- B) 24 months applies to unlisted equity shares, not listed.
- C) 36 months is not a current threshold.
- D) Listed equity is not always short-term.
</details>

**Q9.** Which of the following is ALWAYS treated as a short-term capital asset irrespective of holding period?
A) Listed equity shares  B) Depreciable asset  C) Immovable property  D) Physical gold
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A **depreciable asset** is always treated as a short-term capital asset regardless of how long it is held.
- A) Listed equity is long-term after 12 months.
- C) Immovable property is long-term after 24 months.
- D) Physical gold is long-term after 24 months.
</details>

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** Mr. Rao sells listed equity shares (STT-paid) after holding them for 10 months at a profit. The gain is:

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

- A) Not long-term — held under 12 months.
- C) Slab rate applies to non-111A short-term gains, not STT-paid equity.
- D) Not exempt.
</details>

**Q37.** Ms. Iyer sells unlisted equity shares after holding them for 20 months. The gain is:

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Unlisted equity shares need to be held **more than 24 months** to be long-term. 20 months ≤ 24 months = **short-term**, taxed at slab rate.
- A), D) Not long-term — under 24 months; also no indexation for equity.
- C) Not exempt.
</details>

**Q38.** An investor sells physical gold (jewellery) held for 30 months. This gain is:

- C) Indexation is gone for gold.
- D) Not exempt.
</details>
