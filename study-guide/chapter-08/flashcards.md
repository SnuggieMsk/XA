# Chapter 8: Capital Gains — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check.

**Q1.** Under which head and in which year is the gain on transfer of a capital asset taxed?
> **A:** Under the head **"Capital Gains"**, in the **previous year in which the transfer takes place** (on accrual basis).

**Q2.** Define "capital asset" under Section 2(14).
> **A:** **Property of any kind** held by an assessee, whether or not connected with his business/profession (plus specified FPI/AIF securities and high-premium ULIPs).

**Q3.** Name four things excluded from "capital asset".
> **A:** (1) Stock-in-trade/raw material; (2) Personal effects (movable personal-use items like car, furniture, clothes); (3) Rural agricultural land in India; (4) Certain specified gold bonds (e.g. 6.5% Gold Bonds 1977, Special Bearer Bonds 1991).

**Q4.** Are jewellery and paintings capital assets even if for personal use?
> **A:** **Yes.** Jewellery, precious/semi-precious stones, archaeological collections, drawings, paintings, sculptures and any work of art are NOT treated as personal effects — they are capital assets and their sale is taxed.

**Q5.** Is rural agricultural land in India a capital asset? What about urban or foreign agri-land?
> **A:** Rural agri-land in India is **excluded** (not a capital asset). But agricultural land in an **urban area** in India, or in a **foreign country**, IS a capital asset.

**Q6.** General holding-period rule (transfer on/after 23 July 2024) for short-term vs long-term.
> **A:** Short-term if held **not more than 24 months**; long-term if held **more than 24 months**.

**Q7.** Which assets become long-term after just 12 months?
> **A:** Listed shares (equity/preference), listed securities, units of UTI, units of equity-oriented funds, zero coupon bonds, REIT/InvIT units, and (from FY 2025-26) ULIPs without 10(10D) exemption.

**Q8.** Which assets are "always short-term" regardless of holding period?
> **A:** Depreciable assets; Market Linked Debentures (MLDs); units of Specified Mutual Funds (SMFs) acquired on/after 1 Apr 2023; and unlisted bonds/debentures transferred after 23 July 2024 (Section 50AA).

**Q9.** How is the holding period of demat securities determined?
> **A:** By the **FIFO** method — the security that first entered the demat account is deemed sold first.

**Q10.** From what date is the holding period of bonus shares counted, and what is their cost of acquisition?
> **A:** Holding period from the **date of allotment of the bonus shares**. Cost of acquisition = **Nil** (if issued on/after 01-04-2001).

**Q11.** For an asset acquired by gift/will/inheritance, whose holding period and cost are used?
> **A:** The **previous owner's** holding period is included, and the **cost to the previous owner** is taken as the cost of acquisition (Section 49(1)).

**Q12.** Name four examples of "transfer" under Section 2(47).
> **A:** Sale, exchange, relinquishment of an asset, and extinguishment of rights (also: maturity/redemption of zero-coupon bonds).

**Q13.** Name four transactions NOT regarded as transfer.
> **A:** Distribution on total/partial partition of an HUF; transfer under gift/will/irrevocable trust; redemption of Sovereign Gold Bonds by an individual; conversion of bonds/debentures into shares of that company (also: MF consolidation, securities lending, Gold↔EGR, liquidation distribution by company).

**Q14.** What is "full value of consideration", and how is consideration in kind valued?
> **A:** The amount received/receivable for the transfer. If received in kind, the **fair market value** of the asset received is taken.

**Q15.** Are STT and CTT deductible when computing capital gains?
> **A:** **No.** Securities Transaction Tax and Commodities Transaction Tax are specifically not deductible. (Brokerage, stamp duty, registration, legal fees directly connected with the transfer are deductible.)

**Q16.** Cost of acquisition for shares acquired on or before 31-03-2001?
> **A:** **Higher of** the actual cost paid OR the Fair Market Value as on **01-04-2001**.

**Q17.** Explain the Section 112A grandfathering cost for equity bought before 31-01-2018.
> **A:** Cost = **higher of**: (a) actual cost; (b) **lower of** {FMV as on 31-01-2018, full value of consideration}. This protects gains accrued up to 31 Jan 2018.

**Q18.** What problem does indexation solve, and what index is used?
> **A:** It removes the effect of **inflation** so only the real gain is taxed. It uses the **Cost Inflation Index (CII)** notified by CBDT each year.

**Q19.** Write the indexed cost of acquisition formula.
> **A:** Indexed cost = Cost of acquisition × (CII of year of transfer) ÷ (CII of year asset first held, or CII of 2001-02, whichever is later).

**Q20.** After 23 July 2024, when is indexation still available?
> **A:** Only for **land or building (or both) acquired before 23 July 2024**, and only as a parallel calculation against the 12.5% method.

**Q21.** What is the STCG rate under Section 111A and when does it apply?
> **A:** **20%**, on transfer of equity shares, equity-oriented MF units, equity-oriented high-premium ULIPs, or business-trust units where the transaction is **chargeable to STT**. Other STCG is taxed at slab rates.

**Q22.** What is the LTCG rate under the new provisions, generally?
> **A:** **12.5%** on all categories of assets, without indexation.

**Q23.** What is the LTCG treatment of STT-paid listed equity / equity funds (Section 112A)?
> **A:** LTCG **exceeding ₹1,25,000** is taxed at **12.5%** (the first ₹1,25,000 of such gains each year is exempt).

**Q24.** Immovable property acquired before 23 July 2024 but sold after — how is the tax computed?
> **A:** A **parallel calculation**: compute 20% **with indexation** and 12.5% **without indexation**, and pay the **lower** tax. Any loss arising from indexation is no longer allowed.

**Q25.** Section 54 — who, what original asset, what reinvestment, what time limit?
> **A:** Individual/HUF; original = **residential house (LTCA)**; reinvest gains in **one residential house in India** (two if gains ≤ ₹2 crore); time = buy 1 year before to 2 years after, or construct within 3 years. Max exemption = lower of ₹10 crore / amount invested.

**Q26.** Section 54EC — what reinvestment, what limit, what window?
> **A:** Reinvest LTCG from land/building in **bonds of NHAI or REC** within **6 months**; exemption = lower of **₹50,00,000** / amount invested.

**Q27.** Section 54F differs from 54 how?
> **A:** 54F covers an LTCA **other than a residential house** (e.g. shares, gold). Exemption is **proportionate** = Investment × LTCG ÷ Net sale consideration, and you reinvest the **net consideration** (not just the gain) in one house.

**Q28.** What is the Capital Gains Account Scheme (CAGS)?
> **A:** A scheme to **deposit (park)** the unutilised capital gains/consideration before the deadline to claim exemptions like 54/54B/54D/54F/54G/54GA, until you actually buy/construct the new asset.

**Q29.** Section 54B — original and new asset?
> **A:** Original = **agricultural land used for agriculture for 2 years**; reinvest in **new agricultural land** within 2 years. Available to Individual/HUF; original can be short-term or long-term.

**Q30.** Section 115F applies to whom?
> **A:** A **Non-Resident Indian** who reinvests LTCG (from foreign-currency Indian shares/debentures/G-secs) in specified Indian assets within 6 months; exemption is proportionate.

**Q31.** What does Section 54H provide?
> **A:** Where the transfer (under s.54/54B/54D/54EC/54F) was by **compulsory acquisition** and consideration is received later, the reinvestment time limits run from the **date of receipt** of the consideration.

**Q32.** How are "other" capital gains earned in foreign currency converted to INR?
> **A:** At the **TT buying rate** on the **last day of the month immediately preceding** the month of transfer (e.g. transfer on 15 May → use the 30 April rate).

**Q33.** What is the cost of improvement, and from which date is it counted?
> **A:** All capital-nature additions/alterations made **on or after 01-04-2001** (by assessee or previous owner). Pre-01-04-2001 improvements are ignored; for MLDs/SMFs/goodwill, cost of improvement = Nil.

**Q34.** In the text's Section 54 example (sold ₹3 cr, indexed cost ₹1.97 cr, new house ₹1.5 cr), what was the final tax?
> **A:** **Nil.** Old method: LTCG ₹1,02,71,740 fully covered by the ₹1.5 cr s.54 exemption → tax Nil. The taxpayer pays the **lower** of old (Nil) and new (₹6,25,000), i.e. **Nil**.
