## 8.1 Basic Concepts
Any income arising from the **transfer of a capital asset** is taxed under the head **"Capital Gains"**, in the **previous year in which the transfer takes place** (accrual basis).

The five building blocks: **capital asset, transfer, sale consideration, cost of acquisition, and the dates of purchase and transfer.**

**Analogy:** Capital gain = (price you sell for) − (what it cost you + costs of selling). The tax law just adds careful definitions for each of those words.

---

## 8.2 Capital Asset — Section 2(14)

**Capital asset** means **property of any kind** held by an assessee (whether or not connected with business/profession). It also specifically includes securities held by an **FPI/FII** or a **Category I/II AIF** per SEBI rules, and a "high-premium ULIP" (a ULIP to which the section 10(10D) exemption does not apply) — from FY 2025-26 any such ULIP is a capital asset.

**Specific inclusions:** all property — movable, immovable, tangible, intangible — including rights of management/control of an Indian company, business undertaking, a partner's share in a firm, route permit, leasehold right, right to subscribe shares, goodwill, licence to manufacture, gold, jewellery, securities, etc.

### Exclusions (NOT capital assets):
| # | Excluded | Note |
|---|----------|------|
| 1 | **Stock-in-trade**, consumable stores, raw material held for business | These are business assets |
| 2 | **Personal effects** (movable: clothes, furniture, car, scooter, TV, fridge, instruments, gun) | Personal/household use |
| 3 | **Rural agricultural land in India** | But urban agri-land and foreign agri-land ARE capital assets |
| 4 | Certain **specified Bonds** (6.5% Gold Bonds 1977; 7% Gold Bonds 1980; National Defence Gold Bonds 1980; Special Bearer Bonds 1991; Gold Deposit Bonds 1999; Gold Monetization Scheme certificates 2015) | — |

⚠️ **Even though they're for personal use**, these are STILL capital assets (so their sale is taxed): **jewellery, precious/semi-precious stones, archaeological collections, drawings, paintings, sculptures, any work of art.**

💡 Trap: A personal car is *not* a capital asset → no capital gains tax on selling your old car. But a *painting* hanging in your home *is* a capital asset.

## 8.3 Types of Capital Asset — Short-Term vs Long-Term

The **holding period** decides whether a gain is **short-term (STCG)** or **long-term (LTCG)**. Tax is higher on STCG than LTCG, so this classification matters.

⚠️ **Key date:** The Finance Act, 2024 (w.e.f. **23 July 2024**) changed holding periods and rates. These notes assume **transfer on or after 23 July 2024**.

### General rule
- **Short-term:** held for **not more than 24 months** before transfer.
- **Long-term:** held for **more than 24 months**.

### Exception 1 — 12-month assets (long-term if held > 12 months):
a) Listed shares (equity or preference); b) Listed securities (debentures, bonds, derivatives, G-secs, units); c) Units of UTI (listed or unlisted); d) Units of Equity-Oriented Fund (listed or unlisted); e) Zero Coupon Bonds (listed or unlisted); f) ULIP without 10(10D) exemption (FY 2025-26 on); g) High-premium equity-oriented ULIP (FY 2020-21 to 2024-25).

### Exception 2 — "Always short-term" (no holding-period test):
- **Depreciable assets** — always STCG.
- **Market Linked Debentures (MLDs)** and **units of Specified Mutual Funds (SMFs)** acquired on/after **1 April 2023** — always STCG (Section 50AA).
- **Unlisted bonds / unlisted debentures** transferred/redeemed/matured after 23 July 2024 — always STCG (Section 50AA).

### Holding-period table (Table 8.1, transfer on/after 23 July 2024)
| Asset | Listed | Unlisted |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Equity shares | > 12 months | > 24 months |
| Units of Equity-Oriented Fund | > 12 months | > 12 months |
| Units of REIT / InvIT | > 12 months | — |
| Other units (not 65%+ debt) | > 24 months | > 24 months |
| SMF type 1 (65%+ in debt/MM) | **Always short-term** | Always short-term |
| Market linked debentures | **Always short-term** | Always short-term |
| Preference shares | > 12 months | > 24 months |
| Debentures | > 12 months | Always short term (unlisted) |
| Government securities (bonds/debentures) | > 12 months | > 24 months |
| Zero coupon bonds | > 12 months | > 12 months |
| Other bonds | > 12 months | Always short term (unlisted) |
| Immovable property (land & building) | > 24 months | > 24 months |
| High-premium equity-oriented ULIP | > 12 months | > 12 months |
| Any other asset (e.g. physical gold) | > 24 months | > 24 months |

💡 Quick mental model: **Listed equity-type → 12 months; almost everything else → 24 months; MLDs/SMFs/depreciable/unlisted bonds → always short-term.**

### 8.3.1 Calculating Period of Holding (special cases — Table 8.2)
Period normally runs from **date of purchase → date of transfer**. Special rules:
| Case | Holding counted from |
|------|----------------------|
| Listed shares via broker | Broker's note date (if followed by delivery + transfer deed) |
| Demat securities | **FIFO** — first in is first sold |
| **Bonus shares** | Date of **allotment of bonus shares** |
| Sweat equity / ESOP shares | Date of allotment / exercise |
| Preference→equity conversion | Includes period preference shares were held |
| Bonds/debentures→shares conversion | Includes period of the original asset |
| **Right shares** | Date of allotment of right shares |
| Renouncement of right | From company's offer date to renouncement |
| Amalgamated company shares | Includes holding in amalgamating company |
| Resulting company (demerger) | From holding date of demerged-company shares |
| Acquired by **gift/will/inheritance** (s.49(1)) | Includes **previous owner's** holding period |
| Gold ↔ EGR | Includes period held before conversion |

💡 Big exam point: for **inherited/gifted** assets and **bonus shares from a conversion chain**, the **previous owner's holding counts** — so an inherited house your father held 20 years is automatically long-term in your hands.

## 8.4 Transfer of Capital Asset — Section 2(47)
"Transfer" = passing of property/rights from one person to another. Examples:
- **Sale** — buyer acquires the seller's property for a price.
- **Exchange** — mutual transfer of one thing for another (neither being only money), per s.118 Transfer of Property Act.
- **Relinquishment** — owner withdraws and abandons rights (e.g. a retiring partner relinquishing his interest).
- **Extinguishment of rights** — rights in the asset are extinguished (not mere loss of the asset itself).
- **Maturity/redemption of zero-coupon bonds** — treated as transfer.

### 8.4.1 Transactions NOT regarded as transfer (Sections 46 & 47)
No capital gain arises on these:
1. Distribution of capital asset on **total/partial partition of an HUF**.
2. Transfer under a **gift, will, or irrevocable trust** (except ESOP shares/warrants).
3. **Transfer between two non-residents** of specified securities (bonds/GDRs/RDBs/IFSC-exchange transfers in foreign currency, certain G-secs).
4. **Redemption of Sovereign Gold Bonds** by an individual.
5. **Conversion of securities** — bonds/debentures→shares of that company; FCEB→shares; preference→equity.
6. **Consolidation of mutual fund** schemes.
7. **Lending of securities** under SEBI's Securities Lending Scheme.
8. **Rollover of Fixed Maturity Plans (FMPs)** per SEBI rules (now largely irrelevant since 65%+ debt schemes are always short-term under s.50AA).
9. **Distribution of assets in kind on liquidation** by a company (but the *shareholder* is taxed under s.46).
10. **Conversion of Gold ↔ Electronic Gold Receipt (EGR)** issued by a vault manager.

💡 Why this matters: if a transaction "is not a transfer", **no capital gains tax** arises *at that point* (the gain is deferred to a later real transfer).

## 8.5 Computation of Capital Gains

### The formulas
**Short-term capital gain:**
```
STCG = Full value of consideration
       − Expenditure on transfer
       − Cost of acquisition
       − Cost of improvement
```
**Long-term capital gain:** same formula, but cost of acquisition (and improvement) may be **indexed** *only* for land/building bought before 23 July 2024 (parallel calculation).

### 1. Full Value of Consideration
The amount received/receivable for the transfer (cash or, if in kind, fair market value). Special cases (Table 8.3):
| Case | Full value taken as |
|------|---------------------|
| Conversion of capital asset into stock-in-trade | FMV on date of conversion |
| ESOP securities gifted / via irrevocable trust | Market value on date of transfer |
| Unquoted shares transferred below FMV | FMV on date of transfer |
| Consideration not ascertainable | FMV on date of transfer |

### 2. Expenditure on transfer
Brokerage, commission, stamp duty, registration fee, travelling and legal expenses **directly connected with the transfer** are deductible.
⚠️ **STT and CTT are NOT deductible.** Investment-adviser fees: most likely **not** deductible (unless directly connected to the transfer, like brokerage/stamp duty).

### 3. Cost of Acquisition (Table 8.4 — key rows)
| Situation | Cost of acquisition |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Shares purchased on/after 01-04-2001 | Price actually paid |
| Shares acquired on/before 31-03-2001 | Higher of (actual cost, **FMV as on 01-04-2001**) |
| Listed equity/eq-MF/business-trust units (STT-paid, LTCA) bought ≤ 31-01-2018, sold after 01-04-2018 (Section 112A "grandfathering") | Higher of: (a) actual cost; (b) lower of {FMV as on **31-01-2018**, full value of consideration} |
| Right shares | Price actually paid |
| Renouncement of right | Nil |
| **Bonus shares** issued on/after 01-04-2001 | **Nil** (if issued before 01-04-2001: FMV as on 01-04-2001) |
| Sweat equity / ESOP shares | FMV considered for perquisite valuation (s.17(2)(vi)) |
| Acquired by gift/will/inheritance (s.49(1)) | **Cost to the previous owner** (or FMV on date previous owner acquired, if cost can't be determined) |
| Conversion of preference→equity | Cost of the preference shares |
| Stock-in-trade converted to capital asset | FMV of stock on date of conversion |

### 4. Indexed Cost of Acquisition (indexation)
**The problem indexation solves:** Inflation alone makes prices rise. Taxing the full nominal "gain" would tax inflation. So the law inflates your *cost* using the **Cost Inflation Index (CII)** (notified by CBDT each year), reducing the taxable gain.

⚠️ **Big change:** For transfers **on/after 23 July 2024**, indexation is **no longer available — EXCEPT for land/building acquired before 23 July 2024** (used as a parallel calculation).

**Formula:**
```
Indexed Cost of      =  Cost of      ×   CII of year of transfer
Acquisition             Acquisition      ───────────────────────────────
                                          CII of year asset first held
                                          (or CII of 2001-02, whichever is later)
```

**Worked example (from text):** House purchased FY 2002-03 for ₹50,00,000; CII 2002-03 = 105, CII 2020-21 = 301.
Indexed cost = 50,00,000 × 301 / 105 = **₹1,43,33,333**. Selling at ₹3.5 crore, the gain is computed against ₹1.43 crore (not the original ₹50 lakh), wiping out the "inflation" part.

### 5. Cost of Improvement
All **capital-nature** additions/alterations made **on or after 01-04-2001** (by assessee or previous owner). Improvements before 01-04-2001 are ignored.
⚠️ For goodwill / right to manufacture, and for **MLDs / SMFs**, cost of improvement = **Nil**.

### 6. Indexed Cost of Improvement
Same formula as indexed cost of acquisition (only relevant for land/building pre-23 July 2024).

### 8.5.1 Foreign currency capital gains (Rule 115A)
For a **non-resident (except FPI)** buying Indian company shares/debentures in foreign currency: compute the gain **in the original foreign currency** first (no indexation), then convert to INR. Conversion uses **average TT buying & selling rate (SBI)** for cost/consideration/expenditure, and **TT buying rate** for the resultant gain.
- For **other** capital gains: convert into INR at the TT buying rate on the **last day of the month preceding** the month of transfer. (e.g. transfer on 15 May 2022 → use 30 April 2022 rate.)

## 8.5.2 Tax Rates on Capital Gains (transfers on/after 23 July 2024)

### Short-term capital gains
| STCG type | Rate |
|-----------|------|
| **Section 111A** — equity shares, equity-oriented MF units, equity-oriented high-premium ULIPs, units of business trust, **chargeable to STT** | **20%** |
| All other STCG | **Slab rate** of the assessee |

### Long-term capital gains
| LTCG type | Rate |
|-----------|------|
| All categories of assets (general) | **12.5%** (no indexation) |
| **Section 112A** — listed equity, equity-oriented fund units, equity ULIPs (no 10(10D)), business-trust units, **STT-paid** | **12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1,25,000** (first ₹1,25,000 exempt) |
| Immovable property (land/building) | See special rule below |

### Immovable property LTCG (the parallel calculation)
- Property **acquired AND transferred before 23 July 2024** → old rules: **indexation + 20%**.
- Property **acquired before** but **transferred on/after 23 July 2024** → do a **parallel calculation**: compare **20% with indexation** vs **12.5% without indexation**, and pay the **lower tax**. (Any loss arising from indexation is no longer allowed.)
- Property **acquired on/after 23 July 2024** → **12.5% without indexation**.

💡 **Memory hook:** New STCG (111A) = **20%**; New LTCG (general & 112A) = **12.5%**; equity LTCG gets **₹1,25,000** free each year.

## 8.5.3 Exemptions for Capital Gains (Table 8.5)
Reinvest the gain (or consideration) in a specified new asset → exemption. **Capital Gains Account Scheme (CAGS)** lets you "park" the money until you reinvest.

| Section | Assessee | Original asset (LTCA unless noted) | Reinvest in | Time limit | Max exemption |
|---------|----------|-----------------------------------|-------------|------------|---------------|
| **54** | Individual/HUF | Residential house (LTCA) | One residential house in India (two if gains ≤ ₹2 cr) | Buy: 1 yr before–2 yr after; Construct: 3 yr | Lower of ₹10 cr / amount invested + CAGS |
| **54B** | Individual/HUF | Agricultural land used 2 yrs (ST or LT) | New agricultural land | 2 yrs | Amount invested + CAGS |
| **54D** | Any | Land/building of industrial undertaking (compulsory acquisition) | Land/building for new industrial undertaking | 3 yrs from acquisition | Amount invested + CAGS |
| **54EC** | Any | Land/building (LTCA) | Bonds of **NHAI / REC** | **6 months** | Lower of ₹50,00,000 / amount invested |
| **54EE** | Any | Any (LTCA) | Units of notified fund (none notified yet) | 6 months | Lower of ₹50,00,000 / amount invested |
| **54F** | Individual/HUF | Any LTCA **other than** residential house | One residential house in India | Buy: 1 yr before–2 yr after; Construct: 3 yr | Proportionate: Investment × LTCG / Net sale consideration (eligible investment ≤ ₹10 cr) |
| **54G** | Any | Industrial undertaking in urban area (ST/LT) | Assets of industrial undertaking in non-urban area | 1 yr before–3 yr after | Amount invested + CAGS |
| **54GA** | Any | Industrial undertaking in urban area (ST/LT) | Assets of industrial undertaking in SEZ | 1 yr before–3 yr after | Amount invested + CAGS |
| **54GB** | Individual/HUF | Residential property (LTCA) | Equity shares of eligible company / eligible start-up | By return due date / 1 yr | Proportionate: Investment in new asset × CG / Net sale consideration |
| **115F** | Non-Resident Indian | Foreign-currency Indian shares/debentures/G-secs (LTCA) | Indian shares/debentures/deposits/G-secs | 6 months | Proportionate: Investment × CG / Net sale consideration |

⚠️ **Section 54H:** If the original transfer was by **compulsory acquisition** and consideration is received later, the timelines run from the **date of receipt** of consideration.

**Worked example (Section 54 with the new parallel calc):** Mr. A bought a house 14 Apr 2011 for ₹1 crore; sold Dec 2024 for ₹3 crore; plans to buy a new house for ₹1.5 crore. CII 2011-12 = 184; CII 2024-25 = 363.

| | Old provisions (₹) | New provisions (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sale consideration | 3,00,00,000 | 3,00,00,000 |
| Less: Cost (new) | — | (1,00,00,000) |
| Less: Indexed cost (old) = 1,00,00,000 × 363/184 | (1,97,28,260) | — |
| Long-term capital gain | 1,02,71,740 | 2,00,00,000 |
| Less: Exemption u/s 54 | (1,50,00,000) | (1,50,00,000) |
| Gain after exemption | **Nil** | 50,00,000 |
| Tax @ 20% (old, indexed) | Nil | — |
| Tax @ 12.5% (new) | — | 6,25,000 |

Final tax = **lower of the two = Nil** in this example (because the indexed gain is fully sheltered by the s.54 exemption).

## 📌 One-Page Recap
- **Capital asset** = property of any kind; **excludes** stock-in-trade, personal effects, rural agri-land, certain gold bonds. Jewellery/art ARE capital assets.
- **Holding period (from 23 Jul 2024):** listed equity-type **> 12 months** = LT; most else **> 24 months**; MLDs/SMFs/depreciable/unlisted bonds **always short-term**.
- Inherited/gifted assets: **previous owner's holding period and cost** carry over. Bonus shares (post-2001) cost = **Nil**.
- **Indexation gone** from 23 Jul 2024, except land/building bought earlier (parallel calc).
- **STT/CTT not deductible.**
- **Rates:** STCG 111A (STT equity) = **20%**; other STCG = slab. LTCG general & 112A = **12.5%**; equity LTCG (112A) gets **₹1,25,000** exemption.
- Immovable property bought before but sold after 23 Jul 2024 → pay **lower of** 20%-with-indexation or 12.5%-without.
- **Exemptions:** 54 (house→house), 54F (any→house), 54EC (₹50L NHAI/REC bonds, 6 months), 54B (agri land), etc.
