## Section A — Numerical MCQs (40 questions × 1 mark = 40 marks)

**Q1.** Mr. Rao sells listed equity shares on 10-Aug-2025 for ₹6,50,000. He had bought them on 1-Feb-2023 for ₹4,00,000 and paid STT on both sides. What is his tax on this gain (ignore cess)?
A) ₹15,625  B) ₹14,063  C) ₹31,250  D) ₹50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The shares were held from Feb-2023 to Aug-2025, i.e. more than 12 months, so this is a **long-term capital gain (LTCG)** on listed equity taxed under **Section 112A**. Formula: Gain = Sale − Cost = ₹6,50,000 − ₹4,00,000 = ₹2,50,000. Section 112A gives a ₹1,25,000 exemption, then taxes the rest at **12.5%**. Taxable LTCG = ₹2,50,000 − ₹1,25,000 = ₹1,25,000. Tax = 12.5% × ₹1,25,000 = **₹15,625**. Option C (₹31,250) forgets the exemption (12.5% × ₹2,50,000). Option D wrongly applies 20% to the gain. Option B (₹14,063) is a rounding distractor.
</details>

**Q2.** An investor sells an equity mutual fund (listed) after holding it for 7 months, booking a gain of ₹80,000 (STT paid). What is the tax under the applicable section (ignore cess)?
A) ₹10,000  B) ₹12,000  C) ₹16,000  D) Nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Holding period is 7 months, which is **not more than 12 months**, so this is a **short-term capital gain (STCG)** on an equity-oriented fund, taxed under **Section 111A at 20%**. Formula: Tax = 20% × Gain = 20% × ₹80,000 = **₹16,000**. There is no ₹1,25,000 exemption for STCG (that applies only to LTCG under 112A), so D is wrong. A (₹10,000) uses the old 12.5% LTCG rate, and B (₹12,000) uses the outdated 15% STCG rate. The correct current STCG rate under 111A is 20%.
</details>

**Q3.** Ms. Iyer sells a residential plot on 1-Sep-2025 for ₹95,00,000. She had purchased it on 1-Jan-2021 for ₹55,00,000. What is her capital gains tax (ignore exemptions, surcharge and cess)?
A) ₹8,00,000  B) ₹12,00,000  C) ₹4,00,000  D) ₹5,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Immovable property (land) held for more than **24 months** is a long-term capital asset. For transfers on or after 23-Jul-2024, LTCG on property is taxed at **12.5% without indexation** under Section 112. Gain = ₹95,00,000 − ₹55,00,000 = ₹40,00,000. Tax = 12.5% × ₹40,00,000 = **₹5,00,000**. Option A applies the old 20% rate. Option C (₹4,00,000) wrongly uses 10%, and B (₹12,00,000) is an arbitrary distractor. The post-July-2024 rule is a flat 12.5% with no indexation.
</details>

**Q5.** Under the **new regime FY 2025-26 slabs** (0–4L nil, 4–8L 5%, 8–12L 10%, 12–16L 15%, 16–20L 20%, 20–24L 25%, above 24L 30%), what is the income tax (before cess) on a total income of ₹16,00,000?
A) ₹1,40,000  B) ₹1,20,000  C) ₹1,50,000  D) ₹2,40,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Tax is computed slab by slab: 0–4L → nil; 4–8L (₹4,00,000 × 5%) = ₹20,000; 8–12L (₹4,00,000 × 10%) = ₹40,000; 12–16L (₹4,00,000 × 15%) = ₹60,000. Total = ₹20,000 + ₹40,000 + ₹60,000 = **₹1,20,000**. At exactly ₹16,00,000 nothing enters the 20% band. Option A (₹1,40,000) over-counts. Options C and D apply flat rates instead of the slab method.
</details>

The rebate exactly offsets the ₹60,000 tax, leaving nothing payable.
</details>

**Q11.** What is the real (inflation-adjusted) rate of return if the nominal return is 11% and inflation is 6%?
A) 5.00%  B) 4.72%  C) 5.66%  D) 4.50%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Real return = (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1 = (1.11 ÷ 1.06) − 1 = 1.047169 − 1 = **0.04717 ≈ 4.72%**. Option A (5.00%) is the crude subtraction (11% − 6%), which overstates the real return because it ignores compounding of inflation on the base. Options C and D are arithmetic distractors. Always use the division formula, not simple subtraction.
</details>

**Q12.** An investment grew from ₹2,00,000 to ₹3,38,000 over 4 years. What is the CAGR (rounded)?
A) 17.25%  B) 14.05%  C) 13.30%  D) 12.00%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — CAGR = (End ÷ Start)^(1/n) − 1 = (₹3,38,000 ÷ ₹2,00,000)^(1/4) − 1 = (1.69)^(0.25) − 1. The 4th root of 1.69 ≈ 1.1405, so CAGR ≈ **14.05%**. Option A (17.25%) is the simple average (69% ÷ 4). Options C and D are close distractors. CAGR uses the geometric (compounding) root, not a simple annual average.
</details>

**Q13.** A goal costs ₹5,00,000 today. With inflation of 7% per year, what will it cost in 5 years (rounded)?

Option B uses simple interest (₹5,00,000 × (1 + 0.07×5) = ₹6,75,000). Options A and C are distractors. Inflation compounds, so use the power formula.
</details>

**Q14.** A person invests ₹10,000 per month via SIP for 3 years at 12% p.a. (1% per month). Using the future value of an ordinary annuity, what is the approximate corpus?
A) ₹3,60,000  B) ₹3,90,000  C) ₹4,03,200  D) ₹4,30,769
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — FV of an ordinary annuity = P × [((1+i)^n − 1) ÷ i], where P = ₹10,000, i = 0.01 monthly, n = 36 months. (1.01)^36 = 1.430769, so [(1.430769 − 1) ÷ 0.01] = 43.0769. FV = ₹10,000 × 43.0769 = **₹4,30,769**. Option A is just the sum invested (₹3,60,000) ignoring growth. Options B and C undershoot the compounding. The annuity factor captures monthly compounding.
</details>

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Within the pre-deceased son's branch, his widow and his surviving children share that one share in **equal proportions** (Rule 4). The branch share is ₹48,00,000, split between 2 people (widow + 1 child) = ₹48,00,000 ÷ 2 = **₹24,00,000** each. Option A gives her the whole branch share. Option C divides by 3, option D by 4. The branch's single share is split equally among its members.
</details>

**Q65.** Combining everyone, verify Mr. Gopal's own widow's share equals:
