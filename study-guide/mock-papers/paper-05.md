**Total: 100 marks · 70 questions · Suggested time: 2 hours · Negative marking: 25% of the marks for a wrong answer.**

> This paper is numerical and case-heavy — it leans hard on income-tax computation (heads of income, old vs new regime slabs, 87A rebate, residential status, Chapter VI-A deductions). ## Section A — Numerical MCQs (40 questions × 1 mark = 40 marks)

**Q1.** Under the new tax regime (FY 2025-26), Mr. Arun has a total income of exactly ₹12,00,000. What is his final tax payable (after 87A rebate, before cess)?
A) ₹60,000  B) ₹0  C) ₹71,250  D) ₹90,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — New-regime slabs FY 2025-26: 0–4L nil; 4–8L @5% = ₹20,000; 8–12L @10% = ₹40,000. Tax before rebate = ₹20,000 + ₹40,000 = ₹60,000. Section 87A gives a rebate of up to ₹60,000 when total income is ≤ ₹12,00,000, so the rebate wipes out the entire ₹60,000. Tax payable = ₹0. A) is the pre-rebate tax. C)/D) ignore the rebate. The trick: at exactly ₹12L the rebate fully covers the tax.
</details>

**Q2.** A resident individual (age 45) opts for the OLD regime in FY 2025-26 with total income of ₹4,80,000. Tax payable after 87A rebate and 4% cess is:
A) ₹11,960  B) ₹0  C) ₹11,500  D) ₹24,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Old-regime slabs: 0–2.5L nil; 2.5L–5L @5%. Tax = 5% × (4,80,000 − 2,50,000) = 5% × 2,30,000 = ₹11,500. Section 87A (old regime) gives a rebate of up to ₹12,500 when total income ≤ ₹5,00,000. Since ₹11,500 < ₹12,500, the whole tax is rebated → ₹0, so no cess either. A) adds cess to the un-rebated figure; C) forgets the rebate; D) misapplies a flat rate.
</details>

**Q3.** Mr. Bose (new regime, FY 2025-26) has total income of ₹16,00,000. Tax before cess is:
A) ₹1,20,000  B) ₹1,50,000  C) ₹1,10,000  D) ₹2,40,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Slabs: 0–4L nil; 4–8L @5% = ₹20,000; 8–12L @10% = ₹40,000; 12–16L @15% = ₹60,000. Total = 20,000 + 40,000 + 60,000 = ₹1,20,000. Income exceeds ₹12L so no 87A rebate. B) wrongly taxes the whole ₹16L slab-by-slab using 15% on too wide a band; C)/D) are common mis-slabs.
</details>

**Q4.** An Indian citizen with Indian income below ₹15 lakh comes on a visit to India and stays 181 days in FY 2025-26 (he was a non-resident in all earlier years). His residential status is:
A) Resident and Ordinarily Resident  B) Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident  C) Non-Resident  D) Deemed Resident
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — For an Indian citizen/PIO visiting India whose Indian income is up to ₹15 lakh, the basic test is 182 days or more in the relevant year (the 60-day + 365-day test does not apply to such visitors). 181 days is one day short of 182, so he is a Non-Resident. A)/B) require him to first qualify as resident, which he doesn't. D) deemed-resident applies only when Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh and he isn't taxed anywhere else.
</details>

**Q5.** Mr. Khan stayed in India for 70 days in FY 2025-26 and 400 days in total across the 4 preceding years. He is not an Indian citizen and not of Indian origin (foreign national). His residential status is:
A) Non-Resident  B) Deemed Resident  C) Cannot be determined  D) Resident
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — For an ordinary foreign national, the resident test is: 182+ days in the year, OR 60+ days in the year AND 365+ days in the 4 preceding years. He has 70 days (≥60) in the year and 400 days (≥365) in the preceding 4 years, so both parts of the second test are satisfied → Resident. A) ignores the 60+365 test; B) needs Indian income > ₹15L; C) is determinable.
</details>

**Q6.** For a resident to be "Ordinarily Resident," he must NOT satisfy either NOR condition. Which stay makes a resident "Not Ordinarily Resident"?
A) In India 730 days or more in the preceding 7 years  B) Resident in 8 of the 10 preceding years  C) In India 729 days or less in the preceding 7 years  D) In India 182 days in the current year
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A resident is NOR if he was a non-resident in 9 of the 10 preceding years, OR was in India for 729 days or less in the 7 preceding years. So "729 days or less" triggers NOR. A) is the opposite (it would make him ordinarily resident). B) is about residency count but the threshold is "non-resident in 9 of 10," not "resident in 8 of 10." D) is just the basic resident test.
</details>

### Case 1 — Full income-tax computation: salaried professional, old vs new regime

Ms. Nandita (age 38, resident) has the following figures for FY 2025-26:

| Item | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
