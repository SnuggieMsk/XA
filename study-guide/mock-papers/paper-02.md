## Section A — Numerical MCQs (40 questions × 1 mark = 40 marks)

**Q1.** A salaried employee has a monthly basic + DA of ₹40,000. How much does the EMPLOYEE contribute to EPF each month at the statutory rate?
A) ₹3,200  B) ₹4,800  C) ₹1,468  D) ₹3,333
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The employee's EPF contribution is a flat 12% of (basic + DA). 12% × ₹40,000 = ₹4,800, and this entire ₹4,800 goes into the EPF account. A (8%) is the special reduced rate for new women employees, not the standard rate. C is the EPS slice (8.33% of the ₹15,000 ceiling). D is wrong arithmetic. Formula: Employee EPF = 12% × (basic + DA).
</details>

**Q2.** For the same employee (basic + DA ₹40,000), the EMPLOYER also pays 12%. How much of the employer's contribution is diverted to the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS)?
A) ₹4,800  B) ₹3,332  C) ₹1,250  D) ₹1,468
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — EPS gets 8.33% of salary, but the EPS wage is capped at a ceiling of ₹15,000 per month. So EPS = 8.33% × ₹15,000 = ₹1,249.50 ≈ ₹1,250. The cap is the trick: 8.33% × ₹40,000 = ₹3,332 (option B) ignores the ceiling and is wrong. A is the full 12%. D mixes things up. The rest of the employer's 12% (₹4,800 − ₹1,250 = ₹3,550) goes to EPF.
</details>

**Q3.** Continuing Q2: of the employer's ₹4,800, how much goes to the employee's EPF account (the EPF slice) after the EPS portion is removed?
A) ₹3,550  B) ₹4,800  C) ₹1,468  D) ₹3,332
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Employer EPF slice = total employer 12% minus the EPS amount. ₹4,800 − ₹1,250 = ₹3,550. (Equivalently, 3.67% × ₹40,000 = ₹1,468 PLUS the 8.33% the employee earns above the ₹15,000 ceiling — but the simplest exam route is ₹4,800 total minus ₹1,250 EPS = ₹3,550.) Option C (₹1,468) is only the 3.67%-on-full-salary figure and ignores that EPS is capped at ₹15,000, so the EPF slice is actually larger. The EPS cap means more of the employer money lands in EPF.
</details>

**Q4.** Gratuity for an employee COVERED by the Payment of Gratuity Act with last drawn salary (basic + DA) ₹52,000 and 18 years 8 months of service is:
A) ₹5,40,000  B) ₹5,70,000  C) ₹5,26,154  D) ₹6,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Formula = (15 × last drawn salary × years) ÷ 26. Service of 18 years 8 months rounds UP to 19 years (because the part-year exceeds 6 months). So gratuity = (15 × 52,000 × 19) ÷ 26 = 14,820,000 ÷ 26 = ₹5,70,000. Option A uses 18 years (forgets the rounding up). C divides by an odd number. D is a round guess. The "÷26" comes from 15 days' salary per year out of a 26-working-day month.
</details>

**Q5.** A retiree's NPS corpus at age 60 is ₹50,00,000. He uses only the MINIMUM mandatory portion for annuity and withdraws the rest as lump sum. How much can he withdraw as tax-free lump sum?
A) ₹20,00,000  B) ₹40,00,000  C) ₹30,00,000  D) ₹25,00,000

**Correct: C)** — At normal exit (age 60), a minimum of 40% of the corpus must buy an annuity, and a maximum of 60% can be taken as lump sum (tax-free under Sec 10(12A)). Maximum lump sum = 60% × ₹50,00,000 = ₹30,00,000. Option B (40%) is the annuity portion, not the lump sum. A is 40% mislabelled. D is wrong. So minimum annuity = ₹20,00,000, lump sum = ₹30,00,000.
</details>

**Q7.** A 30-year-old wants ₹50,000 per month (today's money) at retirement. With 6% annual inflation over the next 30 years, what will be the equivalent monthly expense at retirement (rounded)?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Inflation-adjusted future cost = Present cost × (1 + inflation)^years = 50,000 × (1.06)^30. (1.06)^30 ≈ 5.7435, so 50,000 × 5.7435 ≈ ₹2,87,175. Option B uses (1.06)^22 roughly; C just adds a little; A triples it by eye. Each year prices rise 6%, and compounding over 30 years multiplies the cost about 5.74 times.
</details>

**Q8.** If something costs ₹100 today and inflation is 6% per year, the official workbook says it costs roughly how much in 20 years?

</details>

**Q9.** Nominal expected return on a retirement portfolio is 10% and inflation is 6%. The real (inflation-adjusted) rate of return is approximately:
A) 4.00%  B) 1.67%  C) 16.00%  D) 3.77%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Real return = [(1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation)] − 1 = (1.10 ÷ 1.06) − 1 = 1.03774 − 1 = 0.03774 ≈ 3.77%. Option A (simply 10% − 6%) is the common shortcut that slightly overstates the true real return. C adds the rates. The real return tells you how much your money grows in actual purchasing power.
</details>

**Q10.** A SIP of ₹10,000 per month is invested for 12 months at 12% p.a. (1% per month), contributions at month-end. The future value at the end of 12 months (ordinary annuity) is approximately:
A) ₹1,20,000  B) ₹1,26,825  C) ₹1,28,093  D) ₹1,34,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Future value of an ordinary annuity = P × [((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i], where P = 10,000, i = 0.01, n = 12. (1.01)^12 = 1.126825, so ((1.126825 − 1) ÷ 0.01) = 12.6825. FV = 10,000 × 12.6825 = ₹1,26,825. Option A is just the contributions with no growth. C (₹1,28,093) is the annuity-DUE value (×1.01) — wrong because payments are at month-end. The bracket factor turns 12 deposits into more than ₹1.2 lakh because of compounding.
</details>

**Q11.** What lump sum must be invested TODAY to grow to ₹1 crore in 20 years at 9% p.a. compounded annually (present value)?
A) ₹17,84,309  B) ₹25,00,000  C) ₹14,86,436  D) ₹10,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Present value = FV ÷ (1 + r)^n = 1,00,00,000 ÷ (1.09)^20. (1.09)^20 ≈ 5.6044, so PV = 1,00,00,000 ÷ 5.6044 ≈ ₹17,84,309. Option C uses ~6.73 (an 11% factor). B and D are guesses. PV strips out 20 years of 9% growth to tell you the amount needed up front.
</details>

**Q12.** A retiree needs to withdraw ₹3,00,000 at the end of each year for 15 years. If the corpus earns 8% p.a., the corpus required today (present value of an ordinary annuity) is approximately:
A) ₹45,00,000  B) ₹30,00,000  C) ₹27,81,500  D) ₹25,68,090
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — PV of ordinary annuity = P × [1 − (1 + r)^−n] ÷ r = 3,00,000 × [1 − (1.08)^−15] ÷ 0.08. (1.08)^15 ≈ 3.1722, so (1.08)^−15 ≈ 0.31524; [1 − 0.31524] ÷ 0.08 = 0.68476 ÷ 0.08 = 8.5595. PV = 3,00,000 × 8.5595 ≈ ₹25,68,090. Option A simply multiplies 3,00,000 × 15 (ignores that the corpus keeps earning 8%). C is the annuity-due figure. The corpus is less than the naive total because it keeps earning interest.
</details>

**Q18.** Two indemnity policies of ₹5,00,000 each (Company A and Company B) cover the same flood risk. The ascertained loss is ₹30,000 and Company A settles it. Under the contribution principle, Company B's proportionate share is:
