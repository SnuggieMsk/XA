**Q1.** Ravi buys 1,000 listed equity shares at ₹150 (STT paid) on 10-May-2023 and sells all on 20-Jun-2025 at ₹360. This is his only capital gain in FY 2025-26. What is his capital gains tax liability (ignore cess)?
A) ₹26,250  B) ₹10,625  C) ₹42,000  D) ₹21,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — These are listed shares with STT, held more than 12 months (May-2023 to Jun-2025 ≈ 25 months), so it is a Long-Term Capital Gain taxed under Section 112A at 12.5% on the gain above the ₹1,25,000 exemption. Gain = (360−150) × 1,000 = ₹2,10,000. Taxable = 2,10,000 − 1,25,000 = ₹85,000. Tax = 12.5% × 85,000 = **₹10,625**. Option A (₹26,250) forgets the ₹1.25 lakh exemption (12.5% × 2,10,000). Option C uses 20% STCG rate, wrong because the holding period is over 12 months. Option D (₹21,000) is 10% of the gross gain — the old pre-23-Jul-2024 rate, no longer applicable.
</details>

**Q2.** Sunita sells an unlisted equity share lot for a gain of ₹4,00,000 after holding it for 30 months (acquired and sold after 23-Jul-2024). What is the LTCG tax (ignore cess and surcharge)?

Option D wrongly subtracts the ₹1.25 lakh exemption (12.5% × 2,75,000) which does not apply to unlisted shares.

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The employer's 8.33% goes to EPS, but EPS contribution is capped on a wage ceiling of ₹15,000. So EPS = 8.33% × 15,000 = **₹1,250** (the balance of the employer's 12% goes to EPF). Option A is the total employer contribution (12% × 40,000 = ₹4,800). Option B wrongly applies 8.33% to the full ₹40,000. Option D (3.67% × 40,000 ≈ ₹1,468... actually ₹1,468) is not even that — ₹1,800 looks like a random EPF-share figure; the wage-ceiling cap is the key trap.
</details>

**Q14.** An investor's portfolio grows from ₹4,00,000 to ₹9,00,000 over 6 years. What is the CAGR?

**Correct: C)** — The base policy can pay at most its sum insured ₹4,00,000, but a 10% co-pay applies to the admissible claim. **Q47.** The super top-up has a deductible of ₹4,00,000. After the base policy pays ₹3,60,000 and Suresh has borne ₹40,000 co-pay, the remaining bill is ₹6,00,000 − ₹3,60,000 = ₹2,40,000. How much does the top-up pay?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Q69.** What is the tax on Mr. Verma's ₹3,25,000 listed-equity LTCG (STT, long-term) in FY 2025-26 (ignore cess)?

**Q70.** During the year, Mrs. Verma is hospitalised; the admissible claim is ₹4,00,000 under the health policy (SI ₹10,00,000, 15% co-pay). How much does the insurer pay, and how much does the family bear?
A) Insurer ₹4,00,000; family nil  B) Insurer ₹60,000; family ₹3,40,000  C) Insurer ₹3,40,000; family ₹60,000  D) Insurer ₹3,40,000; family nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A 15% co-pay means the family bears 15% of the admissible claim and the insurer pays the rest. Family co-pay = 15% × 4,00,000 = ₹60,000; insurer pays 85% × 4,00,000 = **₹3,40,000**. The claim (₹4,00,000) is within the ₹10,00,000 sum insured, so no SI cap applies. Option A ignores the co-pay. Option B reverses the shares. Option D forgets the family's ₹60,000 co-pay.
</details>
