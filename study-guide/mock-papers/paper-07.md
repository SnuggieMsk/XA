## Section A — Numerical MCQs (40 questions × 1 mark = 40 marks)

**Q1.** A Hindu male dies intestate leaving a widow, two sons and one daughter, all surviving. Under Section 10 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, what fraction of the estate does the widow get?
A) 1/3  B) 1/2  C) 1/5  D) 1/4
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Section 10 Rule 1 gives the widow one share; Rule 2 gives each surviving son, each daughter and the mother one share each. Heirs here = widow (1) + son (1) + son (1) + daughter (1) = 4 equal shares (no surviving mother is mentioned). Each share = 1/4. So the widow gets 1/4. A) 1/3 wrongly counts only 3 heirs; C) 1/5 wrongly adds a 5th heir; B) 1/2 is the spouse share under some other religions, not the Hindu Class I rule.
</details>

**Q2.** Mr. A buys 12,000 listed shares at ₹80. The company issues bonus 1:3 (one bonus for every three held). What is the cost of acquisition of the 4,000 bonus shares for capital-gains purposes?
A) ₹3,20,000  B) ₹1,06,667  C) ₹0  D) ₹80 each
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — When bonus shares are allotted without payment on the basis of existing holdings, their cost of acquisition is NIL (Section 55). Bonus = 12,000 ÷ 3 = 4,000 shares, all at nil cost. A) and B) wrongly spread the original cost onto the bonus; D) uses the original purchase price, which applies only to the original shares, not the free bonus shares.
</details>

**Q3.** Mr. B holds 2,000 shares bought at ₹150 each (face value ₹100). The company splits each ₹100 share into two ₹50 shares. After the split, what is the cost of acquisition per share?
A) ₹150  B) ₹100  C) ₹75  D) ₹50
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A split is not a transfer; the original cost is spread proportionately over the larger number of shares. Total cost = 2,000 × ₹150 = ₹3,00,000; after split he holds 4,000 shares; cost per share = ₹3,00,000 ÷ 4,000 = ₹75. A) ignores the split; B) and D) confuse face value with cost.
</details>

**Q4.** A Hindu male dies intestate. He is survived by his mother, his widow and one son. There are no other heirs. What share does the son receive?
A) 1/3  B) 1/2  C) 1/4  D) Whole estate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Class I heirs sharing one share each: mother (1) + widow (1) + son (1) = 3 equal shares. Each = 1/3, so the son gets 1/3. B) wrongly drops the mother; C) wrongly adds a 4th heir; D) ignores that mother and widow are also Class I heirs.
</details>

**Q5.** A company buys back 600 listed shares from Mr. C at ₹400 each on 1-Feb-2025. He had bought them at ₹250 each. Under the rules effective 1-Oct-2024, how much is taxed as dividend (income from other sources) in his hands?
A) ₹90,000  B) Nil  C) ₹1,50,000  D) ₹2,40,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — From 1-Oct-2024 the entire buyback proceeds are taxed as dividend under "income from other sources" with NO deduction for cost. Dividend = 600 × ₹400 = ₹2,40,000. The cost (600 × ₹250 = ₹1,50,000) is separately treated as a capital loss, not netted here. A) wrongly subtracts cost; C) is the cost itself; B) is the pre-Oct-2024 position when proceeds were exempt under Section 10(34A).
</details>

**Q6.** Following Q5, what is the capital loss available to Mr. C from the buyback?
A) ₹2,40,000  B) ₹1,50,000  C) ₹90,000  D) Nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Under the post-Oct-2024 rule, the cost of acquisition of the bought-back shares becomes a capital loss in the shareholder's hands (long- or short-term by holding period). Cost = 600 × ₹250 = ₹1,50,000 = the capital loss. A) is the dividend, not the loss; C) wrongly nets dividend minus cost; D) ignores the loss provision.
</details>

**Q7.** Mr. D renounces (sells) his rights entitlement on 300 listed shares to Mr. E for ₹40 per right. He had paid nothing for the rights. What is his taxable capital gain on renunciation?
A) ₹0  B) ₹4,000  C) ₹6,000  D) ₹12,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Cost of acquisition of a right renounced is NIL. Gain = sale consideration − nil = 300 × ₹40 = ₹12,000. This is short-term and taxed at normal slab rates (it is a right, not a listed equity share, so Section 111A does not apply). A) wrongly treats the right as free of gain; B) and C) use wrong prices.
</details>

**Q8.** A Hindu male dies intestate survived by his widow, one surviving daughter, and the two children (a son and a daughter) of a pre-deceased son. Total heirs at the top level take how many shares between them?
A) 4 shares  B) 3 shares  C) 2 shares  D) 5 shares
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — At the top level: widow takes one share, surviving daughter takes one share, and the branch of the pre-deceased son takes ONE share between its members (Rule 3). So 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 shares. The pre-deceased son's two children then split that one share. A) wrongly counts the two grandchildren as separate top-level shares; C) drops a share; D) over-counts.
</details>

**Q9.** Continuing Q8, the pre-deceased son's branch (his son + daughter) takes 1/3 of the estate between them. What fraction does each grandchild get?
A) 1/3 each  B) 1/12 each  C) 1/9 each  D) 1/6 each
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — The branch's 1/3 is split equally between the surviving son and daughter of the pre-deceased son (Rule 4). 1/3 ÷ 2 = 1/6 each. A) ignores the split; B) and C) divide incorrectly.
</details>

**Q10.** Mr. F bought 8,000 shares at ₹105 on 1-Apr-2020. The company issued bonus 1:2 (one bonus for two) on 1-Sep-2023. He sold ALL shares (12,000) at ₹140 on 1-Aug-2024. What is the LTCG on the 8,000 original shares (no exemption applied yet)?
A) ₹2,80,000  B) ₹4,20,000  C) ₹1,68,000  D) ₹5,60,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Original shares: held > 12 months, so long-term. Gain = 8,000 × (₹140 − ₹105) = 8,000 × ₹35 = ₹2,80,000. B) wrongly uses all 12,000; C) uses a wrong rate; D) uses a wrong cost.
</details>

**Q11.** Continuing Q10, the 4,000 bonus shares (cost nil, allotted 1-Sep-2023, sold 1-Aug-2024) generate what gain, and of what type?
A) ₹5,60,000 LTCG  B) ₹1,40,000 STCG  C) ₹4,20,000 STCG  D) ₹5,60,000 STCG
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Bonus shares' holding starts from allotment (1-Sep-2023) to sale (1-Aug-2024) = 11 months < 12 months, so SHORT-term. Cost is nil. Gain = 4,000 × ₹140 = ₹5,60,000, all STCG (Section 111A, 20%). A) wrongly calls it long-term; B) and C) use wrong share counts.
</details>

**Q12.** A Hindu female dies intestate leaving no children and no husband, but her mother and father survive. Under Section 15, who inherits first?
A) Mother and father  B) Heirs of the husband  C) Heirs of the father  D) Sons and daughters
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Section 15 order: (a) sons/daughters and husband; (b) heirs of husband; (c) mother and father; ... Since there are no children and no husband, the next applicable entry that exists here is (c) mother and father. B) and C) come later; D) does not exist here.
</details>

**Q13.** Mr. G acquired 20,000 preference shares at ₹10 each on 1-Jan-2010. They convert 2:1 into 10,000 equity shares (FMV ₹25). He sells the equity at ₹35 on 25-Aug-2024 (STT paid). What is the LTCG?
A) ₹1,50,000  B) ₹3,50,000  C) ₹1,00,000  D) ₹2,50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Conversion of preference into equity is NOT a transfer (Section 47(xb)); cost and holding carry over from the preference shares. Sale = 10,000 × ₹35 = ₹3,50,000. Cost = 20,000 × ₹10 = ₹2,00,000. LTCG = ₹3,50,000 − ₹2,00,000 = ₹1,50,000. B) ignores cost; C) and D) use FMV wrongly.
</details>

**Q14.** Mr. H buys 10,000 units of a mutual fund at ₹15. The fund segregates a portfolio: NAV of asset moved to segregated portfolio is ₹3, total NAV before segregation is ₹15. What is the cost of acquisition of the segregated units?
A) ₹15,000  B) ₹30,000  C) ₹1,50,000  D) ₹1,20,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Cost of segregated units = total cost × (NAV transferred ÷ total NAV) = ₹1,50,000 × (3 ÷ 15) = ₹30,000. A) is per-unit confusion; C) is total cost; D) is the main-portfolio cost (₹1,50,000 − ₹30,000).
</details>

**Q15.** Continuing Q14, what is the cost of acquisition that remains attached to the main-portfolio units?
A) ₹1,50,000  B) ₹30,000  C) ₹1,20,000  D) ₹1,35,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Main-portfolio cost = original cost − segregated cost = ₹1,50,000 − ₹30,000 = ₹1,20,000. A) ignores the reduction; B) is the segregated cost; D) miscomputes.
</details>

**Q16.** Under the Hindu Succession Act, which of the following is NOT a Class I heir of a Hindu male?
A) Mother  B) Widow of a pre-deceased son  C) Father  D) Daughter of a pre-deceased daughter
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Father is a Class II heir, not Class I. The mother, the widow of a pre-deceased son and the daughter of a pre-deceased daughter are all in the Class I list. So C) is the odd one out.
</details>

**Q17.** Mr. I held 10,000 shares of A Ltd bought at ₹58 on 1-Apr-2023. A Ltd amalgamates into AB Ltd and he gets 8,000 shares, which he sells at ₹100 on 1-Sep-2024 (listed, STT paid). What is the LTCG?

**Correct: A)** — On amalgamation, cost and holding carry from the original (amalgamating) shares. Sale = 8,000 × ₹100 = ₹8,00,000. Cost = 10,000 × ₹58 = ₹5,80,000. LTCG = ₹8,00,000 − ₹5,80,000 = ₹2,20,000 (held > 12 months). B) uses 8,000 × ₹58 for cost; C) ignores cost; D) uses wrong figures.
</details>

**Q18.** Continuing Q17, what tax rate applies to that ₹2,20,000 LTCG, and what is the tax (ignore cess/surcharge)?
A) 20% → ₹44,000  B) 12.5% on full → ₹27,500  C) 12.5% on amount above ₹1,25,000 → ₹11,875  D) Exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Section 112A: listed equity LTCG is taxed at 12.5% only on the amount exceeding the ₹1,25,000 exemption. Taxable = ₹2,20,000 − ₹1,25,000 = ₹95,000. Tax = 12.5% × ₹95,000 = ₹11,875. A) uses STCG rate; B) forgets the exemption; D) ignores that the excess is taxable.
</details>

**Q19.** Mr. J holds 1,000 shares costing ₹500 each, bought 1-Apr-2024. He exercises a rights issue and is allotted 500 shares at ₹250 on 1-Dec-2024, then sells them at ₹520 on 25-Jan-2025 (listed, STT paid). What is the capital gain and type?

## Section B — Case Studies (6 cases × 5 sub-questions × 2 marks = 60 marks)

### Case 1 — Intestate Succession of a Hindu Male

Mr. Rajan, a Hindu, dies intestate on 1-May-2026 leaving a net estate of ₹2,40,00,000. He is survived by: his widow Mrs. Rajan; his mother; one surviving son Arjun; and the branch of a pre-deceased son Vikram. Vikram left behind a widow and two children (one son, one daughter), all surviving. There is no will. Distribution follows the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (Sections 8–10).

| Heir | Status |
|---|---|
| Widow (Mrs. Rajan) | surviving |
| Mother | surviving |
| Son Arjun | surviving |
| Pre-deceased son Vikram's branch | widow + son + daughter |

**Q41.** How many equal top-level shares does the estate split into?
A) 6  B) 5  C) 4  D) 3
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Top level: widow (1) + mother (1) + son Arjun (1) + Vikram's branch (1) = 4 shares. Vikram's branch takes ONE share between its members (Rule 3). A) wrongly counts the branch members separately; B) and D) miscount.
</details>

**Q42.** What is the rupee value of the widow Mrs. Rajan's share?
A) ₹40,00,000  B) ₹80,00,000  C) ₹48,00,000  D) ₹60,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Each of the 4 equal shares = ₹2,40,00,000 ÷ 4 = ₹60,00,000. The widow takes one full share = ₹60,00,000. A) divides by 6; C) divides by 5; B) divides by 3.
</details>

**Q43.** Within Vikram's branch (which holds ₹60,00,000), his widow, son and daughter share equally. What does Vikram's widow get?
A) ₹20,00,000  B) ₹30,00,000  C) ₹15,00,000  D) ₹60,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Under Rule 4(1), within a pre-deceased son's branch his widow and surviving sons/daughters take equal proportions. Branch share ₹60,00,000 ÷ 3 = ₹20,00,000 each. B) splits into 2; C) splits into 4; D) gives the whole branch share to one person.
</details>

**Q44.** What fraction of the TOTAL estate does Vikram's surviving daughter receive?
