# Chapter 17: Behavioural Finance in Practice — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** Anchoring bias occurs when people rely on _______ when making decisions.
A) all available information  B) pre-existing information  C) no information at all  D) forecasts of future prospects
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Anchoring means latching onto a piece of *pre-existing information* (an "anchor") and judging everything relative to it. A and C describe the opposite; D describes forecasting, not anchoring.
</details>

**Q2.** According to which theory do people treat money differently depending on its origin and intended use?
A) Capital market theory  B) Modern portfolio theory  C) Prospect theory  D) Mental accounting theory
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — **Mental accounting** is putting money into separate mental "buckets" (e.g., treating a bonus as fun money but salary as serious money), even though money is fungible. Prospect theory is about gains/losses vs a reference point.
</details>

**Q3.** Which of the following is a premise of Prospect Theory?
A) Choices are evaluated relative to a reference point  B) People are risk-averse about gains but risk-seeking about losses  C) Monetary losses hurt more than monetary gains  D) All of the above
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — All three are core premises of Prospect Theory. Loss aversion (losses hurt ~2x more than equal gains) is the famous one.
</details>

**Q4.** Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky (1979) introduced the:
A) Capital market theory  B) Modern portfolio theory  C) Prospect theory  D) Mental accounting theory
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Prospect Theory (1979) is the foundational paper of behavioural finance. Modern portfolio theory is Markowitz.
</details>

**Q5.** Behavioural finance differs from standard finance because it:
A) Precludes the impact of investor psychology  B) Includes the impact of investor psychology  C) Accepts the Efficient Markets Hypothesis  D) Rejects market anomalies
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Behavioural finance *includes* psychology. Standard finance assumes rational investors and efficient markets; behavioural finance accepts that anomalies exist because of human biases.
</details>

**Q6.** An investor sells funds whenever an asset class did well last year and buys into it. This is:
A) Mental accounting  B) Chasing past performance  C) Home country bias  D) Framing
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Buying last year's winners is chasing past performance — "driving by looking in the rear-view mirror." It ignores reversion to the mean.
</details>

**Q7.** The best structural antidote to chasing past performance is:
A) Buying only index funds  B) A pre-decided asset allocation policy with upper limits per asset class  C) Using a credit card budget  D) Buying foreign securities
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Pre-set upper limits per asset class (and per security) prevent piling into the recent winner. The other options don't directly address the bias.
</details>

**Q8.** Under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), a resident may remit up to ____ per year for overseas investment.
A) USD 25,000  B) USD 1 million  C) USD 250,000  D) USD 50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — LRS limit is USD 250,000 per resident per year. This helps counter home country bias.
</details>

**Q9.** The cure for "buying insurance only for tax saving" is to:
A) Buy more endowment policies  B) Stop buying insurance  C) Highlight the need for adequate pure insurance and the poor returns of bundled products  D) Only buy ULIPs
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Term + separate investment (e.g., PPF) beats endowment. The adviser highlights adequate pure protection and the weak returns of investment-cum-insurance products.
</details>

**Q10.** A client owns 2 large-cap, 4 mid-cap and 6 small-cap funds. The chapter suggests this could be replaced for similar diversification by:
A) One sector fund  B) A single top-500 index fund  C) Twelve more funds  D) A gold ETF
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Diversification comes from spread, not the *number* of funds. A broad top-500 index fund provides similar diversification with far less complexity.
</details>

**Q11.** Telling a client "here's what you'll lose if you don't invest this way" versus "here's what you'll gain" is an example of:
A) Anchoring  B) Framing  C) Overconfidence  D) Action bias
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Framing is wording the same fact as a gain or a loss. The recommendation is identical; only the persuasion differs.
</details>

**Q12.** When most employees rate themselves above the 50th percentile in performance (an arithmetic impossibility), it illustrates:
A) Mental accounting  B) Loss aversion  C) Overconfidence bias  D) Home country bias
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — People systematically overestimate their abilities. In investing this becomes mistaking luck for skill.
</details>

**Q13.** Agreeing in advance on rebalancing rules so that profit-booking happens automatically when prices rise is an example of a:
A) Bias  B) Nudge  C) Tactical call  D) Framing tool
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A nudge is a pre-built rule that makes the good choice automatic. Rebalancing triggers nudge you to buy low and sell high.
</details>

**Q14.** During a sharp market fall, the adviser's most important role is to:
A) Encourage rapid trading to recover losses  B) Be the calm influence keeping the client on the pre-planned journey  C) Switch everything to last year's best performer  D) Cancel the financial plan
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Through the greed/fear cycle the adviser is the cool anchor. Panic-trading (A) and chasing winners (C) are the very mistakes to avoid.
</details>

**Q15.** A client wants to fully fund a daughter's foreign education even if it badly compromises her own retirement. The adviser should:
A) Agree without comment  B) Refuse to advise  C) Objectively show the compromise to retirement and suggest a partial education loan the daughter repays later  D) Tell her to ignore retirement
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The adviser's objective role is to quantify the trade-off and offer alternatives (e.g., partial education loan), helping an emotional client decide well.
</details>

**Q16.** Why is using a debit-card-funded separate account a good tool against retail therapy?
A) It earns higher interest  B) It provides a self-imposed brake / built-in expense limit  C) It is tax-free  D) It increases credit score
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Spending only from a pre-funded account creates a hard stop. Unlike credit cards, you cannot overspend.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Mr. Rao, 38, is your client. After a stressful quarter at work he has (i) been online-shopping heavily on his credit card, (ii) moved most of his portfolio into a small-cap fund because it "doubled last year," and (iii) refuses to invest abroad saying "I only trust Indian companies." He also tells you he made 4× on a single stock last year and now wants to put 60% of his portfolio into individual stocks he picks himself. He bought an endowment policy years ago "for tax."

**Q1.** Identify the behavioural bias behind each of Mr. Rao's behaviours.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- Credit-card shopping under stress → **retail therapy** (emotional spending), worsened by credit-card use (no brake).
- Moving into last year's small-cap winner → **chasing past performance** (ignores reversion to the mean).
- Refusing foreign investment → **home country bias**.
- Wanting 60% in self-picked stocks after a 4× win → **overconfidence bias** (mistaking luck for skill).
- Endowment "for tax" → **buying insurance only for tax saving**.
</details>

**Q2.** What practical step do you recommend for the retail-therapy spending?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Make him conscious the spending is stress-driven, then set a specific budget — ideally a **separate account** pre-funded with the budgeted amount, spent only via debit card/cash. Stop using the credit card for discretionary spending so there is a built-in expense brake.
</details>

**Q3.** How do you address the small-cap concentration and the desire for 60% direct stocks?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Apply a **pre-decided asset allocation policy** with **upper limits per asset class and per security**. Explain reversion to the mean (last year's winner often reverts). On the 4× stock, explain it was largely **luck**, unlikely to repeat; 60% in self-picked stocks distorts the asset allocation and exposes him to concentrated risk. As the adviser, stand "between the client and his big mistake."
</details>

**Q4.** What is your advice on the endowment policy and home country bias?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- **Insurance:** Check whether his cover is *adequate*. Likely it isn't — endowment bought for tax tends to be inadequate. Recommend a pure **term plan** for adequate protection and investing the difference (e.g., PPF/equity), which beats endowment for the same outlay.
- **Home country bias:** Educate him on global diversification benefits and that the **LRS** allows up to USD 250,000/year; Indian MFs offer S&P 500 / Nasdaq 100 funds for easy access.
</details>

**Q5.** Six months later, markets crash and Mr. Rao wants to sell everything in panic. What is your role?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

This is the **fear** half of the greed/fear cycle. Be the **calm, objective influence**: remind him of the pre-agreed plan and **rebalancing nudges** (a fall is the time to buy the cheaper asset, not panic-sell). Prevent him from locking in losses and abandoning the long-term journey.
</details>
