## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** What is the broad subject matter of "Behavioural Finance in Practice"?
A) How to calculate portfolio returns mathematically  B) How real, emotional humans actually behave with money and how an adviser manages that  C) How to file tax returns  D) How to register as an investment adviser
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The chapter is about emotions, biases and the adviser's role in managing client behaviour. A) is portfolio maths, not behaviour. C) is taxation. D) is regulation. The behavioural logic: classical finance assumes robot-like investors, but this chapter studies the messy, emotional reality.
</details>

**Q2.** "Retail therapy" refers to:
A) Buying shares of retail companies  B) Going on a shopping or buying binge to reduce stress  C) Therapy sessions offered at retail stores  D) A type of mutual fund
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Retail therapy = a shopping/buying binge done to relieve emotional stress. A) confuses it with retail-sector stocks. C) is a literal misreading. D) is unrelated. The behavioural logic: spending gives momentary relief from stress, like scratching an itch.
</details>

**Q3.** According to the chapter, what is the FIRST step an adviser uses to tackle a client's retail therapy?
A) Confiscate the client's credit cards  B) Make the client conscious that the behaviour is induced by stress  C) Increase the client's income  D) Recommend a high-return investment
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Step one is awareness: make the client realise the spending is triggered by stress, not need. A) is too forceful and not in the text. C) and D) don't address the emotional cause. Behavioural logic: you cannot fix a habit you don't recognise.
</details>

**Q4.** What is the SECOND step to control retail therapy?
A) Stop the client from shopping entirely  B) Have a specific budget for indulging, possibly via a separate account  C) Sell all the client's investments  D) Report the client to SEBI
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Step two sets a specific budget; in extreme cases a separate account holds only the budgeted amount. A) is unrealistic and not advised. C) and D) are absurd. Behavioural logic: a built-in spending limit is a self-imposed brake.
</details>

**Q5.** Why does the chapter say credit cards are NOT good for most people?
A) They charge annual fees  B) They induce impulsive spending because there is no built-in brake  C) They are illegal in India  D) They give too many reward points
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Credit cards let you spend money you don't have, inducing impulsive spending with no built-in expense limit. A) is a minor point, not the chapter's reason. C) is false. D) is irrelevant. Behavioural logic: a debit card or cash imposes a natural brake; credit removes it.
</details>

**Q6.** A "separate account holding only the budgeted amount" is an example of:
A) A self-imposed brake on spending  B) Tax evasion  C) A nominee account  D) An overdraft facility
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — It is a self-imposed brake / built-in expense limit. B) is illegal and unrelated. C) is an estate concept. D) is the opposite — it allows more borrowing. Behavioural logic: by ring-fencing money, the client physically cannot overspend.
</details>

**Q7.** Which Nobel laureate said "Investing should be dull. It shouldn't be exciting"?
A) Daniel Kahneman  B) Amos Tversky  C) Paul Samuelson  D) Harry Markowitz
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The quote about investing being dull (and going to Las Vegas for excitement) is attributed to Paul Samuelson. A) and B) are linked to Prospect Theory. D) is linked to portfolio theory. Behavioural logic: real investing is patient and boring, not thrilling.
</details>

**Q8.** "Action bias" is best defined as:
A) The urge to do something rather than sit still  B) A bias toward action movies  C) A legal action against an adviser  D) The tendency to avoid all decisions
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Action bias is the human need to "do something," whereas investing rewards patience. B) is nonsense. C) is legal jargon. D) is the opposite (inaction). Behavioural logic: doing nothing feels wrong, so investors trade unnecessarily.
</details>

**Q9.** Action bias most often turns investing into:
A) Saving  B) Trading (too frequent and too many transactions)  C) Insurance buying  D) Tax planning
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Action bias causes frequent, numerous transactions, turning investment into trading. A) is the opposite tendency. C) and D) are unrelated activities. Behavioural logic: hyperactivity destroys returns through costs and bad timing.
</details>

**Q10.** What is the chapter's antidote to over-trading from action bias?
A) Charge higher fees  B) Prime the client in advance with the benefits of patience and long-term investing  C) Block the client's trading account permanently  D) Recommend day trading
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Prime (mentally prepare) the client in advance about patience and long-term investing. A) is unrelated. C) is too extreme and not in the text. D) is the opposite. Behavioural logic: preparing the mind before the itch strikes builds resistance.
</details>

**Q11.** "Chasing past performance" means:
A) Buying last year's winning assets or securities expecting them to keep winning  B) Reviewing a fund's historical NAV for compliance  C) Selling losers quickly  D) Tracking an index passively
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — It is investing in last year's winners (e.g. gold rises so they buy gold). B) is a routine review. C) is a different behaviour. D) is the opposite of chasing. Behavioural logic: it assumes the recent past predicts the future, which is often wrong.
</details>

**Q12.** Chasing past performance fails mainly because of:
A) Government regulation  B) Reversion to the mean — asset classes cycle and returns drift back to average  C) High brokerage fees  D) Currency fluctuations
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Most asset classes have cycles of growth and decline with an inevitable reversion to the mean. A), C) and D) are not the chapter's stated reason. Behavioural logic: yesterday's hot asset is often tomorrow's laggard.
</details>

**Q13.** The chapter compares chasing past performance to:
A) Driving a car by looking in the rear-view mirror  B) Watching paint dry  C) Scratching a mosquito bite  D) Sailing without a compass
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Investing on past performance is "like driving a car by looking in the rear-view mirror" — bound to crash. B) describes dull investing (the good thing). C) describes emotional spending. D) is not used in the text. Behavioural logic: you must look forward, not backward.
</details>

**Q14.** What tool prevents over-exposure to a single asset class?
A) A pre-decided asset allocation policy with upper limits for each asset class  B) Buying more of the winning asset  C) A credit card  D) An endowment policy
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Upper limits per asset class in a pre-set allocation policy prevent over-exposure. B) worsens it. C) and D) are unrelated. Behavioural logic: rules set in calm times protect you in emotional times.
</details>

**Q15.** "Home country bias" is the tendency to:
A) Prefer investing only in your own country's securities due to familiarity  B) Invest only abroad  C) Avoid equities  D) Buy only government bonds
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Home country bias is preferring domestic securities because they feel familiar. B) is the opposite. C) and D) are unrelated asset choices. Behavioural logic: familiarity feels safe, but it costs diversification.
</details>

**Q16.** The main cost of home country bias is:
A) Higher taxes  B) Loss of the diversification benefit from assets that don't move in tandem with local markets  C) Currency conversion fees  D) Legal penalties
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Investors lose diversification from foreign assets that move differently from local ones. A), C) and D) may exist but are not the central cost cited. Behavioural logic: putting all eggs in one (home) basket raises risk.
</details>

**Q17.** Which is NOT listed as a reason investors avoid foreign securities?
A) Lack of knowledge of opportunities abroad  B) Home-country laws may restrict it  C) High cost / lack of access  D) Foreign markets are always loss-making
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — "Foreign markets are always loss-making" is false and not a listed reason. A), B) and C) are all explicitly listed. Behavioural logic: the barriers are practical and informational, not that foreign markets lose money.
</details>

**Q18.** Under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), a resident of India can remit up to how much per year for overseas investment?

**Q24.** The chapter argues that a term policy plus PPF will:

**Q25.** Over-diversification means:

</details>

**Q26.** The chapter says true diversification of an asset class can come simply from:

</details>

**Q27.** Owning 2 large-cap, 4 mid-cap and 6 small-cap funds instead of one index fund is an example of:

</details>

**Q28.** Putting one's entire portfolio in a couple of securities is:

</details>

**Q29.** The adviser's job between the two extremes of diversification is to find:
A) The golden mean — appropriate diversification based on risk profile, resources and time frame  B) The cheapest fund  C) The highest-return stock  D) The largest number of holdings
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The adviser finds the golden mean suited to the client's risk profile, resources and time frame. B), C) and D) miss the balance. Behavioural logic: the right answer is neither too many nor too few holdings.
</details>

**Q30.** "Framing" in behavioural finance means:
A) Putting a portfolio statement in a frame  B) How the same fact is worded (e.g. gain-frame vs loss-frame)  C) Registering a complaint  D) Drawing a chart
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Framing is how a fact is presented; the same recommendation can be worded as a gain or as a loss avoided. A) is literal. C) and D) are unrelated. Behavioural logic: wording, not just facts, decides whether a client is convinced.
</details>

**Q31.** Some clients respond to "here's what you'll gain," while others respond to "here's what you'll lose by not doing this." This shows the importance of:
A) Anchoring  B) Framing  C) Action bias  D) Home country bias
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — This is framing — gain-frame vs loss-frame for the same recommendation. A), C) and D) are other biases. Behavioural logic: matching the frame to the client's preference improves persuasion.
</details>

**Q32.** Overconfidence bias is the human tendency to:

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — People overestimate their talents; in surveys most rate themselves 60–75th percentile (an arithmetical impossibility). A) is the opposite. C) and D) are unrelated. Behavioural logic: we systematically think we're better than we are.
</details>

**Q33.** In investing, overconfidence often shows up as:

</details>

**Q34.** A "nudge" is best described as:
