# Chapter 6: Miscellaneous Aspects of Retirement Planning — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check.

**Q1.** In one line, what is the investment adviser's core job in retirement planning?
> **A:** To fit together pensions, benefits, assets and income sources into a plan that produces **reliable monthly income** in retirement.

**Q2.** Name four things an adviser should understand before recommending anything.
> **A:** The client's **time horizon, investment experience, goals, and risk tolerance** (plus all assets, liabilities and income sources).

**Q3.** What is the formula for the future cost of an expense?
> **A:** **Future Expense = Present Expense × (1 + inflation)^years.**

**Q4.** At 7% inflation, what does Rs. 10,000/month today become in 25 years?
> **A:** **Rs. 54,724/month** (10,000 × 1.07^25).

**Q5.** What is the real (inflation-adjusted) rate of return formula?
> **A:** **Real return = [(1 + return) ÷ (1 + inflation)] − 1.**

**Q6.** Compute the real return for 8% return and 6% post-retirement inflation.
> **A:** (1.08 ÷ 1.06) − 1 = **1.89% p.a.**

**Q7.** Which Excel function gives the corpus required, and what are its arguments?
> **A:** **=PV(rate, nper, pmt, [fv], [type])** — rate (real return per period), nper (months in retirement), pmt (inflated monthly expense, negative), fv/type usually blank/0.

**Q8.** Which Excel function gives the monthly savings needed?
> **A:** **=PMT(rate, nper, pv, [fv], [type])** — rate (return per period), nper (months to retirement), pv (corpus target).

**Q9.** Why is pmt entered as a negative number in the PV calculation?
> **A:** Because it's a **cash outflow** (an expense), so it's prefixed with a minus sign.

**Q10.** For a Rs. 1.18 crore corpus at 12% retiring at 60, how does monthly saving change by start age 30 vs 40?
> **A:** Start at **30 → Rs. 3,376/month**; start at **40 → Rs. 11,928/month** (about 3.5× more) — because compounding has less time.

**Q11.** What is a "step-up" investment strategy?
> **A:** Increasing contributions periodically (a % each year or via lump sums) so savings start lower and rise with income — maximising the corpus.

**Q12.** Mr. A needs Rs. 2 crore in 20 years at 12%. What fixed monthly saving (PMT) does he need?
> **A:** **Rs. 20,217/month** (he can instead step up 7% annually to start lower).

**Q13.** How much can be lost by withdrawing Rs. 75,000 from EPF with 30 years to retirement at 8.5%?
> **A:** About **Rs. 8.66 lakh** of future corpus (the compounded future value of that Rs. 75,000).

**Q14.** What is a UAN and why does it matter on a job change?
> **A:** **Universal Account Number** — a 12-digit lifelong EPF ID enabling **online transfer** of EPF between employers (keep KYC updated).

**Q15.** Why transfer (not withdraw) EPF when changing jobs within 5 years?
> **A:** Withdrawing within 5 years is **taxable**; transferring is **tax-free** and **adds the old service period** to total service, preserving compounding.

**Q16.** How does NPS handle a job change compared with EPF?
> **A:** Easier — you **cannot have multiple NPS accounts**; the same **PRAN** moves with you, or continues under the **All Citizen Model**.

**Q17.** What are the four evaluation factors for a pre-retirement (accumulation) product?
> **A:** **Cost, Return, Risk, Tax efficiency.**

**Q18.** Which products best fit the pre-retirement accumulation stage?
> **A:** Long-term products: **EPF, NPS** (also PPF and certain mutual funds).

**Q19.** What two factors evaluate income-generating products in the retirement stage?
> **A:** **Inflation** (income must beat it for longevity) and **Capital protection**.

**Q20.** In the retirement stage, two product baskets are needed. What are they?
> **A:** One that **generates income** and one that **grows the corpus** (horizon ~10–15 years).

**Q21.** What are the priorities for products in the post-retirement (beyond ~75) stage?
> **A:** **Capital protection, low return, least risk**, plus **liquidity** to leave money for heirs.

**Q22.** Roughly what fraction of current expenses are retirement expenses (if liabilities cleared)?
> **A:** About **50%–60%** of current expenses (but medical costs rise with age).

**Q23.** What is philanthropy in this context?
> **A:** Giving part of one's income/wealth for the benefit of society and the needy — increasingly done by small earners too, not just the wealthy.

**Q24.** Why should an adviser discuss philanthropy with clients?
> **A:** It strengthens client relationships and **builds trust**, and is a meaningful, natural part of advising.

**Q25.** What are the three steps to discuss philanthropy with a client?
> **A:** (1) **Starting the conversation**, (2) **Timing it right**, (3) **Following up**.

**Q26.** Name two natural moments to raise charitable giving.
> **A:** A **liquidity event** (business sale/inheritance), **drafting/revisiting a will**, a **life event** (retirement, marriage, birth of children/grandchildren), or the **annual client meeting**.

**Q27.** Growth-oriented investments suit which stage of retirement saving?
> **A:** The **accumulation stage** (pre-retirement), where the long horizon allows riding out downside risk.

**Q28.** What does inflation do to the retirement corpus required?
> **A:** It **increases** the corpus required (it raises future expenses).
