# Chapter 6: Miscellaneous Aspects of Retirement Planning — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** Growth-oriented investments are most suitable for which stage of retirement saving?
A) Accumulation stage  B) Distribution stage  C) Income stage  D) Post-retirement stage
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — In the **accumulation (pre-retirement)** stage the horizon is long, so the portfolio can ride out downside risk to grow the corpus.
</details>

**Q2.** Inflation does which of the following to retirement planning?
A) Reduces the periodic savings required  B) Reduces the nominal return  C) Increases the retirement corpus required  D) Increases the value of the corpus created
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Inflation raises future expenses, so it **increases the corpus required**.
</details>

**Q3.** The retirement corpus should be reviewed:
A) Every year only  B) Every time there's a significant change in financial situation  C) Only near the distribution period  D) Not at all
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Because the long horizon involves many changing variables, review whenever the financial situation changes significantly (periodic monitoring).
</details>

**Q4.** Which is a solution to manage inadequacy of the retirement corpus *close to* retirement?
A) Invest in riskier assets  B) Postpone retirement  C) Reduce periodic savings  D) Lower the corpus target arbitrarily
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Near retirement, risk capacity is low, so **postponing retirement** (working longer) is the safe fix. Taking more risk could backfire with no time to recover.
</details>

**Q5.** Life expectancy directly affects which feature of the retirement goal?
A) Period in retirement  B) Monthly expenses in retirement  C) Remaining working years  D) Returns earned on the corpus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Life expectancy sets the **years in retirement** (how long income must last), which drives the corpus size.
</details>

**Q6.** The future cost of an expense is calculated as:
A) Present × (1 − inflation)^years  B) Present × (1 + inflation)^years  C) Present ÷ (1 + inflation)^years  D) Present × inflation × years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Inflation compounds, so **Future = Present × (1 + inflation)^years**.
</details>

**Q7.** With 7% inflation, Rs. 10,000/month today is needed at what level in 25 years?
A) Rs. 25,000  B) Rs. 54,724  C) Rs. 70,000  D) Rs. 1,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 10,000 × (1.07)^25 ≈ **Rs. 54,724/month**.
</details>

**Q8.** The real rate of return when nominal return is 8% and post-retirement inflation is 6% is:
A) 2.00%  B) 1.89%  C) 14.00%  D) 0.50%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — (1.08 ÷ 1.06) − 1 = **1.89% p.a.**
</details>

**Q9.** Which Excel function is used to calculate the retirement corpus required?
A) FV  B) PMT  C) PV  D) RATE
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — **PV(rate, nper, pmt, [fv], [type])** gives the present value (corpus) of all future inflation-rising expenses, discounted at the real return.
</details>

**Q10.** In the PV calculation for retirement corpus, why is the monthly expense (pmt) entered as a negative number?
A) It's an error  B) It represents a cash outflow (expense)  C) Excel requires negatives for rates  D) To increase the corpus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Expenses are **cash outflows**, so pmt is prefixed with a minus sign.
</details>

**Q11.** Which Excel function calculates the monthly savings needed to reach a corpus?
A) PV  B) PMT  C) NPER  D) IRR
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **PMT(rate, nper, pv, [fv], [type])**, where pv is the target corpus.
</details>

**Q12.** For a Rs. 1.18 crore corpus at 12% return retiring at 60, who needs the smallest monthly saving?
A) Someone starting at 40  B) Someone starting at 35  C) Someone starting at 30  D) All save the same
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Starting at **30** needs only **Rs. 3,376/month** (vs Rs. 6,280 at 35 and Rs. 11,928 at 40) because compounding has more time.
</details>

**Q13.** A step-up investment strategy means:
A) Investing a single lump sum once  B) Increasing contributions periodically over time  C) Decreasing contributions as income falls  D) Withdrawing in steps
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — You **increase contributions** by a percentage each year (or via lump sums), starting lower and rising with income.
</details>

**Q14.** Mr. A needs Rs. 2 crore in 20 years at 12%. The fixed monthly saving required is about:
A) Rs. 10,000  B) Rs. 20,217  C) Rs. 30,000  D) Rs. 8,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Using PMT, about **Rs. 20,217/month**.
</details>

**Q15.** Withdrawing Rs. 75,000 from EPF with 30 years to retirement (8.5% p.a.) potentially costs the corpus about:
A) Rs. 75,000  B) Rs. 2 lakh  C) Rs. 8.66 lakh  D) Rs. 20 lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The lost future value ≈ 75,000 × 1.085^30 ≈ **Rs. 8.66 lakh** — compounding is destroyed by early withdrawal.
</details>

**Q16.** What is a UAN?
A) A pension scheme  B) A 12-digit lifelong EPF identifier  C) An annuity option  D) A tax form
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Universal Account Number**, a 12-digit number that stays the same for life and enables **online EPF transfer** across employers.
</details>

**Q17.** Compared with EPF, transferring an NPS corpus when changing jobs is easier because:
A) NPS pays higher interest  B) You cannot hold multiple NPS accounts; the same PRAN moves with you  C) NPS has no tax rules  D) NPS is government-guaranteed
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — One subscriber = one **PRAN**; it moves with you or continues under the **All Citizen Model**. EPF can fragment into multiple accounts.
</details>

**Q18.** The four factors to evaluate a pre-retirement (accumulation) product are:
A) Cost, Return, Risk, Tax efficiency  B) Inflation, Liquidity, Heirs, Risk  C) Capital protection, Income, Cost, Tax  D) Return, Annuity, Pension, Insurance
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — **Cost, Return, Risk, Tax efficiency** drive accumulation-stage product choice.

**Q19.** Which two factors are used to evaluate income-generating products in the retirement stage?
A) Cost and Return  B) Inflation and Capital protection  C) Risk and Liquidity  D) Tax and Growth
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Income must **beat inflation** (longevity) and the product must offer **capital protection**.

**Q20.** In the post-retirement stage (beyond ~75 years), the primary objective shifts to:

**Q21.** The three steps an adviser follows to discuss philanthropy are:

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Ms. Kavya is **35 years old** and wants to retire at **60**. Her current relevant monthly expense (the part she must replace) is **Rs. 24,000** in today's terms. She assumes **pre-retirement inflation 7%**, **post-retirement inflation 6%**, a **return of 8%** during retirement, and life expectancy to **85** (25 years in retirement). For the accumulation phase she expects **12% returns**.

**Q1.** What will Ms. Kavya's monthly expense be at the start of retirement (age 60)?
<details><summary>Step-by-step answer</summary>

**Future Expense = Present × (1 + inflation)^years**, years = 60 − 35 = 25, inflation 7%.
- = 24,000 × (1.07)^25 = 24,000 × 5.4274 ≈ **Rs. 1,30,258/month**.

This matches Scenario 3 in the workbook.
</details>

**Q2.** What real rate of return should she use to size her retirement corpus, and why?
<details><summary>Step-by-step answer</summary>

**Real return = [(1 + return) ÷ (1 + post-retirement inflation)] − 1**
- = (1.08 ÷ 1.06) − 1 = **1.89% p.a.** (≈ 1.89%/12 per month).

**Why:** During retirement the corpus earns 8% but living costs rise 6%, so only ~1.89% is *real* growth in purchasing power. Discounting future expenses at the real rate automatically accounts for rising costs, so the corpus stays adequate every year.
</details>

**Q3.** Using the Excel PV approach, what corpus does she need at 60? (State the function and inputs.)
<details><summary>Step-by-step answer</summary>

**=PV(rate, nper, pmt)** with:
- **rate** = 1.89%/12 per month
- **nper** = 25 × 12 = **300 months**
- **pmt** = −1,30,258 (the inflated monthly expense, negative because it's an outflow)
- fv/type left blank (0)

Result (from the workbook) ≈ **Rs. 3.11 crore**. This is the lump sum that, growing at the real rate, can pay her rising monthly expenses for 25 years.
</details>

**Q4.** If her target corpus were Rs. 1.18 crore and she invests at 12%, how much must she save monthly starting at 35 versus if she had started at 30? What's the lesson?
<details><summary>Step-by-step answer</summary>

Using **=PMT(rate=12%/12, nper, pv=1.18 crore)**:
- Start at **35** (25 years = 300 months) → **Rs. 6,280/month**.
- Start at **30** (30 years = 360 months) → **Rs. 3,376/month**.

**Lesson:** Starting 5 years earlier nearly **halves** the monthly requirement. Procrastination forces a much larger monthly outflow because compounding has less time — start early and step up contributions with income.
</details>

**Q5.** Midway, Ms. Kavya changes jobs. She has an old EPF balance after 3 years of service. Should she withdraw it or transfer it? Explain the consequences.
<details><summary>Step-by-step answer</summary>

She should **transfer** it (via her **UAN**, online) to the new employer's EPF account.
- **Tax:** Withdrawing EPF before **5 years** of service makes it **taxable**; transferring is **tax-free** and **adds her old 3 years to her total service**, helping her cross the 5-year tax-free threshold.
- **Compounding:** Keeping the corpus invested preserves the **compounding** that builds her retirement goal; withdrawing forces her to "start over" with higher required contributions and risks under-funding retirement.
</details>
