# Chapter 10: Taxation of Debt Products — Short Notes

## 🎯 What you'll learn
- The two ways money comes to you from debt: **interest** and **capital gains**
- How bonds, debentures, G-Secs, T-Bills, tax-free bonds and Market Linked Debentures (MLDs) are taxed
- How the **new (post-2023) mutual fund rules** split funds into 4 types and tax them
- How **Non-Resident Indians (NRIs)** are taxed and how **DTAA** rescues them from double tax

---

## First, the big picture: two kinds of income from debt

Think of a **debt instrument** as a loan you (the investor) give to a company or the government. You don't own a piece of the company (no voting rights, no dividend). You just get:
1. **Interest** — the periodic "rent" on your loaned money. Taxed under the head **"Income from Other Sources"** (unless you trade as a business).
2. **Capital gains** — the profit when you *sell* or *redeem* the instrument for more than you paid. Taxed under the head **"Capital Gains"**.

> **Jargon:** *Assessee* = the taxpayer (the person being assessed for tax). *Redemption* = the issuer paying you back at maturity. *Transfer* = selling to someone else.

### 10.1.1 Interest income — accrual vs receipt (Section 145)
Two accounting methods decide *when* interest is taxed:
- **Mercantile (accrual) system:** tax it when it is *earned/due*, even if not yet received.
- **Cash system:** tax it only when *received*.

**Analogy:** Mercantile is like marking your pocket-money as "mine" the moment your aunt promises it; cash system is marking it "mine" only when the cash hits your hand.

💡 Some interest is fully exempt under **Section 10** (e.g., tax-free bonds — see below).

### 10.1.2 Capital gains basics
Gain/loss on transfer or redemption of a security held as an **investment (capital asset)** is a capital gain. For **Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs)**, securities are *always* a capital asset, so their gains are *always* capital gains. Tax depends on: nature of security, **period of holding**, and residential status.

## 10.2 Types of Debt Products

### 10.2.1 Coupon Bonds
A bond has three features: **maturity** (tenure), **coupon** (interest rate), **principal** (face value). Example: *"7.4% CG Bond 2027"* = Central Govt bond, matures 2027, pays 7.40%.

> **Yield-to-Maturity (YTM):** the interest rate that makes the present value of all future cash flows equal today's market price. (Just the "true return" baked into the price.)

**Tax on interest from bonds:** taxed under "Other Sources" at **normal slab rates**. You may deduct expenses incurred wholly to earn that interest (and commission to a banker to realise it). Exception: some bonds get a *concessional rate* and then **NO deduction** (incl. 80C–80U) is allowed.

#### 💡 Worked example — interest timing (mercantile vs cash)
Mr. A buys 1,000 bonds @ ₹100, coupon 7.50% p.a., interest paid half-yearly (30 Jun & 31 Dec). Per half-year interest = 1,000 × 100 × 7.50% × 6/12 = **₹3,750**.
For FY 2024-25 (Apr 2024–Mar 2025):
- **Cash system:** taxes whatever is *received* in the year → ₹3,750 (Jun) + ₹3,750 (Dec) = **₹7,500**.
- **Mercantile:** taxes whatever *accrues* in the year. Jan–Mar 2024 interest (₹1,875) belongs to FY23-24; Jan–Mar 2025 interest accrues now (₹1,875). So FY24-25 ≈ ₹1,875 + ₹3,750 + ₹1,875 = ₹7,500 too, but split differently across periods.

#### Holding period & rates for bonds (Table 10.1)
| Type | Period to be **long-term** | Tax rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Listed** bonds/debentures | **12 months** | LTCG: **12.50%** (no indexation); STCG: slab rates |
| **Unlisted** bonds/debentures **or Market Linked Debentures** (even if listed) | Not applicable | **Always Short-Term**, taxed at slab rates, *regardless of how long held* |

⚠️ **Trap:** Unlisted bonds and **all MLDs** can NEVER be long-term. The gain is always short-term → slab rates.

#### 💡 Worked example — LTCG on listed bonds (Example 2)
400 listed bonds bought @ ₹1,200 (01-01-2016), sold @ ₹2,000 (31-07-2024). Held 8 yrs 7 months > 12 months → **long-term**.
- Full value = 400 × 2,000 = ₹8,00,000
- Cost = 400 × 1,200 = ₹4,80,000
- **LTCG = ₹3,20,000**, taxed at **12.50%** = ₹40,000.

If the *same* bonds were **unlisted** (Example 3): the ₹3,20,000 is **short-term** → taxed at slab rates.

#### 💡 Worked example — accrued interest reduces sale price (Example 4)
9% listed bond, FV ₹10,000, bought 01-04-2024 @ ₹10,500; sold 01-11-2024 @ ₹13,000 **inclusive of accrued interest**. Held 7 months → short-term.
- Interest accrued Oct (1 month) = 10,000 × 9% × 1/12 = **₹75** (taxed as interest).
- Adjusted sale price = 13,000 − 75 = ₹12,925.
- STCG = 12,925 − 10,500 = **₹2,425** at slab rates.

⚠️ **Trap:** When a bond is sold "cum-interest," strip out the accrued interest first (taxed as interest), THEN compute capital gain. You can't tax the same rupee twice.

#### NRI long-term gains on bonds (Table 10.3)
| Section | Assessee | Income | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **115E** | Non-Resident Indian | LTCG on Govt securities or listed debentures of Indian public co. bought in foreign currency | **12.50%** (no indexation, no forex benefit) |
| **112(1)(c)(iii)** | Non-resident | LTCG on any **unlisted** security (other than a bond/debenture) | **12.50%** (no indexation, no forex benefit) |

### 10.2.2 Government Securities (G-Secs)
A **G-Sec** is a tradable debt of the Central/State Govt — the safest investment ("risk-free gilt-edged"). Types:
- **Cash Management Bills (CMBs):** very short (<91 days), issued at discount, no coupon.
- **Treasury Bills (T-Bills):** 91/182/364 days, issued at discount, no interest. Return = face value − issue price.
- **Dated G-Secs:** 5–40 yrs, fixed/floating coupon, half-yearly.
- **State Development Loans (SDLs):** State govt bonds, half-yearly interest.

**Tax:**
- **CMBs & T-Bills:** the discount gain on redemption/transfer is **always Short-Term Capital Gain** → slab rates.
- **Dated G-Secs & SDLs:** taxed **like listed bonds**.

⚠️ No TDS under **Section 193** on interest on Central/State Govt securities to residents — **except** 8% Savings (Taxable) Bonds 2003 and 7.75% Savings (Taxable) Bonds 2018, and even there TDS only if interest > ₹10,000/year.

### 10.2.3 Tax-Free Bonds
**Interest is fully exempt** under Section 10 (issued by notified bodies like NABARD). BUT **capital gains on sale/redemption are STILL taxable** (same as coupon bonds).

#### 💡 Worked example (Example 10)
5,000 listed tax-free NABARD bonds @ ₹120, redeemed @ ₹150 (held > 12 months).
- Interest: **exempt** (₹0 tax).
- LTCG = (150 − 120) × 5,000 = **₹1,50,000** at **12.50%**.

## 10.3 Mutual Funds

A mutual fund pools investors' money and invests it. Earnings come as **(1) Capital Gains** and **(2) Dividends (IDCW = Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal)**.

### The NEW 4-way classification (effective FY 2023-24)
Before FY23-24 there were just 2 types. Now **four**:
1. **Equity-oriented MF** (covered in Chapter 11)
2. **Specified MF Type 1** — invests **65% or more** in debt/money-market (directly or indirectly) AND bought **after 01-04-2023**
3. **Specified MF Type 2** — *not* Type 1; invested **less than 35%** in Indian equity directly, bought **on/after 01-04-2023 but sold before 01-04-2025** (e.g., debt-hybrid, gold/silver funds, international funds, FoF in that window)
4. **All Other Mutual Funds**

### 10.3.3 Tax on dividend from MFs
- **Resident:** taxed at slab rates. Can deduct interest expense to earn the dividend, **capped at 20% of dividend**. No other expense allowed.
- **Non-resident:** taxed at **20%** (subject to DTAA), **NO expense deduction**, **no Chapter VI-A (80C–80U)** even under old regime.

#### 💡 Worked example (Example 11)
Resident invests ₹50 lakh in debt MF, gets dividend ₹5,00,000, pays ₹1,50,000 interest on loan.
- Max deductible = 20% × 5,00,000 = **₹1,00,000** (even though ₹1,50,000 was paid).
- **Taxable dividend = ₹4,00,000** at slab rates.
- If **non-resident:** entire **₹5,00,000 taxed at 20%**, no deduction.

### 10.3.4 / 10.3.5 Specified MF (Type 1 & Type 2) — Section 50AA
For SMFs (and MLDs), **Section 50AA** says: **all gains are Short-Term Capital Gains regardless of holding period**, taxed at **normal slab rates**.
- Applies *prospectively* — only to SMFs **acquired on/after 01-04-2023**. SMFs bought on/before 31-03-2023 follow normal "Other MF" rules.
- Computation: Full value − (cost of units + transfer expenses) = STCG.

### 10.3.6 Other Mutual Funds
- **Listed** units → long-term if held **> 12 months**. **Unlisted** units → long-term if held **> 24 months**.
- **LTCG = 12.50%**. **STCG = slab rates**.

#### 💡 Worked example (Example 12)
1,000 units of a debt-oriented MF @ ₹150 (01-01-2017), sold @ ₹300 (15-03-2025). Held 8+ years > 24 months → **long-term**. (Bought before 01-04-2023, so it's "Other MF," not SMF.)
- LTCG = (300 − 150) × 1,000 = **₹1,50,000** at **12.50%**.

## 10.4 Taxation of Non-Residents (NRIs)

**Problem:** double taxation — taxed in India *and* in the country of residence.

**Solution: DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement).** Where eligible, the NRI is taxed at the rate in the **DTAA or the Income Tax Act, whichever is more beneficial** (lower).

#### 💡 Worked examples
- Mr. A (USA resident) earns ₹10,00,000 interest from Indian debentures. India-USA DTAA Article 11 → concessional **15%**. Plus tax credit in USA for Indian tax paid.
- Dividend ₹2,20,000 from Indian shares: Act = 20%, India-USA DTAA = 25% → pick **20%** (Act is lower). If he were a UK resident, India-UK DTAA = **10%** → pick 10%.

**To claim DTAA:** NRI must obtain a **Tax Residency Certificate (TRC)** + file **Form 10F** (self-declaration).

### Chapter XII-A — concessional regime for NRIs (Indian citizens / PIO)
- For **Indian citizens or Persons of Indian Origin (PIO)** who are non-resident. (*PIO* = he/parent/grandparent born in undivided India.)
- Investments must be in **convertible foreign exchange** while non-resident.
- **Eligible investments:** shares of Indian company; debentures/deposits in a non-private Indian company; certain Govt securities.
- **No indexation.** Investment income capped at **20%**; LTCG at **12.50%** (+ surcharge + cess).
- **Reinvestment exemption:** if net consideration is reinvested within **6 months** in eligible assets, LTCG is exempt (fully if all reinvested, proportionately if partly). If the new asset is sold within **3 years**, the earlier-exempt gain becomes taxable.
- Option to keep 20% rate on debenture/deposit/G-Sec income even after becoming resident; can opt out while non-resident.

### Section 89A — overseas retirement funds
For Indian residents who once held a foreign retirement account (USA, Canada, UK notified), income is taxed in India **at the time of withdrawal**, not on accrual — fixing the timing mismatch.

## 10.5 Market Linked Debentures (MLDs)
A debenture whose **returns are linked to a market index/security**, not a fixed coupon. Underlying debt component, returns paid only at maturity.
- **Principal Protected** (principal guaranteed) vs **Non-Protected** (principal at risk).
- Issued by companies with **net worth ≥ ₹100 crore**; mainly for HNIs; tenure usually **12–36 months**.

**Tax (Section 50AA):** MLDs transferred/redeemed/matured **on/after 01-04-2023** are taxed as **Short-Term Capital Gain irrespective of holding period** (slab rates) — *even if issued/bought earlier*.

### 10.6 Benefits NOT allowed under Section 50AA (MLDs & SMFs)
- **No benefit of holding period** → always short-term.
- **No cost of improvement** → taken as **nil** (even if incurred by previous owner).

## 📌 One-Page Recap
- **Interest** → "Other Sources," slab rates (mercantile = accrual, cash = receipt). **Tax-free bond interest** = exempt under §10, but its capital gains are still taxed.
- **Listed bonds/debentures/G-Secs:** LT if held > 12 months → **12.50%**; else slab.
- **Unlisted bonds, T-Bills, MLDs, both SMF types:** gains are **always short-term → slab rates**.
- **MLDs & SMFs** governed by **§50AA**: no holding-period benefit, no cost of improvement.
- **Other MF:** LT if > 24 months (unlisted) / > 12 months (listed) → 12.50%.
- **MF dividends:** resident slab (deduct interest up to 20%); NR 20%, no deduction.
- **NRIs:** rescued from double tax by **DTAA** (lower of DTAA/Act); need **TRC + Form 10F**. **Chapter XII-A** gives Indian-citizen/PIO NRIs 20% on investment income, 12.50% LTCG, plus a 6-month reinvestment exemption.
