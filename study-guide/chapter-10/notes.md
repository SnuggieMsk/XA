# Chapter 10: Taxation of Debt Products — Short Notes

## 🎯 What you'll learn
- The two ways money comes from debt products: **interest income** and **capital gains**, and how each is taxed.
- The main debt products: coupon bonds, government securities (G-Secs, T-Bills, SDLs), tax-free bonds, market linked debentures (MLDs).
- How mutual funds are taxed after the big post-2023 rule changes (the four new categories).
- How Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are taxed, including DTAA relief.

> Think of a **debt instrument** as you lending money to a company or government. You don't own a piece of them (no voting, no share of profit), but they promise to (a) pay you **interest** on the loan and (b) return your money (**principal/redemption value**) at the end.

---

## 10.1 The two sources of income from debt

When you hold a debt product, money reaches you in two flavours:

| Source | What it is | Taxed under the head |
|---|---|---|
| **Interest income** | The periodic payment for lending (the "coupon") | **Income from Other Sources** (unless it's your business) |
| **Capital gains** | Profit/loss when you sell or redeem the instrument | **Capital Gains** |

### Interest income — accrual vs receipt (Section 145)
Income from interest is computed using the **method of accounting** you regularly follow:
- **Mercantile (accrual) system** → interest is taxed when it *becomes due* (accrues), even if not yet received.
- **Cash system** → interest is taxed only when *actually received*.

**Analogy:** Mercantile is like marking a chore "done" the moment it's owed to you; cash is marking it only when the cash lands in your pocket.

💡 You can deduct expenses spent *wholly and exclusively* to earn the interest (e.g., a banker's commission to collect it).

⚠️ Some special bonds are taxed at a **concessional (lower) rate** — but then **no deductions at all** (not even Sections 80C–80U) are allowed against that interest.

### Capital gains basics
Gain/loss on transfer or redemption of a security is a **capital gain** if you held it as a **capital asset** (i.e., as an investment).
- **FPIs (Foreign Portfolio Investors):** their securities are **always** capital assets → always taxed as capital gains.
- Tax depends on: **nature of security, period of holding, residential status**.

---

## 10.2 Types of Debt Products

### 10.2.1 Coupon Bonds
A bond pays a fixed **coupon** (interest) and returns **face value** at maturity. Example: *7.4% CG Bond 2027* = a Central Government bond maturing 2027 paying 7.40%.

**Market value** = present value of all future cash flows. The discount rate used is the **Yield-to-Maturity (YTM)**.

#### Tax on bond interest
Taxed under **Other Sources** at **normal slab rates**. Deduct expenses wholly/exclusively for earning it.

**💡 Worked example (mercantile vs cash):**
Mr. A buys bonds, 7.5% coupon, interest paid half-yearly (Jun 30 & Dec 31).
- Under **mercantile**, interest *accrues* each period (so April–June accrues into 2024-25 even if paid later).
- Under **cash**, you count the full amount only when received.

#### Tax on capital gains from bonds — holding period & rates (Table 10.1)

| Bond type | Period to be **long-term** | Long-term rate | Short-term rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Listed** bonds/debentures | **12 months** | **12.50%** (no indexation) | applicable slab rates |
| **Unlisted** bonds/debentures **or any Market Linked Debenture (even if listed)** | Not applicable | — | **Always short-term**, taxed at applicable rates **regardless of holding period** |

**LTCG computation (listed bonds):**
Full value of consideration − Cost of acquisition − Transfer expenses − exemptions (Sec 54EE–54F) = LTCG.

**💡 Worked example (listed, LTCG):** 400 listed bonds bought @₹1,200 (01-01-2016), sold @₹2,000 (31-07-2024).
- Held 8 yr 7 mth (>12 mth) → **long-term**.
- Sale 400×2,000 = ₹8,00,000; Cost 400×1,200 = ₹4,80,000 → **LTCG ₹3,20,000 @ 12.50%**.

**⚠️ Same bonds but UNLISTED:** the ₹3,20,000 is taxed as **short-term capital gain at normal slab rates** — unlisted bonds are *always* short-term, no matter how long held.

**💡 Worked example (short-term, interest stripped out):** 9% listed bond, face ₹10,000, bought @₹10,500 (01-04-2024), sold 01-11-2024 for ₹13,000 *inclusive of accrued interest*.
- Interest accrued to 31-10 = ₹10,000 × 9% × 1/12 = ₹75.
- Adjusted sale price = ₹13,000 − ₹75 = ₹12,925.
- STCG = ₹12,925 − ₹10,500 = **₹2,425** (taxed at applicable rate). The ₹75 is taxed separately as interest.

⚠️ **Trap:** Accrued-but-unreceived interest baked into the sale price must be *carved out* and taxed as interest, NOT as capital gain.

#### Non-residents — LTCG on bonds (Table 10.3)
| Section | Who | What | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **115E** | Non-Resident Indian | LTCG on Govt securities or listed debentures of an Indian public company **bought in foreign currency** | **12.50%** (no indexation/forex benefit) |
| **112(1)(c)(iii)** | Non-resident | LTCG on any unlisted security (other than bond/debenture) | **12.50%** (no indexation/forex benefit) |

For listed bonds, a non-resident's LTCG is **always 12.50%, no indexation**.

### 10.2.2 Government Securities (G-Secs)
A **G-Sec** is a tradable instrument acknowledging the Government's debt — the **safest** investment ("risk-free gilt-edged"). Types:

| Type | Tenure | Interest? | How return arises |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cash Management Bills (CMBs)** | < 91 days | No | Bought at discount, redeemed at face value |
| **Treasury Bills (T-Bills)** | 91 / 182 / 364 days (≤1 yr) | No | Discount to face value |
| **Dated G-Secs** | 5–40 yrs | Yes (half-yearly) | Coupon + principal at maturity |
| **State Development Loans (SDLs)** | long | Yes (half-yearly) | Issued by State Governments |

**Taxability:**
- **CMBs & T-Bills:** no interest; profit on redemption/transfer is **short-term capital gain** at applicable rates (since maturity <1 year).
- **Dated G-Secs & SDLs:** taxed **like listed bonds** (12.50% LTCG if held >12 months and listed).

💡 **No TDS** under Section 193 on interest paid to residents on Central/State Govt securities — **except** 8% Savings (Taxable) Bonds 2003 and 7.75% Savings (Taxable) Bonds 2018, where TDS applies only if interest in the year exceeds **₹10,000**.

### 10.2.3 Tax-Free Bonds
- **Interest is fully exempt** under Section 10 (e.g., NABARD bonds).
- **Capital gains on transfer/redemption are STILL taxable** — same as coupon bonds.

**💡 Worked example:** 5,000 tax-free NABARD bonds bought @₹120 (listed), redeemed @₹150.
- Interest = exempt (no tax).
- LTCG = (150−120)×5,000 = **₹1,50,000 @ 12.50%** (held >12 months, listed).

## 10.3 Mutual Funds

A **mutual fund** pools investors' money and invests it. Types: equity-oriented, debt-oriented, money market/liquid, balanced/hybrid, gold/silver, international, fund-of-funds. Any of these can be listed as an **ETF** (bought/sold on the exchange).

### The BIG change: four tax categories from FY 2023-24
Until FY 2022-23 there were just **two** buckets (equity-oriented & others). Now there are **four**:

| # | Category | Defining test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Equity-Oriented Funds** | (Covered in Chapter 11) |
| 2 | **Specified Mutual Fund (SMF) Type 1** | ≥ **65%** in debt/money-market (directly or indirectly), bought **after 01-04-2023** |
| 3 | **Specified Mutual Fund (SMF) Type 2** | Other funds that invested **< 35%** in Indian equity, bought **after 01-04-2023 but sold before 01-04-2025** (debt-hybrids, gold/silver, international, FoF) |
| 4 | **Other Mutual Funds** | Everything else |

### 10.3.3 Dividend from mutual funds (IDCW)
- **Resident:** taxed at slab rates. Can deduct **interest expense up to 20% of dividend**. No other expense allowed.
- **Non-resident:** taxed at flat **20%** (subject to DTAA), **no deductions at all** (not even Chapter VIA / 80C–80U).

**💡 Worked example:** Mr. A (resident) gets ₹5,00,000 dividend, paid ₹1,50,000 interest on the borrowed money.
- Max deductible interest = 20% × 5,00,000 = ₹1,00,000.
- Taxable dividend = 5,00,000 − 1,00,000 = **₹4,00,000** at slab rates.
- If he were **non-resident**: full **₹5,00,000 taxed @20%**, no deduction.

### 10.3.4 / 10.3.5 Capital gains on SMF Type 1 & Type 2 — Section 50AA
**Rule:** Gains on SMFs are **ALWAYS short-term, regardless of holding period**, taxed at normal slab rates.
- Section 50AA applies only to SMFs **acquired on or after 01-04-2023**. SMFs bought on/before 31-03-2023 follow normal mutual-fund rules.
- STCG = Full value − cost of acquisition − transfer expenses.

⚠️ **Trap:** A debt fund you've held 6 years still gives you *short-term* gains if it's a post-April-2023 SMF. No long-term benefit, no 12.5% rate.

### 10.3.6 Capital gains on Other Mutual Funds
| | Listed | Unlisted |
|---|---|---|
| Period to be **long-term** | **12 months** | **24 months** |
| **LTCG rate** | **12.50%** | **12.50%** |
| **STCG** | applicable slab rate | applicable slab rate |

**💡 Worked example:** 1,000 units of a debt-oriented MF bought @₹150 (01-01-2017, *pre-April-2023, so an "Other MF"*), sold @₹300 (15-03-2025). Held 8+ years (>24 months) → **LTCG = ₹1,50,000 @ 12.50%**.

## 10.4 Taxation of Non-Residents

A non-resident risks **double taxation** (taxed in India *and* home country). Solution: **DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement)**.
- Where eligible, a non-resident pays tax at the rate in the **DTAA or the Income Tax Act, whichever is more beneficial**.
- **Example:** Mr. A (NR, US resident) earns ₹10,00,000 interest. India-USA DTAA Article 11 caps it at **15%**, and he gets US tax credit for Indian tax paid.
- **Dividends:** Act rate = 20%. If India-USA DTAA = 25%, he picks the lower Act rate (20%). If he's a UK resident, India-UK DTAA = 10% → picks 10%.

📄 To claim DTAA: need a **Tax Residency Certificate (TRC)** + **Form 10F** (self-declaration).

### Chapter XII-A (concessional regime for NRIs of Indian origin)
- For **Indian citizens / Persons of Indian Origin** who are non-residents, investing **convertible foreign exchange** in Indian shares, debentures/deposits (in non-private cos.), certain Govt securities.
- **No indexation.** Investment income capped at **20%**; LTCG at **12.50%**.
- **Reinvestment exemption:** if LTCG is reinvested within **6 months** in eligible assets → exempt (fully if all net consideration reinvested, proportionately if partial). But if the new asset is sold within **3 years**, the earlier-exempted gain is taxed in that year.

### Section 89A — overseas retirement funds
For residents who once worked abroad: income from **specified retirement accounts** in **USA, Canada, UK** is taxed **on withdrawal**, not on accrual — removing a double-tax mismatch.

## 10.5 Market Linked Debentures (MLDs)
An **MLD** is a debenture whose return is tied to a market index/security, not a fixed coupon. Two types: **Principal Protected** and **Principal Non-Protected**. Issued by companies with net worth ≥ ₹100 crore; tenure usually 12–36 months; returns paid at maturity.

**Tax (Section 50AA, from FY 2023-24):** Gains on MLDs **transferred/redeemed/matured on or after 01-04-2023** are **ALWAYS short-term**, taxed at slab rates — even if the MLD was bought earlier and held for years.

## 10.6 Benefits denied under Section 50AA (MLDs + SMF Type 1 & 2)
- **No benefit of holding period** → always short-term.
- **No cost of improvement** → taken as **nil**.

## 📌 One-Page Recap
- **Interest** = Other Sources, slab rate (accrual or cash per your accounting method).
- **Capital gain** = depends on listed/unlisted + holding period + residential status.
- **Listed bonds/G-Secs/SGB:** LTCG (>12 mth) @ **12.50%**, no indexation.
- **Unlisted bonds:** **always short-term** (slab rate).
- **MLDs and SMF Type 1 & 2 (Section 50AA):** **always short-term**, no holding-period or cost-of-improvement benefit.
- **Other (debt) MFs:** listed long-term at >12 mth, unlisted at >24 mth; LTCG @12.50%.
- **Dividend from MF:** resident slab (20% interest deduction); non-resident flat 20%, no deduction.
- **NRIs:** DTAA or Act, whichever lower; need TRC + Form 10F.
