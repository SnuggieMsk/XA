## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** All investment instruments are compared across which three broad parameters?
A) Tax, Tenure, Trust  B) Risk, Return, Liquidity  C) Safety, Speed, Simplicity  D) Cost, Coverage, Compliance
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The workbook states every investment is compared on **Risk, Return and Liquidity**. These are the three universal yardsticks (with tax and lock-in often added). A, C and D mix up real factors with invented catchy lists; none of them is the trio the workbook names. Think of it as: how much can I lose (risk), how much do I earn (return), and how fast can I get my cash back (liquidity).
</details>

**Q2.** For which products is performance-data disclosure specifically **mandated by SEBI**?
A) Bank FDs and PPF  B) Physical gold and real estate  C) Mutual Funds and Portfolio Management Services  D) ULIPs and life insurance
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The workbook says disclosure is mandated by SEBI for **Mutual Funds and PMS**. A (FD/PPF) are bank/government products, not SEBI-mandated disclosure. B (gold/real estate) have no such mandate. D (ULIPs/insurance) are regulated by IRDAI, not SEBI. SEBI regulates market-linked securities products, which is why MF and PMS data must be disclosed.
</details>

**Q3.** Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) invests:
A) Exclusively in equities  B) Only in government securities  C) Mostly in corporate bonds  D) Equally in gold and equity
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The workbook defines ELSS as a scheme that invests **exclusively in equities**. B, C and D describe debt or other assets, which an ELSS does not focus on. Because it is all-equity, its risk of capital loss is high — that is the trade-off for its tax benefit.
</details>

**Q4.** What is the lock-in period of an ELSS?
A) 5 years  B) 1 year  C) 15 years  D) 3 years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — ELSS has a **3-year lock-in**, after which it can be withdrawn anytime. A (5 years) is the ULIP lock-in. C (15 years) is the PPF lock-in. B (1 year) is invented. ELSS has the shortest lock-in among the common Section 80C tax-savers.
</details>

**Q5.** The risk of capital loss in PPF is described as:
A) High  B) Low (sovereign risk)  C) Moderate  D) Very high
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — PPF carries **Low** risk because it is a government scheme (sovereign risk). A and D (high) describe equity products like ELSS. C (moderate) describes NPS Tier 1. The government backing is what makes PPF among the safest options.
</details>

**Q6.** Capital gains on ELSS are taxed at what rate (per the workbook)?
A) 10%  B) 15%  C) 12.50%  D) 20%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The workbook states ELSS capital gains are taxed at **12.50%**. The other figures are plausible-looking tax rates but are not the number given. Memorise 12.50% as the ELSS capital-gains rate cited in this chapter.
</details>

**Q7.** Under NPS Tier 1, what is the maximum equity exposure allowed for selected subscribers?
A) 100%  B) 50%  C) 75%  D) 25%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — NPS Tier 1 allows equity investment up to a maximum of **75%** for selected subscribers. A (100%) is ELSS, not NPS. B and D are not the stated cap. NPS also allows G-secs, corporate bonds and up to 5% Alternative Investments for certain subscribers.
</details>

**Q8.** At age 60, what portion of the NPS Tier 1 corpus can be withdrawn tax-free?
A) 40%  B) 100%  C) 50%  D) 60%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — **60%** of the NPS corpus can be withdrawn tax-free at age 60. A (40%) is the part that must be used to buy an annuity. B and C are wrong fractions. So: 60% tax-free lump sum, 40% compulsorily annuitised.
</details>

**Q9.** The 40% of the NPS corpus not withdrawn tax-free must be used to:
A) Buy gold bonds  B) Buy annuity product(s) from an insurance company  C) Reinvest in equity  D) Pay tax dues
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The remaining **40% must buy annuity product(s) from an insurance company**, and that annuity income is fully taxable. A, C and D are not requirements. The design forces a retirement income stream rather than a one-time spend.
</details>

**Q10.** What is the maximum annual contribution allowed in PPF?
A) Rs. 1,00,000  B) Rs. 1,50,000  C) Rs. 2,50,000  D) Rs. 50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — PPF allows a maximum contribution of **Rs. 1,50,000 each year**. A and D are lower invented figures. C (Rs. 2.5 lakh) is the ULIP premium threshold, not the PPF cap. The Rs. 1.5 lakh figure aligns with the Section 80C limit.
</details>

**Q11.** Tax on PPF returns is:
A) 12.50%  B) Nil (tax-free)  C) 10%  D) Taxable each year
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — PPF returns attract **Nil tax** (tax-free), giving a moderate but attractive post-tax return. A is the ELSS rate. C is invented. D describes a tax-saving bank FD, whose interest is taxable each year. Tax-free status is a key PPF advantage.
</details>

**Q12.** What is the lock-in period for a PPF account?
A) 3 years  B) 5 years  C) 15 years  D) 8 years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — PPF has a **15-year lock-in** from inception, with partial withdrawal possible after 5 years. A is the ELSS lock-in, B is the ULIP lock-in, D is the SGB final-redemption period. PPF is a long-term commitment.
</details>

**Q13.** Interest on a tax-saving bank FD is:
A) Tax-free  B) Taxable each year  C) Taxed only at maturity at 12.50%  D) Exempt up to Rs. 1.5 lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A tax-saving bank FD's returns are **taxable each year**, giving moderately low post-tax returns. A (tax-free) describes PPF. C and D are invented treatments. Yearly taxation is exactly why the FD's post-tax return is lower than PPF's even at similar headline rates.
</details>

**Q14.** What is the minimum investment size for Portfolio Management Services (PMS)?
A) Rs. 1,000  B) Rs. 1 crore  C) Rs. 50 lakh  D) Rs. 5 lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — PMS has a minimum investment of **Rs. 50 lakh**. A (Rs. 1,000) is the mutual fund minimum. B (Rs. 1 crore) is the AIF minimum. D is invented. The ladder is: MF Rs. 1,000 → PMS Rs. 50 lakh → AIF Rs. 1 crore.
</details>

**Q15.** What is the minimum investment size for an Alternative Investment Fund (AIF)?
A) Rs. 50 lakh  B) Rs. 1 crore  C) Rs. 25 lakh  D) Rs. 10 lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — AIFs require a minimum of **Rs. 1 crore**. A (Rs. 50 lakh) is for PMS. C and D are invented. AIFs sit at the top of the minimum-investment ladder because they suit HNIs seeking complex strategies.
</details>

**Q16.** In an equity mutual fund, the minimum investment can be as low as:
A) Rs. 50 lakh  B) Rs. 1 crore  C) Rs. 1,000  D) Rs. 10,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Mutual funds can be entered with as little as **Rs. 1,000**, making them suitable for retail investors. A and B are the PMS and AIF minimums (far higher). D is plausible but not the figure given. The low minimum is what makes MFs mass-market.
</details>

**Q17.** Operating expenses of a PMS are capped at:
A) 1.00%  B) 0.50%  C) 2.50%  D) No cap
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — In PMS, upfront charges are not allowed and **operating expenses are capped at 0.50%**; the management/performance fee is as agreed. A and C are invented. D is wrong because there IS a cap on operating expenses (the fee itself is negotiable, but operating expenses are capped).
</details>

**Q18.** A mutual fund is managed by an AMC; a ULIP is managed by:
A) A bank  B) SEBI  C) A life insurance company  D) The investor directly
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A ULIP is a pooled scheme managed by a **life insurance company**, whereas a mutual fund is managed by an Asset Management Company. A, B and D are not the managers. This difference is why ULIPs are regulated by IRDAI and MFs by SEBI.
</details>

**Q19.** The purpose of a ULIP is best described as:
A) Investment only  B) Insurance only  C) Insurance cum investment  D) Tax payment
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A ULIP is **insurance cum investment**, combining life cover with market-linked investing. A (investment only) describes a mutual fund. B (insurance only) describes a pure term plan. D is nonsense. The dual purpose is the defining ULIP feature.
</details>

**Q20.** ULIP maturity proceeds are tax-free if the sum assured is at least how many times the annual premium?
A) 5 times  B) 10 times  C) 20 times  D) 2 times
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Withdrawals/maturity are tax-free if the **sum assured is at least 10 times the annual premium**. A, C and D are wrong multiples. This "10x" rule is a classic exam number; pair it with the Rs. 2.5 lakh premium threshold (post-1 Feb 2021).
</details>

**Q21.** Per the Finance Act 2021, ULIPs issued on/after 1 Feb 2021 lose tax exemption if any year's premium exceeds:
A) Rs. 1.5 lakh  B) Rs. 2.5 lakh  C) Rs. 5 lakh  D) Rs. 1 lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 10(10D) (as amended) removes exemption for such ULIPs if premium in any year exceeds **Rs. 2.5 lakh**. A (Rs. 1.5 lakh) is the PPF/80C limit. C and D are invented. Below Rs. 2.5 lakh the maturity stays exempt.
</details>

**Q22.** Compared to mutual funds, ULIP disclosure standards are:
A) Higher and more transparent  B) Identical  C) Lower, with fewer comparison services  D) Not regulated at all
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — ULIPs have **lower disclosure standards and less availability of comparison services**, while mutual funds have transparent daily/periodic disclosures. A is the opposite. B is wrong as they differ. D is wrong as ULIPs are regulated by IRDAI. Transparency favours mutual funds.
</details>

**Q23.** A ULIP requires compulsory premium contribution for at least how many years (except single-premium plans)?
A) 3 years  B) 5 years  C) 10 years  D) 1 year
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — ULIPs need compulsory contribution for **5 years** with no redemption for at least 5 years; discontinuation charges apply if premiums stop earlier. A is the ELSS lock-in. C and D are wrong. The 5-year rule is the ULIP liquidity constraint.
</details>

**Q24.** An actively managed fund aims to:
A) Replicate the benchmark exactly  B) Generate additional return (alpha) over the benchmark  C) Avoid all equities  D) Guarantee returns
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — An active fund manager seeks to **generate alpha** (return above the benchmark) by selecting securities. A describes an index fund. C and D are not the objective. The trade-off is higher cost for the chance of higher net return.
</details>

**Q25.** Compared to an actively managed fund, an index fund's cost is:
A) Higher  B) Lower  C) The same  D) Zero
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — An index fund is **lower cost** because it simply replicates the benchmark instead of paying for active decision-making. A is the active fund. C is wrong as they differ. D is wrong — index funds still have (low) costs. Lower cost is the index fund's core appeal.
</details>

**Q26.** Direct equity investing carries what kind of risk relative to equity funds?
A) Diversified risk  B) Concentrated risk on the invested security  C) No risk  D) Sovereign risk
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Direct equity carries **concentrated risk** on the specific stocks chosen, while equity funds spread risk across many securities (diversification). A describes the fund. C and D are wrong. Diversification is the fund's safety advantage over single-stock buying.
</details>

**Q27.** An Exchange Traded Fund (ETF) is priced:
A) At end-of-day NAV only  B) On a minute-to-minute basis like any security  C) On the last-3-days average  D) Once a week
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — An ETF trades on the exchange and is priced **minute-to-minute** like any security. A (end-of-day NAV) describes an index fund. C is SGB issue pricing. D is invented. Live pricing is what distinguishes an ETF from an ordinary index fund.
</details>

**Q28.** An index fund (non-ETF) is priced at:
A) Live intraday prices  B) End-of-day NAV  C) Last-3-days average  D) Auction price
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A regular index fund is bought/sold from the AMC at the **end-of-day NAV**. A (intraday) describes an ETF. C is SGB pricing. D is invented. The NAV-based, once-a-day pricing is standard for non-exchange-traded funds.
</details>

**Q29.** Per the workbook, liquidity of ETFs on Indian stock exchanges is generally:
A) Excellent for all ETFs  B) Very poor except for a couple of ETFs  C) Guaranteed by SEBI  D) Better than physical cash
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The workbook warns that ETF liquidity on Indian exchanges is **very poor except for a couple of ETFs**, with large NAV-vs-price gaps and wide buy-sell spreads. A overstates it. C and D are invented. This is a key exam trap — ETFs sound liquid but often aren't here.
</details>

**Q30.** Which gold form does NOT incur a GST cost on purchase by the investor?
A) Physical gold  B) Gold fund  C) Gold ETF  D) Sovereign Gold Bond
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — **Gold ETF** does not incur GST cost (the workbook marks GST cost "No" for Gold ETF). Physical gold (A) incurs GST. Gold funds (B) and SGBs (D) involve physical gold purchase where GST is incurred but accounted as an asset. So among these, Gold ETF is the GST-free entry.
</details>

**Q31.** Which gold investment pays interest of 2.50% p.a.?
A) Physical gold  B) Gold fund  C) Sovereign Gold Bond  D) Gold ETF
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Only the **Sovereign Gold Bond** pays **2.50% p.a. interest**, payable semi-annually. Physical gold (A), gold funds (B) and gold ETFs (D) produce no income — only capital gains. The interest is a unique SGB advantage.
</details>

**Q32.** When is a Sovereign Gold Bond first redeemable on its anniversary date, and when is its final redemption?
A) After 3 years; final after 5 years  B) After 5 years; final after 8 years  C) After 1 year; final after 5 years  D) After 8 years; final after 15 years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — SGBs are redeemable on anniversary dates **after 5 years**, with **final redemption after 8 years**. The other combinations are invented. If redeemed with RBI there is no capital-gains tax; selling in the market earlier gives only moderate liquidity.
</details>

**Q33.** In the workbook's table, REITs are described as investing primarily in:

A, B and D are not what the table lists. Always answer per the source table.
</details>

**Q34.** Personal Accident Insurance covers death due to:
A) Any cause  B) Accident only  C) Illness only  D) Old age only
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Personal Accident Insurance covers death due to **accident only**, while life insurance covers death due to any cause. A describes life insurance. C and D are not what PA covers. PA also uniquely covers disability arising from accident.
</details>

**Q35.** Between a company deposit and a company debenture from the same company, which is typically secured?
A) Company deposit  B) Company debenture  C) Both equally  D) Neither
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A **company debenture** is normally secured, so it carries relatively lower risk; a company deposit is unsecured (higher risk). A is wrong (deposits are unsecured). C and D ignore the security difference. More security → lower risk → slightly lower return on the debenture.
</details>
