# Chapter 19: Comparison of Products Across Categories — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** The three broad parameters for comparing investment instruments are:
A) Tax, brokerage, dividends  B) Risk, return, liquidity  C) Age, income, goals  D) NAV, AUM, expense ratio
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Risk, Return and Liquidity are the universal yardsticks (extended with tax and lock-in).
</details>

**Q2.** Among common 80C tax-savers, which has the shortest lock-in?
A) PPF  B) Tax-saving Bank FD  C) ELSS  D) NPS Tier 1
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — ELSS has a **3-year** lock-in; PPF 15 years, tax-saving FD 5 years, NPS until age 60.
</details>

**Q3.** At age 60, what portion of the NPS Tier-1 corpus can be withdrawn tax-free?
A) 40%  B) 100%  C) 60%  D) 25%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — 60% is tax-free; the remaining 40% must buy an annuity, and the annuity income is fully taxable.
</details>

**Q4.** The minimum investment for a PMS and an AIF respectively is:
A) Rs.1,000 and Rs.50 lakh  B) Rs.50 lakh and Rs.1 crore  C) Rs.1 crore and Rs.50 lakh  D) Rs.25 lakh and Rs.50 lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — PMS = Rs.50 lakh, AIF = Rs.1 crore. (Mutual funds can be as low as Rs.1,000.)
</details>

**Q5.** A ULIP maturity is tax-free (older rules) only if:
A) The premium exceeds Rs.2.5 lakh  B) The sum assured is at least 10× the annual premium  C) It is held for 3 years  D) It is issued by a bank
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Exemption needs sum assured ≥ 10× annual premium. Note: for ULIPs issued on/after 1 Feb 2021, exemption is lost if any year's premium exceeds Rs.2.5 lakh.
</details>

**Q6.** For ULIPs issued on or after 1 Feb 2021, the exemption under Sec 10(10D) is lost if the premium in any year exceeds:
A) Rs.1.5 lakh  B) Rs.2.5 lakh  C) Rs.5 lakh  D) Rs.10 lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The Finance Act 2021 threshold is Rs.2.5 lakh.
</details>

**Q7.** Compared to an actively managed equity fund, an index fund generally has:
A) Higher cost and tries to beat the benchmark  B) Lower cost and tries to replicate the benchmark  C) Higher cost and replicates the benchmark  D) Lower cost and tries to beat the benchmark
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Index funds replicate the benchmark at lower cost; active funds chase alpha at higher cost.
</details>

**Q8.** A major drawback of Gold ETFs on Indian exchanges is:
A) GST on every purchase  B) Storage costs  C) Poor liquidity with wide bid-ask spreads (except a few)  D) Purity concerns
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Indian ETF liquidity is generally poor with large spreads. ETFs notably do NOT incur GST cost to the buyer and have no purity/storage issues.
</details>

**Q9.** Which gold form pays periodic interest?
A) Physical gold  B) Gold ETF  C) Gold Fund  D) Sovereign Gold Bond
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — SGB pays 2.50% p.a., semi-annually; the others pay no income.
</details>

**Q10.** Sovereign Gold Bonds, if redeemed with the RBI at maturity, are:
A) Fully taxable as capital gains  B) Not taxed (no capital gains)  C) Taxed at 12.5%  D) Subject to GST
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — No tax if redeemed with RBI. Capital gains arise only if sold in the market before maturity; interest is always taxable.
</details>

**Q11.** Compared with direct equity, equity mutual funds offer:
A) More concentrated risk  B) Diversification of risk across many securities  C) Guaranteed returns  D) No management cost
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Funds spread risk across many securities; direct equity is concentrated on the chosen stocks.
</details>

**Q12.** For the same company, a company deposit versus a debenture typically is:
A) Secured, lower return  B) Unsecured, higher return  C) Unsecured, lower return  D) Secured, higher return
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Deposits are unsecured (higher risk → higher return); debentures are usually secured (lower risk → lower return).
</details>

**Q13.** The maximum loss for the buyer of an index option is:
A) Unlimited  B) The strike price  C) The premium paid  D) Twice the premium
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — An option buyer's loss is capped at the premium paid.
</details>

**Q14.** Which best describes liquidity of physical real estate vs REITs/InvITs?
A) Real estate is more liquid  B) Both equally liquid  C) Real estate is poor/illiquid; REITs/InvITs are relatively better (may be listed)  D) Neither is liquid
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Real estate is lumpy and illiquid; REITs/InvITs spread exposure and, being listed, have better liquidity.
</details>

**Q15.** Personal Accident insurance differs from life insurance in that it:
A) Covers death from any cause  B) Covers death only due to accident and also covers disability  C) Does not cover disability  D) Replaces the need for life insurance
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Personal accident covers accidental death plus disability; life insurance covers any-cause death but not disability. PA is an add-on, not a substitute.
</details>

**Q16.** Why is computing the post-expense return of a ULIP harder than for a mutual fund?
A) ULIP returns are guaranteed  B) Mortality and other charges are deducted from the fund value  C) ULIPs have no NAV  D) ULIPs are not market-linked
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Insurance (mortality) and other charges are deducted from the fund value, making the true post-expense return difficult to compute. ULIPs are market-linked.
</details>

**Q17.** A Fixed Maturity Plan (FMP) is characterised by:
A) Instant liquidity like a savings account  B) Pre-defined return if held to maturity but very poor liquidity  C) Guaranteed equity returns  D) No lock-in
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — FMPs give a pre-defined return if held to maturity but, though listed, have very poor liquidity.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Mr. Khanna, 45, wants to (a) save tax under 80C, (b) build a long-term retirement corpus, (c) add some gold to his portfolio in the most cost-efficient and income-generating way, and (d) ensure his family is protected if he dies or is disabled in an accident. He is a salaried, moderately conservative investor with a regular income and two dependents.

**Q1.** Between ELSS, PPF and a tax-saving Bank FD for his 80C need, compare risk, lock-in and tax on returns.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- **ELSS:** all-equity → **high** capital-loss risk; **3-year** lock-in (shortest); gains taxed at **12.50%**; market-linked, potentially high returns.
- **PPF:** sovereign → **low** risk; **15-year** lock-in (partial withdrawal after 5 years); returns **tax-free**; max Rs.1.5 lakh/yr.
- **Tax-saving Bank FD:** moderately low risk; locked for the tenure; interest **taxable yearly** → moderately low post-tax return.

For a moderately conservative investor, a blend (e.g., PPF for safety + some ELSS for growth, given his long horizon to retirement) fits — ELSS also gives the quickest exit at 3 years.
</details>

**Q2.** He considers NPS Tier-1 for retirement. Explain the withdrawal/tax rule at age 60 and the risk control available.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

At 60, **60% of the corpus is tax-free** and **40% must be used to buy an annuity** (annuity income is **fully taxable**). Risk can be controlled by choosing a **higher proportion of Government securities** (lowering equity exposure) — suitable for his moderately conservative profile. Equity can go up to 75% for selected subscribers if he wanted more growth.
</details>

**Q3.** For gold, he wants the most cost-efficient AND income-generating option. Which do you recommend, and why over the alternatives?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB).** They pay **2.50% p.a. interest** (no other gold form pays income), have **no GST cost and no storage/safety cost**, no purity concerns, and are **tax-free if redeemed with RBI** at maturity. Physical gold has GST + storage + purity issues; Gold Funds incur GST in the fund; Gold ETFs avoid GST but pay no income and have **poor exchange liquidity** in India. Trade-off: SGB has a longer horizon (redeem after 5 yrs, final 8 yrs), which suits a long-term holder.
</details>

**Q4.** For protection, what mix of life and personal accident insurance do you advise?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Start with **adequate life insurance** (ideally a pure **term plan**) because he has two dependents — life insurance covers death from **any cause**, which is the essential first need. Then **add a Personal Accident policy** as a supplement: it covers **accidental death and disability** (including permanent total disability), which life insurance does **not** cover. PA is an add-on over adequate life cover, not a replacement.
</details>

**Q5.** He also asks whether to invest Rs.10 lakh through PMS instead of mutual funds. What do you tell him?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

He cannot use PMS at that amount: the **minimum PMS investment is Rs.50 lakh** (and AIF is Rs.1 crore). For Rs.10 lakh, **mutual funds** are appropriate (minimum as low as Rs.1,000), with **SEBI-capped fees**, **transparent daily disclosure**, **diversification**, and **anytime redemption** (possible exit load). PMS suits HNIs wanting customised/concentrated portfolios and is held in the investor's own demat account, but it isn't an option here.
</details>
