## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** All investment instruments are compared across which three broad parameters?
A) Tax, Tenure, Trust  B) Risk, Return, Liquidity  C) Safety, Speed, Simplicity  D) Cost, Coverage, Compliance
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The workbook states every investment is compared on **Risk, Return and Liquidity**. These are the three universal yardsticks (with tax and lock-in often added). A, C and D mix up real factors with invented catchy lists; none of them is the trio the workbook names. Think of it as: how much can I lose (risk), how much do I earn (return), and how fast can I get my cash back (liquidity).
</details>

**Q2.** For which products is performance-data disclosure specifically **mandated by SEBI**?
A) Bank FDs and PPF  B) Physical gold and real estate  C) Mutual Funds and Portfolio Management Services  D) ULIPs and life insurance
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The workbook says disclosure is mandated by SEBI for **Mutual Funds and PMS**. A (FD/PPF) are bank/government products, not SEBI-mandated disclosure. B (gold/real estate) have no such mandate. D (ULIPs/insurance) are regulated by IRDAI, not SEBI. SEBI regulates market-linked securities products, which is why MF and PMS data must be disclosed.
</details>

**Q3.** Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) invests:

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The workbook defines ELSS as a scheme that invests **exclusively in equities**. B, C and D describe debt or other assets, which an ELSS does not focus on. Because it is all-equity, its risk of capital loss is high — that is the trade-off for its tax benefit.
</details>

**Correct: D)** — ELSS has a **3-year lock-in**, after which it can be withdrawn anytime. A (5 years) is the ULIP lock-in. C (15 years) is the PPF lock-in. B (1 year) is invented. ELSS has the shortest lock-in among the common Section 80C tax-savers.

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** A retail investor wants a tax-saving (80C) product with the **shortest lock-in** and is comfortable with high risk. Which fits best?
A) PPF  B) Tax-saving bank FD  C) ELSS  D) NPS Tier 1
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — **ELSS** has the shortest lock-in (3 years) and is all-equity (high risk), matching a risk-tolerant investor. PPF (A) locks in for 15 years. Tax-saving FD (B) is locked for its tenure (typically 5 years). NPS (D) locks in till age 60. Shortest lock-in + high-risk tolerance points squarely to ELSS.
</details>

**Q37.** An investor wants a tax-saving product with **sovereign safety and fully tax-free returns**. Which is best?
A) ELSS  B) PPF  C) Tax-saving bank FD  D) AIF Cat 3
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **PPF** is sovereign (government-backed, low risk) and its returns are entirely tax-free. ELSS (A) is high-risk equity with gains taxed at 12.50%. The FD (C) is taxable each year. AIF (D) is a high-risk HNI product, not a tax-saver. Safety + tax-free returns is PPF's signature combination.
</details>

**Q38.** A High Net-worth Investor wants a **customised, concentrated portfolio held in their own demat account**. Which product fits?
A) Equity mutual fund  B) PMS  C) Index fund  D) ULIP
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **PMS** holds investments in the investor's own demat account and can be customised and concentrated (not SEBI broad-based). A mutual fund (A) is pooled, common to all and SEBI broad-based. An index fund (C) just tracks a benchmark. A ULIP (D) is insurance-cum-investment. Customisation + own demat = PMS.
</details>

**Q39.** An investor with Rs. 1,000 to start and wanting a SEBI-regulated, broad-based, retail product should choose:
A) PMS  B) AIF Cat 3  C) Equity mutual fund  D) Real estate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — An **equity mutual fund** allows entry from Rs. 1,000, is SEBI-regulated for broad-basing and is meant for retail investors. PMS (A) needs Rs. 50 lakh. AIF (B) needs Rs. 1 crore. Real estate (D) needs a large lump sum. Small ticket + retail + broad-based = mutual fund.
</details>

**Q40.** An HNI wants exposure to **complex or leveraged trading strategies including derivatives**. Which is designed for that?
A) Index fund  B) Tax-saving FD  C) AIF Category 3  D) PPF
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — **AIF Category 3** undertakes diverse/complex trading strategies, including listed or unlisted derivatives, and suits HNIs seeking leveraged strategies. An index fund (A) merely tracks a benchmark. FD (B) and PPF (D) are low-risk fixed products. Complex/leveraged = AIF Cat 3.
</details>

**Q41.** An investor wants market-linked investing but also **life insurance cover in the same product**. Which fits?
A) Mutual fund  B) ULIP  C) ETF  D) Index fund
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A **ULIP** is insurance-cum-investment, providing life cover plus market-linked returns in one product. A mutual fund (A), ETF (C) and index fund (D) are pure investments with no insurance. If the client wants both in one wrapper, it's a ULIP.
</details>

**Q42.** A cost-conscious investor simply wants to **match the benchmark return at the lowest cost** without trying to beat it. Best choice?
A) Actively managed fund  B) Index fund  C) PMS  D) AIF Cat 3
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — An **index fund** replicates the benchmark at low cost — exactly matching the goal. An active fund (A) costs more while trying to beat the benchmark. PMS (C) and AIF (D) are high-cost, high-ticket products. Low cost + track the benchmark = index fund.
</details>

**Q43.** An investor wants gold exposure with **no purity concerns, no storage cost, that also earns interest**. Which is ideal?
A) Physical gold  B) Gold ETF  C) Sovereign Gold Bond  D) Gold fund
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — **Sovereign Gold Bonds** have no purity concerns (no physical gold), no storage cost, and pay 2.50% p.a. interest. Physical gold (A) has purity and storage issues and no income. Gold ETF (B) and gold fund (D) have no purity/storage worries but pay no interest. Interest + no storage = SGB.
</details>

**Q44.** An investor wants gold exposure with **intraday (live) pricing on the exchange**. Which fits?
A) Gold fund  B) Physical gold  C) Gold ETF  D) Sovereign Gold Bond
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A **Gold ETF** can be bought as per the prevailing price at the moment (intraday) on the exchange. A gold fund (A) is priced at end-of-day NAV. Physical gold (B) trades at prevailing dealer price but is not an exchange security. SGBs (D) are priced on a last-3-days basis at issue. Live exchange pricing = Gold ETF.
</details>

**Q45.** An investor wants property-type exposure that is **diversified across many properties, professionally managed, and relatively liquid**. Best fit?
A) Direct real estate  B) REIT/InvIT  C) Physical gold  D) Company deposit
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **REITs/InvITs** spread exposure over many properties/projects, are professionally managed, allow a lower minimum, and (being listed) have relatively better liquidity. Direct real estate (A) is concentrated, lumpy and illiquid. Gold (C) and a company deposit (D) are not property exposure. Diversified + liquid property = REIT/InvIT.
</details>

**Q46.** An investor wants **instant liquidity** for their parked savings even at the cost of some penalty. Which fits best?

**Correct: B)** — A **bank FD** offers instant liquidity from the bank (though with possible interest clawback or premature penalty). An FMP (A) is listed but effectively very illiquid. A closed-ended AIF (C) has lock-in and poor liquidity. An SGB (D) only redeems with RBI after 5 years. Instant access = bank FD.
</details>

**Q47.** A debt investor wants **better post-tax returns by withdrawing amounts that have a large capital element**. Which approach does the workbook suggest?

**Correct: B)** — The workbook says post-tax returns can be better by investing in an **open-ended debt fund growth scheme and withdrawing needed amounts**, since each withdrawal has a large element of capital (not just interest). FD (A) interest is fully taxed. FMP (C) is held to maturity. Company deposit (D) interest is taxed. Growth-option withdrawals are the tax-efficient route.
</details>

**Q48.** An income-earner with dependents needs cover against **death from any cause**. Which is essential?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Life insurance** covers death from any cause and is essential for income earners with dependents. Personal accident (A) covers only accidental death/disability — an add-on, not a substitute. Health insurance (C) covers medical bills, not death payout. A ULIP (D) is investment-heavy and not the core protection here. Any-cause death cover = life insurance.

**Q49.** A client already has adequate life insurance and now wants cover specifically for **permanent total disability from an accident**. Recommend:
A) More life insurance  B) Personal accident insurance  C) PPF  D) A debt fund
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

More life insurance (A) won't pay for disability. PPF (C) and a debt fund (D) are investments, not insurance. PA is the add-on for accident disability.

**Q50.** Between a company deposit and a company debenture of the same company, an investor who wants **slightly higher return and accepts higher risk** should pick:

**Q51.** An investor wants index exposure as an **actual long-term/lifetime investment** rather than a short derivative position. Which fits?

Index options (B) are similarly short-dated (some long-dated exist) and are derivative positions. For a buy-and-hold long-term position, the index fund is right.
