## 1. Elements of Life Insurance Products

Life insurance covers the **life of the insured**. The benefits come in two flavours:
- **Death cover** — pays only if the insured **dies within** a specified period. No death = no payout.
- **Survival benefit** — pays if the insured **survives** a specified period.

> **Term insurance = only death benefit.** Investment-cum-insurance policies = a mix of both.

### Key elements (with simple meanings)
| Element | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Insured** | The person whose life is covered (individual, minor, or joint lives). |
| **Proposer / Policyholder** | The buyer, if different from the insured — must have **insurable interest**. |
| **Term of contract** | Period during which cover is available. |
| **Sum assured** | The amount insured. |
| **Payment of sum assured** | Paid on death (death benefit) or term expiry (survival benefit); lump sum or instalments. |
| **Premium payable** | Depends on sum assured + term; modes: monthly/quarterly/half-yearly/annual/single. |
| **Bonus / Guaranteed bonus / Reversionary bonus** | Extra amounts added to sum assured (only in **investment-cum-insurance** policies). |

### 💡 The minimum sum assured rule (IRDAI)
For life products with term **> 10 years**:
- **Minimum sum assured = 10 × annual premium** if age **below 45**.
- **Minimum sum assured = 7 × annual premium** if age **above 45**.

For term **< 10 years**: **minimum sum assured = 5 × annual premium** for everyone.

> **Why it matters:** This ensures a policy is "real" insurance, not just a savings wrapper. (It also links to the tax rule that maturity is tax-free only if sum assured ≥ 10× annual premium.)

### Bonus types (only in investment-cum-insurance policies)
- **Bonus:** added to sum assured, declared periodically as a % of sum assured; paid at maturity or on the insured event.
- **Guaranteed bonus:** paid for the first few years (say 5), as a % of sum assured; received at the end of term.
- **Reversionary bonus:** based on insurer's performance, declared at the insurer's discretion, after the guaranteed-bonus period, for **participating** policies (these have higher premiums than **non-participating** policies).

### Policy lapse, surrender value & paid-up
- **Lapse:** if premium isn't paid even within the **grace period**, the policy lapses and no claim is payable.
- **Surrender value:** a traditional investment-cum-insurance policy in force **≥ 3 years** (**2 years** if term < 10 years), with full premiums paid, acquires a cash/surrender value.
  - Minimum surrender value = **30%** of all premiums paid, rising to **90%** in the last two years.
  - **Single premium** policy: guaranteed surrender value ranges from **70%** of premium (if surrendered within first 3 years) to **90%** in the last two years.
  - **ULIPs:** can be surrendered before 5 years despite the 5-year lock-in; a **discontinuation charge (max Rs. 6,000)** applies, then the balance moves to a discontinuation policy fund. The surrender value is paid only at the **end of the 5-year lock-in**.
- **Paid-up:** instead of lapsing, the sum assured is **proportionately reduced**.

#### 📖 Worked paid-up example (Surinder)
- 25-year policy, sum assured Rs. 10,00,000, **half-yearly** premiums.
- Full term = 25 years × 2 = **50 premiums** for the full Rs. 10,00,000.
- Surinder paid for 5 years = **10 premiums**, then stopped.
- Paid-up sum assured = 10,00,000 × (10 ÷ 50) = **Rs. 2,00,000**.

> **Logic:** You "earned" the proportion of cover equal to the proportion of premiums you actually paid.

### Nomination
The policyholder's right to name who receives the money on a death claim. Can be made at purchase or later, and **changed any number of times**.

## 2. Life Insurance Needs Analysis

How much cover do you need? It depends on the economic value of a human life — the **Human Life Value (HLV)**.

> **HLV** = the **present value of the expected income** over the working life that is available for the dependents.

There are two main methods.

### 2.1 HLV (Income) Method
Only **two assumptions** are needed:
1. By how much income grows each year (e.g., **6% inflation**).
2. Post-tax return on the sum assured (e.g., **8%**).

The income is discounted to today's value using a special **adjusted (real) rate**.

#### 💡 The key thumb rule
If income growth rate = post-tax return rate, the discounting rate becomes **0**:
> Adjusted rate = (1 + 8%) / (1 + 8%) − 1 = **0**

When the rate is 0, HLV = **current annual income × number of years to retirement** (no discounting needed).
- Example: PMT = Rs. 10,00,000, Nper = 27 years, rate = 0 → HLV = **Rs. 2,70,00,000**.

Two consequences to remember:
- **Younger people have higher HLV** (more years left AND faster income growth).
- As a person **gets older**, HLV **falls** (the rate stays the same but Nper drops, so the present value falls).

> ⚠️ **Trap (True/False question in workbook):** "If income grows at 8% instead of 6%, HLV goes DOWN." → **FALSE.** A higher growth rate makes the discounting rate smaller, which makes HLV **higher** (or equal).

### 2.2 Needs-Based Method
Assumes insurance proceeds will cover the family's **expenditure needs and liabilities**. The corpus is built to generate the income the family needs, plus pay off loans, minus existing assets and existing insurance.

#### The adjusted (real) discount rate formula
```
Adjusted rate = ((1 + investment return) / (1 + inflation)) − 1
```
- **Investment return** = rate the corpus earns (8%).
- **Inflation** = rate the family's expenses grow (6%).
- **Logic:** We discount future income by the *gap* between what the money earns and how fast costs rise. A corpus earning 8% while expenses rise 6% effectively grows ~1.89% in "real" terms.

Example: ((1 + 8%) / (1 + 6%)) − 1 = **1.89%**.

#### 📖 Worked example — Anil (full calculation)
**Given:** Monthly income Rs. 1,50,000; insurance premium Rs. 25,000/month; EMI Rs. 32,000 (on a Rs. 40 lakh house loan); personal expenses Rs. 10,000. Wife is 49, expected to live to 80. Inflation 6%, return 8%. Existing cover Rs. 1 Cr; other investments Rs. 50 lakh; house worth Rs. 50 lakh.

**Step 1 — Income to be replaced (what the family actually needs):**
- Total income − (personal needs + EMI + insurance premium)
- = 1,50,000 − (10,000 + 32,000 + 25,000) = 1,50,000 − 67,000 = **Rs. 83,000/month**
- = **Rs. 9,96,000 per annum**

**Step 2 — Adjusted rate:**
- ((1 + 8%) / (1 + 6%)) − 1 = **1.89%**

**Step 3 — Corpus needed (PV function):**
- Rate = 1.89%, Nper = 80 − 49 = **31 years**, PMT = Rs. 9,96,000 (payments at the beginning of each year)
- `PV(1.89%, 31, −996000, , 1)` = **Rs. 2,36,43,984**

**Step 4 — Add liabilities:**
- + Loan outstanding Rs. 40,00,000 → total required = **Rs. 2,76,43,984**

**Step 5 — Deduct existing resources:**
- − Existing cover Rs. 1,00,00,000 − Investments Rs. 50,00,000
- **Additional insurance cover required = Rs. 1,26,43,984**

> 💡 Note: the personal residence (Rs. 50 lakh) is **not deducted** — the family won't sell their home.

> ⚠️ **Trap (workbook T/F):** "Needs-based requirement is lower when the insured has more investments." → **TRUE** (more existing assets reduce the gap).

> **Important relationship:** The **HLV method always gives a higher or equal** figure than the needs-based approach, because overall expenditure cannot exceed income.

## 3. Types of Life Insurance Products

### Term Insurance
- **Pure risk cover** — pays only if the insured **dies during the term**. Survive = nothing paid.
- **Lowest premium** (no investment component) → the most affordable, best way to cover full protection needs.
- Three key factors: **sum assured, premium, length of coverage**.
- Premium can be **level** (same every year) or rising with age. Payouts can be lump sum, fixed monthly income, or both. Some plans extend cover to age 85 or 100 (much higher premium).

#### Term insurance with Return of Premium (worked example)
The premium is higher; the extra is invested to refund premiums at the end. The implied return is found with Excel's **RATE** function.
- Sum assured Rs. 1,00,00,000; tenure 30 years.
- Pure term premium = Rs. 9,416/yr; return-of-premium = Rs. 17,473/yr.
- **Extra premium = 17,473 − 9,416 = Rs. 8,057/yr.**
- Premium returned at maturity = 30 × 17,473 = **Rs. 5,24,190**.
- RATE(Nper=30, PMT=8,057, PV=0, FV=−5,24,190, beginning of period) = **4.61% p.a.**

> 💡 So the "free" refund actually earns just **4.61%** on the extra you paid — usually poor.

### Endowment
- **Investment-cum-insurance**, same premium each year.
- On survival: investment portion + actual returns (declared yearly as **accrued bonus**, paid at end).
- On death during term: sum assured + bonus accrued till death.
- Use **RATE** (lump sum) or **XIRR** (staggered payouts) to find the implied return.

#### 📖 Worked example
- Sum insured Rs. 1 Cr, 30 years. Endowment premium Rs. 3,16,332/yr; pure term Rs. 9,416/yr.
- Higher illustrated maturity value = Rs. 2,14,00,000.
- Extra premium (PMT) = 3,16,332 − 9,416 = **Rs. 3,06,916/yr**.
- RATE(30, 3,06,916, 0, −2,14,00,000, beginning) = **5% p.a.**

> ⚠️ Endowment implied returns are normally just **3–5% p.a.**, and even that is **not guaranteed**. Since the protection is the priority and the Rs. 3,16,332 premium is far costlier than Rs. 9,416, an endowment can often be ruled out.

### Whole Life Insurance
- Investment-cum-insurance covering the **entire life** (or up to an upper age). Premium fixed throughout.
- On death: full sum assured + bonuses.
- Originally used to **pass wealth to the next generation** free of estate duty; with estate duty abolished, it's just another investment-cum-insurance product to be judged on cover adequacy + return.

### Unit-Linked Insurance (ULIP)
- Unlike whole-life/endowment, the **insured chooses the portfolio** (debt/equity mix) for the savings part.
- Risk cover charges are deducted from premium; the balance is invested. **NAV announced daily.**
- On death: sum assured, or NAV, or higher/total of both (depends on structure). On survival: NAV returned.
- More **transparent** than endowment/whole-life; **no indicated inherent return** (depends on markets). Single-premium ULIPs available.

#### 📖 Case study — Mr Jeevan (Term + MF vs ULIP)
Age 35; cover needed Rs. 100 lakh; tenure 20–25 years.
- **Option 1:** Term premium Rs. 1,100/month + invest Rs. 25,000/month in a **mutual fund**.
- **Option 2:** Invest Rs. 26,100/month in a **Type II ULIP** (on death: sum assured + fund value; on maturity: fund value). Sum assured Rs. 100 lakh, term 25 years.

| Feature | Term + Mutual Fund | ULIP (Type II) |
|---------|--------------------|----------------|
| Divisibility | Fully divisible (2 separate contracts); can pause investing | First 5 years indivisible; non-payment → discontinued policy account, cover stops |
| Liquidity | No lock-in (small exit load if exiting in 12/18 months) | Investment locked 5 years |
| Risk choice | Any risk profile via MFs | Limited options from insurer |
| Switch between providers | Possible (exit load/cap-gains) | Not possible |
| Switch within provider | Possible (load/tax) | Possible, no tax, usually a few free switches/year |
| Transparency | High; sophisticated regulation, independent monitoring; max costs known | Evolving; costs debited by cancelling units (not in NAV) → harder to compare |
| Tax on maturity | Capital gains tax applies | Exempt if sum assured ≥ 10× annual premium (here Rs. 1 Cr > 10 × Rs. 3,13,200) |

> ⚠️ **Finance Act 2021:** ULIP maturity proceeds with **annual premium > Rs. 2.5 lakh** are taxed as **capital gains**, for policies taken **on/after 01.02.2021**.

> 💡 **Bottom line:** Consider a ULIP instead of "Term + MF" **only if insurance cover is genuinely required**. If not, the ULIP's mortality charge is just an extra cost. Older, wealthy investors often don't need large cover and struggle to qualify for the 10× rule cheaply.

### Mortgage (Reducing Term) Insurance
- Sum assured **decreases over time** — designed to match a falling home-loan balance.
- In theory cheaper than a full level-term plan (less over-insurance), but **in practice it's often NOT cheaper** — sometimes even more expensive.
- Since most home loans are prepaid, premium for cover no longer needed is **wasted** → these plans are unpopular.

### Riders (add-ons for extra premium)
- **Double sum assured rider** — pays twice the sum insured on death within the term.
- **Critical illness rider** — pays a fixed sum on diagnosis of a life-threatening illness + **30-day survival**. If it reduces the life cover by the paid amount, it's an **accelerated sum insured** (cheaper). Often a term policy with an *equal* critical-illness accelerated cover is **cheaper than a standalone critical-illness policy**.
- **Accident death/disability rider** — periodic payout for temporary disability, or a sum on permanent disability from an accident.
- **Waiver of premium rider** — triggers if disability/loss of income makes premiums hard to pay.
- **Guaranteed insurability option rider** — enhance cover later **without a medical exam** (great for young earners).
- **Income benefit rider** — nominee gets periodic income instead of a lump sum on death.

> **Group insurance** is another cheaper option (e.g., all employees of a company) — lower premium for the same cover, simpler claims.

## 4. Facilities under Life Insurance Policies

### Loan against policy
- **Pure protection (term)** policies have **no surrender value** → no loan possible.
- Most insurers lend against the **surrender value** of investment-cum-insurance policies; banks/NBFCs may too.
- Insurer loan rates are usually **lower** than bank/NBFC rates. Use banks/NBFCs mainly to combine several policies into one larger, convenient loan.
- Such a loan can also help **rebuild a damaged credit score**.

### Nomination & change of nomination
Right to name who receives the claim; can be changed any number of times. Under the 2015 amendment, if the nominee is spouse/parent/children, they inherit **beneficially** (not as trustees).

### Policy assignment
- **Assignment** = transferring the interests in the policy (usually to take a loan).
- An assignment **cancels** an existing nomination — **except** when assigned to the insurer itself for a loan (then nomination is only affected to the extent of the insurer's interest).
- Nomination is **reinstated** once the policy is re-assigned back.
- Must be recognised by the insurer via **endorsement** after a written notice.

## 5. Insurance under the Married Women's Property Act (MWPA), 1874

Normally, creditors of the insured could lay claim to insurance proceeds even from beneficial nominees. **MWPA solves this.**

When a **married man** buys a policy under the **MWP Act, 1874**:
- Nominees can only be the **spouse or children or both** (NOT parents).
- The nominees acquire **all rights** to the claim; the **insured himself has no right** to the money.
- Nominees **cannot be changed** after purchase. Even after divorce, the wife stays the nominee.
- Any **survival benefit** is paid to the **nominees**, not the insured.
- The MWP declaration must be made **only at the time of buying** the policy (works for term policies, online or offline).

> 💡 Since the 2015 "beneficial nominee" amendment, MWPA has lost some relevance (and is rigid — can't change nominees). But it remains uniquely useful to **shield proceeds from the insured's creditors**.

## 6. Multiple Policies — Benefits, Limits, Provisions

- Life insurance premiums are **telescopic**: premium does NOT rise in the same proportion as sum assured. A larger sum assured costs less *per thousand* → buying from the **same company** can be cheaper.
- **Telescopic rates stop after a level** (say Rs. 2 Cr per insurer). For Rs. 8 Cr cover, it may be wise to buy **4 policies of Rs. 2 Cr each** — so you can drop some later as needs fall.
- The proposal form must **list all existing policies and pending proposals**. Omission = withholding a material fact → grounds for **repudiation/cancellation within the first 3 years**.

## 7. Criteria to Evaluate Life Insurance Products

### 7.1 Online vs Offline Term Plans — consider:
- Trust in the **brand** lasting until the claim.
- Whether the **full sum insured** is really available for the tenure.
- Premium **after health/financial evaluation** (advertised premiums are for "super healthy" people).
- Availability of **riders** (accelerated critical illness, permanent disability).
- **Claim settlement ratio** (less important if full disclosure was made).

### 7.2 Traditional vs ULIP
- Traditional: insured **can't choose** investments; returns usually low. For policies issued **on/after April 1, 2023** with premium **> Rs. 5 lakh**, maturity proceeds are taxable as **"Income from other sources"**.
- ULIP: insured **chooses** funds and **bears the investment risk** (can reduce, not eliminate, by picking low-risk funds).

### 7.3 Investment-linked vs Pure Term
Decouple insurance from investment. Check **cover adequacy first**; if inadequate, reject. Only then judge the investment return and compare with similar products.

### 7.4 Online vs Offline plans
Online policies are often **cheaper** (some "web only"), with instant issuance. Brokers/web aggregators offer online comparisons and payment.

## 8. Global Coverage

- **Defined benefit policies** (life, critical illness, accidental death) **pay wherever the risk occurs** worldwide — easy to verify the event happened.
- Residents may use the **Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)** — up to **USD 2,50,000 per annum** — to buy **foreign** insurance policies (denominated in foreign currency), e.g., to fund a child's overseas education.
- ⚠️ These foreign policies and their grievance redressal are governed by the **foreign country's** regulations → use caution.
- Evaluate any investment-cum-insurance foreign policy like any other: **protection needs first**, then compare the investment portion with similar LRS-available instruments.

## 📌 One-Page Recap
- Life products give **death cover** and/or **survival benefit**. Term = death only; investment-cum-insurance = both.
- **Minimum sum assured:** term > 10 yrs → **10×** premium (age < 45) / **7×** (age > 45); term < 10 yrs → **5×** for all.
- **Surrender value:** traditional policy in force ≥ 3 yrs (2 yrs if term < 10), min **30%** of premiums → **90%** at the end; single premium **70% → 90%**; ULIP discontinuation charge **max Rs. 6,000**.
- **Paid-up** sum assured = sum assured × (premiums paid ÷ total premiums due). Surinder: 10,00,000 × 10/50 = Rs. 2,00,000.
- **HLV** = PV of future income for dependents. If income growth = return, rate = 0 and HLV = income × years to retirement. HLV is higher for the young, falls with age. **HLV ≥ needs-based** figure.
- **Adjusted rate = ((1+return)/(1+inflation)) − 1** → 1.89% in Anil's case; additional cover needed = **Rs. 1,26,43,984**.
- Products: **Term** (cheapest, pure cover), **Endowment** (~3–5% return), **Whole Life**, **ULIP** (choose funds, daily NAV, transparent), **Mortgage** (reducing, usually not cheaper).
- **Return-of-premium** term implies ~4.61% return; **endowment** ~5%.
- **ULIP tax:** maturity tax-free if sum assured ≥ 10× premium; but premium > Rs. 2.5 lakh (policies on/after 01.02.2021) → taxed as capital gains.
- **MWPA, 1874:** nominees = spouse/children only, can't be changed, shields from creditors; insured has no rights.
- Premiums are **telescopic**; list all existing policies on the proposal form.
- Defined-benefit policies pay worldwide; LRS limit **USD 2,50,000/yr** for foreign policies.
