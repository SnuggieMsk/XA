# Chapter 2: Life Insurance Products — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check.

**Q1.** What are the two basic benefits a life insurance product can provide?
> **A:** **Death cover** (paid only if the insured dies within the period) and **survival benefit** (paid if the insured survives the period). Term = death only; investment-cum-insurance = both.

**Q2.** Who is the "proposer" vs the "insured"?
> **A:** The **insured** is the person whose life is covered. The **proposer/policyholder** is the buyer when different from the insured — and must have **insurable interest** in the insured.

**Q3.** State the IRDAI minimum sum assured rule for term > 10 years.
> **A:** **10 × annual premium** if age below 45; **7 × annual premium** if age above 45.

**Q4.** What is the minimum sum assured if the policy term is LESS than 10 years?
> **A:** **5 × annual premium** for all individuals, regardless of age.

**Q5.** What is a reversionary bonus, and which policies get it?
> **A:** A bonus declared at the insurer's discretion based on its performance, after the guaranteed-bonus period, for **participating** policies (which charge higher premiums than non-participating).

**Q6.** When does a traditional investment-cum-insurance policy acquire a surrender value?
> **A:** When in force at least **3 years** (or **2 years** if term < 10 years) with full premiums paid. Minimum surrender value = **30%** of premiums paid, rising to **90%** in the last two years.

**Q7.** What is the surrender value range for a single-premium policy?
> **A:** From **70%** of total premium (surrendered within first 3 years) to **90%** in the last two years.

**Q8.** What happens if you surrender a ULIP before 5 years?
> **A:** A **discontinuation charge (max Rs. 6,000)** is levied; the balance moves to a discontinuation policy fund. The surrender value is paid only at the **end of the 5-year lock-in**.

**Q9.** What is a "paid-up" policy?
> **A:** Instead of lapsing, the sum assured is **proportionately reduced** = sum assured × (premiums paid ÷ total premiums due).

**Q10.** Surinder: 25-yr policy, sum assured Rs. 10,00,000, half-yearly premiums, paid 5 years then stopped. Paid-up value?
> **A:** Total premiums = 25 × 2 = 50; paid = 10. Paid-up = 10,00,000 × (10/50) = **Rs. 2,00,000**.

**Q11.** Define Human Life Value (HLV).
> **A:** The **present value of expected income** over the working life that is available for the dependents — what insurance should compensate for if the earner dies.

**Q12.** Why is HLV higher for younger people?
> **A:** More **years left to retirement** (higher Nper) AND income tends to **grow faster** — both raise the present value.

**Q13.** True or False: If income grows at 8% instead of 6%, HLV goes DOWN.
> **A:** **FALSE.** Higher income growth lowers the discounting rate, so HLV goes **up** (or equal). If growth = return, rate = 0 and HLV = income × years to retirement.

**Q14.** What is the adjusted (real) discount rate formula and its logic?
> **A:** **((1 + investment return) / (1 + inflation)) − 1.** It captures the *gap* between what the corpus earns and how fast expenses rise. E.g., ((1.08)/(1.06)) − 1 = **1.89%**.

**Q15.** In Anil's example, what monthly and annual income needed replacing?
> **A:** 1,50,000 − (10,000 personal + 32,000 EMI + 25,000 premium) = **Rs. 83,000/month = Rs. 9,96,000/year**.

**Q16.** In Anil's example, what was the corpus and the final additional cover required?
> **A:** Corpus (PV at 1.89%, 31 yrs, Rs. 9,96,000) = Rs. 2,36,43,984; + Rs. 40 lakh loan = Rs. 2,76,43,984; − Rs. 1 Cr cover − Rs. 50 lakh investments = **Rs. 1,26,43,984**.

**Q17.** Why isn't Anil's house value deducted in the needs calculation?
> **A:** The family won't sell their **residential home** — personal assets like the house and jewellery are excluded.

**Q18.** Which gives a higher number — HLV method or needs-based method?
> **A:** **HLV is always higher or equal**, because overall expenditure cannot exceed income.

**Q19.** Why is term insurance the cheapest form of life cover?
> **A:** It is **pure risk cover** — it only covers death, with **no investment component**, so the premium buys protection and nothing else.

**Q20.** Return-of-premium term: pure premium Rs. 9,416, RoP premium Rs. 17,473, 30 yrs, refund Rs. 5,24,190. What's the implied return?
> **A:** Extra premium = Rs. 8,057/yr. RATE(30, 8,057, 0, −5,24,190, beginning) = **4.61% p.a.** — a poor return on the extra paid.

**Q21.** What return does the workbook's endowment example imply, and what's typical?
> **A:** **5% p.a.** in the example; endowment implied returns are normally **3–5% p.a.** and **not guaranteed**.

**Q22.** How is an endowment's accrued bonus paid?
> **A:** Declared yearly (not pre-defined at start) but **paid only at the end of the tenure** (or with sum assured on death).

**Q23.** What was Whole Life insurance originally designed for?
> **A:** To **pass wealth to the next generation free of estate duty** (death insurance proceeds were exempt). With estate duty abolished, it's judged like any investment-cum-insurance product.

**Q24.** What distinguishes a ULIP from endowment/whole-life?
> **A:** The **insured chooses the portfolio** (debt/equity mix) for the savings portion; NAV is announced **daily**; it's more transparent but has **no indicated inherent return**.

**Q25.** When is a ULIP's maturity value tax-free?
> **A:** When the sum assured is **at least 10× the annual premium**. (Jeevan's Rs. 1 Cr cover > 10 × Rs. 3,13,200 premium → exempt.)

**Q26.** What did the Finance Act 2021 change for ULIPs?
> **A:** ULIPs with **annual premium > Rs. 2.5 lakh** have maturity proceeds taxed as **capital gains** — for policies taken **on/after 01.02.2021**.

**Q27.** In the Jeevan comparison, why is "Term + Mutual Fund" more transparent than a ULIP?
> **A:** Mutual funds have sophisticated regulation, many independent monitors, known maximum costs, and units can't be cancelled to meet costs — allowing proper return comparison. ULIP costs are debited by cancelling units (not in NAV).

**Q28.** What is mortgage (reducing term) insurance, and why is it unpopular?
> **A:** Sum assured falls with the home loan balance. In practice it's often **not cheaper** (sometimes more expensive) than level term, and since loans are usually prepaid, premium for unused cover is **wasted**.

**Q29.** What is an "accelerated sum insured" critical illness rider?
> **A:** On a critical-illness claim, the life cover is **reduced** by the amount paid out. It's cheaper than an independent rider, and often cheaper than a standalone critical-illness policy.

**Q30.** What does a Guaranteed Insurability Option rider do?
> **A:** Lets the insured **increase cover later without a fresh medical exam** — useful for young earners expecting higher income but possible future health issues.

**Q31.** Why can't you take a loan against a pure term policy?
> **A:** Pure protection policies acquire **no surrender value**, so there's nothing to lend against. Loans are available only against investment-cum-insurance policies.

**Q32.** What happens to a nomination when a policy is assigned?
> **A:** Assignment **cancels** the nomination — except when assigned to the insurer for its own loan (then only affected to the extent of the insurer's interest). Nomination is reinstated when re-assigned back.

**Q33.** Under the MWP Act, 1874, who can be the nominees and can they be changed?
> **A:** Only the **spouse or children or both** (NOT parents). Nominees **cannot be changed** after purchase — even after divorce, the wife stays the nominee.

**Q34.** What is the main remaining benefit of an MWP Act policy?
> **A:** It **shields the policy proceeds from the insured's creditors** — they go to the nominees regardless of claims on the insured's assets.

**Q35.** What does "telescopic premium" mean?
> **A:** Premiums do **not rise in the same proportion** as the sum assured — a larger cover costs less per thousand, so buying from one company is often cheaper.

**Q36.** Why might someone buy 4 policies of Rs. 2 Cr instead of one Rs. 8 Cr policy?
> **A:** Telescopic rates stop after a level (~Rs. 2 Cr per insurer). Splitting lets the insured **drop individual policies later** as insurance needs fall.

**Q37.** How are traditional-policy maturity proceeds taxed if premium > Rs. 5 lakh?
> **A:** For policies issued **on/after April 1, 2023** with premium > Rs. 5 lakh, maturity proceeds are taxable as **"Income from other sources."**

**Q38.** Do defined-benefit life policies pay if death occurs outside India?
> **A:** **Yes** — life, critical illness and accidental death policies pay wherever the risk occurs, because the event is easy to verify anywhere.

**Q39.** How can a resident Indian buy a foreign insurance policy, and what's the limit?
> **A:** Using the **Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)** — up to **USD 2,50,000 per annum**. But foreign rules govern the policy and grievance redressal, so caution is needed.

**Q40.** What's the right way to evaluate any investment-cum-insurance product?
> **A:** **Decouple** insurance from investment; check **cover adequacy first** (reject if inadequate); only then assess the investment return and compare with similar products.
