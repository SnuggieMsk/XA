# Chapter 2: Life Insurance Products — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** Which product provides ONLY a death benefit and no survival benefit?
A) Endowment  B) Whole life  C) Term insurance  D) ULIP
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — **Term insurance** is pure risk cover: it pays only if the insured dies during the term. The others are investment-cum-insurance products that combine death cover with a survival/maturity component.
</details>

**Q2.** For a life policy with a term of more than 10 years bought by a 35-year-old, the minimum sum assured is:
A) 5 × annual premium  B) 7 × annual premium  C) 10 × annual premium  D) 20 × annual premium
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — For term > 10 years and age **below 45**, the minimum sum assured is **10 ×** annual premium. (It would be 7× above 45, and 5× for any term under 10 years.)
</details>

**Q3.** A policy with term less than 10 years requires a minimum sum assured of:
A) 5 × annual premium for all  B) 7 × annual premium  C) 10 × for under-45s  D) No minimum
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — For term **< 10 years**, the minimum is **5 × annual premium for everyone**, regardless of age.
</details>

**Q4.** Surinder took a 25-year endowment policy (sum assured Rs. 10,00,000) with half-yearly premiums and stopped after 5 years. The paid-up sum assured is:
A) Rs. 5,00,000  B) Rs. 2,00,000  C) Rs. 1,00,000  D) Rs. 4,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Total premiums due = 25 × 2 = 50; paid = 5 × 2 = 10. Paid-up = 10,00,000 × (10 ÷ 50) = **Rs. 2,00,000**. (Half-yearly means 2 premiums a year — a common trap.)
</details>

**Q5.** Human Life Value is best defined as:
A) The face value of the policy  B) The present value of expected future income available for dependents  C) The total premiums paid  D) The market value of assets
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — HLV is the **present value of the income** the earner would have provided to dependents over the working life. It is what insurance must replace.
</details>

**Q6.** If income growth equals the post-tax investment return, the adjusted discounting rate is:
A) Negative  B) Equal to inflation  C) Zero  D) 8%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Adjusted rate = ((1+r)/(1+g)) − 1; if r = g this equals **0**. Then HLV simply = current annual income × years to retirement (e.g., Rs. 10,00,000 × 27 = Rs. 2,70,00,000).
</details>

**Q7.** All else equal, as a person grows older their HLV:
A) Rises  B) Stays the same  C) Falls  D) Becomes zero immediately
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The discount rate stays the same but the number of years left (Nper) **falls**, so the present value (HLV) **falls**. This is why younger people have higher HLV.
</details>

**Q8.** In Anil's needs analysis (income Rs. 1,50,000; personal Rs. 10,000; EMI Rs. 32,000; premium Rs. 25,000), the monthly income to be replaced is:
A) Rs. 1,50,000  B) Rs. 1,18,000  C) Rs. 83,000  D) Rs. 67,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Replace only what the family actually needs: 1,50,000 − (10,000 + 32,000 + 25,000) = 1,50,000 − 67,000 = **Rs. 83,000/month** (Rs. 9,96,000/year). The earner's own personal expense, EMI and premium stop.
</details>

**Q9.** The needs-based insurance requirement compared to the HLV-based requirement is:
A) Always higher  B) Always lower or equal  C) Always equal  D) Unrelated
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The HLV figure is **always higher or equal** because expenditure cannot exceed income; the needs-based figure (built on expenses/liabilities net of assets) is therefore **lower or equal**.
</details>

**Q10.** A term plan with return of premium implied a return of 4.61% on the extra premium. This tells us:
A) It is an excellent investment  B) The "free" refund actually earns a poor return  C) The premium is guaranteed to double  D) It beats mutual funds
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 4.61% p.a. on the extra Rs. 8,057/year is a **poor return**. The refund isn't really "free" — you funded it by overpaying premium that could have earned more elsewhere.
</details>

**Q11.** Typical implied investment returns on endowment policies are:
A) 12–15% p.a. guaranteed  B) 3–5% p.a. and not guaranteed  C) Exactly 8%  D) Zero
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Endowment implied returns are normally **3–5% p.a.** (5% in the workbook example) and **even that is not guaranteed** — actual returns can be higher or lower.
</details>

**Q12.** In a ULIP, who chooses the asset allocation (debt/equity mix) and who bears the investment risk?
A) The insurer chooses; insurer bears the risk  B) The insured chooses; insured bears the risk  C) IRDAI decides both  D) The agent chooses; insurer bears the risk
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — In a ULIP the **insured chooses** the fund mix and **bears the investment risk** (can reduce, not eliminate, by picking low-risk funds). NAV is announced daily.
</details>

**Q13.** A ULIP's maturity proceeds are tax-free when:
A) The sum assured is at least 10× the annual premium  B) The premium exceeds Rs. 2.5 lakh  C) Always  D) Only for term plans
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Tax-free if sum assured ≥ **10× the annual premium**. But under Finance Act 2021, ULIPs with annual premium **> Rs. 2.5 lakh** (policies on/after 01.02.2021) are taxed as capital gains.
</details>

**Q14.** Why is mortgage (reducing term) insurance often unpopular in practice?
A) It is illegal  B) It pays nothing on death  C) Loans are usually prepaid, so premium for unused cover is wasted, and it's often not cheaper than level term  D) It covers only the bank
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — In theory it's cheaper (less over-insurance), but in practice it is often **no cheaper (or more expensive)**, and since home loans are frequently prepaid, the premium for cover that's no longer needed is **wasted**.
</details>

**Q15.** Which rider lets the insured increase cover later without a fresh medical examination?
A) Waiver of premium rider  B) Double sum assured rider  C) Guaranteed insurability option rider  D) Income benefit rider
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The **Guaranteed Insurability Option** rider allows enhancing cover without a new medical exam — valuable for young earners. (Waiver of premium triggers on disability; income benefit pays the nominee periodically.)
</details>

**Q16.** Under the MWP Act, 1874, the nominees can be:
A) Anyone the insured chooses  B) Only spouse or children or both (not parents), and cannot be changed  C) Only parents  D) The insured himself
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — MWPA nominees are limited to **spouse and/or children (not parents)** and **cannot be changed** later — even after divorce the wife stays the nominee. The insured has no rights to the money.
</details>

**Q17.** You can take a loan against which type of policy?
A) Pure term policy  B) Any policy with a surrender value (investment-cum-insurance)  C) None  D) Only group policies
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Loans are available against the **surrender value** of investment-cum-insurance policies. Pure term policies have **no surrender value**, so no loan is possible.
</details>

**Q18.** "Telescopic" life insurance premiums mean:
A) Premiums rise faster than the sum assured  B) Premiums do not rise in the same proportion as the sum assured  C) Premiums are fixed by law  D) Premiums fall every year
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A larger sum assured costs **less per thousand**, so premiums don't scale up proportionally. This is why buying a bigger cover from one company can be cheaper.
</details>

**Q19.** Maturity proceeds of a traditional (non-ULIP) policy issued on/after April 1, 2023 with annual premium above Rs. 5 lakh are taxed as:
A) Tax-free  B) Capital gains  C) Income from other sources  D) Business income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Such proceeds are taxable as **"Income from other sources."** (The capital-gains treatment applies to high-premium ULIPs, not traditional policies.)
</details>

**Q20.** A defined-benefit life policy where death occurs abroad will:
A) Not pay  B) Pay only 50%  C) Pay, since the event is easily verifiable anywhere  D) Pay only after RBI approval
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Defined-benefit policies (life, critical illness, accidental death) **pay wherever the risk occurs**, because the event and circumstances are ascertainable anywhere in the world.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Anjali, aged 38, earns Rs. 1,20,000 per month. Her breakdown: personal expenses Rs. 15,000/month, home-loan EMI Rs. 30,000/month (loan outstanding Rs. 35,00,000), and life-insurance premium Rs. 5,000/month. She wants to provide income for her husband (currently 40, expected to live to 78). Assume **inflation = 6%**, **return on investment = 8%**. She already has life cover of **Rs. 50,00,000** and investments worth **Rs. 20,00,000**. Her house is worth Rs. 60,00,000.

Use payment at the beginning of each year. (You may use the rounded adjusted rate to 2 decimals.)

**Q1.** Calculate the annual income that needs to be replaced for the family.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Replace only what the family actually needs (Anjali's own personal expense, EMI and premium will stop with her):
- Monthly income to replace = 1,20,000 − (15,000 + 30,000 + 5,000) = 1,20,000 − 50,000 = **Rs. 70,000/month**
- Annual = 70,000 × 12 = **Rs. 8,40,000 per year**
</details>

**Q2.** Compute the adjusted (real) discounting rate and the number of years over which income must be provided.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- Adjusted rate = ((1 + 8%) / (1 + 6%)) − 1 = (1.08 / 1.06) − 1 = **1.89%** (to 2 decimals)
- Years = husband's life expectancy − current age = 78 − 40 = **38 years** (Nper)
</details>

**Q3.** The PV function gives a corpus of approximately **Rs. 2,29,00,000** for Rate 1.89%, Nper 38, PMT Rs. 8,40,000 (payments at beginning of year). Add liabilities and deduct existing resources to find the additional cover required.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Step-by-step:
1. Corpus to generate the income = **Rs. 2,29,00,000** (approx.)
2. Add loan outstanding: + Rs. 35,00,000 → **Rs. 2,64,00,000** total required
3. Deduct existing life cover: − Rs. 50,00,000
4. Deduct investments: − Rs. 20,00,000
5. Additional cover required = 2,64,00,000 − 70,00,000 = **Rs. 1,94,00,000 (approx.)**

Note: the **house (Rs. 60 lakh) is NOT deducted** — the family won't sell their home.
</details>

**Q4.** Anjali instead considers an endowment plan for this cover. The illustration shows an implied return of about 5% p.a. Why might her adviser steer her toward a term plan plus a separate investment?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- The **primary purpose** of life insurance is **protection**, and a term plan delivers the full Rs. 1.94 Cr cover at a **far lower premium** than an endowment of similar cover.
- An endowment's implied return of ~**5% p.a. is low and not guaranteed**; she can likely earn more by investing the difference herself (e.g., in mutual funds).
- The adviser should **decouple** insurance from investment: secure adequate cover cheaply via term, then invest separately and compare returns with similar products.
- Term + separate investment is also **more divisible and transparent** (she can pause investing without losing cover).
</details>

**Q5.** Anjali is worried that if she dies, her creditors might claim the insurance money. How can she protect the proceeds for her husband and children, and what's the trade-off?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

She can buy the policy under the **Married Women's Property Act, 1874** (the declaration must be made **at the time of purchase**). Then:
- The proceeds go **only to her spouse and/or children** (not parents) and are **protected from her creditors'** claims.
- The nominees acquire all rights; she herself has no right to the money.

**Trade-off:** Under MWPA the **nominees cannot be changed** later — it's rigid. Also, since the 2015 "beneficial nominee" amendment already lets spouse/children inherit beneficially, MWPA's main remaining advantage is the **creditor protection**.
</details>
