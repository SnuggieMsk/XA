## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** A life insurance benefit that is paid only on the death of the insured within a specified period is called:
A) Survival benefit  B) Death cover  C) Maturity benefit  D) Reversionary bonus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Death cover pays only if the insured dies within the specified period; if death does not occur, no benefit is paid.
A) Survival benefit is the opposite — it pays when the insured *survives* a specified period. C) Maturity benefit is just a form of survival benefit, not the defined term here. D) Reversionary bonus is an add-on to the sum assured in participating policies, not a death-only cover.
</details>

**Q2.** Term insurance policies provide for:
A) Only survival benefit  B) Both death and survival benefit  C) Only death benefit  D) Only a guaranteed bonus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Term insurance is pure risk cover; it pays only if the insured dies during the term, i.e., only death benefit.
A) and B) describe investment-cum-insurance policies which combine death and survival features. D) Bonuses are relevant only to investment-cum-insurance policies, not pure term plans.
</details>

**Q3.** The person whose life is being insured is called the:
A) Proposer  B) Insured  C) Nominee  D) Assignee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The "insured" is the person whose life is covered, and can be an individual, minor or joint lives.
A) The proposer/policyholder is the buyer when different from the insured. C) The nominee receives the money on a death claim. D) An assignee is someone to whom the policy interest is transferred.
</details>

**Q4.** If the person buying the policy is different from the person whose life is insured, the buyer must have:
A) A medical certificate  B) An insurable interest in the person covered  C) A PAN card  D) A bank guarantee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Such a buyer (proposer/policyholder) must have an insurable interest in the individual being covered.
A), C) and D) are not the defining requirement for a proposer who is different from the insured; insurable interest is the key legal requirement.
</details>

**Q5.** "Sum assured" in a life insurance contract refers to:
A) The premium paid each year  B) The amount being insured  C) The bonus added at maturity  D) The surrender value
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The sum assured is the amount being insured.
A) Premium is the cost paid for the cover. C) Bonus is an extra amount added to the sum assured in investment-cum-insurance policies. D) Surrender value is the cash value returned if a policy is given up.
</details>

A) 5 times applies only when the term is less than 10 years. B) 7 times applies for individuals above 45 (term over 10 years). D) 15 times is not a defined limit.
</details>

A) 5 times applies to terms below 10 years. C) 10 times is for those below 45. D) 12 times is not a defined figure.

**Q8.** If a life insurance policy has a term of LESS than 10 years, the minimum sum assured for all individuals is:

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — For terms less than 10 years, the minimum sum assured is 5 times the annual premium for all individuals regardless of age.
B) 7 times and C) 10 times apply to terms over 10 years (depending on age). D) 3 times is not a defined limit.
</details>

**Q9.** Non-payment of premium due on a policy within the grace period allowed makes the policy:
A) Mature  B) Lapse  C) Paid-up automatically  D) Assigned to the insurer
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — If premium is not paid within the grace period, the policy lapses and no claim is payable on a lapsed policy.
A) Maturity happens at the end of the term, not from non-payment. C) Paid-up is an *option* in investment-cum-insurance policies, not automatic. D) Assignment is a deliberate transfer of policy interests, unrelated to non-payment.
</details>

**Q23.** The economic value attached to a human life, used to size insurance cover, is called the:

**Q42.** A 25-year-old and a 55-year-old have identical incomes and assumptions. Whose Human Life Value is higher and why?

**Q43.** Under the HLV method, if the assumed rate of income growth EQUALS the post-tax return on investment, the HLV equals:

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

</details>

**Q44.** "If income grows at 8% instead of 6% (return still 8%), the HLV goes DOWN." This statement is:

**Q47.** The adjusted (real) discounting rate used in the needs-based corpus calculation is given by:
