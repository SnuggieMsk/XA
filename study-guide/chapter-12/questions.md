# Chapter 12: Taxation of Other Products — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** The ESOP perquisite is calculated using the FMV on which date?
A) Grant date  B) Vesting date  C) Exercise date  D) Allotment date
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Perquisite = (FMV on exercise date − price paid) × shares. Vesting/allotment FMV is not used.
</details>

**Q2.** ESOP: FMV on exercise ₹6,500, price paid ₹500, 100 shares. Perquisite value?
A) ₹5,00,000  B) ₹6,00,000  C) ₹6,50,000  D) ₹65,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — (6,500 − 500) × 100 = **₹6,00,000**, taxed as salary perquisite.
</details>

**Q3.** For ESOP shares sold later, the cost of acquisition is:
A) The price paid by the employee  B) The FMV on the exercise date  C) Nil  D) The grant-date FMV
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Cost = FMV on exercise (already taxed as perquisite); holding starts from allotment date.
</details>

**Q4.** Redemption of an SGB by an individual on maturity is:
A) Taxed at 12.50% LTCG  B) Not treated as transfer (Section 47), so no capital gain  C) Taxed at slab rate  D) Subject to STT
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Individual redemption is exempt under Section 47 (interest, however, is taxable).
</details>

**Q5.** A trust (non-individual) redeems an SGB held 15 months. Tax?
A) Exempt  B) LTCG @12.50%  C) Slab rate  D) STCG @20%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The Section 47 exemption is only for individuals; a non-individual pays LTCG @12.50% if held >12 months (else slab).
</details>

**Q6.** SGBs pay interest at:
A) 5% p.a.  B) 2.50% p.a. on nominal value  C) 7.75% p.a.  D) Linked to gold price
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 2.50% p.a., half-yearly, taxed as Income from Other Sources; no TDS.
</details>

**Q7.** An employee's own contribution to NPS gets a deduction under:
A) 80C only  B) 80CCD(1), limited to 10% of salary  C) 80CCD(2)  D) 80D
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 80CCD(1): lower of contribution or 10% of salary, within the ₹1.5 lakh cap.
</details>

**Q8.** The additional ₹50,000 NPS deduction is available under:
A) 80CCD(1)  B) 80CCD(1B)  C) 80CCD(2)  D) 80CCC
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 80CCD(1B) gives an extra ₹50,000 over and above the ₹1.5 lakh cap.
</details>

**Q9.** Maximum total NPS-related deduction for an individual?
A) ₹1,50,000  B) ₹50,000  C) ₹2,00,000  D) No limit
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — ₹1.5 lakh (80C/80CCC/80CCD(1)) + ₹50,000 (80CCD(1B)) = ₹2,00,000.
</details>

**Q10.** On final closure of NPS, the exemption is:
A) 40% of corpus  B) 60% of corpus  C) 100%  D) Nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Up to 60% of the corpus is exempt; effectively the whole permitted lump-sum is tax-free.
</details>

**Q11.** Employer welfare contributions (PF+NPS+Superannuation) become a taxable perquisite above:
A) ₹5,00,000  B) ₹7,50,000  C) ₹2,50,000  D) ₹1,50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Excess over ₹7,50,000 is taxed as a perquisite.
</details>

**Q12.** Which REIT income is NOT passed through to unit-holders (taxed at REIT level)?
A) Rental income  B) Interest from SPV  C) Capital gains on property  D) Dividend from SPV
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Capital gains are taxed at the REIT level. Rental, SPV interest and SPV dividend pass through.
</details>

**Q13.** How does InvIT taxation differ from REIT?
A) No difference  B) InvIT rental income is taxed at InvIT level, not passed through  C) InvIT pays no tax  D) InvIT capital gains pass through
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — REIT rental passes through; InvIT rental is taxed in the InvIT's own hands.
</details>

**Q14.** Redemption of REIT units is taxed as:
A) LTCG @12.50%  B) Income from Other Sources (Sec 56(2)(xii))  C) STCG @20%  D) Exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Redemption → Other Sources under 56(2)(xii). Transfer (other than redemption) → capital gains.
</details>

**Q15.** Business income of a Category-I AIF (a trust) is taxed:
A) In the unit-holders' hands  B) Exempt  C) In the AIF's hands at maximum marginal rate  D) At 12.50%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Pass-through does not apply to business income; a trust-AIF pays at MMR. Other income passes through to investors.
</details>

**Q16.** Category-III AIFs are taxed:
A) With pass-through status  B) Like a normal trust/company/LLP (no pass-through)  C) Always exempt  D) At 10%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Pass-through is only for Cat-I and II. Cat-III is taxed as its legal form (unless a specified fund under 10(4D)).
</details>

**Q17.** A Gold ETF held 7 years is sold. Capital gain is taxed at:
A) Slab rate  B) 20%  C) 12.50% (LTCG)  D) Exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Held >12 months → LTCG @12.50%. (Special trap: bought after 01-04-2023 and sold before 01-04-2025 → always short-term.)
</details>

**Q18.** Maturity proceeds of a life policy (issued after 01-04-2012) are taxable if the annual premium exceeds:
A) 5% of sum assured  B) 10% of sum assured  C) 20% of sum assured  D) 15% of sum assured
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 10% for post-01-04-2012 policies (20% for 01-04-2003 to 31-03-2012). Death proceeds always exempt.
</details>

**Q19.** A ULIP loses its Section 10(10D) exemption if issued on/after 01-02-2021 with aggregate premium exceeding:
A) ₹1,50,000  B) ₹2,50,000  C) ₹5,00,000  D) ₹10,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Over ₹2,50,000 → treated as a capital asset.
</details>

**Q20.** A reverse-mortgage loan received by a senior citizen is:
A) Taxed as Other Sources  B) Exempt under Section 10(43)  C) Capital gain  D) Taxed at 12.50%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 47(xvi) says it's not a transfer; Section 10(43) exempts the loan amount.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Mr. Mehta, a resident individual employee (old tax regime), has the following for FY 2024-25:
1. He **exercised ESOPs** on 01-04-2024: 1,000 shares, FMV on exercise ₹400, price paid ₹150. Shares **allotted 01-05-2024**, listed, STT applicable. He **sold all 1,000 on 10-08-2024 @₹600** (STT paid).
2. He **bought an SGB** (100 units) at nominal ₹4,00,000 on 01-05-2019, and **redeemed it on maturity** for ₹6,50,000.
3. NPS: his **own contribution ₹1,80,000** (salary ₹15,00,000, basic+DA ₹12,00,000); his **employer contributed ₹1,20,000** (10% of salary). He has separately exhausted ₹1,50,000 under 80C via PPF.
4. He holds units in a **Category-I AIF (trust)**; the AIF earned business income (his 20% share = ₹2,00,000) and interest income (his share = ₹50,000), all credited to him.

**Q1.** Compute the ESOP perquisite and the capital gain on sale.
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- **Perquisite (salary):** (FMV on exercise ₹400 − price paid ₹150) × 1,000 = **₹2,50,000**.
- **Capital gain:** Cost = exercise FMV ₹400 × 1,000 = ₹4,00,000; holding from 01-05-2024 to 10-08-2024 ≈ 3 months (≤12 months) → **STCG**.
- STCG = (600 − 400) × 1,000 = **₹2,00,000 @20% (Sec 111A)** = ₹40,000 tax (+ surcharge/cess).
</details>

**Q2.** How is the SGB redemption taxed?
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- Mr. Mehta is an **individual** redeeming on **maturity** → **Section 47**: not a transfer → **no capital gain**, even though he gained ₹2,50,000.
- (Only the annual 2.5% interest was taxable each year as Other Sources.)
</details>

**Q3.** Compute Mr. Mehta's NPS deductions (old regime).
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- 80C cap of ₹1,50,000 is already used by PPF → **80CCD(1) = nil** (cap exhausted).
- **80CCD(1B):** additional ₹50,000 from his own contribution → **₹50,000**.
- **80CCD(2) (employer):** lower of ₹1,20,000 actual or 10% of salary (10% × 12,00,000 basic+DA = ₹1,20,000) → **₹1,20,000** (over and above the ₹1.5 lakh cap).
- **Total NPS deduction = 50,000 + 1,20,000 = ₹1,70,000.** (His own ₹1,80,000 contribution gives only the ₹50,000 1B benefit since 80C is full.)
</details>

**Q4.** How is the AIF income taxed in Mr. Mehta's hands?
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- **Business income share (₹2,00,000):** taxed at the **AIF level** (a trust → MMR); **exempt for Mr. Mehta** under Section 10(23FBB).
- **Interest income share (₹50,000):** pass-through → taxed **in Mr. Mehta's hands** as interest (Other Sources), retaining its character.
- So only **₹50,000** is added to his income from the AIF.
</details>

**Q5.** Summarise Mr. Mehta's taxable additions from the above (ignore surcharge/cess).
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

| Item | Amount | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| ESOP perquisite | ₹2,50,000 | Salary (slab) |
| ESOP sale STCG | ₹2,00,000 | 111A @20% |
| SGB redemption | ₹0 | Exempt (Sec 47) |
| AIF interest share | ₹50,000 | Other Sources (slab) |
| NPS deductions | (₹1,70,000) | Reduces taxable income |

Net: salary/other-sources additions ₹2,50,000 + ₹50,000 = ₹3,00,000 (slab), STCG ₹2,00,000 @20%, less ₹1,70,000 NPS deduction.
</details>
