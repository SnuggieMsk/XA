## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Under an Employee Stock Option Plan (ESOP), the option given to an employee to buy shares is best described as a:
A) Right and an obligation  B) Right but not an obligation  C) Obligation but not a right  D) Neither a right nor an obligation
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The workbook states the option "confers a right but not an obligation on the employee." The employee may exercise it but is never forced to.
- A) Wrong: there is no obligation to buy; calling it an obligation is incorrect.
- C) Wrong: it is fundamentally a right, not an obligation.
- D) Wrong: it clearly is a right (to purchase shares at a fixed price).

Intuition: ESOPs reward loyalty — if the share price falls below the exercise price, the employee can simply walk away, which only makes sense if exercising is optional.
</details>

**Q2.** The waiting period an employee must complete before becoming entitled to exercise an ESOP is called the:
A) Exercise period  B) Grant period  C) Vesting period  D) Lock-in window
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The "vesting period" is defined as the time an employee must wait to get the right to buy the specified shares.
- A) Wrong: the exercise period/date is when the employee actually buys, after vesting.
- B) Wrong: granting is when the ESOP is first offered, not the waiting period.
- D) Wrong: "lock-in" is a loose related idea but the workbook's defined term is vesting.

Intuition: Vest = "becomes yours to use." You can only exercise once the right has vested.
</details>

**Q3.** At the time of allotment of shares under an ESOP, the difference between FMV and the price paid by the employee is taxed under the head:
A) Capital gains  B) Salary (as a perquisite)  C) Income from other sources  D) Profits and gains of business
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The value of shares allotted free or at a concessional rate is a perquisite, taxable as salary under Section 17(2)(vi).
- A) Wrong: capital gains arise only later, when the shares are sold.
- C) Wrong: "other sources" is used for things like SGB interest, not ESOP perquisites.
- D) Wrong: business income is unrelated; the employee earns this as employment compensation.

Intuition: The discount you get from your employer is a benefit *from your job* — so it is salary.
</details>

**Q4.** For computing the ESOP perquisite, the Fair Market Value (FMV) to be used is the FMV on the:
A) Grant date  B) Vesting date  C) Exercise date  D) Allotment date
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Step 1 of the workbook's method: take the FMV on the date the option is *exercised*. The FMV on vesting or allotment is explicitly not relevant.
- A) Wrong: grant date FMV is never used.
- B) Wrong: the workbook explicitly says the FMV on the vesting date shall not be considered.
- D) Wrong: tax arises in the year of allotment, but the FMV used is the exercise-date FMV.

Intuition: You "lock in" the value when you choose to buy (exercise), so that date's price is the reference.
</details>

**Q5.** When ESOP shares are later sold, the period of holding for capital gains is counted from the date of:
A) Grant  B) Vesting  C) Exercise  D) Allotment
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Holding period runs from the date of *allotment* of shares to the date of sale.
- A), B), C) Wrong: grant, vesting and exercise all precede allotment and are not the starting point for holding.

Intuition: You only truly own the shares once they are allotted to you, so the clock starts there.
</details>

**Q6.** For ESOP shares, the cost of acquisition used to compute capital gains is the:
A) Price paid by the employee  B) FMV on the exercise date  C) FMV on the grant date  D) FMV on the sale date
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The FMV on the date of exercising the option becomes the cost of acquisition.
- A) Wrong: using only the price paid would double-tax the perquisite already taxed as salary.
- C) Wrong: grant-date FMV is irrelevant throughout.
- D) Wrong: the sale-date value is the sale consideration, not the cost.

Intuition: The exercise-date FMV was already taxed as salary, so it becomes your "purchase cost" to avoid taxing the same amount twice.
</details>

**Q7.** ESOP perquisite tax can be deferred only for employees of an eligible start-up referred to in Section:
A) 80C  B) 80-IAC  C) 80CCD  D) 115UA
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

- A) Wrong: 80C is a general investment deduction.
- C) Wrong: 80CCD deals with NPS contributions.
- D) Wrong: 115UA is the REIT taxation section.

Intuition: Start-ups pay talent partly in ESOPs; deferment helps employees who have no cash yet to pay the tax.
</details>

**Q8.** To be an eligible start-up under Section 80-IAC, total turnover in the relevant previous year must not exceed:
A) Rs. 25 crore  B) Rs. 50 crore  C) Rs. 100 crore  D) Rs. 250 crore

**Q9.** Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) are issued by the:

**Q10.** SGBs carry a fixed interest rate of:

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — SGBs pay 2.50% per annum (fixed) on the nominal value, credited half-yearly.
- B), C), D) Wrong: these rates are not the SGB rate stated in the workbook.

Intuition: The main attraction of SGBs is gold price appreciation; the 2.5% interest is a modest top-up.
</details>

**Q11.** The interest on SGBs is taxable under which head of income?
A) Capital gains  B) Salary  C) Income from other sources  D) Business income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — SGB interest is chargeable under "Income from other sources" at the assessee's applicable rates.
- A) Wrong: capital gains apply to transfer/redemption, not the periodic interest.
- B), D) Wrong: it is neither employment nor business income.

Intuition: Interest you receive from a bond you simply hold is classic "other sources" income.
</details>

**Q12.** Is TDS deducted on the interest paid on SGBs?

- A), B), D) Wrong: no TDS applies, so no rate is deducted at source (the investor still pays tax on it when filing).

Intuition: No tax is *withheld*, but the interest is still taxable — you declare and pay it yourself.
</details>

**Q13.** The maturity (tenor) of a Sovereign Gold Bond is:

**Q14.** Premature redemption of an SGB is permitted after the:

**Q15.** Under Section 47, redemption of SGBs by an individual is:
A) Treated as a transfer and taxed as LTCG  B) Not treated as a transfer, so no capital gain arises  C) Taxed at 20% always  D) Taxed at slab rate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 47 provides that redemption by an individual is not a "transfer," so no capital gains tax arises.
- A), C), D) Wrong: because there is no "transfer," no capital gains computation (and hence no rate) applies for an individual on redemption.

Intuition: No transfer = no capital gain. The government rewards individuals who hold SGBs to redemption.
</details>

**Q22.** For a self-employed individual, the NPS deduction under Section 80CCD(1) is limited to what percentage of gross total income?

**Q23.** At final withdrawal on closure/exit from NPS, the lump sum is exempt up to what percentage of the total corpus?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

- B) Wrong: 40% is not the figure used here.
- D) Wrong: the exemption is framed as 60% of corpus, even though it covers the full permitted withdrawal.

</details>

**Q28.** REITs are described as which kind of entity for tax purposes?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Q29.** For which type of income does an InvIT differ from a REIT in tax treatment?

- A), B) Wrong: SPV interest and dividend are treated identically for both.
- D) Wrong: capital gains on property are taxed at the trust level for both.

Intuition: InvITs invest in infrastructure (roads, towers) rather than rent-yielding buildings, so rental pass-through is handled differently.
</details>

**Q35.** Under Section 10(43), the loan amount received under a notified reverse mortgage scheme is:
