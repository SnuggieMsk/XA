# Chapter 7: Concepts in Taxation — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** Income earned during FY 2024-25 is assessed in which assessment year?
A) AY 2023-24  B) AY 2024-25  C) AY 2025-26  D) AY 2026-27
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) AY 2025-26** — The AY is the FY immediately following the previous year. PY = FY 2024-25, so AY = FY 2025-26. The income year and assessment year are never the same.
</details>

**Q2.** Which body administers the Income Tax Act, 1961?
A) RBI  B) SEBI  C) CBDT  D) Ministry of Corporate Affairs
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) CBDT** — The Central Board of Direct Taxes administers the Act under the Ministry of Finance.
</details>

**Q3.** A Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR) is taxed in India on:
A) Indian income only  B) Worldwide income  C) Only income received in India  D) Only foreign income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Worldwide income** — ROR pays tax on global income. NR and (mostly) RNOR are taxed only on Indian income; RNOR adds foreign income from an India-controlled business/profession.
</details>

**Q4.** An individual stays in India for 182 days or more in the previous year. He is:
A) Non-resident  B) Resident  C) Deemed resident only  D) RNOR automatically
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Resident** — 182+ days in the PY satisfies the basic residence test. Whether he is ROR or RNOR is a second step based on the 9-of-10-years / 729-days tests.
</details>

**Q5.** For an Indian citizen visiting India whose Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh, the relevant stay threshold (with 365 days in 4 preceding years) is:
A) 60 days  B) 120 days  C) 182 days  D) 240 days
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) 120 days** — When Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh, the threshold drops to 120 days (plus 365 days in the 4 preceding years). At ≤₹15 lakh the 182-day test applies.
</details>

**Q6.** Mr. C returns to India, stays 298 days in FY 2024-25, was non-resident in 9 of the last 10 years and spent 578 days in India over the last 7 years. His status is:
A) ROR  B) RNOR  C) Non-resident  D) Deemed resident
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) RNOR** — 298 ≥ 182 → resident. He satisfies an NOR condition (non-resident 9/10 years AND ≤729 days in 7 years), so he is Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident. His offshore bank interest is not taxed in India.
</details>

**Q7.** When is an HUF a non-resident?
A) When the Karta is a non-resident  B) When control & management is wholly outside India  C) When all members live abroad  D) When it earns only foreign income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) When control & management is wholly outside India** — Even a single management decision in India makes the HUF resident. The Karta's status only decides ROR vs RNOR.
</details>

**Q8.** Which is NOT one of the five heads of income?
A) Salary  B) Capital Gains  C) Dividends  D) Income from House Property
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Dividends** — The five heads are Salary, House Property, Business/Profession, Capital Gains, Other Sources. Dividend income is taxed under "Other Sources" but is not itself a head.
</details>

**Q9.** The standard deduction on salary under the new tax regime is:
A) ₹40,000  B) ₹50,000  C) ₹75,000  D) ₹1,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) ₹75,000** — New regime ₹75,000; Old regime ₹50,000.
</details>

**Q10.** An employer's contribution to NPS for a private-sector employee is deductible under 80CCD(2) up to what % of salary in the new regime?
A) 10%  B) 12%  C) 14%  D) 20%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) 14%** — New regime private-sector employees get 14% (same as Govt). Old regime caps private sector at 10%.
</details>

**Q11.** Income from a vacant plot of land (no building) is taxed under:
A) Income from House Property  B) Capital Gains  C) Business/Profession or Other Sources  D) Salary
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Business/Profession or Other Sources** — House Property requires a building (and land appurtenant thereto). Vacant land and under-construction property are not "buildings", so their income falls under PGBP or Other Sources.
</details>

**Q12.** The standard deduction available on Net Annual Value of let-out house property is:
A) 30%  B) 50%  C) ₹2,00,000  D) ₹50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A) 30%** — Section 24(a) gives a flat 30% of Net Annual Value. The ₹2,00,000 is the interest limit for self-occupied property in the old regime, not the standard deduction.
</details>

**Q13.** Under Section 43(5), which of the following is NOT a speculative transaction?
A) Buying shares settled without delivery  B) A derivatives transaction  C) A commodity contract settled in cash  D) A non-delivery sale of shares
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) A derivatives transaction** — Hedging transactions and derivatives are specifically excluded from being speculative. Non-delivery settlements of commodities/shares are speculative.
</details>

**Q14.** A long-term capital loss (LTCL) can be set off against:
A) Any income  B) Salary income  C) Long-term capital gains only  D) Any capital gain
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Long-term capital gains only** — LTCL is set off only against LTCG. (STCL can be set off against any capital gain.) Capital losses are never set off against other heads.
</details>

**Q15.** Speculative business losses can be carried forward for how many years?
A) 4 years  B) 8 years  C) Indefinitely  D) Cannot be carried forward
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A) 4 years** — Speculative loss carries forward 4 years (set off only against speculative profit). Normal business loss carries forward 8 years; unabsorbed depreciation indefinitely.
</details>

**Q16.** The combined ceiling under sections 80C + 80CCC + 80CCD(1) (Section 80CCE) is:
A) ₹1,00,000  B) ₹1,50,000  C) ₹2,00,000  D) ₹2,50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) ₹1,50,000** — Section 80CCE caps the aggregate of these three at ₹1,50,000.
</details>

**Q17.** Under the new regime (FY 2025-26), the maximum Section 87A rebate (income up to ₹12,00,000) is:
A) ₹12,500  B) ₹25,000  C) ₹50,000  D) ₹60,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D) ₹60,000** — From FY 2025-26, new-regime rebate is up to ₹60,000 for income ≤ ₹12,00,000, with marginal relief just above. (Old regime: ₹12,500 ≤ ₹5L; new regime AY 2024-25: ₹25,000 ≤ ₹7L.)
</details>

**Q18.** The Maximum Marginal Rate of tax for an individual is:
A) 30%  B) 34.944%  C) 42.744%  D) 39%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) 42.744%** — 30% slab + 37% surcharge on it (11.1%) + 4% cess (1.644%) = 42.744%. (34.944% is the firm rate at 12% surcharge — a distractor.)
</details>

**Q19.** A DTAA usually relieves double taxation by:
A) Exempting all foreign income  B) The residence country giving credit for tax paid in the source country  C) The source country refunding tax  D) Charging tax only once at a flat 10%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Residence country giving credit for source-country tax** — Most DTAAs allocate taxing rights or give the residence country a credit for tax paid to the source country.
</details>

**Q20.** Which investment is classified as EEE?
A) 5-year tax-saving bank FD  B) Senior Citizen Savings Scheme  C) Public Provident Fund (PPF)  D) Bank savings account
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) PPF** — PPF and ELSS are EEE (exempt at all three stages). 5-year tax-saving FD and SCSS are ETE. Under the new regime the first "E" disappears, turning many into TEE.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Mr. Raghav's tax year (FY 2024-25).** Mr. Raghav, an Indian citizen aged 45, was working abroad for several years and was a non-resident in 9 of the last 10 financial years. He returned to India and stayed **210 days** in FY 2024-25; over the **7 preceding years** he had spent a total of **600 days** in India. During FY 2024-25 he had:
- Taxable **salary** of ₹15,00,000
- A **let-out house** earning rent of ₹3,00,000; municipal taxes paid ₹20,000; interest on the housing loan ₹6,00,000
- **Interest of ₹4,00,000** in a bank account in Dubai
- He gifted ₹25,00,000 to his **wife**, who earned ₹1,20,000 interest on it
- He invested ₹1,80,000 in 80C-eligible instruments

He files under the **Old tax regime**.

**Q1.** Determine Mr. Raghav's residential status for FY 2024-25.
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

- Stay = 210 days ≥ 182 → **Resident**.
- NOR test: he was non-resident in **9 of 10** preceding years (condition satisfied) — and also spent only **600 days (≤729)** in the 7 preceding years (also satisfied). He needs only one.
- Therefore **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)** for FY 2024-25.
</details>

**Q2.** Is the ₹4,00,000 Dubai bank interest taxable in India?
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**No.** An RNOR is taxed on Indian income plus foreign income only if it is from a business controlled from / profession set up in India. Passive offshore bank interest is *not* such income, so the ₹4,00,000 is **not taxable in India**.
</details>

**Q3.** Compute the income from the let-out house property (Old regime).
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

| Step | Amount (₹) |
|------|-----------:|
| Annual Value (rent) | 3,00,000 |
| Less: Municipal taxes paid | (20,000) |
| Net Annual Value (NAV) | 2,80,000 |
| Less: Standard deduction 30% of NAV | (84,000) |
| Less: Interest on loan (no limit for let-out) | (6,00,000) |
| **Income from house property** | **(4,04,000) loss** |

So the house property gives a **loss of ₹4,04,000**.
</details>

**Q4.** How much of this house-property loss can be set off this year, and how much carries forward?
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

In the **Old regime**, house-property loss set off against other heads is capped at **₹2,00,000**.
- Set off against salary this year: **₹2,00,000**.
- Balance carried forward = 4,04,000 − 2,00,000 = **₹2,04,000**, available for **8 years** but only against future house-property income.
</details>

**Q5.** Compute Mr. Raghav's total taxable income for FY 2024-25 (Old regime), including clubbing.
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

| Item | Amount (₹) |
|------|-----------:|
| Salary | 15,00,000 |
| House property loss set off (capped) | (2,00,000) |
| Dubai interest (RNOR – not taxable) | 0 |
| Wife's interest ₹1,20,000 **clubbed** (s.64) | 1,20,000 |
| Gross Total Income | 14,20,000 |
| Less: 80C (invested 1,80,000, capped) | (1,50,000) |
| **Total Income** | **12,70,000** |

Note: the ₹1,20,000 interest earned by the wife on the gifted money is clubbed in Mr. Raghav's income and taxed at his slab. The ₹2,04,000 unabsorbed house-property loss is carried forward.
</details>
