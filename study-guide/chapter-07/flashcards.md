# Chapter 7: Concepts in Taxation — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check.

**Q1.** Which Act governs income tax in India, and which body administers it?
> **A:** The **Income Tax Act, 1961**, under the Ministry of Finance; administered by the **Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT)**. The Income Tax Rules, 1962 carry out its purposes, plus CBDT Circulars and Notifications.

**Q2.** Define Previous Year and Assessment Year.
> **A:** **Previous Year (PY)** = the financial year in which income is earned (Section 3). **Assessment Year (AY)** = the FY immediately after the PY, in which the income is assessed (Section 2(9), 12 months from 1 April). E.g. income earned FY 2021-22 (PY) is assessed in AY 2022-23.

**Q3.** Name the seven categories of "Person" under Section 2(31).
> **A:** Individual; HUF; Company; Partnership firm (incl. LLP); AOP/BOI; Local authority; Artificial juridical person.

**Q4.** Who is an "assessee" (Section 2(7))?
> **A:** A person liable to pay tax or any sum under the Act, or against whom any proceeding (re income, loss or refund) has been initiated.

**Q5.** How does residential status differ from citizenship?
> **A:** They are unrelated. You can be a foreign citizen yet a tax-resident of India, or an Indian citizen yet a non-resident. Residential status under the Income Tax Act and under FEMA are also separate concepts.

**Q6.** What income does a Resident & Ordinarily Resident (ROR) pay tax on in India?
> **A:** **Worldwide income** — income received/accruing in India *and* income accruing outside India.

**Q7.** What does an RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident) get taxed on?
> **A:** Indian income (received/accruing in India), **plus** foreign income only if it is **from a business controlled from India or a profession set up in India**. Other foreign income is not taxed.

**Q8.** What does a Non-Resident pay tax on in India?
> **A:** Only **Indian income** — income received or deemed received in India, and income accruing or deemed to accrue in India.

**Q9.** State the basic 182-day test of residence.
> **A:** An individual is **resident** if he stays in India for **182 days or more** during the relevant previous year.

**Q10.** State the secondary residence test (60-day rule).
> **A:** Resident if in India **60+ days** in the FY **and 365+ days** during the 4 preceding years. (For non-PIO foreigners this is one of the two tests; for Indian citizens leaving/visiting, modified thresholds apply.)

**Q11.** What does ₹15 lakh of Indian income trigger for Indian citizens/PIOs visiting India?
> **A:** If Indian income **exceeds ₹15 lakh**, the threshold drops to **120 days** (plus 365 days in 4 preceding years). At ≤₹15 lakh, the 182-day test applies.

**Q12.** What is the "deemed resident" rule under Section 6(1A)?
> **A:** An **Indian citizen** is deemed resident (regardless of days in India) if Indian income **> ₹15 lakh** AND he is **not liable to tax in any other country**. A deemed resident is always treated as **RNOR**.

**Q13.** When is a resident treated as Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)?
> **A:** If he satisfies ANY one: (a) non-resident in **9 of the 10** preceding years; (b) in India **729 days or less** in the **7** preceding years; or (c) resident only via the deemed-resident clause (f).

**Q14.** When is an HUF resident vs non-resident?
> **A:** **Resident** unless control & management of its affairs is **wholly outside India** (even one decision in India ⇒ resident). **Non-resident** only if control & management is wholly outside India.

**Q15.** Name the five heads of income.
> **A:** (1) Salary, (2) House Property, (3) Profits & Gains from Business or Profession, (4) Capital Gains, (5) Income from Other Sources.

**Q16.** What is the standard deduction on salary, Old vs New regime?
> **A:** **₹50,000 (Old)** and **₹75,000 (New)**.

**Q17.** What is the 80CCD(2) limit on employer's NPS contribution for private-sector employees in each regime?
> **A:** **10%** of salary in the Old regime; **14%** of salary in the New regime (14% for Govt employees in both).

**Q18.** When does an employer's contribution to PF/superannuation/NPS become taxable salary?
> **A:** EPF contribution above **12%** of salary is taxable; and the **aggregate** of employer contributions to superannuation + EPF + NPS exceeding **₹7,50,000** is taxed as salary (s.17(2)(vii)).

**Q19.** What is the maximum gratuity exemption under section 10(10)?
> **A:** **₹20,00,000** (one of the "least of" limits). Government employees get full exemption.

**Q20.** What is the standard deduction for house property income, and under which section?
> **A:** **30% of Net Annual Value**, under **Section 24(a)**.

**Q21.** What is the home-loan interest deduction for a self-occupied house, Old vs New regime?
> **A:** Up to **₹2,00,000** in the Old regime (₹30,000 if construction not completed within 5 years); **not available (Nil)** in the New regime. Let-out property has **no limit**.

**Q22.** Up to how much house-property loss can be set off against other heads, and in which regime?
> **A:** Up to **₹2,00,000** against other heads in the **Old regime**. New regime: not allowed against other heads (deemed set off). Unadjusted loss carries forward 8 years against house-property income only.

**Q23.** Under Section 43(5), what is a speculative transaction — and which are excluded?
> **A:** A purchase/sale of a commodity (incl. shares) settled **otherwise than by actual delivery**. **Hedging transactions and derivatives are NOT speculative.**

**Q24.** How long can speculative vs normal business losses be carried forward?
> **A:** Speculative loss: **4 years** (set off only against speculative profit). Normal business loss: **8 years**. Unabsorbed depreciation: **indefinitely**.

**Q25.** State the set-off rules for capital losses.
> **A:** **LTCL** → only against **LTCG**. **STCL** → against any capital gain (STCG or LTCG). Capital losses **cannot** be set off against any other head. Carry forward **8 years**.

**Q26.** What is a condition for carrying forward losses?
> **A:** The **return must be filed by the due date under Section 139(1)** (condonation may be sought from the AO/CBDT).

**Q27.** Can a loss under "Income from Other Sources" be carried forward?
> **A:** **No.** It can be set off against any head (except gambling) in the same year but cannot be carried forward.

**Q28.** What does Section 60 (clubbing) cover?
> **A:** Transfer of **income without transferring the asset** → the income is still taxed in the **transferor's** hands.

**Q29.** Give the Section 64 clubbing example with the wife's gift.
> **A:** Mr. A gifts ₹30,00,000 to his wife; she earns ₹1,50,000 interest. The ₹1,50,000 is **clubbed in Mr. A's income** and taxed at **his** slab rate.

**Q30.** What is Gross Total Income (Section 80B(5))?
> **A:** Total income under the five heads computed **before** any Chapter VI-A deduction. Steps: compute 5 heads → club income → set off losses.

**Q31.** What is the 80C / 80CCE combined ceiling?
> **A:** 80C + 80CCC + 80CCD(1) together cannot exceed **₹1,50,000** (Section 80CCE).

**Q32.** Which Chapter VI-A deductions ARE available in the new regime?
> **A:** **80CCD(2)** (employer NPS, up to 14%) and **80CCH** (Agniveer corpus). Most others (80C, 80D, 80CCD(1)/(1B), 80G, etc.) are not.

**Q33.** State the Section 87A rebate amounts.
> **A:** Old regime: up to **₹12,500** if income ≤ ₹5,00,000. New regime (AY 2024-25): up to **₹25,000** if income ≤ ₹7,00,000. New regime (FY 2025-26): up to **₹60,000** if income ≤ ₹12,00,000. Marginal relief applies just above the thresholds.

**Q34.** Against which tax is the 87A rebate NOT available?
> **A:** Against tax on **LTCG under Section 112A** (listed equity/equity MF/ULIP/business trust gains chargeable to STT) and on accumulated provident fund balance (s.111).

**Q35.** What is the Maximum Marginal Rate, and how is it built up?
> **A:** **42.744%** = 30% highest slab + surcharge (30%×37% = 11.1%) + cess (4% on 30%+11.1% = 1.644%). (Section 2(29C).)

**Q36.** Give the effective tax rate formula and the partnership-firm example.
> **A:** Effective rate = Applicable rate × (1 + Surcharge) × (1 + Cess). Firm with income > ₹1 cr: 30% × 1.12 × 1.04 = **34.944%**.

**Q37.** What is a DTAA and how does it usually relieve double taxation?
> **A:** A **Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement** between countries; relief is usually given by the **residence country granting credit** for tax paid in the source country.

**Q38.** Classify PPF, ELSS, and 5-year tax-saving FD into EEE/EET/ETE.
> **A:** PPF and ELSS = **EEE**; 5-year tax-saving FD and Senior Citizen Savings Scheme = **ETE**. (First "E" disappears in the new regime, turning many into TEE.)
