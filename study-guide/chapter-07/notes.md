# Chapter 7: Concepts in Taxation — Short Notes

## 🎯 What you'll learn
- The basic vocabulary of Indian income tax (the "grammar" you need before anything else)

- **Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (DTAA)** — avoiding being taxed twice

## 7.1 The Framework — who makes the rules?

Income tax in India runs on the **Income Tax Act, 1961** (we'll just call it "the Act"). It sits under the **Ministry of Finance**. The day-to-day administration is done by the **Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT)**.

- The **Income Tax Rules, 1962** spell out the "how-to" details.
- The CBDT also issues **Circulars** (clarifications) and **Notifications** (official announcements).

**Analogy:** The *Act* is the constitution (the big law), the *Rules* are the user-manual, and *Circulars/Notifications* are the sticky-notes the referee adds when players are confused.

💡 Why an investment adviser cares: the **same profit on selling a security can be taxed completely differently** depending on the type of security, how long you held it, and whether you acted as a *trader* or an *investor*.

## 7.2 Key Concepts (the vocabulary)

### 7.2.1 Previous Year (PY) vs Assessment Year (AY)
India uses the **Financial Year (FY): April 1 to March 31**.

- **Previous Year (PY):** the FY in which you *earn* the income. (Section 3 of the Act.)
- **Assessment Year (AY):** the FY *immediately after* the PY, in which you *file and get assessed* on that income. (Section 2(9) defines AY as "a period of 12 months commencing on the 1st day of April every year.")

**Analogy:** PY is the year you play the match; AY is the next year when the scorecard is reviewed and the prize/penalty is decided.

**Worked example:** You earned income between 1 April 2021 and 31 March 2022.
- PY = **FY 2021-22**
- AY = **FY 2022-23**

### 7.2.2 Person — Section 2(31)
"Person" is a wide bucket. It includes:

| # | Type of Person |
|---|----------------|
| a | Individual |
| b | Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) |
| c | Company |
| d | Partnership firm (including LLP) |
| e | Association of Persons (AOP) / Body of Individuals (BOI) |
| f | Local authority |
| g | Artificial juridical person (not in any category above) |

⚠️ For (e), (f), (g): they count as a "person" **even if not formed to make a profit**.

### 7.2.3 Assessee — Section 2(7)
An **assessee** is the person *liable to pay tax* (or any sum) under the Act. It also includes anyone against whom a proceeding (about income, loss, or refund) has been started.

**Analogy:** "Assessee" = the player whose scorecard is being checked.

---

## 7.3 Income — Section 2(24)
The Act gives an **inclusive** definition: it *lists* things that are income, but anything else "in the nature of income" can also be taxed even if not listed.

Common items: profits/gains from business or profession, **dividend**, **capital gains**, **gifts from a non-relative** (covered by section 56), property received below market price (section 56), and certain employment perquisites.

## 7.4 Residential Status — the single most important concept

Your **residential status decides which income India can tax**. This is determined under **Section 6** of the Act, fresh **every financial year**.

⚠️ **Citizenship ≠ Residential status.** You can be a citizen of another country but a *resident* of India for tax, and vice-versa. Also, residential status under the **Income Tax Act** and under **FEMA** are different things.

| | Income Tax Act | FEMA |
|---|----------------|------|
| When determined | Every financial year | Day to day, at the time of an investment/remittance |
| Based on | Stay during the FY + prior FYs (different tests for Indian citizens/PIOs) | Days in preceding FY, but can change by stated intention now |
| What it impacts | Tax to be paid and TDS | Where/what you can invest, remittances, bank accounts |

**Why it matters (the logic):**
- A **resident** pays Indian tax on **worldwide income**.
- A **non-resident** pays Indian tax only on **Indian income**.

From **FY 2020-21**, status depends on **citizenship + period of stay + total Indian income**.

### Three categories
a) **Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR)**
b) **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR / NOR)**
c) **Non-Resident (NR)**

### 7.4.1 The "Deemed Resident" test — Section 6(1A)
An **Indian citizen** is *deemed resident* (regardless of days in India) if **both**:
1. His **Indian Income** (total income excluding income from foreign sources) **exceeds ₹15 lakh** in the PY, **and**
2. He is **not liable to tax in any other country** by reason of domicile/residence etc.

"Income from foreign sources" = income that accrues/arises outside India (except from a business controlled in / profession set up in India) and is not deemed to arise in India.

⚠️ A **deemed resident is always treated as RNOR** (not ROR).

### 7.4.2 Tests of Residence (day-count) — Section 6
A person is **Resident** if they meet conditions specific to their category.

**Part 1 — Indian Citizens:**
| Clause | Condition to be RESIDENT |
|--------|--------------------------|
| (a) | Stays in India **182 days or more** in the relevant PY; OR |
| (b) | Visiting India with **Indian Income ≤ ₹15 lakh** and in India **182+ days**, OR leaves India for employment; OR |
| (c) | Leaves India as crew of an Indian ship and in India **182+ days** and **365+ days** in the 4 preceding years; OR |
| (d) | Visiting India with **Indian Income > ₹15 lakh** and in India **120+ days** and **365+ days** in 4 preceding years; OR |
| (e) | Not visiting/leaving (and not under (a)): in India **60+ days** in the FY **and 365+ days** in the 4 preceding years; OR |
| (f) | Deemed-resident test: Total income (excluding foreign-source) > ₹15 lakh and not liable to tax in any other country |

**Part 2 — Foreign nationals who are Persons of Indian Origin (PIO)** — a PIO is someone whose he/his parents/grandparents were born in *undivided India*:
| Clause | Condition |
|--------|-----------|
| (a) | In India **182+ days**; OR |
| (b) | Visiting with **Indian Income ≤ ₹15 lakh**, in India **182+ days** and **365+ days** in 4 preceding years; OR |
| (c) | Visiting with **Indian Income > ₹15 lakh**, in India **120+ days** and **365+ days** in 4 preceding years; OR |
| (d) | Not visiting (and not under (a)): in India **60+ days** in FY and **365+ days** in 4 preceding years |

**Part 3 — All other foreign nationals (non-PIO):**
| Clause | Condition |
|--------|-----------|
| (a) | In India **182+ days**; OR |
| (b) | In India **60+ days** (but < 182) in the FY **and 365+ days** in the 4 preceding years |

### When is a Resident treated as RNOR (Not Ordinarily Resident)?
A resident is **NOR** if he satisfies **any ONE** of:
- (a) He was a **non-resident in at least 9 out of the 10** preceding years; OR
- (b) He was in India for **729 days or less** during the **7** preceding years; OR
- (c) He became resident only by clause (f) above (the deemed-resident route).

If **none** of these is satisfied → he is **ROR**.

💡 **Memory hook:** "**9-out-of-10** non-resident" OR "**729 days in 7 years**" → you're the *gentler* RNOR (foreign income mostly safe).

### 7.4.3 Residential Status of an HUF
- **Resident:** in *every* case **except** where control & management of its affairs is **wholly outside India** during the year. (Even one decision taken in India ⇒ resident.)
- **Non-Resident:** control & management **wholly outside India**.
- **RNOR:** a resident HUF is NOR if the **Karta (Manager)** satisfies either: non-resident 9 of 10 preceding years, OR in India 729 days or less in the 7 preceding years.

## 7.5 Scope of Income (what gets taxed for each status)

This is the *payoff* of residential status. Table 7.1:

| Nature of income | ROR | RNOR | NR |
|------------------|:---:|:----:|:--:|
| Received / deemed received **in India** | Taxable | Taxable | Taxable |
| Accrues/arises / deemed to accrue **in India** | Taxable | Taxable | Taxable |
| Accrues outside India **from a business controlled from / profession set up in India** | Taxable | Taxable | Not taxable |
| Any other income accruing **outside India** | Taxable | Not taxable | Not taxable |

**In words:** ROR = worldwide income. RNOR = Indian income + foreign income only if from an India-controlled business/profession. NR = Indian income only.

**Worked example (Example 1 from text):** Mr. C (Indian citizen) returns to India in FY 2024-25; stay = 298 days (≥182 ⇒ **Resident**). In the 7 preceding years he spent **578 days** (≤729) and was non-resident in 9 of 10 preceding years. He satisfies an NOR condition ⇒ **RNOR for FY 2024-25**. So foreign bank interest is *not* taxed in India.

**Worked example (Example 2):** Mr. A is ROR with a USA bank account. Interest there **is taxable in India** (worldwide income). If he were RNOR, that USA interest would **not** be taxed in India.

💡 Citizenship never comes "automatically" by staying X years (Example 4) — that's immigration law, not tax law.

## 7.6 The Five Heads of Income

Total income is the **sum of income computed under five heads**:

| # | Head | Quick meaning |
|---|------|---------------|
| 1 | **Salary** | Pay from an employer (needs employer–employee relationship) |
| 2 | **House Property** | Rent/annual value from a building + land |
| 3 | **Profits & Gains from Business or Profession (PGBP)** | Income from running a business/profession |
| 4 | **Capital Gains** | Profit on selling a capital asset (Chapter 8) |
| 5 | **Income from Other Sources (IFOS)** | Everything else not exempt (Chapter 9) |

### 7.6.1 Income from Salary — Section 17(1)
"Salary" includes wages, annuity, pension, gratuity, fees, commission, advance of salary, perquisites/profits in lieu of salary, leave encashment, employer's PF contribution **above 12% of salary**, pension scheme & Agniveer corpus contributions, etc.

- **Needs an employer-employee relationship.** Without it, income falls under business or other sources.
- Taxed on **due or receipt basis, whichever is earlier** (ESOPs are an exception, see Ch.12).

**Key salary deductions — Old vs New regime:**

| Item | Old Regime | New Regime |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Standard deduction | ₹50,000 | ₹75,000 |
| Employee's NPS 80CCD(1B) | up to ₹50,000 | Not available |
| Employer's NPS 80CCD(2) – private sector | up to **10%** of salary | up to **14%** of salary |
| Employer's NPS 80CCD(2) – Govt employee | 14% | 14% |
| Employer EPF | taxable if > 12% of salary | taxable if > 12% of salary |
| HRA exemption | Available | Not allowed |
| Leave Travel Allowance | Allowed | Not allowed |

⚠️ Where **employer's combined contribution to superannuation + EPF + NPS exceeds ₹7,50,000**, the excess is taxed as salary [section 17(2)(vii)].

**Gratuity exemption (section 10(10)) — same in both regimes:**
- **Govt employee:** death-cum-retirement gratuity is **fully exempt**.
- **Covered by Payment of Gratuity Act:** exempt = least of (15 days' salary per completed year of service / **₹20,00,000** / actual gratuity).
- **Other cases:** exempt = least of (½ month's salary per completed year / **₹20,00,000** / actual gratuity).

**HRA exemption (Old regime) = least of:**
1. HRA received
2. Rent paid **−** 10% of salary
3. **50%** of salary (Mumbai/Delhi/Kolkata/Chennai) or **40%** elsewhere

**Worked example (text Example 2):** Mr. B, basic ₹15,00,000, employer EPF ₹21,600 (≤12% ⇒ Nil add-back), employer NPS ₹2,10,000. Total employer contribution ₹2,31,600 (< ₹7.5 lakh ⇒ no 17(2)(vii) add-back).
- Gross salary = 15,00,000 + 2,10,000 (employer NPS as perquisite) = 17,10,000
- Old: −SD 50,000 = 16,60,000; −80C (EPF 21,600 + PPF 1,50,000, capped at 1,50,000) = 1,50,000; −80CCD(1B) NPS 50,000; −80CCD(2) employer NPS 10% of 15,00,000 = 1,50,000 → deductions 3,50,000 → **Taxable 13,10,000**.
- New: −SD 75,000 = 16,35,000; only 80CCD(2) at 14% = 2,10,000 → **Taxable 14,25,000**.

### 7.6.2 Income from House Property
Taxable if income arises from a **building or land appurtenant thereto**, in the hands of the **owner** (or deemed owner).

⚠️ Vacant land or an **under-construction** property is *not* a "building" → its income is PGBP or Other Sources, not House Property.

**Three categories:** (a) Self-occupied, (b) Let-out, (c) Deemed let-out. You may pick **any two** non-let-out houses as self-occupied; the rest are deemed let-out.

**Computation:**
| Step | Self-occupied | Let-out / Deemed let-out |
|------|--------------|--------------------------|
| Annual Value (A) | NIL | Reasonable expected rent or actual rent received, whichever higher (deemed let-out = reasonable expected rent) |
| Less Municipal taxes (B) | — | only if actually paid by owner |
| Net Annual Value (C = A−B) | — | xxx |
| Less Standard deduction (E) = **30% of NAV** (section 24(a)) | — | (xxx) |
| Less Interest on home loan (F) (section 24(b)) | up to **₹2,00,000** (Old); **Nil** (New) | **no limit** |
| Income (G) | usually a loss = the interest | xxx |

**Interest rules (Old regime, self-occupied):** up to **₹2,00,000** if loan taken on/after 1 Apr 1999 and construction completed within 5 years; otherwise capped at **₹30,000**. **Pre-construction period interest** is allowed in **5 equal instalments** from the year of acquisition/completion.

**Loss set-off:** House-property loss can be set off against other heads up to **₹2,00,000** (Old regime). New regime: **not allowed against other heads** (but deemed set off). Unadjusted loss carries forward **8 years**, set off only against house property income.

**Worked example (text Example 2):** Salary 15,00,000; rent 2,20,000; interest on let-out loan 5,00,000.
- House property income = 2,20,000 − 30% (66,000) − 5,00,000 = **(3,46,000) loss**.
- Old: set off ₹2,00,000 against salary → GTI 13,00,000; −80C 1,50,000 → **Taxable 11,50,000**; carry forward **₹1,46,000**.
- New: no inter-head set off (taxable 15,00,000) but the **₹1,46,000 loss is still carried forward** (set off only against future house-property income).

### 7.6.3 Profits & Gains from Business or Profession (PGBP)
Business (Section 2(13)) includes any trade, commerce, manufacture, or adventure in their nature. Profits computed by the assessee's **accounting method**: cash system → receipt basis; mercantile system → accrual basis. Some assessees may use **presumptive taxation** (but not commission/brokerage earners).

**Speculative vs non-speculative (Section 43(5)):** a purchase/sale of any commodity (incl. shares) settled **otherwise than by actual delivery** is *speculative*. **Exceptions:** hedging transactions and **derivatives** are *not* speculative.
⚠️ **Speculative loss** can be set off **only against speculative profit**, and carried forward **4 years** (vs 8 years for normal business loss).

### 7.6.4 & 7.6.5 Capital Gains & Other Sources
Covered in detail in Chapters 8 and 9. (Capital gain = profit on transfer of a capital asset; STCG/LTCG depend on holding period.)

## 7.7 Clubbing of Income (Sections 60–64)
Sometimes *another person's* income is added ("clubbed") to your income to stop tax avoidance by gifting assets around. Income is first computed in the recipient's hands (after their deductions) under the relevant head, then clubbed in the transferor's hands **under the same head**. If it's a loss, the loss is clubbed too.

- **Section 60:** Transfer of *income* without transferring the *asset* → income still taxed in transferor's hands.
- **Section 64:** Clubbing of income of **spouse, son's wife, minor child, certain AOP, property gifted to HUF**.

**Worked example:** Mr. A gifts ₹30,00,000 to his wife; she earns ₹1,50,000 interest. That ₹1,50,000 is **clubbed in Mr. A's income** and taxed at *his* slab.

**Section 60 example:** E owns 100 debentures; assigns interest of 50 to nephew N (asset kept) → that interest is clubbed with E. Gifts 50 debentures to son P (asset + income both gone) → not clubbed under s.60 (but if son is a minor, clubbed under s.64).

## 7.8 Set-off and Carry-Forward of Losses

**Intra-head** = within the same head. **Inter-head** = across heads.

### 7.8.1 Capital gains losses
- **Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL):** set off **only against LTCG**.
- **Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL):** set off against **any capital gain (STCG or LTCG)**.
- Capital losses **cannot** be set off against any *other* head (inter-head not allowed).
- Carry forward: **8 assessment years**. STCL → against any CG; b/f LTCL → only against LTCG.
- ⚠️ Carry-forward allowed **only if return filed by the section 139(1) due date**.

### 7.8.2 Business losses
- Normal business loss: set off against any income **except salary** (and gambling). Carry forward **8 years**.
- Speculative loss: only against speculative profit; carry forward **4 years**.
- **Unabsorbed depreciation:** carried forward **indefinitely**, set off against any income **except salary**.

### 7.8.3 House property loss
Set off against other heads up to **₹2 lakh** (Old regime); New regime not allowed against other heads (deemed set off). Carry forward **8 years**, only against house-property income.

### 7.8.4 Other Sources loss
Set off against any head **except gambling**; **cannot be carried forward**.

**Summary (Table 7.4):**
| Loss type | Same-year set off | Carry forward | Future set-off against |
|-----------|-------------------|:-------------:|------------------------|
| House Property | Any head (≤₹2L, Old) | 8 yrs | House property only |
| Business (non-spec) | Any income except salary | 8 yrs | Business income |
| Speculation | Speculation profit only | 4 yrs | Speculation profit |
| Short-term Capital | Any capital gain | 8 yrs | Any capital gain |
| Long-term Capital | LTCG only | 8 yrs | LTCG only |
| Other Sources | Any head except gambling | Cannot c/f | — |

## 7.9–7.13 Exemptions, Deductions, Rebate, GTI, Total Income

- **Exempt incomes:** Chapter III (sections 10–13B) — not included in total income.
- **Deductions (Chapter VI-A):** allowed from Gross Total Income to encourage saving/social spending. (See Key Numbers table.)
- **Gross Total Income (GTI), Section 80B(5):** total income under the 5 heads **before** Chapter VI-A deductions. Steps: (1) compute 5 heads → (2) club income → (3) set off losses.
- **Total Income:** GTI **minus** Chapter VI-A deductions → the base for tax.

**Major Chapter VI-A deductions:**
| Section | For | Limit | In New Regime? |
|---------|-----|-------|:--------------:|
| 80C | LIC, PF, principal of home loan, small savings | ₹1,50,000 | No |
| 80CCD(1) | Employee/self NPS | 10% salary (emp.) / 20% GTI | No |
| 80CCD(1B) | Extra NPS / NPS Vatsalya | ₹50,000 | No |
| 80CCD(2) | Employer NPS | 14% (Govt) / 10% (others) salary | **Yes** |
| 80CCH | Agniveer Corpus | 100% | **Yes** |
| 80D | Medical insurance | ₹25k–₹1,00,000 (seniors) | No |
| 80DD | Disabled dependent | ₹75,000 (₹1,25,000 severe) | No |
| 80DDB | Specified diseases | ₹40,000 (₹1,00,000 senior) | No |
| 80E | Education loan interest | No limit | No |
| 80EEB | Electric-vehicle loan interest | ₹1,50,000 | No |
| 80G | Donations | 50%/100% of qualifying amount | No |
| 80GG | Rent paid (no HRA) | least of: rent − 10% of income / ₹5,000 p.m. / 25% of income | No |
| 80TTA | Savings a/c interest | ₹10,000 | No |
| 80TTB | Senior citizen deposit interest | ₹50,000 | No |
| 80U | Own disability | ₹75,000 (₹1,25,000 severe) | No |

⚠️ **Section 80CCE:** 80C + 80CCC + 80CCD(1) together **cannot exceed ₹1,50,000**.

### 7.11 Rebate under Section 87A
A rebate that can wipe out tax for small incomes.
- **Old regime:** up to **₹12,500** for resident individual with total income ≤ **₹5,00,000**.
- **New regime (from AY 2024-25):** up to **₹25,000** if income ≤ **₹7,00,000**; with **marginal relief** just above ₹7 lakh (tax ≈ income over ₹7 lakh, up to ₹7,28,000).
- **New regime (from FY 2025-26):** up to **₹60,000** if income ≤ **₹12,00,000**; marginal relief applies up to ~**₹12,71,250**.

⚠️ The 87A rebate is **not** available against tax on **LTCG under section 112A**.

## 7.14 Computation of Tax Payable (Non-corporate)
Tax at applicable slab + special rates → add **surcharge** (if applicable) and **Health & Education Cess (4%)** → reduce AMT credit / section 89 relief / foreign tax credit → add interest & late fees → subtract advance tax/TDS/TCS/self-assessment tax → **net payable or refund**.

## 7.15 Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (DTAA)
Two ways countries tax income:
- **Residence-based:** your home country taxes your worldwide income.
- **Source-based:** the country where the income arises taxes it.

India uses **both** → the same income can be taxed twice. A **DTAA** is an agreement between countries to avoid this, usually by the **residence country giving credit** for tax paid in the source country.

**Worked example:** Mr. IS (ROR) earns in France and pays French tax. India taxes his global income but gives him **credit for the French tax paid** (India is his residence country).

---

## 7.16 Taxation Regime — EEE / EET / ETE
A tax-saving investment has 3 stages: (1) you invest, (2) it earns returns, (3) you withdraw. Each stage is **E**(xempt) or **T**(axable).
- **EEE** (all three exempt): PPF, ELSS.
- **ETE** (exempt-tax-exempt): 5-year tax-saving FD; Senior Citizen Savings Scheme.
- **EET** (taxed only at withdrawal): chargeable to tax only at transfer/withdrawal.

💡 In the **new regime** the first "E" (deduction at investment) usually disappears, so many EEE products become **TEE**.

---

## 7.17 Maximum Marginal Rate (MMR) — Section 2(29C)
The rate (incl. surcharge + cess) on the **highest income slab** for an Individual/AOP/BOI.

| Particular | Rate (%) |
|-----------|----------|
| Highest slab rate (A) | 30 |
| Surcharge = A × 37% | 11.1 |
| Health & Education cess = (A+B) × 4% | 1.644 |
| **MMR** | **42.744** |

## 7.18 Effective Rate of Tax
Not defined in the Act; the rate *including* surcharge and cess actually borne.

**Formula:** Effective tax rate = Applicable rate × (1 + Surcharge rate) × (1 + Cess rate).

**Example:** Partnership firm with income > ₹1 crore: 30% × (1+12%) × (1+4%) = **34.944%**.
For an individual on slabs: effective rate = total tax ÷ total income. e.g. ₹4,29,000 tax on ₹20,00,000 = **21.45%**.

## 📌 One-Page Recap
- **PY** = year you earn; **AY** = next year you're assessed. India uses FY (Apr–Mar).
- **Residential status (Section 6)** decides scope: **ROR = worldwide**, **RNOR = Indian + India-controlled business income**, **NR = Indian only**.
- Basic day-tests: **182 days** in the PY (resident), or **60 days + 365 days/4 yrs**. Indian-income > ₹15 lakh triggers stricter 120-day and deemed-resident tests.
- **NOR if:** non-resident 9/10 yrs OR ≤729 days in 7 yrs (or deemed-resident route).
- **5 heads:** Salary, House Property, PGBP, Capital Gains, Other Sources.
- **Clubbing (60–64):** spouse/minor/son's wife income added to transferor.
- **Losses:** LTCL→LTCG only; STCL→any CG; capital losses never inter-head; carry forward 8 yrs (speculation 4). House-property loss inter-head ≤₹2L (Old only).
- **GTI = 5 heads − losses**; **Total Income = GTI − Chapter VI-A**.
- **87A rebate:** ₹12,500 (≤₹5L Old) / ₹25,000 (≤₹7L New) / ₹60,000 (≤₹12L New, FY25-26).
- **MMR = 42.744%**; **Effective rate** = rate × (1+surcharge) × (1+cess).
