## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Income from Other Sources (IFOS) is best described as which kind of head of income?
A) The first head, taking priority over all others  B) A head only for salaried persons  C) The residual ("catch-all") head  D) A head only for companies
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — IFOS is the residual head: any income that is **not exempt** and is **not chargeable under the other four heads** (Salaries, House Property, PGBP, Capital Gains) is taxed here.
- A) Wrong — it is the last, residual head, not a priority head.
- B) Wrong — it applies to all assessees, not only salaried persons.
- D) Wrong — it is not company-specific.
</details>

**Q2.** Which of the following are the other four heads of income under which an income is first tested before falling into IFOS?
A) Salaries, House Property, PGBP, Capital Gains  B) Salaries, Dividends, Interest, Gifts  C) Wages, Rent, Profit, Bonus  D) Income, Expenditure, Profit, Loss
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The four specific heads are Salaries, Income from House Property, Profits and Gains from Business or Profession (PGBP), and Capital Gains. Anything not exempt and not under these four falls under IFOS.
- B), C), D) Wrong — these mix up types of receipts/accounting terms with the statutory heads of income.
</details>

**Q3.** Dividend income is taxable under which head?
A) Salaries  B) Capital Gains  C) Income from Other Sources  D) PGBP always
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Dividend income (including buy-back proceeds received by a shareholder) is one of the incomes always taxable under IFOS.
- A), B) Wrong — dividend is neither salary nor a capital gain.
- D) Wrong — dividend is not, by default, business income for an ordinary shareholder.
</details>

**Q38.** Ms. R receives free shares (no consideration) with aggregate FMV of ₹45,000 during the year, from a non-relative. How much is taxable?
A) ₹45,000  B) ₹0  C) ₹5,000  D) ₹50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Rule: tax arises only if aggregate FMV **exceeds** ₹50,000. Here aggregate FMV = ₹45,000, which is ≤ ₹50,000, so nothing is taxable.
- A) Wrong — the full FMV is taxed only once the threshold is crossed.
- C) Wrong — there is no "excess over threshold" concept; it is all-or-nothing, and here it is nothing.
- D) Wrong — ₹50,000 is the threshold, not the taxable amount.
</details>

**Q39.** Ms. R instead receives free shares of aggregate FMV ₹70,000 from a non-relative. How much is taxable?
A) ₹20,000  B) ₹50,000  C) ₹70,000  D) ₹0
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Since aggregate FMV ₹70,000 **exceeds** ₹50,000, the **whole** ₹70,000 is taxable. The ₹50,000 is a threshold, not a basic exemption that is subtracted.
- A) Wrong — ₹70,000 − ₹50,000 = ₹20,000 would be wrong; you do not subtract the threshold.
- B) Wrong — ₹50,000 is the trigger, not the taxable figure.
- D) Wrong — once the threshold is crossed, the amount is taxable.
</details>

**Q40.** Mr. B buys shares of FMV ₹2,00,000 from a friend for ₹1,40,000. The taxable IFOS amount is:
A) ₹2,00,000  B) ₹1,40,000  C) ₹60,000  D) ₹10,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

- A), B) Wrong — neither the full FMV nor the price paid is the taxable amount.
- D) Wrong — you do not subtract ₹50,000 from the ₹60,000.
</details>
