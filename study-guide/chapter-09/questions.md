# Chapter 9: Income from Other Sources — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** Income from Other Sources is best described as:
A) The first head computed  B) A residual head for income not exempt and not under the other four heads  C) Only dividend income  D) A head only for non-residents
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) A residual head** — IFOS catches income not exempt and not chargeable under Salary, House Property, PGBP or Capital Gains; some incomes are always taxed here.
</details>

**Q2.** Since which assessment year is dividend taxable in the hands of the shareholder?
A) AY 2018-19  B) AY 2020-21  C) AY 2021-22  D) AY 2024-25
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) AY 2021-22** — DDT was abolished by the Finance Act 2020, so from AY 2021-22 dividend is taxed in the shareholder's hands at slab rate.
</details>

**Q3.** Dividend income is taxed at:
A) A flat 10%  B) 15% under DDT  C) The shareholder's applicable slab rate  D) 12.5%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Shareholder's applicable slab rate** — Under IFOS, dividend is taxed at the recipient's slab (subject to residential status; DTAA/MLI for non-residents).
</details>

**Q4.** From 1 October 2024, buy-back proceeds received by a shareholder are taxed as:
A) Capital gains  B) Dividend under IFOS  C) Business income  D) Exempt income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Dividend under IFOS** — with no deduction for expenses. The cost of the bought-back shares becomes a capital loss for set-off.
</details>

**Q5.** When buy-back proceeds are taxed as dividend, the cost of the bought-back shares is treated as:
A) An expense against the dividend  B) A capital loss eligible for set-off  C) Ignored entirely  D) A deduction under 80C
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) A capital loss eligible for set-off** — No expense deduction is allowed from the dividend, but the acquisition cost becomes a capital loss.
</details>

**Q6.** A loan by a closely held company to its shareholder out of accumulated profits is:
A) Tax-free  B) Business income  C) Deemed dividend under s.2(22)  D) Capital gain
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Deemed dividend** — Section 2(22)(e) treats such loans/advances as deemed dividend, taxed under IFOS.
</details>

**Q7.** "Interest on securities" under Section 2(28B) includes interest on:
A) Personal loans  B) Securities of the central/state government and company/local-authority debentures  C) Savings bank deposits only  D) Foreign equity dividends
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — s.2(28B) covers interest on government securities and on debentures/other money-securities issued by a local authority/company/statutory corporation.
</details>

**Q8.** Since "security" is not defined in the Income Tax Act, its meaning is taken from:
A) The Companies Act, 2013  B) Section 2(h) of the SCRA, 1956  C) FEMA  D) The RBI Act
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Section 2(h) of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956** — covering bonds, debentures, security receipts, government securities, pooled investment vehicles.
</details>

**Q9.** An assessee following the mercantile system is taxed on interest on securities on a:
A) Receipt basis  B) Accrual basis  C) Cash basis  D) Due-or-receipt, whichever earlier
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Accrual basis** — Under Section 145, mercantile → accrual; cash system → receipt basis.
</details>

**Q10.** Which is NOT a deduction allowed against gross interest on securities?
A) Collection charges  B) Interest on money borrowed to buy the securities  C) Securities Transaction Tax  D) Other revenue expenditure to earn the income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) STT** — Allowed deductions are collection charges, borrowing interest, and revenue expenditure wholly/exclusively to earn the income. STT is not a permissible deduction here.
</details>

**Q11.** Securities held as stock-in-trade are sold at a profit. This profit is:
A) Capital gains  B) Income from other sources  C) Business income (PGBP)  D) Exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Business income** — Stock-in-trade → PGBP. Investment securities → capital gains.
</details>

**Q12.** The gift threshold for movable property/securities (aggregate) in a year is:
A) ₹10,000  B) ₹25,000  C) ₹50,000  D) ₹1,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) ₹50,000** — If the aggregate FMV (or FMV minus consideration) exceeds ₹50,000, the whole amount is taxed.
</details>

**Q13.** You receive shares of FMV ₹80,000 without consideration from a friend. The taxable amount is:
A) ₹0  B) ₹30,000  C) ₹50,000  D) ₹80,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D) ₹80,000** — Since ₹80,000 > ₹50,000, the whole FMV is taxable (the ₹50,000 is a threshold, not a deduction). A friend is not a relative, so no exemption.
</details>

**Q14.** You receive shares of FMV ₹44,000 without consideration. The taxable amount is:
A) ₹44,000  B) ₹0  C) ₹6,000  D) ₹50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) ₹0** — ₹44,000 ≤ ₹50,000, so nothing is taxed.
</details>

**Q15.** A gift of shares received from which of the following is taxable (if > ₹50,000)?
A) Father  B) Brother's wife  C) Cousin  D) Mother's sister (Mausi)
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Cousin** — Cousins are NOT relatives. Father, brother's wife, and Mausi (mother's sister) are relatives, so their gifts are exempt.
</details>

**Q16.** Which event makes a gift exempt regardless of amount?
A) On the donee's birthday  B) On the occasion of the individual's marriage  C) On Diwali  D) On a work anniversary
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) On the occasion of the individual's marriage** — Also exempt: received under a will/inheritance, or in contemplation of death of the donor.
</details>

**Q17.** The fair market value of shares and securities for gift taxation is computed under:
A) Rule 8D  B) Rule 11UA  C) Rule 115A  D) Section 56  
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rule 11UA** — It prescribes different methods for quoted and unquoted shares/securities. (Rule 115A is for foreign-currency capital gains.)
</details>

**Q18.** In an HUF, for the gift provision, who is treated as a relative?
A) Only the Karta  B) No one  C) Every member of the HUF  D) Only blood relatives
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Every member of the HUF** — so gifts among members are not taxed.
</details>

**Q19.** TDS on income from units (e.g. mutual funds) is deducted under:
A) Section 192  B) Section 194  C) Section 194K  D) Section 195
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Section 194K** — for units. Section 194 covers dividends from companies.
</details>

**Q20.** Interest income earned by a non-resident may be taxed at:
A) Always 30%  B) Concessional rates in certain cases  C) Always exempt  D) 12.5%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Concessional rates in certain cases** — For non-residents, certain interest incomes are taxable at concessional rates per the special tax rates.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Mr. Bose — Income from Other Sources (FY 2024-25).** Mr. Bose, a resident individual in the 30% slab, had the following during the year:
1. **Dividend** of ₹2,00,000 from Indian listed companies.
2. **Buy-back** of his shares by an Indian company on 15 November 2024; he received **₹5,00,000**. He had originally bought those shares for ₹4,00,000.
3. **Interest on debentures** (held as investment) of ₹1,50,000; he paid ₹10,000 collection charges and ₹20,000 interest on a loan taken to buy them.
4. Received **shares worth FMV ₹90,000 free** from a **friend**.
5. Received **shares worth FMV ₹3,00,000** as a gift from his **father** on his birthday.

**Q1.** How is the ₹2,00,000 dividend taxed?
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

Taxable under **IFOS at his slab rate (30%)** — since DDT was abolished (AY 2021-22 onward), dividend is taxed in the shareholder's hands. Tax ≈ ₹2,00,000 × 30% = **₹60,000** (plus cess), before considering other income.
</details>

**Q2.** What is the tax treatment of the ₹5,00,000 buy-back proceeds (and the ₹4,00,000 cost)?
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

From 1 October 2024, buy-back proceeds are taxed as **dividend** under IFOS — so the **full ₹5,00,000** is taxable as dividend at his slab (no expense deduction). The **₹4,00,000 cost** of the shares is treated as a **capital loss**, available for set-off against capital gains per the applicable provisions.
</details>

**Q3.** Compute the taxable interest on debentures.
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

| Item | Amount (₹) |
|------|-----------:|
| Gross interest | 1,50,000 |
| Less: Collection charges | (10,000) |
| Less: Interest on loan to buy debentures | (20,000) |
| **Taxable interest** | **1,20,000** |

Both deductions are permissible; taxed at his slab rate.
</details>

**Q4.** Is the ₹90,000 of free shares from a friend taxable, and how much?
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

A friend is **not a relative**, and ₹90,000 > ₹50,000, so the **whole ₹90,000** is taxable under IFOS. (If it had been ≤ ₹50,000, nothing would be taxed.)
</details>

**Q5.** Is the ₹3,00,000 of shares gifted by his father taxable?
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**No.** A **father is a relative**, so a gift from him is exempt regardless of amount (the "birthday" occasion is irrelevant — the exemption is because the donor is a relative). The ₹3,00,000 is not taxed.
</details>
