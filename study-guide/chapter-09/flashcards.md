# Chapter 9: Income from Other Sources — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check.

**Q1.** What is "Income from Other Sources" (IFOS)?
> **A:** The **residual head** — any income that is not exempt and not chargeable under the other four heads (Salary, House Property, PGBP, Capital Gains). Some incomes are always taxed here.

**Q2.** Name five incomes computed under IFOS.
> **A:** Dividend income; winnings from lotteries/online games; interest on securities (if not business income); rental of machinery/plant/furniture; sum under a Keyman insurance policy; forfeited advance money on a failed transfer; deemed income under s.56(2). (Any five.)

**Q3.** Which incomes from securities are ALWAYS taxed under IFOS?
> **A:** (a) Dividend income; (b) interest income from securities held as investment; (c) forfeited advance money on a failed transfer of a capital asset; (d) deemed income under s.56(2); (e) specified sum received by a unit-holder from a business trust.

**Q4.** What is "dividend" and what is "deemed dividend"?
> **A:** Dividend = distribution of a company's profits to shareholders. Deemed dividend (s.2(22)) includes distributions on liquidation/capital reduction out of accumulated profits, loans/advances by a closely held company to its shareholder, and buy-back payments.

**Q5.** How is dividend income taxed after the abolition of DDT?
> **A:** From **AY 2021-22**, DDT was abolished, so dividend is taxable **in the hands of the shareholder/unit-holder** under IFOS, at the shareholder's **applicable slab rate** (with DTAA/MLI for non-residents).

**Q6.** How are share buy-back proceeds taxed from 1 October 2024?
> **A:** Buy-back proceeds received by a shareholder are taxed as **dividend** under IFOS, with **no deduction for expenses**. The cost of acquisition of the bought-back shares is treated as a **capital loss** eligible for set-off.

**Q7.** Under which sections is TDS deducted on dividend?
> **A:** **Section 194** (dividends) or **Section 194K** (income from units), as applicable.

**Q8.** Define "interest on securities" under Section 2(28B).
> **A:** (a) Interest on any security of the central/state government; and (b) interest on debentures/other securities for money issued by a local authority, company, or corporation established by a central/state/provincial Act.

**Q9.** Where do we find the meaning of "securities" for this chapter?
> **A:** In **Section 2(h) of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956** — bonds, debentures/debenture stock, security receipts, government securities, pooled investment vehicles.

**Q10.** On what basis is interest on securities charged to tax (Section 145)?
> **A:** On the assessee's method of accounting — **accrual** basis if mercantile system, **receipt** basis if cash system.

**Q11.** What deductions are allowed against gross interest on securities?
> **A:** Collection charges; interest on money borrowed to purchase the securities; and any other revenue expenditure laid out wholly and exclusively to earn that income.

**Q12.** Securities held as stock-in-trade vs investment — how is the sale profit taxed?
> **A:** Stock-in-trade → **business income (PGBP)**. Investment → **capital gains**.

**Q13.** When is a gift of securities NOT taxed because of the amount?
> **A:** When the **aggregate** of (FMV of properties received during the year − consideration paid) **does not exceed ₹50,000**.

**Q14.** If a gift of shares exceeds the ₹50,000 threshold, how much is taxed?
> **A:** The **whole amount** is taxed (not just the excess over ₹50,000). The ₹50,000 is a threshold, not a deduction.

**Q15.** Securities received without consideration: when and how much is taxed?
> **A:** If the aggregate **FMV** of securities received in the year **exceeds ₹50,000**, the **whole aggregate FMV** is taxable as IFOS.

**Q16.** Securities received for inadequate consideration: when and how much is taxed?
> **A:** If the aggregate **(FMV − consideration)** **exceeds ₹50,000**, the **whole difference** is taxable.

**Q17.** How is the fair market value of shares and securities computed?
> **A:** As per **Rule 11UA** of the Income Tax Rules, 1962, with different methods for quoted and unquoted shares/securities.

**Q18.** Name three "specified events" that make a gift exempt regardless of amount.
> **A:** Received (a) on the occasion of the individual's marriage; (b) under a will or by inheritance; (c) in contemplation of death of the payer/donor.

**Q19.** Gifts from which institutions are exempt?
> **A:** A local authority; any fund/foundation/university/educational institution/hospital/trust referred to in section 10(23C); any trust/institution registered under section 12A/12AA/12AB.

**Q20.** Are the following relatives (gift exempt): brother, mother's brother (Mama), and a cousin?
> **A:** Brother — **Yes** (relative). Mama (mother's brother) — **Yes** (relative). Cousin — **No** (not a relative, so a gift > ₹50,000 from a cousin is taxable).

**Q21.** Name three persons who are NOT treated as relatives for the gift provision.
> **A:** **Step-brother/sister, Nephew/Niece, and Cousins.**

**Q22.** In an HUF, who is treated as a relative for the gift provision?
> **A:** **Every member of the HUF** is treated as a relative.

**Q23.** Is a gift from your father-in-law taxable?
> **A:** **No.** Father-in-law (and mother-in-law, son-in-law, daughter-in-law, brother-in-law, sister-in-law) are relatives, so the gift is exempt.

**Q24.** How is foreign-currency interest on securities converted into INR?
> **A:** At the **SBI TT buying rate** on the **last day of the month immediately preceding** the month the income is due (or, if subject to TDS, on the date tax is required to be deducted).

**Q25.** Are concessional tax rates available on interest income for non-residents?
> **A:** Yes — in the case of non-residents, certain interest incomes are taxable at **concessional rates** (per Annexure 1 special tax rates).

**Q26.** A loan given by a closely held company to its shareholder out of accumulated profits is treated as what?
> **A:** **Deemed dividend** under Section 2(22)(e), taxable in the shareholder's hands under IFOS.

**Q27.** You receive free shares worth FMV ₹48,000 in a year. Taxable?
> **A:** **No** — ₹48,000 ≤ ₹50,000, so nothing is taxed.

**Q28.** You buy shares of FMV ₹3,00,000 from a friend for ₹2,20,000. What is taxed?
> **A:** Difference = ₹80,000 > ₹50,000, and a friend is not a relative → the **whole ₹80,000** is taxable as IFOS.
