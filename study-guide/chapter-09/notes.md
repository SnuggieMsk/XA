## 9.1 Introduction — the "catch-all" head
**Income from Other Sources** is the **residual head**: any income that is **not exempt** and is **not chargeable under the other four heads** (Salary, House Property, PGBP, Capital Gains) is taxed here. But some incomes are **always** taxed under this head.

**Analogy:** IFOS is the "miscellaneous drawer" of the income tax house — whatever doesn't fit in the other four labelled drawers, and isn't thrown away (exempt), lands here.

**Items computed under IFOS include:**
1. **Dividend income** (incl. buy-back proceeds received by a shareholder)
2. Winnings from lotteries, etc.
3. Winnings from online games (lottery-type)
4. **Interest on securities** (if not business income)
5. Rental income of machinery, plant or furniture (if not business income)
6. Composite rent from letting plant/machinery/furniture + building
7. Sum under a Keyman insurance policy
8. Interest on compensation / enhanced compensation
9. **Forfeited advance money** from a failed transfer of a capital asset
10. Deemed income (section 56(2))
11. Compensation on termination/modification of employment
12. Sum under a life insurance policy (other than ULIP/Keyman) exceeding aggregate premium paid
13. Specified sum received by a unit-holder from a business trust
14. Any other income not taxable under any other head

Then: **Less attributable expenses → Income from Other Sources.**

⚠️ Items 4, 5, 6 go to IFOS **only if not chargeable as business income**; item 7 (Keyman) goes to IFOS only if not business income **or salary**.

**Always taxed under IFOS (income from securities):** (a) dividend income, (b) interest income from securities held as investment, (c) forfeited advance money on a failed capital-asset transfer, (d) deemed income under s.56(2), (e) specified sum from a business trust.

## 9.2 Dividend Income

### Meaning of Dividend
A **dividend** is normally a company **distributing its profits** to shareholders. But Section 2(22) also treats certain receipts as **"deemed dividend"**:
| Clause | Deemed dividend |
|--------|-----------------|
| a | Distribution of accumulated profits releasing the company's assets |
| b | Distribution of debentures/deposit certificates from accumulated profits; bonus shares to **preference** shareholders out of accumulated profits |
| c | Distribution on **liquidation** out of accumulated profits |
| d | Distribution on **reduction of capital** out of accumulated profits |
| e | **Loan/advance by a closely held company** to its shareholder out of accumulated profits |
| f | **Payment on buy-back** of its own shares from a shareholder (per Companies Act, 2013) |

### Scheme of Taxation — the big shift
- **Up to AY 2020-21:** companies/mutual funds paid **Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT)**, so shareholders were largely exempt.
- **From AY 2021-22:** DDT was **abolished** (Finance Act, 2020). Now **dividend is taxable in the hands of the shareholder/unit-holder** under IFOS, at the **shareholder's applicable slab rate** (subject to residential status). For a non-resident, **DTAA and MLI** provisions also apply.

### Buy-back rule (from 1 October 2024)
**Buy-back proceeds received by a shareholder are taxed as dividend** under IFOS.
- **No deduction for expenses** is allowed from such dividend.
- The **cost of acquisition** of the bought-back shares is treated as a **capital loss** in the shareholder's hands, eligible for set-off as per applicable provisions.

💡 So a buy-back now has two tax effects: the full proceeds are taxed as dividend, while your original cost becomes a capital loss you can set off against capital gains.

### TDS on dividend
Tax is deducted under **Section 194** or **Section 194K** (units), as applicable.

## 9.3 Interest on Securities
Interest on securities is taxable under IFOS **if it is not business income**.

### Meaning of "interest on securities" — Section 2(28B)
a) Interest on any security of the **central or state government**; and
b) Interest on **debentures / other securities for money** issued by a local authority, company, or a corporation established by a central/state/provincial Act.

### Meaning of "securities"
"Security" is not defined in the Income Tax Act, so we use **Section 2(h) of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956**: bonds; debentures/debenture stock; security receipt; government securities; pooled investment vehicle.

### Basis of charge (Section 145)
Interest is computed by the assessee's **method of accounting**:
- **Mercantile system** → taxable on **accrual** basis.
- **Cash system** → taxable on **receipt** basis.

⚠️ Some interest income is **exempt** under Section 10 (see Annexure 2).

### Computation of taxable interest
```
Gross interest from securities
  Less: Collection charges
  Less: Interest on money borrowed to buy the securities
  Less: Any other revenue expenditure wholly & exclusively to earn that income
= Taxable income from securities
```

### Stock-in-trade vs investment
- Securities held as **stock-in-trade** → profit on sale = **business income** (PGBP).
- Securities held as **investment** → profit on sale = **capital gains**.

### Foreign-currency interest conversion
Income from securities earned in foreign currency is converted to INR at the **SBI TT buying rate** on the **last day of the month immediately preceding** the month the income is due. If the income is subject to **TDS**, conversion is done on the **date tax is required to be deducted**.

### Rate of tax
Interest is taxed at the **assessee's applicable rates**. For **non-residents**, certain interest incomes get **concessional rates** (see Annexure 1).

## 9.4 Gift of Securities

### The rule
If a person receives **movable property** (which includes **shares and securities**) **without consideration** or for **inadequate consideration**, the benefit is taxable in the recipient's hands as IFOS — **unless** the total benefit for the year is small.

⚠️ **₹50,000 threshold:** No tax if the **aggregate** difference between the fair market value of properties received during the year and the consideration paid **does not exceed ₹50,000**.

💡 Trap: The ₹50,000 is a **threshold, not an exemption slab**. If the aggregate FMV (or FMV–consideration) **exceeds** ₹50,000, the **whole amount** is taxed, not just the excess over ₹50,000.

### 9.4.1 Computation
- **Received without consideration:** if **aggregate FMV** of securities received in the year **exceeds ₹50,000**, the **whole aggregate FMV** is taxable.
- **Received for inadequate consideration:** if **aggregate (FMV − consideration)** **exceeds ₹50,000**, the **whole difference** is taxable.

**Worked example A (without consideration):** You receive shares worth FMV ₹70,000 free. Since ₹70,000 > ₹50,000, the **entire ₹70,000** is taxable as IFOS.

**Worked example B (just under threshold):** You receive shares of FMV ₹45,000 free. ₹45,000 ≤ ₹50,000 → **nothing** is taxed.

**Worked example C (inadequate consideration):** You buy shares of FMV ₹2,00,000 for ₹1,40,000. Difference = ₹60,000 > ₹50,000 → the **whole ₹60,000** is taxable.

### 9.4.2 Fair Market Value
FMV of shares and securities is computed per **Rule 11UA** of the Income Tax Rules, 1962, which prescribes different methods for **quoted** and **unquoted** shares/securities.

### 9.4.3 When the gift is NOT taxed

**Due to a specified event** — money/property received:
a) on the occasion of the **individual's marriage**;
b) under a **will or by inheritance**;
c) in **contemplation of death** of the payer/donor.

**Due to the status of the donor/payer** — received from:
a) a **specified relative**;
b) any **local authority**;
c) any fund/foundation/university/educational institution/hospital/trust referred to in **section 10(23C)**;
d) any trust/institution registered under **section 12A/12AA/12AB**;
e) a trust created solely for the benefit of a relative of the individual.

**Due to transactions not regarded as transfer** — e.g. distribution on total/partial partition of an HUF (Section 47(i)).

### Who is a "relative"?
For an HUF, **every member is a relative**. For an individual, relatives include (gifts from these are exempt):

| Relatives (gift exempt) |
|--------------------------|
| Husband / Wife |
| Son / Daughter (incl. step & adopted), and their spouses |
| Father / Mother / Step-father / Step-mother |
| Brother (and his wife) / Sister (and her husband); Half-brother/sister |
| Grandfather / Grandmother; Great Grandfather / Great Grandmother |
| Grandson (and wife) / Granddaughter (and husband); Great-grandchildren (and spouses) |
| Father-in-law / Mother-in-law; Son-in-law / Daughter-in-law |
| Brother-in-law (and his wife) / Sister-in-law (and her husband) |
| Father's Brother (Chacha/Tau) & wife; Father's Sister (Bua) & husband |
| Mother's Brother (Mama) & wife; Mother's Sister (Mausi) & husband |
| Spouse's Grandfather/Grandmother; Spouse's Great-Grandfather/Grandmother |

⚠️ **NOT relatives** (gifts from these ARE taxable if > ₹50,000): **Step-brother/sister, Nephew/Niece, Cousins.**

💡 Exam trap: a gift from your **cousin** or **nephew** is taxable; a gift from your **uncle (Mama/Chacha)** is exempt. Notice the asymmetry — uncles/aunts are relatives, but their children (your cousins) and your siblings' children (nephew/niece) are not.

## 📌 One-Page Recap
- **IFOS** is the residual head — income not exempt and not in the other four heads; some incomes are always here.
- **Dividend:** DDT abolished from AY 2021-22 → now taxed in the **shareholder's hands at slab rate** under IFOS. **Buy-back proceeds (from 1 Oct 2024)** taxed as dividend with **no expense deduction**; the cost becomes a **capital loss**. TDS under s.194/194K.
- **Deemed dividend (s.2(22)):** liquidation/capital-reduction distributions, loans by closely held companies to shareholders, buy-backs, etc.
- **Interest on securities (s.2(28B)):** govt securities + company/local-authority debentures. Taxed on accrual (mercantile) or receipt (cash) basis. Deduct collection charges, borrowing interest, related expenses. Concessional rates for some non-resident interest.
- **Gift of securities:** taxable as IFOS if aggregate FMV (or FMV−consideration) **exceeds ₹50,000** — then the **whole** amount is taxed. **Exempt** if from a relative, on marriage, under a will/inheritance, in contemplation of death, or from specified institutions. FMV per **Rule 11UA**.
