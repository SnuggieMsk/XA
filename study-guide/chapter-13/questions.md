# Chapter 13: Tax Provisions for Special Cases — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** The cost of acquisition of bonus shares (allotted after 01-04-2001) is:
A) The market price at allotment  B) Nil  C) The original share's cost  D) FMV on 31-01-2018
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Bonus shares carry nil cost. (If qualifying under 112A, deemed cost = lower of FMV-31Jan2018 and sale price.)
</details>

**Q2.** The holding period of bonus shares is counted from:
A) The original share's purchase date  B) The bonus allotment date  C) The record date  D) The financial year start
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — From the date the bonus shares are allotted.
</details>

**Q3.** 5,000 listed bonus shares (cost nil) held 11 months are sold @₹120, STT paid. Tax?
A) LTCG @12.50%  B) STCG @20% (Sec 111A)  C) Exempt  D) Slab rate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Held ≤12 months → STCG; STT paid → 20% under 111A. Gain = 5,000×120 = ₹6,00,000.
</details>

**Q4.** Splitting and consolidation of shares are:
A) Always taxed as capital gains  B) Not treated as transfer; taxed only on later sale  C) Taxed as dividend  D) Exempt forever
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Not within Section 2(47); cost is apportioned and holding period runs from the original date.
</details>

**Q5.** 2,000 shares costing ₹2,00,000 are consolidated 2:1. Cost per consolidated share?
A) ₹100  B) ₹200  C) ₹400  D) ₹50
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — ₹2,00,000 ÷ 1,000 = **₹200/share**.
</details>

**Q6.** From 01-10-2024, buyback proceeds in the shareholder's hands are taxed as:
A) Capital gains  B) Exempt  C) Dividend (Income from Other Sources)  D) Business income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Entire proceeds = dividend; no deduction. The cost becomes a capital loss.
</details>

**Q7.** Under the post-Oct-2024 buyback rule, the cost of the bought-back shares is treated as:
A) Deductible from the dividend  B) A capital loss (LT/ST per holding)  C) Ignored  D) Added to dividend
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Cost becomes a capital loss eligible for set-off/carry-forward; it is NOT netted against the dividend.
</details>

**Q8.** Until 30-09-2024, on a buyback the tax was paid by:
A) The shareholder at slab rates  B) The company at 20% (+ surcharge + cess); shareholder exempt under 10(34A)  C) Nobody  D) The exchange
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Company paid 20% + 12% surcharge + 4% cess; shareholder's receipt was exempt.
</details>

**Q9.** The cost of acquisition of a renounced rights entitlement is:
A) The market price  B) Nil  C) The subscription price  D) FMV on 31-01-2018
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Nil. The full sale consideration of the right is the gain.
</details>

**Q10.** Gain on renouncing a rights entitlement is taxed at:
A) 20% under 111A  B) 12.50% under 112A  C) Normal slab rates  D) Exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A right is not a listed equity share, so 111A doesn't apply; it's slab rate (usually short-term).
</details>

**Q11.** A shareholder exercises rights and gets shares @₹250; the cost of those shares is:
A) Nil  B) The market price  C) ₹250 (price paid to the company)  D) FMV at allotment
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Cost = price paid to the company (plus any amount a buyer paid the renouncer). Holding from allotment.
</details>

**Q12.** Allotment of shares in an amalgamation (held as capital asset) is:
A) Taxed at 12.50%  B) Not a transfer (Section 47(vii))  C) Taxed as dividend  D) Taxed at slab rates
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — No tax at allotment; tax arises on later sale with original cost and holding period carried over.
</details>

**Q13.** Mr. X bought A Ltd. shares @₹58 (Apr-2023); A merged into AB Ltd. (Aug-2024); he sold AB shares (Sep-2024, listed, STT). The holding period is reckoned from:
A) The merger date  B) The AB allotment date  C) The original A Ltd. purchase date  D) The financial year start
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — From the original acquisition (Apr-2023) → ~17 months → LTCG @12.50%.
</details>

**Q14.** Lending securities under SLB is:
A) A transfer attracting capital gains  B) Not a transfer (Section 47(xv))  C) Taxed as dividend  D) Speculative income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — No capital gain even if exact scrips aren't returned. The lending fee is PGBP/Other Sources.
</details>

**Q15.** A lender earns ₹2,00,000 SLB fee and pays ₹2,000 transaction charges. Taxable income?
A) ₹2,00,000  B) ₹1,98,000  C) ₹2,02,000  D) Nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — ₹2,00,000 − ₹2,000 = **₹1,98,000**, under PGBP or Other Sources.
</details>

**Q16.** Conversion of preference shares into equity of the same company is governed by:
A) Section 47(xb) — not a transfer  B) Section 111A  C) Section 112A  D) Section 50AA
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — 47(xb): no capital gain at conversion; on sale, the equity's cost and holding carry from the preference shares.
</details>

**Q17.** For segregated-portfolio units, the period of holding:
A) Starts fresh from segregation  B) Includes the original main-portfolio holding period  C) Is always short-term  D) Is irrelevant
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The original units' holding period in the main portfolio is included.
</details>

**Q18.** Units of cost ₹15,000; segregated NAV ₹2, total NAV ₹12. Segregated-portfolio cost?
A) ₹2,500  B) ₹12,500  C) ₹15,000  D) ₹3,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — 15,000 × (2 ÷ 12) = **₹2,500**; the main portfolio's cost becomes ₹12,500.
</details>

**Q19.** Consolidation of two equity-oriented mutual fund schemes is:
A) A transfer taxed at 12.50%  B) Not a transfer; cost and holding carry over  C) Taxed as dividend  D) Exempt only for residents
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — No tax on the swap; cost = cost in the consolidating scheme, holding period included.
</details>

**Q20.** A short-term capital loss can be set off against:
A) Only short-term capital gains  B) Long-term capital gains only  C) Both short-term and long-term capital gains  D) Any income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — STCL sets off against both STCG and LTCG. (LTCL only against LTCG.) This is from the Module-end question set.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Mr. Khanna, a resident individual (sales all after 23-07-2024, shares listed with STT), reports for FY 2024-25:
1. **Original holding:** 12,000 shares of Tech Ltd. @₹200 (bought 01-05-2021). On 01-07-2023 he received a **1:3 bonus** (4,000 bonus shares). On 10-09-2024 he sold **all 4,000 bonus shares** @₹260.
2. **Buyback:** Tech Ltd. **bought back 1,000 of his original shares @₹300 on 15-01-2025**.
3. **Rights:** Star Ltd. made a 1:2 rights issue @₹150 (offer 01-08-2024). Mr. Khanna **renounced** his right to 500 shares to a friend for ₹40/share on 20-08-2024.
4. **Amalgamation:** He held 5,000 shares of Old Ltd. @₹80 (bought 01-02-2022); Old Ltd. merged into New Ltd. (01-06-2024) and he received 5,000 New Ltd. shares, which he sold @₹130 on 01-09-2024.

**Q1.** How are the 4,000 bonus shares taxed on sale?
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- Bonus shares: **cost nil**, holding from allotment **01-07-2023** to sale **10-09-2024** ≈ 14 months (>12) → **LTCG**.
- LTCG = (260 − 0) × 4,000 = **₹10,40,000**.
- Sec 112A: first ₹1,25,000 exempt, balance ₹9,15,000 @12.50% = **₹1,14,375** (+ surcharge/cess).
</details>

**Q2.** How is the buyback of 1,000 original shares taxed (buyback on 15-01-2025)?
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- Buyback after **01-10-2024** → new rule.
- **Shareholder:** entire ₹3,00,000 (1,000 × ₹300) taxed as **dividend** (Income from Other Sources), no deduction.
- The **cost ₹2,00,000** (1,000 × ₹200) becomes a **long-term capital loss** (original shares held since 01-05-2021 > 24 months... here listed > 12 months → long-term), available for set-off against capital gains / carry-forward.
- **Company:** no tax.
</details>

**Q3.** How is the renouncement of the Star Ltd. rights taxed?
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- A rights entitlement is a capital asset with **cost nil**.
- Held from offer (01-08-2024) to renouncement (20-08-2024) ≈ short period → **short-term**.
- Gain = 500 × ₹40 = **₹20,000**, taxed at **normal slab rates** (NOT 111A — a right is not a listed equity share).
</details>

**Q4.** How is the sale of New Ltd. (amalgamated) shares taxed?
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- Allotment of New Ltd. shares = **not a transfer (Sec 47(vii))**; cost = cost of Old Ltd. shares = 5,000 × ₹80 = ₹4,00,000; holding from **01-02-2022**.
- Sold 01-09-2024 → held ~2 yr 7 mth (>12 months, listed) → **LTCG**.
- Sale = 5,000 × ₹130 = ₹6,50,000; LTCG = 6,50,000 − 4,00,000 = **₹2,50,000 @12.50%** over ₹1.25 lakh.
- STT-on-acquisition condition is waived since shares were acquired via a Section 47 mode.
</details>

**Q5.** Can the long-term capital loss from the buyback be used against his LTCGs this year?
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- Yes. The buyback creates a **₹2,00,000 long-term capital loss**.
- A **long-term capital loss can be set off only against long-term capital gains**.
- His LTCGs this year: bonus shares ₹10,40,000 + New Ltd. ₹2,50,000. The ₹2,00,000 LTCL can be set off against these LTCGs (reducing the 12.5% taxable base), with any unabsorbed amount carried forward up to 8 assessment years (return filed on time).

⚠️ Note: the renouncement STCG (₹20,000) is taxed at slab rates and is separate from the 12.5% LTCG pool.
</details>
