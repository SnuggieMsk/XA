## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** What are bonus shares?
A) Shares issued to the public at a discount  B) Additional shares issued free to existing shareholders in proportion to shares already held  C) Shares bought back by the company  D) Shares issued to employees as salary
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Bonus shares are additional shares a company issues to its existing shareholders, free of cost, based on the shares they already own; the aim is to reward shareholders and widen the equity base.
- A) is wrong: issuing to the public at a discount is a public/discounted issue, not a bonus.
- C) is wrong: that describes a buyback, the opposite action.
- D) is wrong: shares given as salary are ESOPs/sweat equity, not bonus shares.
</details>

**Q2.** From which date is the period of holding of bonus shares reckoned?
A) Date of purchase of the original shares  B) Date of the company's announcement  C) Date of allotment of the bonus shares  D) Date of sale
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — For bonus shares, the holding period starts on the **date of allotment of the bonus shares**, not the date the original shares were bought. This is why bonus shares are often a short-term capital gains trap.
- A) is wrong: that rule applies to splits, consolidation, mergers and pref-to-equity conversions, not bonus shares.
- B) is wrong: the announcement date is not the allotment date.
- D) is wrong: the sale date ends the holding period, it does not start it.
</details>

**Q3.** What is the cost of acquisition of bonus shares allotted free of cost (after 01-04-2001)?
A) Face value  B) Market value on allotment date  C) Nil  D) Issue price of original shares
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Since the shareholder pays nothing for bonus shares, their cost of acquisition is **nil**. The entire sale price later becomes the capital gain.
- A), B), D) are wrong: no actual amount is paid, so none of these values is used (the only exception is bonus shares issued before 01-04-2001, where FMV on 01-04-2001 may be taken at the assessee's option).
</details>

**Q4.** A listed bonus share sold within how many months of allotment is a short-term capital asset?
A) 6 months  B) 36 months  C) 24 months  D) 12 months
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — For listed shares, the threshold between short-term and long-term is **12 months**. Held for not more than 12 months = short-term; more than 12 months = long-term.
- A) and B) are not the prescribed thresholds.
- C) 24 months applies only to **unlisted** shares.
</details>

**Q5.** Under which section are short-term capital gains on listed equity shares (with STT paid) taxed?
A) Section 111A  B) Section 112A  C) Section 115BBH  D) Section 10(34A)
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Section **111A** governs STCG on equity shares / equity-oriented MF units / business trust units where STT is paid; the rate is 20%.
- B) Section 112A is for long-term gains, not short-term.
- C) Section 115BBH taxes Virtual Digital Assets.
- D) Section 10(34A) was the old buyback exemption for shareholders.
</details>

**Q6.** What is the tax rate under Section 111A for STCG on equity shares (STT paid)?
A) 10%  B) 12.50%  C) 15%  D) 20%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Section 111A short-term capital gains are taxed at a flat **20%**.
- A), B), C) are wrong: 12.50% is the LTCG rate under 112/112A; 10% and 15% are surcharge slab figures, not the STCG rate.
</details>

**Q7.** Long-term capital gains under Section 112A are taxed at 12.50% only on the amount exceeding what annual threshold?
A) Rs. 1,00,000  B) Rs. 1,25,000  C) Rs. 2,50,000  D) Rs. 5,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 112A exempts the first **Rs. 1,25,000** of LTCG each year; only the excess is taxed at 12.50%.
- A) Rs. 1,00,000 was the older exemption limit, now revised.
- C) and D) are not the 112A threshold.
</details>

**Q8.** If bonus shares are held as stock-in-trade, gains on their sale are taxable under which head?
A) Capital gains  B) Income from other sources  C) Profits and gains from business or profession (PGBP)  D) Salary
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Stock-in-trade shares are trading assets, so their sale gain is **business income (PGBP)**, not capital gains.
- A) applies only when shares are held as capital assets (investments).
- B) and D) are unrelated heads.
</details>

**Q9.** Is a stock split treated as a "transfer" under the Income Tax Act?
A) Yes, always  B) No  C) Only if listed  D) Only if the investor sells later
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Splitting (or consolidating) shares is **not** a transfer under Section 2(47), so no tax arises at the time of the split; tax arises only on later sale.
- A) is wrong: it is specifically not a transfer.
- C) and D) misstate the rule — listing is irrelevant, and the split itself is never taxed.
</details>

**Q10.** After a stock split, how is the cost of acquisition of the new shares determined?
A) Set to nil  B) Original cost apportioned proportionately over the new (split) shares  C) Face value of the new shares  D) Market value on split date
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The original amount paid is **divided proportionately** across the larger number of post-split shares.
- A) Nil applies to bonus shares and renounced rights, not splits.
- C) and D) are not used for split shares.
</details>

**Q11.** From which date is the holding period of split/consolidated shares counted?
A) Date of the split/consolidation  B) Date of allotment of new shares  C) Date of acquisition of the original shares  D) Date of sale
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Because the cost is derived from the original shares, the holding period is also counted from the **original acquisition date**.
- A) and B) are wrong: the split/consolidation does not reset the clock.
- D) ends, not starts, the holding period.
</details>

**Q12.** What is consolidation of shares?
A) Dividing shares into smaller units  B) Merging shares to reduce their number without reducing share capital  C) Buying back shares  D) Issuing free shares
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Consolidation merges several shares into fewer, reducing the share count and raising the price per share, while total capital stays the same.
- A) describes a split (the opposite).
- C) is a buyback.
- D) is a bonus issue.
</details>

**Q13.** From 01-10-2024, how are buyback proceeds taxed in the shareholder's hands?
A) Exempt under Section 10(34A)  B) As long-term capital gains  C) As dividend under "Income from Other Sources"  D) As business income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — With effect from 01-10-2024, buyback proceeds are taxed as **dividend** under Income from Other Sources, with no deduction for cost or expenses.
- A) is the pre-Oct-2024 position, now withdrawn.
- B) and D) are wrong heads for buyback proceeds under the new rule.
</details>

**Q14.** Under the post-01-10-2024 buyback rules, what happens to the cost of acquisition of the shares bought back?
A) It is deducted from the buyback proceeds  B) It is ignored entirely  C) It is treated as a capital loss available for set-off  D) It is added to income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The cost of acquisition of the bought-back shares becomes a **capital loss** (long-term or short-term per holding period), which can be set off / carried forward.
- A) is wrong: no deduction is allowed against the dividend; the cost is handled separately as a loss.
- B) is wrong: it is not ignored, it becomes a loss.
- D) is wrong: cost reduces, not increases, taxable income.
</details>

**Q15.** Until 30-09-2024, at what rate did a domestic company pay tax on buyback distributed income (before surcharge and cess)?
A) 10%  B) 15%  C) 20%  D) 30%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Until 01-10-2024 the company paid **20%** (plus 12% surcharge and 4% cess) on the distributed income, while the shareholder's receipt was exempt under Section 10(34A).
- A), B), D) are not the prescribed buyback distribution tax rate.
</details>

**Q16.** What is a rights issue?
A) Free shares to existing shareholders  B) Splitting one share into many  C) Company buying its own shares  D) The right offered to existing shareholders to subscribe to new shares in proportion to their holdings
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — A rights issue gives **existing shareholders the right to buy newly issued shares** in proportion to their current holdings, usually at a price below market.
- A) is a bonus issue.
- B) is a split.
- C) is buyback.
</details>

**Q17.** What is the cost of acquisition of a right that is renounced in favour of another person?
A) Market value  B) Issue price  C) Nil  D) Face value
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The cost of acquisition of the renounced right is **nil**, so the entire fee received is the capital gain.
- A), B), D) are not used; nothing was paid for the right itself.
</details>

**Q18.** Gains from renouncing a right are generally of what nature?
A) Long-term capital gains  B) Short-term capital gains  C) Business income always  D) Exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Rights are held only briefly (offer date to renouncement), so the gain is almost always **short-term capital gain**, taxed at the assessee's slab rate (not 111A, since a right is not a listed equity share).
- A) is wrong: the holding period is too short to be long-term.
- C) is wrong: it is business income only if shares were held as stock-in-trade.
- D) is wrong: such gains are taxable.
</details>

**Q19.** Are gains on renouncement of a listed right covered by Section 111A's 20% rate?
A) Yes  B) No  C) Only if STT is paid  D) Only for unlisted rights
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Even when the right is listed, **a right is not a listed equity share**, so Section 111A does not apply; the gain is taxed at normal slab rates.
- A), C), D) misstate the rule — 111A simply does not cover renouncement of rights.
</details>

**Q20.** When a shareholder exercises a rights issue and buys the shares, what is the cost of acquisition of those shares?
A) Nil  B) The price paid to the company for the shares  C) Market value at allotment  D) Face value
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — When the right is exercised, the cost is the **price actually paid to the company** for the right shares (plus, for a buyer of someone else's right, the amount paid to the renouncer).
- A) Nil applies to a renounced right, not to exercised shares.
- C) and D) are not the cost basis for exercised right shares.
</details>

**Q21.** For amalgamation, at least what percentage in value of the amalgamating company's shareholders must become shareholders of the amalgamated company?
A) 50%  B) 51%  C) 75%  D) 90%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — One condition of amalgamation under the Income Tax Act is that shareholders holding **not less than 75%** in value of the amalgamating company become shareholders of the amalgamated company.
- A), B), D) are not the prescribed threshold.
</details>

**Q22.** Under which section is the allotment of amalgamated-company shares (held as capital asset) NOT treated as a transfer?
A) Section 47(vii)  B) Section 47(xb)  C) Section 47(xv)  D) Section 111A
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Section **47(vii)** excludes the allotment of shares in the amalgamated company (in lieu of amalgamating-company shares held as capital asset) from the definition of transfer.
- B) Section 47(xb) covers preference-to-equity conversion.
- C) Section 47(xv) covers stock lending.
- D) Section 111A is a tax rate provision, not an exemption.
</details>

**Q23.** On later sale of amalgamated-company shares, the cost of acquisition is:
A) Nil  B) Market value at amalgamation  C) Face value of new shares  D) The amount paid for the original shares of the amalgamating company
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — The cost carries over: it is the **amount paid for the original shares** in the amalgamating company, and the holding period also runs from that original acquisition date.
- A), B), C) do not reflect the carry-over rule.
</details>

**Q24.** What does SLB stand for?
A) Stock Lending and Borrowing  B) Securities Loan Bond  C) Short Lending Buyback  D) Secured Loan Brokerage
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — SLB = **Stock Lending and Borrowing**, a system where a lender lends securities to a borrower via an authorised intermediary for a fee.
- B), C), D) are invented expansions.
</details>

**Q25.** Who acts as the Authorised Intermediary for SLB in India?
A) RBI  B) SEBI directly  C) Clearing Corporation of NSE and BSE  D) Individual brokers
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The **Clearing Corporations of NSE and BSE** are approved as the Authorised Intermediary for SLB.
- A) RBI regulates banking, not SLB.
- B) SEBI sets the framework but does not act as the intermediary itself.
- D) Individual brokers are not the AI.
</details>

**Q26.** Which securities are eligible for lending and borrowing under SLB?
A) All listed shares  B) Only government bonds  C) Securities traded in the F&O segment and liquid index ETFs  D) Only unlisted shares
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Only securities **traded in the F&O segment and liquid index ETFs** are eligible for SLB.
- A), B), D) are wrong: the eligibility is restricted to F&O securities and liquid index ETFs.
</details>

**Q27.** Under Section 47(xv), the lending of securities under SLB:
A) Is treated as a transfer  B) Is not treated as a transfer  C) Is taxed as capital gains  D) Is exempt only for institutions
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Lending securities under SLB is **not a transfer** (Section 47(xv)), even if the identical scrips are not returned, so no capital gain arises for the lender.
- A) and C) are the opposite of the rule.
- D) is wrong: the relief applies to all market participants, retail or institutional.
</details>

**Q28.** Under which head is the lending fee earned by a lender in SLB taxable?
A) Capital gains  B) PGBP or Income from Other Sources  C) Salary  D) Exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The lending fee is taxable under **PGBP** (if the lender is in that business) or otherwise under **Income from Other Sources**; related expenses are deductible.
- A) is wrong: lending is not a transfer, so no capital gains.
- C) and D) are incorrect heads/treatment.
</details>

**Q29.** What is the maximum total duration of an SLB contract including rollovers?
A) 1 month  B) 6 months  C) 12 months  D) No limit
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — SLB contracts can run from 1 day to 12 months; with rollovers the total duration must not exceed **12 months** from the original contract date.
- A) is the usual term but not the maximum.
- B) and D) are incorrect.
</details>

**Q30.** Under Section 47(xb), conversion of preference shares into equity shares of the same company is:
A) A taxable transfer  B) Not a transfer  C) Taxed as dividend  D) Taxed as business income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section **47(xb)** provides that converting preference shares into equity shares of the same company is **not a transfer**, so no capital gain arises at conversion.
- A) is the opposite.
- C) and D) are wrong heads.
</details>

**Q31.** On later sale of equity shares obtained by converting preference shares, the holding period is counted from:
A) The conversion date  B) The date the preference shares were acquired  C) The date of sale  D) 01-04-2001
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The holding period (and the cost) carry over, so it runs from the **date the preference shares were originally acquired**.
- A) is wrong: conversion does not reset the clock.
- C) ends the holding period.
- D) is unrelated here.
</details>

**Q32.** When a mutual fund creates a segregated portfolio (side pocket), the unit-holder then holds units in:
A) Only the segregated scheme  B) Only the main scheme  C) Two schemes — the main and the segregated portfolio  D) A new merged scheme
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — On segregation the unit-holder holds the **same number of units in two schemes** — the main portfolio and the segregated portfolio.
- A) and B) are wrong: both schemes exist.
- D) describes consolidation, not segregation.
</details>

**Q33.** For segregated-portfolio units, the period of holding:
A) Starts fresh from the segregation date  B) Includes the period the original units were held in the main portfolio  C) Is always treated as short-term  D) Is irrelevant
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The holding period of segregated units **includes the time the original units were held** in the main portfolio.
- A) is wrong: it does not start fresh.
- C) and D) misstate the rule.
</details>

**Q34.** Consolidation of two equity-oriented mutual fund schemes into one is:
A) A taxable transfer  B) Not treated as a transfer  C) Taxed as dividend  D) Allowed only between equity and debt schemes
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Consolidating two or more schemes of an equity-oriented fund (or two or more non-equity schemes) is **not treated as a transfer**, so no tax arises on the swap of units.
- A) and C) are wrong.
- D) is wrong: both schemes must be the same type (both equity-oriented, or both non-equity), not one of each.
</details>

**Q35.** When a mutual fund scheme is wound up and the balance is paid to unit-holders, the amount received is:
A) Fully exempt  B) Treated like a normal redemption (capital gains as usual)  C) Taxed as dividend  D) Taxed as business income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — On winding up, the amount received by unit-holders is **treated like a normal redemption**, with capital gains computed as usual.
- A) is wrong: it is not exempt.
- C) and D) are incorrect heads.
</details>

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** Mr. A holds bonus shares allotted on 01-09-2023 (listed) and sells them on 01-08-2024. The bonus shares are:
A) Long-term, since original shares were held long  B) Long-term, because the company is listed  C) Exempt  D) Short-term, because held about 11 months from allotment
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — The bonus clock runs from allotment (01-09-2023). From 01-09-2023 to 01-08-2024 is about 11 months, which is not more than 12 months, so they are **short-term**.
- A) is wrong: the original shares' holding does not transfer to bonus shares.
- B) is wrong: listing alone does not make a gain long-term; holding period decides.
- C) is wrong: they are taxable.
</details>

**Q37.** A bonus share (cost nil) is sold for a gain after being held 14 months (listed, STT paid). The gain is taxed under:
A) Section 111A at 20%  B) Section 115BBH at 30%  C) Slab rates  D) Section 112A at 12.50% over Rs. 1,25,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Held more than 12 months (listed) = long-term; with STT paid, equity LTCG falls under **Section 112A at 12.50% on the amount over Rs. 1,25,000**.
- A) 111A is for short-term gains.
- B) 115BBH is for Virtual Digital Assets.
- C) slab rates do not apply to equity LTCG with STT.
</details>

**Q38.** Mr. A acquired 2,000 shares at Rs. 100 each, then the company consolidated 2 shares into 1. After consolidation he holds 1,000 shares. Cost per consolidated share is:
A) Rs. 200  B) Rs. 100  C) Rs. 50  D) Nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Formula: cost per new share = total original cost ÷ number of shares after consolidation. Total cost = 2,000 × Rs. 100 = Rs. 2,00,000; after 2:1 consolidation he has 1,000 shares; Rs. 2,00,000 ÷ 1,000 = **Rs. 200**. Intuition: half the shares, so each carries double the cost.
- B) Rs. 100 ignores the consolidation.
- C) Rs. 50 would be a split, not consolidation.
- D) Nil is for bonus shares.
</details>

**Q39.** Mr. A bought 1,000 shares at Rs. 150 each; the company split 1 share into 2. After the split he holds 2,000 shares. Cost per split share is:
A) Rs. 150  B) Rs. 300  C) Rs. 75  D) Rs. 50
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Cost per new share = total cost ÷ shares after split. Total = 1,000 × Rs. 150 = Rs. 1,50,000; after 1:2 split he has 2,000 shares; Rs. 1,50,000 ÷ 2,000 = **Rs. 75**. Intuition: twice the shares, so each carries half the cost.
- A) ignores the split.
- B) doubles instead of halving.
- D) Rs. 50 is the new face value, not the cost.
</details>

**Q40.** Post-01-10-2024, a company buys back shares from Mr. X for Rs. 62,500 (original cost Rs. 50,000, held over 2 years). How is this treated?
A) Rs. 12,500 capital gain  B) Rs. 62,500 dividend + Rs. 50,000 long-term capital loss  C) Rs. 62,500 exempt  D) Rs. 62,500 business income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Under the new rule the full Rs. 62,500 is taxed as **dividend** (no cost deduction), and the Rs. 50,000 cost becomes a **long-term capital loss** (held over 2 years) eligible for set-off.
- A) is the old "net the gain" approach, no longer applicable.
- C) the old exemption was withdrawn from 01-10-2024.
- D) dividend, not business income.
</details>

**Q41.** In a post-Oct-2024 buyback, the company that buys back the shares:
A) Pays 20% distribution tax  B) Pays no tax  C) Pays 30% tax  D) Pays dividend distribution tax
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — For buybacks on or after 01-10-2024, the **company has no tax liability**; the tax shifts to the shareholder (as dividend).
- A) is the pre-Oct-2024 company position.
- C) and D) are not applicable to buybacks now.
</details>

**Q42.** Mr. Paul renounces his right to buy 500 shares to Mr. X for Rs. 200 per share. His capital gain is:
A) Rs. 1,00,000 short-term, taxed at slab rate  B) Rs. 1,00,000 long-term at 12.50%  C) Rs. 1,00,000 at 20% under 111A  D) Nil, exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Gain = consideration − cost = (500 × Rs. 200) − nil = **Rs. 1,00,000**, short-term, taxed at Mr. Paul's slab rate (a right is not a listed equity share, so 111A does not apply).
- B) is wrong: the right is short-term, not long-term.
- C) is wrong: 111A's 20% does not cover rights.
- D) is wrong: the gain is taxable.
</details>

**Q43.** A buyer purchases right shares by paying Rs. 200/share to the renouncer and Rs. 250/share to the company. His cost of acquisition per share is:
A) Rs. 200  B) Rs. 250  C) Rs. 450  D) Nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — For a buyer who acquires rights entitlement from another shareholder, cost = amount paid to renouncer + amount paid to the company = Rs. 200 + Rs. 250 = **Rs. 450**.
- A) and B) capture only one leg.
- D) Nil is for the original shareholder's renounced right, not for an exercising buyer.
</details>

**Q44.** Mr. X bought A Ltd. shares on 01-04-2023; A Ltd. amalgamated into AB Ltd. on 01-08-2024 and he sold AB Ltd. shares on 01-09-2024. The holding period is reckoned from:
A) 01-08-2024  B) 01-09-2024  C) 01-04-2023  D) The amalgamation announcement date
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — For amalgamation, the holding period of the new shares runs from the **original acquisition date** of the amalgamating-company shares (01-04-2023), making the holding about 17 months (long-term).
- A) and D) are wrong: amalgamation does not reset the clock.
- B) is the sale date.
</details>

**Q45.** A lender earns Rs. 2,00,000 lending fee under SLB and pays Rs. 2,000 transaction charges. Taxable income from lending is:
A) Rs. 2,00,000  B) Rs. 1,98,000  C) Rs. 2,02,000  D) Nil (not a transfer)
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The fee is taxable (PGBP or Other Sources) and related expenses are deductible: Rs. 2,00,000 − Rs. 2,000 = **Rs. 1,98,000**.
- A) ignores the deductible expense.
- C) adds instead of subtracting expenses.
- D) is wrong: although lending is not a transfer (no capital gain), the fee itself is taxable income.
</details>

**Q46.** A borrower in SLB sells the borrowed shares. Any gain or loss on that sale is taxable under:
A) Income from other sources only  B) Always exempt  C) Salary  D) Capital gains or PGBP, as the case may be
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — The borrower buys/sells the stock, so gains or losses fall under **capital gains or PGBP**, depending on whether held as investment or stock-in-trade; the lending fee paid is deductible.
- A) is incomplete.
- B) is wrong: borrower transactions are taxable.
- C) is an unrelated head.
</details>

**Q47.** Mr. X converts 20,000 preference shares (cost Rs. 10 each) into 10,000 equity shares (2:1). On later sale, his cost of acquisition of the equity shares is:
A) Rs. 2,00,000  B) Rs. 1,00,000  C) Nil  D) FMV at conversion
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Cost carries over from the preference shares: 20,000 × Rs. 10 = **Rs. 2,00,000**. The FMV at conversion is ignored because conversion is not a transfer (47(xb)).
- B) wrongly divides by the equity count.
- C) Nil is for bonus shares.
- D) FMV is not used under the carry-over rule.
</details>

**Q48.** A mutual fund segregates its portfolio. The cost of acquisition of the segregated units is computed as: total cost ×
A) (NAV of total portfolio ÷ NAV of segregated asset)  B) (NAV of asset transferred to segregated portfolio ÷ NAV of total portfolio before segregation)  C) (Segregated units ÷ total units)  D) Nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Segregated cost = total portfolio cost × (NAV of asset transferred to segregated portfolio ÷ NAV of total portfolio immediately before segregation). It splits the cost in proportion to value moved to the side pocket.
- A) inverts the ratio.
- C) uses unit counts instead of NAV.
- D) is wrong: a cost is assigned, not nil.
</details>

**Q49.** After segregation, the cost assigned to the segregated units is:

**Correct: B)** — 5% on Rs. 4,00,000 (Rs. 3L–7L band) = Rs. 20,000; plus 10% on Rs. 1,00,000 (Rs. 7L–8L) = Rs. 10,000; total = **Rs. 30,000**.

- C) over-taxes.

**Q95.** Mr. P renounced a right for Rs. 80,000 (cost nil) and separately had a Rs. 50,000 long-term capital loss from a buyback. Assuming set-off is permitted, his net taxable position from these two items is:
A) Rs. 80,000 STCG and a separate Rs. 50,000 LTCL (different categories)  B) Rs. 30,000 net gain by simply subtracting  C) Nil  D) Rs. 1,30,000 gain
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The renouncement produces a **Rs. 80,000 short-term capital gain**; the buyback cost is a **long-term capital loss of Rs. 50,000**. A long-term capital loss can only be set off against long-term capital gains, so it cannot reduce this STCG; they remain in separate categories.
- B) wrongly nets across loss categories.
- C) and D) miscompute.
</details>

**Q96.** A fund consolidates two equity-oriented schemes. Mr. K held units in the consolidating scheme since 01-03-2021 at a cost of Rs. 40,000; he sells the consolidated units on 01-09-2024 for Rs. 70,000. The holding period and cost used are:

**Correct: A)** — Consolidation is not a transfer; holding period includes the time in the consolidating scheme (**from 01-03-2021**) and cost carries over (**Rs. 40,000**), giving a long-term gain of Rs. 30,000.

- D) is nonsensical (cost cannot equal sale value here).
</details>

**Q98.** Mr. Q bought 4,000 listed shares at Rs. 250; a 1:4 bonus gave 1,000 bonus shares. Two years later he sells only the 1,000 bonus shares at Rs. 300 (STT). Treating them as long-term, the LTCG is:

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Mr. Arjun, a resident individual (old tax regime, not a senior citizen), has the following transactions during FY 2024-25, all in listed securities with STT paid where relevant:

1. He bought **10,000 shares of Sunrise Ltd. at Rs. 105** on 01-04-2020. Sunrise declared a **1:2 bonus** (one bonus share for every two held), allotted **01-09-2023**. On **01-08-2024** he sold **all 15,000 shares at Rs. 120 each**.
2. He held **2,000 shares of Delta Ltd. bought at Rs. 100 each**; Delta **consolidated 2 shares into 1** (face value Rs. 100 → Rs. 200). He later sold all consolidated shares at **Rs. 260 each**.
3. He owned **20,000 preference shares of Orbit Ltd. (cost Rs. 10 each)** acquired 01-01-2010, **converted 2:1 into 10,000 equity shares** on 01-01-2023 (FMV Rs. 25), and sold them at **Rs. 35** on 25-08-2024.
4. Orbit Ltd. also **bought back 500 of his other shares at Rs. 125** (cost Rs. 100, held over 2 years) on 20-02-2025 (post-01-10-2024 rule).
5. He **lent F&O-eligible shares** through SLB and earned a **lending fee of Rs. 2,00,000**, incurring **Rs. 2,000** transaction charges.

---

<details><summary>Q-CS-1: Compute the capital gains from the Sunrise Ltd. shares (original + bonus) and their nature.</summary>

**Answer:**
- **Original 10,000 shares:** bought 01-04-2020, sold 01-08-2024 → held >12 months → **long-term**. LTCG = (Rs. 120 − Rs. 105) × 10,000 = **Rs. 1,50,000** (Section 112A, 12.50% over Rs. 1,25,000).
- **Bonus 5,000 shares:** cost **nil**, allotted 01-09-2023, sold 01-08-2024 → about 11 months ≤ 12 → **short-term**. STCG = (Rs. 120 × 5,000) − 0 = **Rs. 6,00,000** (Section 111A, 20%).

**Intuition:** The bonus shares have their own holding clock (from allotment) and zero cost — a classic STCG trap. Total from Sunrise = Rs. 1,50,000 LTCG + Rs. 6,00,000 STCG.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS-2: Compute the capital gain on the Delta Ltd. consolidated shares.</summary>

**Answer:**
- Total cost = 2,000 × Rs. 100 = Rs. 2,00,000. After 2:1 consolidation he holds **1,000 shares**; cost per share = Rs. 2,00,000 ÷ 1,000 = **Rs. 200**.
- Gain = (Rs. 260 − Rs. 200) × 1,000 = **Rs. 60,000**.
- Consolidation is **not a transfer**, so no tax occurred at consolidation; the holding period runs from the original purchase date.

**Formula used:** cost per consolidated share = total original cost ÷ number of shares after consolidation. The total money paid does not change — it is just spread over fewer shares.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS-3: Compute the tax effect of the Orbit Ltd. preference-to-equity conversion and subsequent sale.</summary>

**Answer:**
- **At conversion (01-01-2023):** under Section 47(xb), conversion of preference shares into equity of the same company is **not a transfer** → **no capital gain**. The FMV of Rs. 25 is ignored.
- **At sale (25-08-2024):** holding period carries over from 01-01-2010 → over 14 years → **long-term**. Sale = 10,000 × Rs. 35 = Rs. 3,50,000; cost carries over = 20,000 × Rs. 10 = Rs. 2,00,000; **LTCG = Rs. 1,50,000** (Section 112A, 12.50% over Rs. 1,25,000).

**Intuition:** Conversion is tax-deferred — both the cost and the clock carry over to the new equity shares.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS-4: Explain the tax treatment of the Orbit Ltd. buyback (post-01-10-2024).</summary>

**Answer:**
- **Dividend:** the full proceeds = 500 × Rs. 125 = **Rs. 62,500** are taxed as **dividend** under Income from Other Sources, with **no deduction** for cost or expenses.
- **Capital loss:** the cost = 500 × Rs. 100 = **Rs. 50,000** becomes a **long-term capital loss** (held over 2 years), available for set-off/carry-forward against long-term capital gains.
- **Company:** no tax (buyback after 01-10-2024).

**Intuition:** Post-Oct-2024, the whole buyback amount is dividend (not just the gain), and the cost is split off as a capital loss — they are not netted against each other.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS-5: Compute Mr. Arjun's taxable income from the SLB lending activity.</summary>

**Answer:**
- Lending under SLB is **not a transfer** (Section 47(xv)), so no capital gain arises from the lending itself.
- The **lending fee is taxable** under PGBP (if in that business) or otherwise Income from Other Sources, and related expenses are deductible.
- Taxable income = Rs. 2,00,000 − Rs. 2,000 = **Rs. 1,98,000**.

**Intuition:** SLB lets a long-term holder earn extra income on idle shares; only the net fee (after expenses) is taxed, and getting back equivalent (not identical) scrips does not trigger capital gains.
</details>
