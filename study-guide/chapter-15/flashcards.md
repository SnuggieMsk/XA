# Chapter 15: Tools for Estate Planning — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check.

**Q1.** Which estate-planning tools take effect AFTER death, and which take effect DURING life?
> **A:** After death: **Will, Nomination**. During life: **Family Settlement, Trust, Guardianship, Joint Holding, Gift, Power of Attorney, Mutation**.

**Q2.** Define a Will and name the three key people involved.
> **A:** A Will is the legal declaration of the testator's intention about his property, to take effect after death. **Testator** makes it; **Legatee** receives a portion; **Executor** administers the estate.

**Q3.** When does a Will take effect, and can it be changed?
> **A:** Only **after the testator's death**. While alive, the testator can **revoke or alter** it anytime (if competent). It stays **confidential** until execution.

**Q4.** What is a privileged Will?
> **A:** A Will made (often **orally before 2 witnesses**) by **soldiers in warfare, airmen, or mariners at sea**. All other wills are **unprivileged**.

**Q5.** What is a holograph Will?
> **A:** A Will written **entirely in the testator's own handwriting** — considered a good/strong will.

**Q6.** What happens to a duplicate Will if the testator destroys his own copy?
> **A:** **Both** copies are considered **revoked**.

**Q7.** What are the four essentials for a valid Will (Section 59)?
> **A:** Testamentary capacity & sound mind; knowledge of contents; free from undue influence/fraud/coercion; a voluntary act.

**Q8.** Is registration of a Will compulsory?
> **A:** **No** — it's **optional** (Section 18). A non-registered Will is still genuine. Registered on plain paper with **no stamp duty**.

**Q9.** What special rule applies to a charitable/religious bequest by a non-Hindu?
> **A:** The Will must be **deposited with the registrar within 6 months** of execution, and the testator must **survive 12 months** from execution — else the bequest is invalid.

**Q10.** What are the four modes of revoking a Will?
> **A:** (1) A later Will/codicil; (2) a written declaration to revoke (executed like a Will); (3) burning/tearing/destroying with intent; (4) a subsequent marriage. (But marriage does NOT revoke a Hindu's Will.)

**Q11.** What is a codicil?
> **A:** A **supplementary document** to a Will that explains/alters/adds to it. It must be executed and attested like a Will. Used for **minor** changes.

**Q12.** Succession certificate vs Legal heir certificate — who issues each?
> **A:** **Succession certificate** → **Civil Court** (authority over debts/securities, no Will). **Legal heir certificate** → **Tehsildar** (identifies living heirs; used for insurance/pension/PF).

**Q13.** Probate vs Succession certificate — when is each used?
> **A:** **Probate** is issued when there **IS a Will** (court certifies its genuineness). A **succession certificate** is issued when there is **NO Will**.

**Q14.** In which cities is probate mandatory?
> **A:** **Kolkata, Chennai, and Mumbai** (the Presidency towns), or if the immovable property is there. Elsewhere it's optional.

**Q15.** What is the executor's first responsibility?
> **A:** To pay the **funeral/burial expenses** and obtain the **death certificate**. Then locate/value assets, pay all debts & taxes (lenders have first right), and finally distribute the estate.

**Q16.** Who is an administrator and when is one appointed?
> **A:** A person appointed by the **court** (given letters of administration) when there's no executor, the executor is incapable/refuses, or the executor dies before completing the job.

**Q17.** What is a gift, and who are the parties?
> **A:** A **voluntary transfer of property without consideration** (no payment). **Donor** gives; **Donee** receives. Usually irrevocable.

**Q18.** Are gifts taxable?
> **A:** Taxable as income from other sources, **except** gifts from relatives, gifts on marriage, and inheritances under a Will. But income later earned from the gift is taxed in the **donee's** hands.

**Q19.** Does a Will or a nomination prevail?
> **A:** A **Will supersedes a nomination**. The nominee is usually a **trustee** of the money, not the owner — except beneficial nominees under the Insurance Act and EPF Act.

**Q20.** Who can make a nomination, and how many nominees does SEBI now allow?
> **A:** Only an **individual** can nominate (not firms, trusts, HUF Kartas, PoA holders). SEBI now allows up to **10** nominees.

**Q21.** What is a family settlement and its tax advantages?
> **A:** An instrument to bring peace among same-family members over a property dispute. It's **not a transfer** (no capital gains), **not a gift**, and **no clubbing** applies.

**Q22.** Joint Tenancy vs Tenancy-in-Common — key difference?
> **A:** **Joint Tenancy** has a **right of survivorship** (share passes to surviving tenant, not by Will). **Tenancy-in-Common** has **no survivorship** — the share passes by Will/succession and can be unequal.

**Q23.** Name the three asset-protection strategies.
> **A:** (1) Use **Corporations/LLPs**; (2) **Asset Protection Trusts (APTs)** — but irrevocable, lose control; (3) **Transferring property rights** to spouse/relative/friend.

**Q24.** What major law changed creditor protection in 2016?
> **A:** The **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016** — with defined timelines (default ascertained in 14 days, admission communicated in 7 days).

**Q25.** Define a trust and its three parties.
> **A:** A trust is an obligation attached to property ownership arising from a confidence reposed in an owner for another's benefit. Parties: **Settlor/Author** (creates), **Trustee** (manages, holds legal title), **Beneficiary** (benefits).

**Q26.** What are the five essential elements of a trust?
> **A:** An **author/settlor**, a **trustee**, a **beneficiary**, the **trust property**, and a **specific object/purpose**.

**Q27.** Revocable vs Irrevocable trust?
> **A:** **Revocable** — settlor keeps control, can modify/terminate, NOT asset-protective, taxed in settlor's hands (acts like a Will-alternative). **Irrevocable** — cannot be amended unless the deed expressly allows (Sec 77/78).

**Q28.** Discretionary vs Determinate trust — and how is each taxed?
> **A:** **Discretionary** — trustee has full discretion, beneficiary shares undefined, taxed at **MMR (30%)**. **Determinate** — fixed/computable shares, taxed on the representative assessee at slab rates.

**Q29.** Give four key differences between a Family Trust and a Will.
> **A:** Trust is effective **immediately** (Will only on death); trust **avoids probate** (Will may need it); trust stays **private** (Will can be public); trust covers property **transferred to it** (Will covers property in your name at death).

**Q30.** What is a hybrid trust?
> **A:** A trust with features of **both** determinate and discretionary — trustee pays a **fixed amount** as set by the settlor and has **discretion** over the rest.

**Q31.** Why is a private trust called a "pass-through entity"?
> **A:** It has **no separate legal identity**; income flows through to beneficiaries and is taxed as **their** income. The trustee pays tax only in a **representative** capacity.

**Q32.** What is the rule against trust perpetuities?
> **A:** A trust can't be perpetual. Income accumulation can't exceed the **transferor's lifetime or 18 years** (whichever later). Exception: trusts for public benefit.

**Q33.** Which section exempts transfers to a trust from capital gains, and for which trust?
> **A:** **Section 47(iii)** — exempts transfer to an **irrevocable** trust. Transfers to a **revocable** trust ARE taxed; testamentary transfers (via Will) are exempt.

**Q34.** State the DNI formula and compute it for: taxable income ₹80,000, capital gains ₹20,000, exemptions ₹20,000.
> **A:** **DNI = Taxable Income − Capital Gains + Exemptions** = ₹80,000 − ₹20,000 + ₹20,000 = **₹80,000**.

**Q35.** Name the three types of legal guardianship.
> **A:** **Natural** (by birth — parents until child is 18), **Court-appointed**, and **Testamentary** (appointed under a Will).

**Q36.** Guardian of the Person vs Guardian of the Estate?
> **A:** Guardian of the **Person** handles custody and care; Guardian of the **Estate** handles **financial affairs**.

**Q37.** What is a Power of Attorney and who are the parties?
> **A:** A document giving one person (the **agent/attorney-in-fact/donee**) power to act for another (the **principal/donor**). Both must be major, sound-minded, and competent.

**Q38.** General vs Special POA?
> **A:** **General** = broad authority over multiple transactions. **Special** = narrow, only a specific transaction; authority ends when the transaction completes.

**Q39.** When does a POA get revoked?
> **A:** When the principal revokes; either party becomes insolvent; the holder renounces; the business is completed; or on the **death** of either party. (If the agent has an interest, it can't be revoked without consent — Sec 202.)

**Q40.** Is a Power of Attorney compulsorily registrable?
> **A:** **No** — unless it creates an interest/charge in immovable property. Stamp duty (Article 48) applies and varies by state.
