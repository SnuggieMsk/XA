# Chapter 15: Tools for Estate Planning — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** Which of the following is a tool used DURING the lifetime of an individual?
A) Will  B) Nomination  C) Gift  D) None of these
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A Gift takes effect during life. Will and Nomination are tools used **after death**.
</details>

**Q2.** The person who makes a Will is called the:
A) Executor  B) Legatee  C) Testator  D) Donee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The **testator** makes the Will. The **legatee** receives; the **executor** administers; the **donee** receives a gift.
</details>

**Q3.** A Will written entirely in the testator's own handwriting is a:
A) Holograph Will  B) Privileged Will  C) Sham Will  D) Duplicate Will
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — A **holograph** Will is wholly handwritten by the testator. A privileged will is an oral will by soldiers/mariners; a sham will lacks real intent.
</details>

**Q4.** Registration of a Will under the Registration Act, 1908 is:
A) Compulsory for all  B) Optional  C) Compulsory only for Hindus  D) Banned
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Registration is **optional** (Section 18); an unregistered Will is still genuine, though registration has advantages.
</details>

**Q5.** An instrument used to make minor alterations to an existing Will is a:
A) Trust deed  B) Probate  C) Codicil  D) Gift deed
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A **codicil** explains/alters/adds to a Will and must be executed & attested like a Will. For major changes, write a fresh Will.
</details>

**Q6.** Which document is issued by a Civil Court when a person dies WITHOUT a Will, to collect debts and securities?
A) Probate  B) Legal heir certificate  C) Succession certificate  D) Letters of administration
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A **succession certificate** (civil court) lets heirs collect the deceased's debts/securities when there is no Will. Probate applies when there IS a Will.
</details>

**Q7.** A legal heir certificate is issued by the:
A) Civil Court  B) Tehsildar of the district  C) SEBI  D) Notary
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A legal heir certificate is issued by the **Tehsildar** and identifies living heirs (used for insurance/pension/PF/gratuity). It is NOT conclusive proof under succession laws.
</details>

**Q8.** Probate is mandatory if the Will or immovable property relates to:
A) Delhi or Bangalore  B) Kolkata, Chennai or Mumbai  C) Any rural area  D) Only NRIs
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Probate is mandatory in the three Presidency towns: **Kolkata, Chennai, Mumbai**. Elsewhere it's optional.
</details>

**Q9.** Probate is granted only to:
A) Any legal heir  B) The executor appointed in the Will  C) The Tehsildar  D) The nominee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Probate is given only to the **executor** named in the Will (Section 222). If there's no executor, the court grants **letters of administration**.
</details>

**Q10.** A gift is best defined as:
A) A sale at a discount  B) A voluntary transfer without consideration  C) A loan  D) A nomination
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A gift is a **voluntary transfer of property without consideration** (no payment). Donor gives; donee receives.
</details>

**Q11.** When a Will and a nomination conflict, which prevails?
A) The nomination  B) The Will  C) Neither  D) The earliest dated one
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A **Will supersedes a nomination**; the nominee is generally just a trustee of the money. Exceptions: insurance/EPF beneficial nominees.
</details>

**Q12.** In a Joint Tenancy, on the death of one owner:
A) The share goes to the deceased's legal heirs by Will  B) The share passes to the surviving joint tenant (right of survivorship)  C) The property is sold  D) The state takes it
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Joint Tenancy carries a **right of survivorship**. (In Tenancy-in-Common there's no survivorship — the share passes by Will/succession.)
</details>

**Q13.** A family settlement is advantageous because:
A) It is treated as a transfer attracting capital gains  B) It is treated as a gift  C) It is NOT treated as a transfer or a gift, and clubbing doesn't apply  D) It always requires probate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A family settlement is **not a transfer** (no capital gains), **not a gift**, and **no clubbing** applies.
</details>

**Q14.** A discretionary trust is generally taxed at:
A) Zero  B) Slab rates  C) Maximum Marginal Rate (30%)  D) 10%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A discretionary trust (beneficiary shares undefined) is taxed at the **Maximum Marginal Rate (MMR), 30%**, with some exceptions.
</details>

**Q15.** Which trust does NOT pass through probate and remains private?
A) Will  B) Family trust  C) Testamentary trust  D) None
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A **family trust** avoids probate and stays private, unlike a Will (which may need probate and can become a public record).
</details>

**Q16.** Section 47(iii) of the Income Tax Act exempts capital gains tax on transfers to a/an:
A) Revocable trust  B) Irrevocable trust  C) Public company  D) Nominee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Transfers to an **irrevocable trust** are exempt from capital gains (Sec 47(iii)). Transfers to a revocable trust ARE taxed.
</details>

**Q17.** A trust where the trustee pays a fixed amount and has discretion over the rest is a:
A) Simple trust  B) Determinate trust  C) Hybrid trust  D) Public trust
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A **hybrid trust** combines determinate (fixed) and discretionary features.
</details>

**Q18.** How many parties are required to form a trust?
A) Two  B) Three  C) Four  D) No limit
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Three**: the author/settlor, the trustee, and the beneficiary.
</details>

**Q19.** A guardian appointed under the provisions of a Will is a:
A) Natural guardian  B) Court-appointed guardian  C) Testamentary guardian  D) Guardian of the estate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A **testamentary guardian** is appointed via a Will, mostly for minor children.
</details>

**Q20.** A Special Power of Attorney:
A) Grants broad authority over many transactions  B) Authorises only a specific transaction and ends on its completion  C) Cannot be revoked  D) Must always be registered
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A **Special POA** is narrow — for one specific matter; authority **expires** when the transaction completes. A General POA grants broad authority.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**The Mehta family — choosing the right tools**

Mr. Mehta (62, Hindu) owns: a family business, a house in Mumbai, mutual funds (with a nominee — his younger son), and bank deposits. He has three children. He wants to (a) ensure his special-needs grandson is cared for for life, (b) keep his arrangements private, (c) pass the Mumbai house smoothly, and (d) let his trusted nephew handle bank matters while he travels abroad for treatment.

**Q1.** Mr. Mehta wants the special-needs grandson cared for for life, with arrangements kept private and avoiding court oversight. Which tool fits best, and why?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A **private (family) trust**. Unlike a Will, a trust is effective immediately, **avoids probate**, stays **private**, and can be structured (e.g. discretionary) so the trustee manages and provides for the grandson's lifetime care. A Will alone would become a public record and may go through probate.
</details>

**Q2.** The Mumbai house is the asset he most wants to pass smoothly. If he passes it through a Will, what extra legal step is mandatory in Mumbai?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Probate** is **mandatory** in Mumbai (one of the three Presidency towns). So the executor would have to obtain probate from the court before the house can be transferred to the heirs.
</details>

**Q3.** His mutual funds have his younger son as nominee, but his Will leaves the funds equally to all three children. Who is ultimately entitled to the money?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The **Will supersedes the nomination**. The nominee (younger son) is treated as a **trustee/custodian** of the money; legally it must be distributed equally among all three children as per the Will. (The mutual fund may still validly pay out to the nominee, but the nominee holds it for the rightful heirs.)
</details>

**Q4.** While abroad for treatment, he wants his nephew to operate only his bank account — nothing else. Which instrument and which type should he use?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A **Power of Attorney**, specifically a **Special POA** — limited to operating the bank account. Since he's an NRI executing it abroad, it must be **authenticated/attested by the Indian Embassy/Consulate** and notarized where executed. A General POA would give too-broad powers.
</details>

**Q5.** Mr. Mehta worries that if the business faces creditors, his personal house could be seized. Name two asset-protection approaches from the chapter.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(1) Run the business through a **Corporation or LLP** so the owner isn't personally liable for the entity's debts (except for fraudulent transfers). (2) Use an **Asset Protection Trust (APT)** — assets in the trust aren't legally his, so creditors can't reach them (drawback: it's irrevocable and he loses control). He could also consider that certain assets like **PPF cannot be attached** by creditors.
</details>
