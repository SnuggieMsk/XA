# Chapter 4: Retirement Planning Basics — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** Why has retirement planning become more important over time?
A) Salaries are falling  B) Life expectancy is increasing  C) Inflation has stopped  D) Banks now give retirement loans
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Rising **life expectancy** (the number of years a person is expected to live) means more years to fund without a salary, so a bigger corpus is needed. D is wrong: no bank lends for retirement. C is false — inflation persists and erodes purchasing power.
</details>

**Q2.** A worker retires at 60 and is expected to live to 80. How many years must the corpus fund?
A) 40  B) 60  C) 20  D) 80
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — 80 − 60 = **20 years** of post-retirement life with no work income.
</details>

**Q3.** Which is the correct order of the three phases of retirement planning?
A) Final → Pre-retirement → Preparation  B) Preparation → Pre-retirement → Final retirement  C) Pre-retirement → Preparation → Final  D) Preparation → Final → Pre-retirement
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Preparation (kids' education, house, insurance) → Pre-retirement/initial (physical/psychological changes, learning the rules) → Final retirement (arrangements complete).
</details>

**Q4.** What makes retirement fundamentally different from goals like buying a house or a car?
A) It has a fixed deadline  B) Loans are easily available for it  C) No company gives loans to fund retirement  D) It needs the smallest corpus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — You can borrow for a house/car/education, but **no one lends for retirement**, so you must self-fund. It also needs the *largest* corpus (so D is wrong) and has the longest horizon.
</details>

**Q5.** Which two periods are central to calculating the retirement corpus?
A) Years to retirement & years in retirement  B) Years of schooling & years of work  C) Loan tenure & EMI period  D) Tax year & financial year
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — **Years to retirement** (now → retirement age) drives inflation impact and compounding; **years in/during retirement** (retirement → end of life) drives the size of the corpus needed.
</details>

**Q6.** "Longevity risk" in retirement planning means:
A) Living shorter than expected  B) The risk that inflation falls  C) Underestimating how long you'll live, so money runs out  D) Investments becoming too risky
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Underestimating the years in retirement means the corpus may run out before life ends.
</details>

**Q7.** At 6% inflation, an item costing Rs. 100 today will cost approximately how much in 30 years?
A) Rs. 179  B) Rs. 321  C) Rs. 574  D) Rs. 1,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Future Cost = 100 × (1.06)^30 ≈ **Rs. 574**. (Rs. 179 is 10 years, Rs. 321 is 20 years.)
</details>

**Q8.** Inflation impacts retirement planning by:
A) Reducing the corpus required  B) Raising costs both at the time of retirement and during retirement  C) Only affecting the accumulation stage  D) Increasing the real rate of return
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Inflation raises the cost of expenses *by* the time you retire AND keeps raising them *during* retirement, so it increases the corpus needed.
</details>

**Q9.** Which expenses typically rise *faster* than general inflation in retirement?
A) Housing loan EMIs  B) Children's education  C) Health costs and travel  D) Work-related expenses
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The workbook notes health costs and travel typically increase at a higher rate than standard inflation.
</details>

**Q10.** The real (inflation-adjusted) rate of return when nominal return is 8% and inflation is 6% is approximately:
A) 2.00%  B) 1.89%  C) 14.00%  D) 0.75%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Real return = (1.08 ÷ 1.06) − 1 = **1.89%** p.a. Simple subtraction gives ~2%, but the precise formula gives 1.89%.
</details>

**Q11.** Inflation and the expected rate of return act on the required corpus in which way?
A) Both increase it  B) Both decrease it  C) Inflation increases it; return decreases the savings required  D) Neither has any effect
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Inflation pushes UP the corpus/savings required; the return earned by the corpus pushes DOWN the savings required.
</details>

**Q12.** In which stage can an investor generally take *more* investment risk?
A) Distribution stage  B) Accumulation stage  C) Post-retirement only  D) Risk should always be the same
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — During **accumulation** the long horizon allows more risk for higher return; in **distribution** the ability to take risk falls because losses can't be recovered.
</details>

**Q13.** Under the Replacement Ratio Method with an 80% ratio and pre-retirement income of Rs. 2,00,000, the Year-1 retirement income target is:
A) Rs. 2,00,000  B) Rs. 1,60,000  C) Rs. 40,000  D) Rs. 2,50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Replacement Income (Year 1) = 2,00,000 × 0.80 = **Rs. 1,60,000**.
</details>

**Q14.** Mr. Rajeev (age 50) earns Rs. 1,00,000/month, income grows 10% p.a., retires at 60, wants 50% replacement. His Year-1 replacement income is about:
A) Rs. 1,00,000  B) Rs. 1,29,687  C) Rs. 2,59,374  D) Rs. 1,38,765
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Income at retirement = 1,00,000 × 1.1^10 = Rs. 2,59,374 p.a.; × 0.50 = **Rs. 1,29,687**. (Rs. 1,38,765 is the second year after 7% inflation.)
</details>

**Q15.** The Expense Protection Method estimates retirement income based on:
A) A fixed % of last income  B) A detailed budget of actual expenses  C) The employer's pension  D) Stock market returns
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — It builds the income requirement from a detailed monthly budget of expenses; easier for those near retirement.
</details>

**Q16.** Mr. Ashish has Rs. 45,000 total monthly retirement expense today, 20 years to retire, 6% inflation. His expense at retirement is:
A) Rs. 90,000  B) Rs. 1,44,321  C) Rs. 54,000  D) Rs. 81,411
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 45,000 × (1.06)^20 = **Rs. 1,44,321**.
</details>

**Q17.** In India, statutory superannuation (pension) benefit is provided only to employees covered under:
A) The Companies Act  B) The Employees' Pension Scheme, 1995  C) Section 80C  D) The Gratuity Act
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Only the **EPS, 1995** provides statutory pension. Other establishments must arrange annuities via LIC/IRDAI insurers.
</details>

**Q18.** An approved superannuation fund must have at least what percentage of employees employed in India?
A) 50%  B) 75%  C) 90%  D) 100%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Part B of the Fourth Schedule requires **not less than 90%** of employees employed in India.
</details>

**Q19.** Who must approve a superannuation fund for the employer's contributions to be tax-deductible?
A) IRDAI  B) SEBI  C) Commissioner of Income Tax  D) PFRDA
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The fund must be approved by the **Commissioner of Income Tax** for the employer to treat contributions as a deductible business expense.
</details>

**Q20.** Which IT Act section makes the income earned by trustees of an approved superannuation fund tax-free?
A) Section 10(13)  B) Section 10(25)(iii)  C) Section 36(iv)  D) Section 80C
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Section 10(25)(iii)** excludes income received by trustees on behalf of the fund → it accumulates tax-free. 10(13) = payments to beneficiaries; 36(iv) = employer deduction; 80C = employee deduction.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Mrs. Meera is **40 years old** and plans to retire at **60**. Her current monthly household expense is **Rs. 50,000**. She expects general **inflation of 7% p.a.** Her investments are expected to earn **9% p.a.** during retirement. Assume she will live to **85** (i.e., 25 years in retirement). For simplicity, ignore the difference between pre- and post-retirement expense ratios unless asked.

**Q1.** What will Mrs. Meera's monthly expense be at the start of retirement (age 60)?
<details><summary>Step-by-step answer</summary>

Use **Future Cost = Present × (1 + inflation)^years**.
- Present = Rs. 50,000; inflation = 7% = 0.07; years to retire = 60 − 40 = **20**.
- Future monthly expense = 50,000 × (1.07)^20 = 50,000 × 3.8697 ≈ **Rs. 1,93,484 per month**.

So at 60 she needs roughly **Rs. 1.93 lakh/month** just to maintain today's Rs. 50,000 lifestyle.
</details>

**Q2.** What is her real (inflation-adjusted) rate of return during retirement, used to size the corpus?
<details><summary>Step-by-step answer</summary>

**Real return = [(1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation)] − 1**
- = (1.09 ÷ 1.07) − 1 = 1.018692 − 1 = **0.018692 ≈ 1.87% p.a.**

**Logic:** The corpus grows at 9%, but prices climb at 7%, so the *purchasing power* gain is only ~1.87% per year. This real rate is what we use to discount future retirement income.
</details>

**Q3.** If Mrs. Meera had instead used the **Replacement Ratio Method** assuming her pre-retirement income at 60 is Rs. 3,00,000/month and a replacement ratio of 70%, what is her Year-1 retirement income target?
<details><summary>Step-by-step answer</summary>

**Replacement Income (Year 1) = Pre-retirement income × ratio**
- = 3,00,000 × 0.70 = **Rs. 2,10,000 per month** in the first year of retirement.

Each later year it rises by inflation (×1.07) to preserve purchasing power:
- Year 2 = 2,10,000 × 1.07 = **Rs. 2,24,700/month**.
</details>

**Q4.** Why does starting at 40 instead of 50 make her monthly saving requirement much smaller, even though the target corpus is similar?
<details><summary>Step-by-step answer</summary>

Because of **compounding** over a longer **years-to-retirement** period. Starting at 40 gives 20 years for contributions to grow; starting at 50 gives only 10 years. The longer the period, the greater the compounding effect, so **a smaller monthly allocation from savings** is needed to reach the same corpus. Delay is "catastrophic" because the required monthly amount grows manifold and the early-start benefit is lost.
</details>

**Q5.** Name two reasons her actual retirement expense might be *lower* than the inflated figure in Q1, and one reason it could be *higher*.
<details><summary>Step-by-step answer</summary>

**Lower:** (1) Housing-loan EMIs, children's education, and work-related expenses typically cease by retirement; (2) lower income may push her into a **lower tax bracket**, cutting taxes; long-term savings requirements also stop.

**Higher:** Medical/health costs and travel typically rise *faster* than general inflation, so those line items can push the total back up. This is why a budget review near retirement (Expense Protection Method) is recommended.
</details>
