## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** What does the term "life expectancy" refer to?
A) The age at which one must retire  B) The number of years an individual is expected to live  C) The number of working years  D) The number of years pension is paid
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Life expectancy is the number of years an individual is expected to live, depending on factors like health and scientific advancement.
- A) Wrong: that is the retirement age, a separate concept.
- C) Wrong: working years run from start of career to retirement age, not until death.
- D) Wrong: pension period is "years during retirement," a different figure.
</details>

**Q2.** Why has the need for retirement planning increased over time?
A) Falling life expectancy  B) Increasing life expectancy  C) Rising salaries  D) Falling inflation
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — As average life expectancy rises, people spend more years in retirement with no salary, so they must plan to fund those years.
- A) Wrong: life expectancy is rising, not falling.
- C) and D) Wrong: rising salaries and falling inflation would ease, not create, the need; the driver named in the workbook is longer life.
</details>

**Q3.** In the workbook's example, X has a working life of 40 years assuming the retirement age is:
A) 55 years  B) 58 years  C) 60 years  D) 65 years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The example assumes 60 as the retirement age; starting work in the 20s gives roughly 40 working years.
- A), B), D) Wrong: the workbook specifically states 60 as the assumed retirement age in this illustration.
</details>

**Q4.** In the same example, if life expectancy is 80 years and retirement is at 60, how many post-retirement years must X fund?
A) 10 years  B) 15 years  C) 20 years  D) 25 years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Years during retirement = life expectancy − retirement age = 80 − 60 = **20 years**.
- A), B), D) Wrong: only 80 − 60 = 20 matches the figures given.
</details>

**Q5.** According to the workbook, retirement planning is not only about money accumulation but also about:

- A) Wrong: buying a house is a preparation-stage goal, not the definition of retirement planning.
- C), D) Wrong: neither is stated as the purpose of retirement planning.
</details>

**Q6.** What is described as the biggest roadblock for retirement planning?

**Q7.** Which of these is listed as a phase of retirement planning?

**Q8.** The three phases of retirement planning are Preparation, Pre-retirement, and:
A) Accumulation  B) Distribution  C) Final Retirement  D) Annuity
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The phases are (a) Preparation, (b) Initial/Pre-retirement, (c) Final Retirement.
- A), B) Wrong: accumulation and distribution are stages of the *corpus*, not the three life phases listed.
- D) Wrong: annuity is a payout product, not a phase.
</details>

**Q9.** Which activity belongs to the "preparation stage" of retirement planning?
A) Drawing pension  B) Need for child education and buying a house  C) Final withdrawal of corpus  D) Closing the trust
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The preparation stage includes child education, buying a house, adequate life and health insurance, and recognising the impact of ageing.
- A), C), D) Wrong: drawing pension and final withdrawals belong to the retirement phase, not preparation.
</details>

**Q10.** A key reason retirement differs from other financial goals is that:
A) It is the cheapest goal  B) No company gives loans for meeting retirement needs  C) It has the shortest horizon  D) It requires the smallest corpus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — You can borrow for a house, car, or education, but no company gives loans for retirement, so you must self-fund it.
- A), D) Wrong: retirement needs the *largest* corpus, not the smallest/cheapest.
- C) Wrong: it has the *longest* horizon.
</details>

**Q11.** The workbook says the college education goal is generally at a horizon of:

- A), B), D) Wrong: these do not match the figure stated.

**Q12.** Compared to other goals, the retirement goal has the:
A) Shortest accumulation period  B) Longest accumulation and distribution periods and largest corpus  C) Smallest corpus  D) Most flexible deadline
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The workbook describes retirement as the goal with the longest accumulation and distribution periods and requiring the largest corpus.

**Q13.** The "accumulation stage" of retirement is the stage in which:

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Accumulation is the stage where saving and investing build up the corpus.
- A), D) Wrong: using/spending the corpus is the distribution stage.
- C) Wrong: winding up a trust is unrelated to this definition.
</details>

**Q14.** The "distribution stage" of retirement is when:
A) Savings are first started  B) The corpus created in accumulation is employed to generate income  C) The employer creates a trust  D) Inflation stops
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Distribution is when the accumulated corpus is used to generate the income needed for retirement expenses.
- A) Wrong: that is the accumulation stage.
- C), D) Wrong: neither defines the distribution stage.
</details>

**Q15.** Which is the FIRST step in the retirement planning process?
A) Determine the retirement corpus  B) Determine expenses in retirement  C) Buy an annuity  D) Appoint trustees
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The first step is to determine the expenses that have to be met in retirement.
- A) Wrong: determining the corpus is the last of the listed steps.
- C), D) Wrong: these are not steps in the planning process described.

**Q16.** "Years to retirement" is best defined as the period:
A) From retirement to end of life  B) From current age to retirement age  C) Of the trust's existence  D) Of annuity payments
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Years to retirement is the period from the current point in time (current age) to the year/age of retirement.
- A) Wrong: that is "years during retirement."
- C), D) Wrong: unrelated to this time period.

**Q17.** "Years during retirement" refers to the number of years:
A) From start of career to retirement  B) From the beginning of retirement to the end of life  C) The employer pays salary  D) Before the first job
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — It is the period from the beginning of retirement to the end of life, for which income must be secured.
- A) Wrong: that is the working life / years to retirement.

**Q18.** The risk of underestimating how many years one will live in retirement is called:
A) Inflation risk  B) Longevity risk  C) Liquidity risk  D) Credit risk
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Underestimating the years (longevity) means the money may not last the retirement years — this is longevity risk.
- A) Wrong: inflation risk is about rising prices eroding purchasing power.
- C), D) Wrong: liquidity and credit risk are unrelated concepts here.
</details>

**Q19.** Which of the following is NOT one of the four variables used to calculate the retirement corpus?
A) The periodic income required  B) The expected rate of inflation  C) The employer's profit  D) The rate of return expected from the corpus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The four variables are periodic income required, expected inflation, expected rate of return, and the period of retirement. The employer's profit is not among them.
- A), B), D) Wrong: each of these IS one of the four stated variables.
</details>

**Q20.** Inflation is best defined as:
A) A fall in prices over time  B) A general rise in prices of goods and services over time  C) The interest earned on a deposit  D) A tax on income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Inflation is a general rise in prices over time, which eats away the purchasing power of money.
- A) Wrong: that is deflation.
- C), D) Wrong: interest and tax are unrelated to the definition of inflation.
</details>

**Q21.** Inflation impacts retirement planning by:
A) Reducing expenses  B) Eating away the purchasing power of money over time  C) Increasing investment returns  D) Lowering life expectancy
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — As prices rise, one unit of money buys less; inflation erodes purchasing power.
- A) Wrong: inflation raises, not reduces, the cost of expenses.
- C), D) Wrong: inflation does not increase returns nor affect life expectancy.
</details>

**Q22.** According to the workbook, which expenses typically increase at a higher rate than standard inflation?

**Q29.** The Replacement Ratio Method assumes that:
A) Expenses double at retirement  B) The standard of living remains the same as just before retirement  C) Income falls to zero  D) Inflation is zero
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — It assumes the standard of living stays the same as just before entering retirement, so a percentage of pre-retirement income is replaced.
- A), C) Wrong: it does not assume doubling or zero income.
- D) Wrong: the replacement income is escalated by inflation each year.
</details>

**Q30.** Under the Replacement Ratio Method, the Year-1 replacement income equals:
A) Pre-retirement income × inflation  B) Pre-retirement income × replacement ratio  C) Pre-retirement income ÷ replacement ratio  D) Pre-retirement income + inflation
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Replacement Income (Year 1) = Pre-retirement Income × replacement ratio (e.g. ×0.80).
- A), D) Wrong: inflation is applied to *subsequent* years, not used to set Year 1.
- C) Wrong: it is multiplied by, not divided by, the ratio.
</details>

**Q31.** The Expense Protection Method defines retirement income based on:

- A) Wrong: that describes the Replacement Ratio Method.

**Q32.** For which group is the Expense Protection Method easier to use accurately?
