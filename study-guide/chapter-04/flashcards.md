# Chapter 4: Retirement Planning Basics — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check.

**Q1.** Why is retirement planning increasingly necessary?
> **A:** Because **life expectancy** (years a person is expected to live) keeps rising, so people spend more years without a salary and must fund those years from savings.

**Q2.** A person retires at 60 and lives to 80. How many years must their corpus fund?
> **A:** 20 years of expenses with no salary income (80 − 60).

**Q3.** What is a retirement "corpus"?
> **A:** The total lump sum of money accumulated to generate income through the retirement years.

**Q4.** Name the three phases of retirement planning.
> **A:** (1) Preparation stage, (2) Pre-retirement / initial retirement stage, (3) Final retirement.

**Q5.** Why is the retirement goal fundamentally different from buying a house or funding education?
> **A:** No bank lends money for retirement — you must self-fund it. Other goals can be met with loans.

**Q6.** Why do people dangerously postpone retirement saving?
> **A:** Retirement has no hard deadline, so it's easy to delay; people also raid retirement savings for other goals. This loses compounding and inflates the required amount.

**Q7.** Define the accumulation stage and the distribution stage.
> **A:** Accumulation = years of saving/investing to build the corpus. Distribution = retirement years when the corpus generates income.

**Q8.** What are the four steps of the retirement planning process?
> **A:** (a) Determine retirement expenses, (b) determine income requirement, (c) set time horizon, (d) determine the corpus.

**Q9.** What two time periods are central to corpus calculation?
> **A:** "Years to retirement" (current age → retirement) and "years in retirement" (retirement → end of life).

**Q10.** What is longevity risk?
> **A:** The risk of underestimating how long you'll live, so the money runs out before the end of life.

**Q11.** What four variables determine the retirement corpus?
> **A:** Periodic income required, expected inflation, expected rate of return on the corpus, and the period of retirement.

**Q12.** Define inflation and its effect on money.
> **A:** A general rise in prices over time. It erodes purchasing power — the same money buys less later.

**Q13.** What is the formula for the future cost of an expense?
> **A:** Future Cost = Present Cost × (1 + inflation rate)^number of years.

**Q14.** At 6% inflation, what does an item costing Rs. 100 today cost in 10, 20, and 30 years?
> **A:** Rs. 179 (10y), Rs. 321 (20y), Rs. 574 (30y).

**Q15.** Inflation impacts retirement in which two ways?
> **A:** (1) Raises the cost of expenses by the time you retire; (2) keeps raising expenses *during* retirement, so monthly income needed rises every year.

**Q16.** Living on Rs. 60,000/month today at 6% inflation, what is needed in 10 and 20 years?
> **A:** Rs. 1,07,451/month (10y) and Rs. 1,92,428/month (20y).

**Q17.** Which expenses typically rise faster than general inflation?
> **A:** Health costs and travel.

**Q18.** How does a higher expected rate of return affect the corpus needed?
> **A:** Higher return → lower corpus required (and lower savings needed) — but higher return brings higher risk.

**Q19.** Why take less risk in the distribution stage than in accumulation?
> **A:** In retirement the ability to take risk falls — losses can't be recovered since you can no longer add much to the corpus.

**Q20.** Inflation and return act in opposite directions — how?
> **A:** Inflation pushes the required corpus UP; the return earned on the corpus pushes the savings required DOWN.

**Q21.** Define the real (inflation-adjusted) rate of return and give its formula.
> **A:** The return after adjusting for inflation. Real Return = [(1 + nominal return) ÷ (1 + inflation)] − 1.

**Q22.** Compute the real return for 8% nominal return and 6% inflation.
> **A:** (1.08/1.06) − 1 = 0.01887 ≈ **1.89% p.a.**

**Q23.** What is the Replacement Ratio Method?
> **A:** Estimating retirement income as a percentage of pre-retirement income (e.g. 80%), then raising it by inflation each year.

**Q24.** Mr. Rajeev earns Rs. 1,00,000/month, retires in 10 years, income grows 10% p.a., wants 50% replacement. What is his year-1 replacement income?
> **A:** Income at retirement = 1,00,000 × 1.1^10 = Rs. 2,59,374 p.a.; × 0.50 = **Rs. 1,29,687**.

**Q25.** Continuing Q24, with 7% inflation, what is year-2 income?
> **A:** 1,29,687 × 107/100 = **Rs. 1,38,765**.

**Q26.** Why might retirement income needs be *lower* than working income?
> **A:** Housing loan/kids' education/work expenses end, some savings stop, and lower income can drop you into a lower tax bracket.

**Q27.** What is the Expense Protection Method?
> **A:** Estimating retirement income from a detailed budget of actual expenses (some rise, some fall); more accurate near retirement.

**Q28.** Mr. Ashish: retirement expense Rs. 45,000/month, 20 years to retire, 6% inflation. What's the expense at retirement?
> **A:** 45,000 × 1.06^20 = **Rs. 1,44,321/month**.

**Q29.** What is superannuation?
> **A:** A company-created pension program (company pension plan); funds grow, typically tax-free, until retirement/withdrawal.

**Q30.** In India, is statutory superannuation mandatory?
> **A:** No — except for employees under the Employees' Pension Scheme, 1995. Others must arrange annuities via LIC/IRDAI-approved insurers.

**Q31.** What two arrangements can an employer use for superannuation?
> **A:** (1) Payment by the employer; (2) Funding through an irrevocable Trust.

**Q32.** Why is "pay pension from current revenue as it falls due" not allowed?
> **A:** Payment depends on the employer's yearly profits; if profits fall or the employer goes bankrupt, pensioners may not be paid; AS-15 requires accrual-basis provisioning.

**Q33.** Who must approve a superannuation fund for tax benefits, and under which schedule?
> **A:** The Commissioner of Income Tax, under Part B of the Fourth Schedule of the IT Act.

**Q34.** What minimum percentage of employees must be employed in India for an approved super fund?
> **A:** Not less than 90%.

**Q35.** Match the tax section to the superannuation benefit: 10(13), 10(25)(iii), 36(iv), 80C.
> **A:** 10(13) = payments (death/commutation/incapacity/transfer to NPS) exempt; 10(25)(iii) = fund's income tax-free; 36(iv) = employer's contribution is a business deduction; 80C = employee's contribution deductible.

**Q36.** Name three responsibilities of superannuation trustees.
> **A:** (Any three) hold periodic meetings/keep minutes; collect contributions (via Actuary in defined benefit); invest per IT Rules; arrange pension benefits; deduct TDS on annuities; prepare/audit/submit annual accounts.
