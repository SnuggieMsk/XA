## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Equity investment generally produces which two types of income?
A) Capital gains and dividend income  B) Interest and rent  C) Commission and brokerage  D) Salary and bonus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The chapter states that two types of income are earned from equity products: capital gains (selling above cost) and dividend income (a share of company profits). B) interest/rent are debt and property incomes, not equity. C) brokerage and commission are charges/expenses, not income to the investor. D) salary/bonus are employment incomes, unrelated to equity ownership.
</details>

**Q2.** Dividend declared at an Annual General Meeting (AGM) is deemed to be the income of the shareholder in the previous year in which it is:
A) Received  B) Credited to bank  C) Declared  D) Reinvested
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A final dividend declared at the AGM is income of the year it is *declared*; the date the assessee actually receives it is not material. A) and B) describe receipt, which is the rule for *interim* dividends (taxed when unconditionally made available), not final dividends. D) reinvestment is irrelevant to the timing of taxability.
</details>

**Q3.** Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) was abolished by which Finance Act, shifting dividend tax to the shareholder?
A) Finance Act, 2020  B) Finance Act, 2018  C) Finance Act, 2024  D) Finance Act, 2025
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The Finance Act, 2020 abolished DDT with effect from FY 2020-21, so dividends are now taxable in the hands of shareholders. B) Finance Act 2018 made LTCG on listed shares taxable (Sec 112A). C) Finance Act 2024 set the 12.50%/20% rates and the ₹1.25 lakh limit. D) Finance Act 2025 amended ULIP definitions. Only 2020 dealt with DDT.
</details>

C) is wrong — STT applies mainly to listed/exchange-traded securities, not unlisted shares. D) is too narrow; STT covers equity delivery, equity MF and derivatives, not derivatives alone.
</details>

**Q12.** The period of holding for securities held in Demat form is determined using which method?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

A) LIFO (last-in-first-out) is the opposite and is not used. B) weighted average and D) specific identification are not prescribed for Demat holding period.

**Q13.** FIFO under Section 45(2A) for Demat securities is applied:

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** Mr. A buys listed equity shares and sells them after 10 months (STT paid on both legs). The gain is:

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Listed shares held 10 months (≤12) are short-term; with STT paid, STCG is taxed at 20% under Section 111A. A) needs >12 months holding to be long-term. B) slab rates apply only when STT/listing conditions fail. C) is wrong — STCG on equity is not exempt.
</details>

**Q37.** Mr. B sells listed equity shares held for 18 months with STT paid. His total 112A LTCG for the year is ₹90,000. Tax on this LTCG is:
A) 12.50% of ₹90,000  B) 20% of ₹90,000  C) Nil  D) 10% of ₹90,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — LTCG under Section 112A is exempt up to ₹1,25,000 a year. Since ₹90,000 < ₹1,25,000, no tax is payable. A), B) and D) all wrongly tax an amount that falls within the exempt slab; only the excess over ₹1.25 lakh is taxed at 12.50%.
</details>

**Q38.** A resident shareholder receives dividend of ₹2,00,000 and pays ₹60,000 interest on a loan taken to buy those shares. The maximum interest deduction allowed is:
A) ₹60,000  B) ₹40,000  C) ₹20,000  D) Nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Interest deduction against dividend is capped at 20% of the dividend income. Formula: deduction = min(actual interest, 20% × dividend). Here 20% × ₹2,00,000 = ₹40,000, which is less than the ₹60,000 actually paid, so only ₹40,000 is allowed. A) ignores the cap. C) misapplies the percentage. D) is wrong — some deduction is allowed.
</details>

**Q96.** Mr. R's 112A LTCG for the year is exactly ₹1,25,000 (STT paid). The tax on this LTCG is:
A) ₹15,625  B) ₹25,000  C) Nil  D) ₹6,250
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The full ₹1,25,000 is within the exemption limit, so the taxable excess is ₹0 and tax is Nil. A), B) and D) wrongly tax an amount that does not exceed the ₹1.25 lakh threshold.
</details>

**Q97.** An F&O trader has favourable differences of ₹3,00,000, unfavourable differences of ₹1,20,000, and premium received on sale of options of ₹40,000. Turnover (ICAI method) is:
A) ₹1,80,000  B) ₹4,20,000  C) ₹4,60,000  D) ₹3,40,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Turnover = total of favourable + unfavourable differences + premium on sale of options = ₹3,00,000 + ₹1,20,000 + ₹40,000 = ₹4,60,000. (Differences are added as absolute amounts, not netted.) A) nets the differences. B) omits the premium. D) omits the unfavourable difference.
</details>

**Q98.** Using Q97's turnover of ₹4,60,000, the presumptive income under Section 44AD (digital receipts at 6%) is:
A) ₹27,600  B) ₹36,800  C) ₹46,000  D) ₹2,30,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Presumptive profit = 6% × turnover = 6% × ₹4,60,000 = ₹27,600. B) uses 8% (cash rate). C) uses 10%. D) uses 50%. For digital/banking receipts the rate is 6%.
</details>

**Q99.** Mr. S bought 4,000 listed shares at ₹50 on 01-03-2018 (after grandfathering date), and sold at ₹90 on 01-06-2024 (STT paid). His 112A LTCG and tax (only such gain) are:
A) ₹1,60,000; ₹4,375  B) ₹1,60,000; ₹20,000  C) ₹1,60,000; Nil  D) ₹3,60,000; ₹29,375
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Bought after 31-01-2018, so cost = actual = 4,000 × ₹50 = ₹2,00,000. Sale = 4,000 × ₹90 = ₹3,60,000. Holding > 12 months → LTCG = ₹1,60,000. Taxable = ₹1,60,000 − ₹1,25,000 = ₹35,000; tax = 12.50% × ₹35,000 = ₹4,375. B) taxes more than the excess. C) ignores the excess. D) miscomputes the gain.
</details>

**Q100.** Mr. T (resident) has other income of ₹2,00,000 and LTCG on unlisted shares of ₹3,00,000 (basic exemption ₹2,50,000, old regime). The LTCG charged to tax is:
A) ₹3,00,000  B) ₹2,50,000  C) ₹2,00,000  D) ₹50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A resident can absorb the unused basic exemption against 112 LTCG. Shortfall = ₹2,50,000 − ₹2,00,000 = ₹50,000. This ₹50,000 reduces the LTCG, so taxable LTCG = ₹3,00,000 − ₹50,000 = ₹2,50,000. A) ignores the absorption. C) confuses it with other income. D) wrongly taxes only the shortfall.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Ms. Priya, a resident individual (old tax regime), had the following transactions during FY 2024-25, all on a recognised Indian stock exchange with STT paid on both purchase and sale unless stated otherwise:

1. **Listed equity — Alpha Ltd.:** Bought 2,000 shares on 10-06-2022 at ₹180. Sold all on 20-11-2024 at ₹260.
2. **Listed equity — Beta Ltd.:** Bought 1,000 shares on 01-08-2024 at ₹400. Sold on 15-01-2025 at ₹470.
3. **Old listed equity — Gamma Ltd.:** Bought 1,000 shares on 01-01-2016 at ₹120. FMV on 31-01-2018 was ₹300. Sold on 01-12-2024 at ₹500.
4. **Equity-oriented MF units:** Holds units bought after 31-01-2018; received ₹3,00,000 dividend and paid ₹80,000 interest on a loan taken to buy these units.
5. She also earned a **business profit of ₹6,00,000** and had an **F&O (non-speculative) loss of ₹1,50,000** from exchange-traded derivatives.

Work out the tax treatment of each item.

<details><summary>Q-CS1. What is the capital gain on Alpha Ltd. and how is it taxed?</summary>

Holding = 10-06-2022 to 20-11-2024 = more than 12 months → **long-term**, Section 112A applies (STT paid).
- Sale = 2,000 × ₹260 = ₹5,20,000
- Cost = 2,000 × ₹180 = ₹3,60,000 (bought after 31-01-2018, so actual cost; no grandfathering)
- **LTCG = ₹5,20,000 − ₹3,60,000 = ₹1,60,000**

This LTCG goes into the 112A pool, which gets a single ₹1,25,000 annual exemption (combined with Gamma below), and the excess is taxed at 12.50%.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS2. What is the capital gain on Beta Ltd. and how is it taxed?</summary>

Holding = 01-08-2024 to 15-01-2025 = about 5.5 months (≤ 12 months) → **short-term**, Section 111A applies (listed, STT paid).
- Sale = 1,000 × ₹470 = ₹4,70,000
- Cost = 1,000 × ₹400 = ₹4,00,000
- **STCG = ₹70,000**, taxed at the flat **20%** under Section 111A.
- Tax on this STCG = 20% × ₹70,000 = **₹14,000** (ignoring surcharge/cess). No 80C–80U deduction is allowed against it.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS3. What is the capital gain on Gamma Ltd. (grandfathering applies)?</summary>

Bought on 01-01-2016 (on/before 31-01-2018) → grandfathering applies. Holding > 12 months → long-term (Section 112A).
- Grandfathered cost = **higher of**:
  - (a) actual cost = 1,000 × ₹120 = ₹1,20,000; OR
  - (b) **lower of** [FMV 1,000 × ₹300 = ₹3,00,000] and [sale 1,000 × ₹500 = ₹5,00,000] = ₹3,00,000
  - Higher of ₹1,20,000 and ₹3,00,000 = **₹3,00,000**
- Sale = 1,000 × ₹500 = ₹5,00,000
- **LTCG = ₹5,00,000 − ₹3,00,000 = ₹2,00,000**

Intuition: grandfathering protects the gains that built up before 01-02-2018 (when LTCG was tax-free), so we start the clock from the higher ₹300 FMV instead of the ₹120 cost.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS4. Combine the 112A long-term gains and compute the LTCG tax.</summary>

Total Section 112A LTCG = Alpha ₹1,60,000 + Gamma ₹2,00,000 = **₹3,60,000**.
- The ₹1,25,000 annual exemption is applied **once** to the combined pool.
- Taxable LTCG = ₹3,60,000 − ₹1,25,000 = ₹2,35,000
- **Tax = 12.50% × ₹2,35,000 = ₹29,375** (ignoring surcharge/cess).

Key point: the ₹1.25 lakh limit is per year across all 112A gains, not per scrip — a common trap is to exempt ₹1.25 lakh for each company.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS5. How are the dividend, the interest, and the F&O loss treated?</summary>

**Dividend:** ₹3,00,000 is taxable at slab rates. Interest deduction is capped at 20% × ₹3,00,000 = ₹60,000 (less than the ₹80,000 actually paid, so only ₹60,000 is allowed).
- **Taxable dividend = ₹3,00,000 − ₹60,000 = ₹2,40,000**

**F&O loss:** Exchange-traded F&O is non-speculative business income (Section 43(5)). The ₹1,50,000 loss can be set off against her business profit:
- Business profit ₹6,00,000 − F&O loss ₹1,50,000 = **net business income ₹4,50,000**.
- (If any F&O loss were unabsorbed, it could be carried forward up to 8 assessment years, but only if she files her return by the due date — and it cannot be set off against salary.)

So Priya's heads of income are: business income ₹4,50,000 + taxable dividend ₹2,40,000 (both at slab rates) + STCG ₹70,000 (20%) + LTCG ₹2,35,000 taxable (12.50%).
</details>
