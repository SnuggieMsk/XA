# Chapter 11: Taxation of Equity Products — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** LTCG on listed equity shares (STT paid) is taxed at:
A) 10% flat  B) 12.50% on the amount exceeding ₹1,25,000  C) 20%  D) Exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 112A: first ₹1.25 lakh exempt, excess @12.50%. 20% (C) is the STCG rate under 111A.
</details>

**Q2.** STCG on listed equity shares with STT paid is taxed under:
A) Section 112A @12.50%  B) Section 111A @20%  C) Slab rates always  D) Section 112 @12.50%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 111A taxes such STCG at 20%. If STT not paid, slab rates apply.
</details>

**Q3.** Listed equity shares become long-term capital assets after a holding period of:
A) 6 months  B) 12 months  C) 24 months  D) 36 months
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — >12 months for listed equity. Unlisted (C) needs 24 months.
</details>

**Q4.** Under grandfathering, a share cost ₹100, FMV on 31-01-2018 ₹200, sold @₹250. LTCG is:
A) ₹150  B) ₹50  C) ₹250  D) Nil
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Cost = higher of [₹100] and [lower of (₹200, ₹250)] = ₹200. LTCG = 250 − 200 = **₹50**.
</details>

**Q5.** A share cost ₹100, FMV ₹200 on 31-01-2018, sold @₹150. LTCG is:
A) ₹50  B) Nil  C) Loss of ₹50  D) ₹100
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Cost = lower of (FMV ₹200, sale ₹150) = ₹150. LTCG = 150 − 150 = **Nil**. Grandfathering won't manufacture a loss.
</details>

**Q6.** Holding period of demat shares is determined by:
A) LIFO  B) Weighted average  C) FIFO (Section 45(2A))  D) Specific identification
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — FIFO, applied account-wise.
</details>

**Q7.** Dividend received by a resident from listed shares — maximum allowable deduction?
A) All actual expenses  B) Interest expense up to 20% of dividend  C) Nil  D) ₹1.5 lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Only interest expense, capped at 20% of dividend; no other expense.
</details>

**Q8.** LTCG on unlisted equity shares is taxed at:
A) 20%  B) 12.50% (+ surcharge/cess)  C) Slab rate  D) Exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 112, 12.50%. Holding period to be long-term is >24 months.
</details>

**Q9.** STCG on unlisted equity shares is taxed at:
A) 20% under 111A  B) Normal slab rates  C) 12.50%  D) 30%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 111A's 20% applies only to listed/STT-paid shares; unlisted STCG is at slab rates.
</details>

**Q10.** Forfeiture of premium paid on an unexercised share warrant:
A) Is a short-term capital loss  B) Is ignored — no tax treatment  C) Is a business loss  D) Reduces cost of shares
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The loss on forfeiture is ignored entirely.
</details>

**Q11.** Conversion of a listed share warrant (held 15 months) into shares results in:
A) No tax  B) LTCG @12.50% on (FMV of shares − strike price)  C) STCG @20%  D) Business income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Conversion = transfer of warrant; held >12 months → LTCG @12.50%; FMV (Sec 50D) is the consideration.
</details>

**Q12.** An "equity-oriented fund" must invest at least how much in listed domestic equity?
A) 35%  B) 50%  C) 65%  D) 90%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — ≥65% (or via ETF route, ≥90% in an ETF that itself holds ≥90% equity).
</details>

**Q13.** Income from exchange-traded F&O is generally treated as:
A) Speculative business income  B) Non-speculative business income  C) Capital gains  D) Salary
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 43(5) excludes exchange F&O from speculative; it's non-speculative business income.
</details>

**Q14.** A non-speculative F&O business loss can be set off against all heads EXCEPT:
A) House property  B) Capital gains  C) Salary  D) Other sources
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Business loss cannot be set off against salary income.
</details>

**Q15.** F&O business loss can be carried forward for:
A) 4 assessment years  B) 8 assessment years  C) Indefinitely  D) 2 assessment years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 8 assessment years, set off only against business income, and only if the return is filed by the due date.
</details>

**Q16.** Under Section 44AD, an F&O trader with digital receipts can declare profit at:
A) 6% of turnover  B) 8% of turnover  C) 50% of turnover  D) 30% of turnover
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — 6% for digital receipts (8% for cash), turnover ≤ ₹2 crore (or ₹3 crore).
</details>

**Q17.** Bonus stripping is curbed by:
A) Section 112A  B) Section 94(8)  C) Section 50AA  D) Section 111A
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 94(8): the loss on the original units is ignored and added to the cost of the bonus units.
</details>

**Q18.** Which benefit is NOT allowed against LTCG taxed @12.50%?
A) Basic exemption limit adjustment  B) Deduction under 80C–80U  C) STT deduction  D) None of these
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — No 80C–80U deduction against 12.50% LTCG; also no 87A rebate against 112A LTCG and no foreign-currency computation.
</details>

**Q19.** The surcharge on 112A LTCG of ₹3 crore is capped at:
A) 25%  B) 37%  C) 15%  D) 10%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Surcharge on 111A/112/112A gains is capped at 15% even above ₹2 crore.
</details>

**Q20.** A resident's other income is ₹1,85,000 and LTCG (unlisted shares) is ₹2,50,000. Basic exemption ₹2,50,000. LTCG chargeable to tax?
A) ₹2,50,000  B) ₹1,85,000  C) Nil  D) ₹65,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Shortfall = 2,50,000 − 1,85,000 = ₹65,000 absorbs ₹65,000 of LTCG → chargeable LTCG = 2,50,000 − 65,000 = **₹1,85,000**.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Ms. Anita, a resident individual (total income otherwise above the basic exemption limit), has the following equity transactions in FY 2024-25 (all listed, STT paid, sale after 23-07-2024):
1. **2,000 shares of Sunrise Ltd.** bought 01-03-2017 @₹150; FMV on 31-01-2018 was ₹220; sold @₹400 on 10-08-2024.
2. **1,500 shares of Moon Ltd.** bought 01-06-2024 @₹100; sold @₹130 on 01-12-2024.
3. **Dividend of ₹3,00,000** from her equity MF; she paid ₹90,000 interest on a loan taken to buy the MF units.
4. She is also an active F&O trader: net non-speculative F&O **loss of ₹2,00,000** this year (return filed on time).

**Q1.** Compute the capital gain on Sunrise Ltd. and the tax (assume this is her only 112A LTCG).
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- Held 01-03-2017 → 10-08-2024 (>12 months) → **LTCG (Sec 112A)**.
- Grandfathered cost = higher of [actual 2,000×150 = ₹3,00,000] and [lower of (FMV 2,000×220 = ₹4,40,000) and (sale 2,000×400 = ₹8,00,000)] = **₹4,40,000**.
- LTCG = 8,00,000 − 4,40,000 = **₹3,60,000**.
- Exempt ₹1,25,000 → taxable ₹2,35,000 @12.50% = **₹29,375** (+ surcharge/cess).
</details>

**Q2.** Compute the capital gain on Moon Ltd. and the tax.
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- Held 01-06-2024 → 01-12-2024 (~6 months ≤12) → **STCG (Sec 111A)**.
- STCG = (130 − 100) × 1,500 = **₹45,000**.
- Tax @20% = **₹9,000** (+ surcharge/cess). No 80C–80U deduction against this.
</details>

**Q3.** What is Anita's taxable dividend?
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- Resident → interest deduction capped at 20% of dividend = 20% × 3,00,000 = ₹60,000 (less than ₹90,000 paid).
- **Taxable dividend = 3,00,000 − 60,000 = ₹2,40,000**, taxed at slab rates.
</details>

**Q4.** How is the F&O loss of ₹2,00,000 treated this year and going forward?
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- It is a **non-speculative business loss**: in the current year it can be set off against income under **any head except salary** (e.g., against dividend, other sources, capital gains as permitted).
- Any unabsorbed loss is **carried forward up to 8 assessment years**, set off only against **business income**.
- Because she filed her return **by the due date**, the carry-forward right is preserved.

⚠️ Note: a business loss cannot be set off against salary income.
</details>

**Q5.** Suppose instead Moon Ltd. shares were UNLISTED and held the same ~6 months. How does the tax change?
<details><summary>Worked answer</summary>

- Unlisted shares are short-term if held ≤24 months → still STCG, but **Section 111A's 20% does NOT apply** (no listing/STT).
- The ₹45,000 STCG is taxed at her **normal slab rates** instead of 20%.

This shows the listed-vs-unlisted distinction: listed STCG = 20%; unlisted STCG = slab.
</details>
