# Chapter 11: Taxation of Equity Products — 100 MCQ Question Bank

> This bank has three difficulty tiers — 🟢 Tier 1 (Easy: recall of facts/definitions), 🟡 Tier 2 (Medium: applying rules and comparing treatments), and 🔴 Tier 3 (Hard: multi-step numerical and scenario problems). The exam carries **25% negative marking** (a wrong answer costs you one-quarter of a mark), so do not guess blindly — eliminate options first.

## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Equity investment generally produces which two types of income?
A) Interest and rent  B) Capital gains and dividend income  C) Commission and brokerage  D) Salary and bonus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The chapter states that two types of income are earned from equity products: capital gains (selling above cost) and dividend income (a share of company profits). A) interest/rent are debt and property incomes, not equity. C) brokerage and commission are charges/expenses, not income to the investor. D) salary/bonus are employment incomes, unrelated to equity ownership.
</details>

**Q2.** Dividend declared at an Annual General Meeting (AGM) is deemed to be the income of the shareholder in the previous year in which it is:
A) Received  B) Credited to bank  C) Declared  D) Reinvested
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A final dividend declared at the AGM is income of the year it is *declared*; the date the assessee actually receives it is not material. A) and B) describe receipt, which is the rule for *interim* dividends (taxed when unconditionally made available), not final dividends. D) reinvestment is irrelevant to the timing of taxability.
</details>

**Q3.** Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) was abolished by which Finance Act, shifting dividend tax to the shareholder?
A) Finance Act, 2018  B) Finance Act, 2020  C) Finance Act, 2024  D) Finance Act, 2025
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The Finance Act, 2020 abolished DDT with effect from FY 2020-21, so dividends are now taxable in the hands of shareholders. A) Finance Act 2018 made LTCG on listed shares taxable (Sec 112A). C) Finance Act 2024 set the 12.50%/20% rates and the ₹1.25 lakh limit. D) Finance Act 2025 amended ULIP definitions. Only 2020 dealt with DDT.
</details>

**Q4.** Securities Transaction Tax (STT) is levied on the sale/purchase of:
A) All securities including debt securities  B) Securities other than debt securities or debt mutual funds  C) Only unlisted shares  D) Only derivatives
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — STT applies to securities *other than* debt securities or debt mutual funds. A) is wrong because debt securities are specifically excluded. C) is wrong — STT applies mainly to listed/exchange-traded securities, not unlisted shares. D) is too narrow; STT covers equity delivery, equity MF and derivatives, not derivatives alone.
</details>

**Q5.** STT collected during a calendar month must be remitted to the Central Government by which date of the following month?
A) Last day  B) 15th  C) 7th  D) 1st
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — STT collected in a month must be paid to the Central Government by the 7th of the immediately following month. A), B) and D) are incorrect dates not stated in the chapter; the rule is the 7th.
</details>

**Q6.** SEBI turnover charges are levied at the rate of:
A) ₹10 per crore  B) ₹100 per crore  C) 18% of brokerage  D) ₹10 per lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — SEBI charges a turnover fee of ₹10 per crore for regulating markets, levied on both buy and sell sides. B) and D) are wrong amounts. C) 18% is the GST rate on brokerage/transaction/clearing charges, not the SEBI turnover charge.
</details>

**Q7.** GST on brokerage, exchange transaction charges and clearing charges is charged at:
A) 5%  B) 12%  C) 18%  D) 28%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — GST is charged at 18% on brokerage, transaction and clearing charges. A), B) and D) are other GST slabs that do not apply here; the chapter specifies 18%.
</details>

**Q8.** The two stock depositories in India are:
A) BSE and NSE  B) NSDL and CDSL  C) MCX and NCDEX  D) SEBI and RBI
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — NSDL (National Securities Depository Limited) and CDSL (Central Depository Services Limited) are the two depositories that hold securities in electronic form. A) BSE and NSE are stock *exchanges*, not depositories. C) MCX and NCDEX are commodity exchanges. D) SEBI is the regulator and RBI is the central bank — neither is a depository.
</details>

**Q9.** Stamp duty on listed securities has been levied at unified rates across India with effect from:
A) 1 April 2018  B) 1 April 2020  C) 1 October 2004  D) 31 January 2018
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — From 1 April 2020 stamp duty on listed securities is levied at unified rates across India (the same rate applies even to off-market transactions). A), C) and D) are other significant dates in the chapter (Sec 112A came in 2018; STT-acquisition relief applies to shares before 01-10-2004; grandfathering FMV date is 31-01-2018) but not the stamp-duty unification date.
</details>

**Q10.** For listed equity shares, the holding period to qualify as a long-term capital asset is more than:
A) 36 months  B) 24 months  C) 12 months  D) 6 months
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Listed equity shares are short-term if held for not more than 12 months; held beyond 12 months they become long-term. A) and B) apply to other assets (24 months for unlisted shares). D) 6 months is not used for equity.
</details>

**Q11.** For unlisted equity shares, the long-term holding threshold is more than:
A) 12 months  B) 24 months  C) 36 months  D) 48 months
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Unlisted shares are short-term if held for not more than 24 months, and long-term if held for more than 24 months. A) 12 months is the listed-share threshold. C) and D) are not used here.
</details>

**Q12.** The period of holding for securities held in Demat form is determined using which method?
A) LIFO  B) Weighted average  C) FIFO  D) Specific identification
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Section 45(2A) prescribes the First-In-First-Out (FIFO) method: the securities that entered the Demat account first are treated as sold first. A) LIFO (last-in-first-out) is the opposite and is not used. B) weighted average and D) specific identification are not prescribed for Demat holding period.
</details>

**Q13.** FIFO under Section 45(2A) for Demat securities is applied:
A) Across all accounts combined  B) Account-wise  C) Only to physical shares  D) Only on the exchange's records
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Where an investor has more than one security account, FIFO is applied account-wise, because securities in another account cannot be treated as sold when a different account is debited. A) is wrong as accounts are not combined. C) is wrong — FIFO is specifically for Demat (electronic) holdings. D) is incorrect; it is applied per the investor's Demat accounts.
</details>

**Q14.** Long-term capital gains on listed equity shares under Section 112A are exempt up to an aggregate of how much per year?
A) ₹1,00,000  B) ₹1,25,000  C) ₹1,50,000  D) ₹2,50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Under the Finance Act 2024, no tax is charged on Section 112A LTCG up to ₹1,25,000 in a year; the excess is taxed at 12.50%. A) ₹1,00,000 was the old limit before the change. C) ₹1,50,000 is the 80C deduction cap. D) ₹2,50,000 is the basic exemption limit, not the 112A limit.
</details>

**Q15.** The concessional rate of tax on LTCG exceeding ₹1.25 lakh under Section 112A is:
A) 10%  B) 12.50%  C) 15%  D) 20%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh under Section 112A is taxed at the concessional rate of 12.50% (plus surcharge and cess). A) 10% was the earlier rate. C) 15% is a surcharge rate. D) 20% is the STCG rate under Section 111A.
</details>

**Q16.** Short-term capital gains on listed equity shares (STT paid) are taxed under Section 111A at:
A) 10%  B) 12.50%  C) 15%  D) 20%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — STCG on listed equity (with STT paid) is taxed at the concessional rate of 20% under Section 111A. A), B) and C) are not the 111A rate; 12.50% is the LTCG rate and 15% is a surcharge cap.
</details>

**Q17.** Earlier, LTCG on listed equity shares were exempt under which section before being made taxable in 2018?
A) Section 10(38)  B) Section 10(10D)  C) Section 80C  D) Section 54
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — LTCG on listed equity shares was earlier exempt under Section 10(38) until the Finance Act 2018 made it taxable under Section 112A. B) Section 10(10D) deals with life insurance/ULIP proceeds. C) Section 80C is a deduction provision. D) Section 54 deals with reinvestment exemptions on property.
</details>

**Q18.** The grandfathering of equity investments applies to shares acquired on or before:
A) 1 October 2004  B) 31 January 2018  C) 1 April 2020  D) 23 July 2024
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The Finance Act 2018 grandfathered investments made on or before 31-01-2018, since LTCG was tax-free until then. A) 01-10-2004 relates to STT-acquisition relief. C) 01-04-2020 is the stamp-duty/DDT date. D) 23 July 2024 is the date from which the new rates apply.
</details>

**Q19.** For grandfathering of a listed share, the fair market value as on 31-01-2018 is taken as:
A) The lowest price quoted that day  B) The average price that day  C) The highest price quoted on a recognised exchange that day  D) The face value
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — For listed shares, FMV on 31-01-2018 is the highest price quoted on a recognised stock exchange that day (or the last traded day before if there was no trading). A) lowest and B) average are not used. D) face value is unrelated to FMV.
</details>

**Q20.** LTCG on unlisted equity shares is taxed at:
A) 10%  B) 12.50%  C) 20%  D) Slab rates
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — LTCG on unlisted equity shares is taxable at 12.50% (plus surcharge and cess) under Section 112. A) 10% is outdated. C) 20% is the STCG 111A rate (which needs STT/listing — unavailable for unlisted). D) slab rates apply to *short-term* unlisted gains, not long-term.
</details>

**Q21.** Short-term capital gain on unlisted equity shares is taxed at:
A) 12.50%  B) 20%  C) Normal slab rates  D) Exempt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — STCG on unlisted shares is taxed at the normal slab rates applicable to the assessee, because Section 111A (20%) requires the shares to be listed with STT paid. A) and B) are concessional rates that need listing/STT. D) is wrong — the gain is taxable.
</details>

**Q22.** Preference shares carry a preferential right to dividend and to:
A) Voting on every resolution  B) Repayment of capital on winding-up  C) Free bonus shares  D) Conversion into debentures
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Preference share capital carries a preferential right to payment of dividend (fixed amount or rate) and to repayment of capital in case of winding-up. A) preference shares typically do not carry general voting rights. C) and D) are not defining features of preference share capital.
</details>

**Q23.** A share warrant gives the holder:
A) An obligation to buy shares  B) A right to subscribe to equity shares at a pre-determined price on/after a pre-determined time  C) A guaranteed dividend  D) Ownership of the company immediately
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A share warrant is an option giving the holder a *right* (not an obligation) to subscribe to equity shares at a pre-determined strike price on or after a set time. A) is wrong because it is a right, not an obligation. C) warrants do not pay dividends. D) ownership arises only when the warrant is exercised into shares.
</details>

**Q24.** A warrant holder is required to pay upfront at least what percentage of the strike price (per SEBI ICDR Regulation 13)?
A) 10%  B) 25%  C) 50%  D) 100%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Regulation 13 of SEBI (ICDR), 2018 requires the warrant holder to pay at least 25% of the strike price upfront. A), C) and D) are incorrect figures; the minimum upfront is 25%.
</details>

**Q25.** The tenure of share warrants shall not exceed how long from the date of allotment?
A) 12 months  B) 18 months  C) 24 months  D) 36 months
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The tenure of share warrants shall not exceed 18 months from the date of their allotment in the IPO/Rights Issue/FPO. A), C) and D) are incorrect; the cap is 18 months.
</details>

**Q26.** If a warrant holder does not exercise the option within how many months from full payment, the consideration is forfeited?
A) 1 month  B) 2 months  C) 3 months  D) 6 months
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — If the holder does not exercise the warrants within 3 months from the date of full payment, the consideration is forfeited by the issuer. A), B) and D) are incorrect; the period is 3 months.
</details>

**Q27.** The loss arising from forfeiture of premium paid on a share warrant is:
A) A short-term capital loss  B) A long-term capital loss  C) Ignored for tax purposes  D) A business loss
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The loss arising from forfeiture of the warrant premium has no tax treatment and is ignored while computing taxable income. A), B) and D) are wrong because no capital or business loss is recognised on forfeiture.
</details>

**Q28.** An equity-oriented fund (general case) must invest a minimum of what percentage in equity shares of domestic listed companies?
A) 35%  B) 50%  C) 65%  D) 90%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — In the general case, an equity-oriented fund must invest a minimum of 65% of total proceeds in equity shares of domestic companies listed on a recognised exchange. A) 35% relates to hybrid/specified-fund thresholds. B) 50% is not a defined limit. D) 90% applies only to the special fund-of-fund route (≥90% in another fund which itself holds ≥90% equity).
</details>

**Q29.** ELSS funds carry a compulsory lock-in period of:
A) 1 year  B) 3 years  C) 5 years  D) 7 years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Equity Linked Saving Scheme (ELSS) funds have a compulsory 3-year lock-in, the shortest among tax-saving instruments. A), C) and D) are incorrect; ELSS lock-in is 3 years.
</details>

**Q30.** Investment in ELSS qualifies for deduction under Section 80C up to:
A) ₹1,00,000  B) ₹1,25,000  C) ₹1,50,000  D) ₹2,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Section 80C allows a deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh for ELSS investment, available if the assessee opts for the old tax regime. A) ₹1,00,000 was an old 112A limit. B) ₹1,25,000 is the current 112A LTCG exemption. D) ₹2,00,000 is not the 80C limit.
</details>

**Q31.** A Systematic Transfer Plan (STP) can transfer money between:
A) Two banks  B) Two mutual fund schemes of the same AMC  C) Any two AMCs  D) Equity and debt of different fund houses
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — An STP transfers a fixed amount between two mutual fund schemes of the same Asset Management Company (AMC). A) banks are not involved. C) and D) are wrong because STP works only within the same AMC, not across fund houses.
</details>

**Q32.** Units of an equity-oriented fund are treated as long-term capital assets if held for more than:
A) 12 months  B) 24 months  C) 36 months  D) 48 months
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Units of equity-oriented funds become long-term after a holding period of more than 12 months, the same as listed equity shares. B) 24 months applies to unlisted shares. C) and D) are not used for equity MF units.
</details>

**Q33.** F&O (derivatives) income, when traded as business, is generally treated as:
A) Speculative business income  B) Non-speculative business income  C) Capital gains  D) Salary income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 43(5) excludes exchange-traded derivatives from "speculative", so F&O income/loss is treated as normal (non-speculative) business income, even without delivery. A) is wrong because of the 43(5) exclusion. C) is wrong — F&O is business income, not capital gains. D) salary is unrelated.
</details>

**Q34.** Under bonus stripping (Section 94(8)), the ignored loss on the original units becomes the:
A) Cost of acquisition of the bonus units  B) A carried-forward capital loss  C) A deduction under 80C  D) Dividend income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The loss ignored under Section 94(8) is deemed to be the cost of acquisition of the bonus securities/units held on the date of transfer of the originals. B) is wrong — the loss is not carried forward; it shifts to bonus cost. C) and D) are unrelated tax items.
</details>

**Q35.** Section 87A rebate is:
A) Allowed against LTCG covered under Section 112A  B) Not allowed against LTCG covered under Section 112A  C) Allowed only against dividend income  D) Allowed against STCG under 111A only
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The Section 87A rebate is *not* available against tax payable on LTCG covered under Section 112A; it is allowed only on the tax on the remaining total income. A) directly contradicts this rule. C) and D) misstate the scope — the rule specifically denies the rebate on 112A LTCG.
</details>
