# Chapter 5: Retirement Products — Short Notes

## 🎯 What you'll learn

# PART A — Accumulation Products (5.1)

## 5.1.1 Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)

**Simple idea:** EPF (also called PF) is a **compulsory** savings-cum-retirement scheme for salaried employees of eligible organisations. Money goes in every month from both you and your employer, earns interest, and is meant to be your retirement corpus.

**Jargon:**
- **Basic pay** = the core part of salary (before allowances).
- **Dearness Allowance (DA)** = a cost-of-living top-up added to salary.
- **EPFO** = Employees' Provident Fund Organisation, the body that runs EPF.

### Monthly contribution — the maths
- **Employee:** contributes **12% of (Basic + DA)**. The full 12% goes into the EPF account.
- **Employer:** also contributes **12% of (Basic + DA)**, but it is *split*:

| Where the employer's 12% goes | Rate |
|---|---|
| Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) | **3.67%** |
| Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS)* | **8.33%** |
| Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance (EDLIS) | **0.5%** |
| EPF Admin Charges | **0.50%** |

\*EPS is **subject to a ceiling of Rs. 15,000 monthly salary** (so EPS contribution is capped at 8.33% × 15,000 = Rs. 1,250).

**Worked example:** Basic + DA = Rs. 30,000.
- Employee → 12% × 30,000 = **Rs. 3,600** (all to EPF).
- Employer → EPS = 8.33% × 15,000 (capped) = Rs. 1,250; the rest (3,600 − 1,250 = **Rs. 2,350**) goes to EPF.

**When contribution can be 10% instead of 12%:**
- Company with **fewer than 20 employees**
- Company whose **losses exceed its entire net worth**
- Company in the **beedi, jute, brick, guar gum or coir** industry

**Women employees:** New women employees may contribute **8% instead of 12%** for the **first three years** (Union Budget 2018-19) — to give higher take-home pay and encourage hiring. *But the employer still contributes 12%.* An employee can also contribute *more* than 12% — that's **VPF** (next section).

💡 **Tip:** EPF stays active as long as you are salaried. When you switch jobs, **update your EPF/UAN** with the new employer to keep contributions flowing.

### Interest rate
- Decided by the **Central Board of Trustees (CBT)** with the Ministry of Finance; valid for a financial year (**1 Apr – 31 Mar**).
- Interest is **calculated monthly** but **credited on 31st March** each year.
- ⚠️ From **1 April 2021**, if your **own** EPF + VPF contribution exceeds **Rs. 2.5 lakh** in a year, the interest on the *excess* is **taxable**.
- **Dormant account:** no contributions for **3 consecutive years** → account becomes inactive, but interest still accrues until retirement (this interest is taxable at your slab). Once **inoperative** (no contribution for 3 years after retirement / migration abroad / death), **no interest accrues**. After **7 years** inoperative, unclaimed money goes to the **Senior Citizens' Welfare Fund**; claimable within **25 years**.
- **EPS portion does NOT earn interest**; pension under EPS is payable only **after age 58**.

### Withdrawal
**Full withdrawal allowed:** (a) on retirement, (b) unemployed for more than 2 months.
- After **1 month** unemployed → withdraw **75%**; remaining **25%** only after 2 months. If re-employed after 1 month, the 25% transfers to the new EPF account.
- The 2-month wait does **not** apply to **women quitting for marriage**.

**Partial withdrawal (Table 5.1) — key limits:**

| Purpose | Limit | Service required |
|---|---|---|
| Medical (self/spouse/children/parents) | 6 months Basic+DA *or* total employee share + interest, whichever **lower** | No minimum |
| Marriage (self/son/daughter/sibling) | Up to **50%** of employee's share + interest | **7 years** |
| Education (self/child, post-matric) | Up to **50%** of employee's share + interest | **7 years** |
| Buy land / build house | Land: 24 months Basic+DA; House: 36 months Basic+DA (least of limit / total share / cost) | **5 years** |
| Home loan repayment | Up to 36 months Basic+DA (least of limit/corpus/outstanding); balance must exceed Rs. 20,000 | **10 years** |
| House renovation | Up to 12 months Basic+DA (least of limit/share/cost) | **5 years** |
| Partial withdrawal before retirement | Up to **90%** of accumulated balance | At **age 54**, within 1 year of retirement |

### Tax on EPF (Section 80C + EEE-ish)
- Employee contributions qualify for deduction under **Section 80C**.
- Interest on own contribution **above Rs. 2.5 lakh/year** (including VPF) is **taxable**.
- **Employer contribution:** if aggregate employer contribution to **EPF + NPS + Superannuation exceeds Rs. 7.5 lakh** in a year, the excess (and its interest) is a **perquisite** taxed in the employee's hands.

**Tax on withdrawals:**
- **Before 5 years of service:** taxable — *except* if employment ended due to ill-health/employer's business closing (beyond employee's control), or if the balance is **transferred** to the new employer.
- **After 5 years:** **tax-free**.
- Before 5 years: if amount **< Rs. 50,000**, taxed only if in taxable bracket; if **> Rs. 50,000**, **TDS** applies — **10%** with PAN, **maximum marginal rate** without PAN; **no TDS if Form 15G/15H** submitted.

⚠️ **Trap:** Always **transfer** PF when switching jobs (using UAN) — withdrawing before 5 years can make it taxable and you lose compounding.

## 5.1.2 Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF)

**Idea:** EPF members can contribute **more than the mandatory 12%** — up to **100% of Basic + DA** — into the same EPF account. This extra is the **VPF**.
- **No matching employer contribution** for VPF.
- Invested like EPF (mostly **government securities/debt**); return declared yearly by the government.
- Open-ended; contribute till retirement; withdrawals follow EPF rules.
- **Tax:** contributions/interest/withdrawals exempt up to a limit — but if **EPF + VPF own contribution > Rs. 2.5 lakh/year**, interest on the excess is **taxable**.

💡 **Tip:** VPF is a great tax-efficient booster for someone 50+ wanting to maximise safe retirement savings.

## 5.1.3 Public Provident Fund (PPF)

**Idea:** PPF is a **voluntary**, long-term, government-backed savings product opened at banks/post offices.

**Eligibility:** Only **Indian residents**. NRIs cannot open one. Existing holders who become NRI can keep contributing (post 30 Sep 2024) but cannot extend tenure — and earn **no interest after 30 Sep 2024**. Parents/guardians can open for minors. **No joint or multiple accounts.**

**Key features:**
- **Lock-in: 15 years** (from end of FY of opening). Extendable in **5-year blocks**.
- **Min Rs. 500/year; Max Rs. 1.5 lakh/year** (self + minor's account combined ≤ Rs. 1.5 lakh).
- **Tax: EEE** (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) → contribution deductible under **80C**, interest exempt, maturity exempt.
- **Loan against PPF:** from start of 3rd FY to end of 6th FY; up to **25%** of balance at end of 2nd FY preceding the loan year.

**Interest:** floating, notified **every quarter** by the Central Government. Minors' accounts earn POSA (Post Office Savings Account) rate till 18, then standard PPF rate.

**Partial withdrawals:** allowed after **5 financial years** from end of FY of opening; **one per year**; max = **lower of** 50% of balance at end of preceding year *or* 50% of balance at end of 4th year preceding the withdrawal year. (Use **Form-2**.)

**Extension at maturity (in 5-year blocks):**
- **With contribution (Form-4, within 1 year of maturity):** can withdraw up to **60%** of balance over the block, one withdrawal per year.
- **Without contribution (default):** one withdrawal/year, **any amount up to total balance**.
- Once chosen, the option **can't be switched**; wrong choice → no interest and no 80C deduction on that money.

**Premature closure** (after 5 FYs): allowed for (1) life-threatening illness of self/spouse/parents/children, (2) higher education of self/children, (3) change in residency status.

## 5.1.4 Gratuity

**Idea:** **Gratuity** is a thank-you payment from employer to employee for service. Usually paid at retirement.

- **Eligibility:** minimum **5 years** of continuous service. Exception: paid earlier on **death or disability** from accident/disease.
- **Covered under the Act** if the organisation employs **at least 10 persons** on any single day in the preceding 12 months. Once covered, **always covered** (even if staff drops below 10).

### Formulas (the heart of this section)

**(a) Employees covered under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972:**
> **Gratuity = (15 × last drawn salary × tenure) ÷ 26**
- **15** = 15 days' salary per completed year; **26** = working days in a month (the law's convention).
- **Tenure:** count an extra year if the last part-year is **more than 6 months**.
- **Last drawn salary** = Basic + DA + sales commission.

**Worked example:** Basic = Rs. 60,000; service = 20 years 7 months → counts as **21** (7 > 6).
Gratuity = (15 × 60,000 × 21) ÷ 26 = **Rs. 7.26 lakh**.
*(If it were 20 years 5 months → 20 years.)*

**(b) Employees NOT covered under the Act:**
> **Gratuity = (15 × last drawn salary × tenure) ÷ 30**
- Uses **30 days** and counts only **fully completed years** (drop the extra months).

**Worked example:** Same person not covered: tenure = **20** (drop the 7 months).
Gratuity = (15 × 60,000 × 20) ÷ 30 = **Rs. 6 lakh**.

**(c) Central Government employees:** one-fourth of a month's (Basic + DA) per completed **6-month** period of qualifying service. For **33+ years** of service → **16½ times** Basic+DA, **max Rs. 20 lakh**.

**Death gratuity rates (Table 5.2):**
| Qualifying service | Rate |
|---|---|
| Less than 1 year | 2 × basic pay |
| 1 to <5 years | 6 × basic pay |
| 5 to <11 years | 12 × basic pay |
| 11 to <20 years | 20 × basic pay |
| 20+ years | ½ basic for each 6-month period, **max 33 ×** basic |

**Taxability:**
- **Government employees:** fully **exempt**.
- **Private, covered under Act:** exempt up to **least of** — Rs. 20 lakh, *or* (last salary × 15/26 × completed years), *or* actual received.
- **Private, not covered:** exempt up to **least of** — Rs. 20 lakh, *or* (average salary × ½ × years), *or* actual. (Average = last **10 months'** salary.)

## 5.1.5 Superannuation Benefit (the product view)

Mandatory benefits are often **not enough**, so employers add a **superannuation** plan by contributing to a superannuation fund. Trustees administer it; the scheme is approved by the **Commissioner of Income Tax**.

**Two ways to run a group superannuation scheme:** (1) a **trust fund** with appointed fund managers, or (2) **investment in a superannuation scheme from a life insurer**.

- On retirement, the employee may take **one-third** as **commutation** (a lump sum); the **balance buys an annuity**.
- **Commutation** = taking part of the annuity corpus as a lump sum.
- IT rules cap the employer's combined PF + superannuation contribution at **27% of the employee's earnings**.
- Aggregated with EPF and NPS, employer contributions over **Rs. 7.5 lakh/year** are treated as a **perquisite**. Payment received **on death** from an approved fund is **exempt**.

## 5.1.6 National Pension System (NPS)

**Idea:** A **Central Government social-security** pension scheme. Open to public and private employees (not armed forces) and **all citizens voluntarily**. You contribute during working years, then at retirement use part of the corpus to buy an **annuity** (regular pension) and withdraw the rest.

**Key truth:** **No guaranteed return** — you earn **market returns**; the pension depends on the corpus built.

**Three models:**
- **Government Sector** — Central (except armed forces)/State employees; mandatory on joining.
- **All Citizens model** — voluntary, ages **18–70**.
- **Corporate model** — companies offering NPS to employees.

### Tier I vs Tier II (Table 5.3)
| Particulars | Tier I | Tier II |
|---|---|---|
| Status | Default / **mandatory** | **Voluntary** |
| Withdrawals | Conditional & restricted | Permitted / unrestricted |
| Tax deduction (employee) | Up to **Rs. 2 lakh** (Rs. 1.5 L under 80C + **Rs. 50,000 under 80CCD(1B)**) | Govt employees: 80C up to Rs. 1.5 L; others: **no** exemption |
| Tax deduction (employer) | Up to **14% of Basic+DA** under **80CCD(2)** (treated as perquisite, then deductible) | Not applicable |
| Minimum contribution | Rs. 500/contribution; min Rs. 1,000/year | Rs. 1,000 to activate; Rs. 250 thereafter |
| Maximum | No limit | No limit |

- Tier I is **mandatory** for anyone in NPS. Central Govt employees must contribute **10% of Basic+DA**.

### Investment choices (four asset classes, Table 5.4)
- **E (Equity):** equity market instruments.
- **C (Corporate Debt):** PSU/PFI/infra bonds & money market.
- **G (Government Securities):** Central/State govt securities & money market.
- **A (Alternative Investments):** CMBS, REITs, AIFs, InvITs etc.

**Modes:**
- **Active Choice** — you pick the ratio. Caps: **E up to 75%**, C up to 100%, G up to 100%, **A up to 5%** (A only in Tier I, Active only).
- **Auto Choice** — life-cycle approach; equity-heavy when young, falls with age. Three funds:
  - **Moderate LC (default):** equity capped **50%**.
  - **Conservative LC:** equity capped **25%**.
  - **Aggressive LC:** equity up to **75%**.

These are the **Common Schemes**. New: **Multiple Scheme Framework (MSF)** (w.e.f. 1 Oct 2025) for Non-Government subscribers — customised schemes, each with Moderate-Risk and High-Risk (equity up to 100%) variants, optional Low-Risk; **minimum vesting period 15 years**.

**Changing scheme/fund manager:** allowed **once a financial year** (both Tiers). Investment choice/asset allocation can be changed **4 times a year** (All Citizen & Corporate models).

### Withdrawal & exit rules
- **Partial withdrawal:** after **3 years** investing, up to **25% of the subscriber's own contribution** (not employer's, not returns), for specified needs (wedding, higher studies, house, medical). Max **3 times** in entire tenure. Tier I only.
- **Exit at retirement (age 60/superannuation):** use **min 40%** of corpus to buy an annuity; **up to 60% withdrawn as lump sum**. If corpus **≤ Rs. 5 lakh** → can withdraw **entire** amount.
- **Exit before retirement:** must annuitise **at least 80%**; **20% lump sum**. If corpus **≤ Rs. 2.5 lakh** → withdraw entire amount.
- **Death:** entire corpus to nominee as lump sum (or buy annuity if they wish).
- **Continuation/deferment:** can continue/defer lump sum or annuity purchase up to **age 75**.

### Taxation — "EEE-partly" (E, E, partly Exempt–partly Taxable)
- **First E:** contributions deductible under **80CCD(1)** up to Rs. 1.5 lakh (within 80C/80CCC/80CCD(1) combined; limited to 10% of Basic+DA for salaried, 20% of gross income for non-salaried). **80CCD(2)** = employer's contribution (separate from 80C; up to 14% Basic+DA; not for self-employed). **80CCD(1B)** = extra **Rs. 50,000** exclusive NPS benefit. Total subscriber deduction up to **Rs. 2 lakh**.
- **Second E:** no tax on returns earned.
- **Partly E / partly Taxable:** at exit, **up to 60% lump sum is tax-free** (Sec **10(12A)**); the **40% annuity is taxable** as income when received. **Partial withdrawals tax-exempt** up to 25% of own contribution (Sec **10(12B)**).

## 5.1.7 Unified Pension Scheme (UPS) under NPS
**Optional** scheme for **Central Government employees** (w.e.f. **1 April 2025**) giving an **assured payout**.
- **Eligibility:** existing/new Central Govt employees under NPS; those retired on/before 31 Mar 2025 (min 10 years service); legally wedded spouse of a deceased eligible subscriber. Option is **final and irrevocable**.
- **Contribution:** employee **10% of (Basic+DA)** + matching employer; Central Govt also adds ~**8.5%** to a Pool Corpus.
- **Payout:** **50% of last 12-month average Basic pay** after min **25 years** service (proportionate for 10–25 years); **minimum guaranteed Rs. 10,000/month** after 10+ years; lump sum = **1/10th of last Basic+DA per completed 6-month period**; spouse gets **60%** family payout. Dearness Relief applies. No payout if removed/dismissed/resigned.

## 5.1.8 NPS Vatsalya
A saving-cum-pension scheme (Budget 2024) for **minors (below 18)**. A parent opens & operates it as guardian; runs under **NPS Tier I** rules; at 18 it becomes a normal NPS account.

## 5.1.9 Atal Pension Yojana (APY)
- Introduced **2015–16** for the **unorganised sector**; regulated by **PFRDA**; an extension of NPS (replaced Swavalamban).
- Open to citizens aged **18–40** who are **not** members of any statutory social security scheme and are **not income-tax payers** (effective 1 Oct 2022). Focuses on unorganised-sector citizens.

## Retirement plans from MFs & insurers
- **Insurers:** deferred **pension plans** (premium deductible under 80C) and **annuity products**; options include lifelong pension and spouse continuation; offered as **ULIPs and traditional** plans.
- **Mutual funds:** retirement schemes — mostly **hybrid** (equity + debt), 3–4 variants; some give insurance cover; **lock-in of at least 5 years or till retirement, whichever is earlier**.

# PART C — Distribution Products (5.3)

These generate **periodic income** with **adequate returns** and **lower risk to income/principal**. **Capital protection** matters most here.

## 5.3.1 Annuity from insurance companies
**Definition:** An **annuity** is a fixed stream of payments for life or a fixed period. The **annuitant** (buyer) pays the **annuity provider** (insurer), who pays regular amounts. Main goal: **counter longevity risk** (and partly inflation).

**Two basic types:**
- **Deferred annuity:** payments begin **after** a specified period (>12 months later); bought during working years.
- **Immediate annuity:** payments begin **soon** (within 12 months); usually a **single premium**.

**Purchase modes:** (a) **single premium** (common for immediate), (b) **regular premiums** (level or flexible/variable).
- The **annuity rate** is fixed at purchase and **guaranteed for the whole period**; reviewed periodically with **IRDAI** approval.

**Payout options (chosen at vesting age):**
| Option | Feature |
|---|---|
| Lifetime **without** return of purchase price | Pays till death; principal kept by insurer; **highest** annuity |
| Lifetime **with** return of purchase price | Principal returned to nominee on death; **lowest** annuity |
| **Guaranteed for a certain period** (e.g. 10/20 yrs) | Paid for that period regardless of survival, then till death |
| **Joint annuity** | Spouse continues to receive after annuitant's death |
| Others | inflation-adjusted, fixed-increasing annuities |

**Tax:** Annuities are **NOT tax-exempt** — payments are **income** taxed in the annuitant's hands. The **premium** qualifies under **80C** (within overall limit).

## 5.3.2 Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) from mutual funds
**Idea:** A **tax-efficient** way to draw regular income from a mutual fund — withdraw a **fixed amount** or only the **appreciation**, at a chosen frequency (monthly/quarterly/etc.).

⚠️ **Key difference from a bank FD:** With an FD, the corpus stays intact when you take interest. With an SWP, **units are sold**, so the fund value **reduces** by the units withdrawn.

**Worked example (NAV mechanics):** 8,000 units, withdraw Rs. 5,000/month.
- NAV = Rs. 10 → sell 5,000/10 = **500 units** → 7,500 left.
- Next month NAV = Rs. 20 → sell 5,000/20 = **250 units** → 7,250 left.
- *Higher NAV → fewer units sold; lower NAV → more units sold.*

**Taxation in SWP** (gain portion only):
- **Debt funds (post 1 Apr 2023):** gain taxed at **income slab**, any holding period.
- **Equity funds:** holding **≤ 12 months → 20%**; **> 12 months → 12.5%** (LTCG exemption of **Rs. 1.25 lakh/year** across all equity).

## 5.3.3 Laddering of Bonds / Fixed Deposits
**Idea:** Instead of all maturities in one year, buy bonds/FDs **maturing at staggered dates**.
**Two goals:** (1) **manage interest-rate risk** (avoid locking everything at one rate; reinvest maturing pieces at prevailing rates), (2) **manage cash flow** (predictable income).

**Bond example:** Mr. B splits USD 500,000 into 5 × USD 100,000 maturing in 1–5 years. Each year one matures and is reinvested at the **longest** end (5 years out), keeping the ladder intact.

**FD example:** Mr. C splits Rs. 10 lakh into 5 × Rs. 2 lakh FDs (1–5 years). On maturity each is reinvested into a new 5-year FD → an income loop with annual liquidity. Benefits: less interest-rate impact, funds stay secure/available; can use different banks to diversify.

## 5.3.4 Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme (SCSS)
- **Eligibility:** resident individual **60+**; or **55–60** if retired under superannuation/VRS (open account within **1 month** of receiving benefits); **Defence personnel (excl. civilian) at 50**. **No HUF/NRI/PIO.** Held singly or jointly with spouse (age limit applies to first holder).
- **Deposit:** lump sum, min **Rs. 1,000** (multiples of 1,000), **max Rs. 30 lakh**. Cash only if **< Rs. 1 lakh**; else cheque/DD.
- **Maturity: 5 years**, extendable **once by 3 years** (request within 1 year of maturity).
- **Tax:** investment qualifies under **80C up to Rs. 1.5 lakh**; **interest fully taxable**; **TDS if interest > Rs. 50,000/year**. Interest credited **quarterly**, rate reviewed quarterly by Ministry of Finance.
- **Premature closure penalty:** **1.5%** if exit after 1 but before 2 years; **1%** if between 2 and <5 years. On death, proceeds go to nominee/legal heir.

## 5.3.5 Pradhan Mantri Vaya Vandana Yojana (PMVVY)
- Launched **2017**, run by **LIC**; a **10-year** scheme for citizens **60+**.
- **Pension:** min **Rs. 1,000/month** (purchase price Rs. 1.5 lakh) to max **Rs. 10,000/month** = Rs. 1,20,000/year (purchase price Rs. 15 lakh). Purchase price slightly lower for quarterly/half-yearly/annual modes.
- **Investment returned on maturity.** Ceiling applies to the whole family. **98%** of purchase price returned if surrendered for critical healthcare. After **3 years**, loan up to **75%** of purchase price.
- **Modified-2020** version; sale closed after **31 March 2023** — no new investments now. **Pension income is taxable.**

## 5.3.6 Post Office Monthly Income Scheme (POMIS)
- Government scheme paying **monthly interest**; rates announced **quarterly**.
- **Open by:** single adult; 2–3 adults jointly; guardian for minor/unsound mind; minor **above 10** in own name.
- **Deposit:** min **Rs. 1,000** (multiples of 1,000); max **Rs. 9 lakh single / Rs. 15 lakh joint** (totals across accounts).
- **Interest:** paid monthly; unclaimed interest earns nothing extra; **taxable** in depositor's hands.
- **Premature closure:** none before 1 year; **2% penalty** if closed 1–3 years; **1% penalty** if 3–5 years. **Matures at 5 years.**

## 5.3.7 Reverse Mortgage
**Idea (opposite of a normal mortgage):** In a normal mortgage you borrow a lump sum and repay via EMIs. In a **reverse mortgage**, you **pledge your owned home** to a bank/HFC and **receive periodic income** — turning home equity into retirement cash.

**Key features:**
- **Eligibility:** Indian citizen **60+**; couples as joint borrowers (one ≥60, other ≥55); must **own** a residential property in India with clear title, **free of encumbrances**, **residual life ≥ 20 years**, used as **primary residence**. **No commercial property.**
- Loan amount depends on **age, appraised value, interest rates**.
- **Max monthly payment Rs. 50,000**; max lump sum up to **50%** of eligible loan, capped at **Rs. 15 lakh** (for medical of self/spouse/dependants).
- **Tax:** all RML receipts **exempt under Section 10(43)**.
- **Tenure: max 20 years.** Prepay anytime without penalty. Borrower **remains owner**; need not service the loan while alive and living there. Interest accrues until repayment.
- **Becomes due** only when the **last surviving borrower dies or permanently moves out**; settled from sale proceeds; surplus to heirs (who may instead repay from other funds).
- **RMLEA (Reverse Mortgage Loan Enabled Annuity):** uses the loan to buy a **lifetime annuity** from an insurer; choose with/without return of purchase price; spouse cover available; annuity **exempt from tax**. NHB sets the **loan-to-value (LTV)** ratio.

## 📌 One-Page Recap
- **EPF:** 12% employee + 12% employer (employer split: 3.67% EPF, 8.33% EPS capped at Rs.15k salary, 0.5% EDLIS, 0.5% admin). 80C; tax-free after 5 yrs; interest on own contribution >Rs.2.5 lakh taxable.
- **VPF:** extra contribution (up to 100% Basic+DA), no employer match, EPF rules.
- **PPF:** voluntary, **15-yr** lock-in, Rs.500–1.5 lakh/year, **EEE**, residents only.
- **Gratuity:** 5-yr minimum; covered → ×15/26; not covered → ×15/30; govt fully exempt.
- **Superannuation:** employer pension; 1/3 commutation + annuity; employer cap 27%.
- **NPS:** market-linked; Tier I (locked) vs II (free); exit 60 → min 40% annuity, up to 60% lump sum; EEE-partly; deductions 80CCD(1)/(1B)/(2), up to Rs.2 lakh.
- **UPS:** assured 50% of avg Basic after 25 yrs (Central Govt, 1 Apr 2025).
- **Distribution:** Annuity (taxable income, premium 80C), SWP (sells units; equity LTCG 12.5%), Laddering (stagger maturities), SCSS (60+, 5 yrs, max Rs.30 lakh, interest taxable), PMVVY (LIC, closed since Mar 2023), POMIS (monthly, max Rs.9/15 lakh), Reverse Mortgage (home → income, tax-free, max 20 yrs).
