## 1. What is Non-Life Insurance?

**Non-life (general) insurance** covers the **loss or destruction of assets** and protects against **unexpected large expenses** that would drain your income. (Life insurance covers a *life*; non-life covers *everything else* — health, car, home, travel, liability, etc.)

### 1.1 Elements of Non-Life Products
| Element | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Sum insured** | The insurer's **maximum liability** for claims in the policy period. Insurer may set min/max. |
| **Term** | Usually **1 year**. For health/two-wheeler it can be **2 or 3 years**. |
| **Premium** | Depends on sum insured + assessed risk (age, gender, health for medical; engine CC, place of registration, vehicle age for motor). Paid at policy inception. |
| **Deductible** | The portion of a claim the insured pays first. |
| **Restore / Recharge** | Two health benefits that refill the sum insured (see below). |
| **No claim bonus (NCB)** | Lower premium next year (or extra cover) for each claim-free year. |

> 💡 **Restore vs Recharge (health):**
> - **Restore** = sum insured is brought back to 100% if it is **partially OR fully exhausted**.
> - **Recharge** = sum insured is refilled to 100% **only when it gets reduced due to a claim**.

## 2. Types of Non-Life Insurance Products

### a. Property Insurance
Protects the **structure and contents** of a building against natural and man-made disasters (fire, theft).
- **Not covered:** wilful destruction, normal wear and tear.
- **Valuables** (jewellery, art, antiques) need an **add-on or separate policy**.
- Often taken **with a home loan** (lenders insist on it).

### b. Health Insurance
Reimburses **medical expenses** for the policyholder and covered family members, up to the sum insured.
- Covers **hospitalisation** or **domiciliary treatment** (treated at home when the patient can't be moved).
- **In-patient** = admitted, requiring at least overnight / **24 hours** stay.
- **Day-care treatment** now covered for some procedures (technology lets the patient leave the same day).
- Specified expenses (consultation, medicines, room cost) — **sub-limits** may apply to each head.
- **Pre-existing illnesses** may be excluded for a fixed period at first purchase or after a lapse.
- **Cashless facility:** bills settled directly with the hospital up to the approved sum.
- **Family floater:** one policy covering multiple family members up to the sum insured.
- **Portability:** if you switch insurers, the **no-claim bonus and time-bound exclusion credits** carry over — but only if the **previous policy was maintained without a break**.

### c. Motor Insurance
Indemnifies the insured for accidents arising from the vehicle's use anywhere in India — covering **(i) death/bodily injury to any person** and **(ii) damage to others' property**.
- **Third-party liability insurance is COMPULSORY** — no vehicle may be used in a public place without it. (Own-damage/theft cover is optional.)

**Lesser-known car insurance facts:**
- **No Claim Bonus (NCB):** cuts next year's **own-damage** premium for claim-free years — up to **50%** after **5 claim-free years**.
- NCB belongs to the **person, not the car** — sell your car and the new owner gets no NCB; you can **transfer your NCB to your new car**.
- The own-damage premium **varies by insurer** — online comparison can save money.
- A new insurer gives the NCB if you **shift at renewal**.

### d. Personal Accident Insurance
Pays if the insured suffers **bodily injury solely and directly from an accident** caused by **external, violent and visible means**. Covers:
- **Permanent total disablement**
- **Permanent partial disablement**
- **Temporary total disablement**

> ⚠️ Calculating permanent total disability cover (like life cover) is **often overlooked**, so this cover is frequently **inadequate**.

### e. Critical Illness Insurance
Pays a **lump sum** if the insured contracts a specified disease (cancer, heart attack, stroke, kidney failure, multiple sclerosis…).
- **No payment on death** (unlike life insurance).
- Subject to a **minimum survival period of 30 days** after diagnosis.
- The lump sum can be used **however the claimant wishes** (generating income, repaying a mortgage).
- Available with life policies or as standalone.

> 💡 People wrongly treat critical illness as a small top-up. It should really be sized like **life insurance** (income-replacement). Large covers usually come only as a **standalone policy** or as an **accelerated sum insured rider** on a life policy — not as a health-policy rider.

### f. Overseas Travel Insurance
Provides medical, financial and other assistance during **international travel** — medical help, baggage delay, accident, extra covers. **Reimbursement up to the maximum** in the policy. May be **mandatory** for some countries.

### g. Liability Insurance
Indemnifies against **damages payable under law** for personal injury to third parties or damage to their property. Liability can arise under **common law (negligence)** or **statutory law on a no-fault basis** (even without negligence).
- Most common example: **professional indemnity** plans (Doctors, Lawyers, Investment Advisers).
- Covers legal defence costs and damages.
- **Does NOT cover** intentional damage or damages from criminal activities.

### h. Fidelity Insurance
Covers losses an **employer** suffers from an **employee's forgery, fraud or dishonesty** (money or goods). Taken where employees handle **large sums of cash or valuables**.

### i. Directors and Officers (D&O) Liability Insurance
Insures directors/managers/supervisors against **personal liability and defence costs** from claims alleging a **wrongful act** in their duties. Triggered by investigations, shareholder claims, regulatory/compliance issues, employment-practice violations, discrimination allegations.

### j. Keyman Insurance
A **life** insurance policy a **company buys on a key executive's life**. The **company** is the beneficiary and pays the premiums (protecting the firm against the loss of that person).

## 3. Multiple Insurance Policies — Benefits & Limitations

- **Multiple indemnity policies (e.g., health) make little sense** — total claim cannot exceed the combined sum insured, and you must deal with multiple insurers at claim time.
- In health insurance, **premium per extra lakh of cover drops sharply** as cover rises — a Rs. 100-lakh policy may cost only a few thousand rupees more than a Rs. 50-lakh one.
- Sometimes multiple policies are **unavoidable** (employer cover + your own). Then **contribution does NOT apply** unless the chosen policy's sum insured is **less than the claim**.
- **Choose the employer policy first.** If the claim is within that sum insured → no contribution. If it **exceeds** it → use another policy, and contribution applies.
- A common strategy: a **base policy (Rs. 5 lakh) + super top-up (Rs. 10 lakh) with a Rs. 5 lakh deductible** → total Rs. 15 lakh cover at an economical premium (super top-up premium is low).
  - A single Rs. 15-lakh policy is **only slightly more expensive** (the difference is the admin simplicity of one policy).
- **Multiple defined-benefit policies (life, critical illness, accidental) ALL pay** — no limit on how many you hold.

### 3.1 Top-up vs Super Top-up (key difference)
Both pay claims **above a threshold (deductible)**. The difference is **how the threshold applies**:
- **Top-up:** threshold applies to **EACH individual claim**.
- **Super Top-up:** threshold applies to the **TOTAL of all claims** in the year (aggregate).

> 💡 This is why a Super Top-up is more generous — you only cross the deductible **once per year**, not once per claim.

#### 📖 Worked example
Setup: Base health plan **Rs. 5 lakh**; a **Top-up** (Case 1) or **Super Top-up** (Case 2) of **Rs. 10 lakh** above a **Rs. 5 lakh threshold**.

**Single claim of Rs. 8 lakh** (both cases the same):
- Rs. 5 lakh from Base plan (exhausted).
- Rs. 3 lakh from the Top-up/Super Top-up after the Rs. 5 lakh threshold; balance limit left = Rs. 7 lakh.

**Multiple claims — where they differ:**
| Claim | Top-up (threshold per claim) | Super Top-up (threshold on aggregate) |
|-------|------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| 1st: Rs. 3 lakh | Rs. 3 lakh from Base; Base left Rs. 2 lakh | Rs. 3 lakh from Base; Base left Rs. 2 lakh; aggregate threshold applied = Rs. 3 lakh |
| 2nd: Rs. 6 lakh | Rs. 2 lakh from Base (exhausted); Rs. 1 lakh from Top-up after Rs. 5 lakh threshold; Top-up left Rs. 9 lakh | Rs. 2 lakh from Base (exhausted); balance threshold Rs. 2 lakh applied; Rs. 4 lakh from Super Top-up; Super Top-up left Rs. 6 lakh; aggregate threshold now Rs. 5 lakh |
| 3rd: Rs. 2 lakh | Claim < Rs. 5 lakh threshold → **nothing** payable from Top-up | Threshold (Rs. 5 lakh) already met → **Rs. 2 lakh** from Super Top-up; left Rs. 4 lakh |
| 4th: Rs. 4 lakh | Claim < Rs. 5 lakh threshold → **nothing** payable from Top-up | Threshold already met → **Rs. 4 lakh** from Super Top-up; left Nil |
| **Total Rs. 15 lakh** | Base Rs. 5 lakh + Top-up Rs. 1 lakh = **Rs. 6 lakh paid** | Base Rs. 5 lakh + Super Top-up Rs. 10 lakh = **Rs. 15 lakh paid** |

> ⚠️ **The big lesson:** With a **Top-up**, individual claims below the threshold pay nothing — so on Rs. 15 lakh of claims only **Rs. 6 lakh** was paid. With a **Super Top-up**, the threshold is crossed once on the aggregate, so the **full Rs. 15 lakh** was paid.

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep the Base plan and Top-up/Super Top-up with the **same company** to simplify claims. For super top-up, inform the insurer even of claims that aren't payable by it, so it can **mark the threshold levels used**.

## 4. Comparison Between Insurance Policies

### 4.1 Health Insurance vs Critical Illness
| Parameter | Health Insurance | Critical Illness |
|-----------|------------------|------------------|
| **Type** | Indemnity policy | Defined Benefit policy |
| **Designed to cover** | Reimburses actual hospitalisation expenses | Lump sum — use for income, mortgage, or any purpose incl. hospitalisation |
| **Claim paid** | On incurring covered hospitalisation expenses | On contracting a pre-specified disease AND surviving **15–30 days** |

### 4.2 Offline vs Online Policies
- Most policies can be bought online; insurers often charge **lower premiums online**.
- Some "**web only**" policies exist.
- **Web aggregators / brokers** provide convenient online comparisons and have become major selling channels.

## 5. Global Coverage

**Will a health policy pay for hospitalisation outside India?**
- **Defined benefit policies** (life, critical illness, accidental death) pay **wherever the risk occurs** — easy to verify.
- **Indemnity policies (health)** require **ascertaining the actual expenses**. The insurer has systems for India but finds it hard abroad → health policies normally **cover expenses in India only**.
- **Exception:** **Overseas travel policies** are indemnity policies **designed country-by-country**, with arrangements to verify foreign expenses.

**Can residents buy foreign general insurance?**
- Yes — via **LRS**, up to **USD 2,50,000 per annum** (common for students going abroad to buy the foreign insurer recommended by the university).
- ⚠️ But foreign rules govern the policy and grievance redressal → exercise caution.

**Students going overseas — Indian or foreign policy?**
- Depends on the country/university. Some bundle compulsory insurance in fees; some require a designated insurer; some just specify conditions; some have no requirement.
- Treatment costs abroad can be **very high**, so cover is advisable either way.
- Indian insurers know major countries'/universities' requirements and offer suitable add-ons.
- **Other things equal, Indian policies tend to be cheaper** than similar foreign-company policies.

## 📌 One-Page Recap
- Non-life insurance covers **asset loss/destruction and unexpected large expenses**; term is usually **1 year** (2–3 for health/two-wheeler).
- **Sum insured** = insurer's maximum liability. **Restore** refills on partial/full exhaustion; **Recharge** refills only when reduced by a claim.
- **Third-party motor insurance is compulsory.** Car **NCB** up to **50%** after 5 claim-free years; NCB belongs to the **person, not the car**.
- **Personal accident** covers permanent total, permanent partial, temporary total disablement.
- **Critical illness** = defined benefit, lump sum, **no death payout**, needs **30-day survival**; size it like life cover.
- **Liability** insurance (e.g., professional indemnity) excludes **intentional/criminal** damage. **Fidelity** = employee fraud. **D&O** = directors' personal liability. **Keyman** = company insures a key person's life.
- **Top-up:** threshold per **claim**; **Super Top-up:** threshold on **aggregate** of claims. (Rs. 15 lakh claims → Top-up pays Rs. 6 lakh, Super Top-up pays Rs. 15 lakh.)
- Multiple **indemnity** policies make little sense (total ≤ combined sum insured); multiple **defined-benefit** policies **all pay**.
- Health (indemnity) usually covers **India only**; **overseas travel** policies are the indemnity exception.
- LRS limit for foreign policies = **USD 2,50,000/year**; Indian policies are usually cheaper.
