# Chapter 3: Non-Life Insurance Products — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check.

**Q1.** What does non-life (general) insurance cover?
> **A:** The **loss or destruction of assets** and protection against **unexpected large expenses** that drain income (health, motor, home, travel, liability, etc.) — everything except a life.

**Q2.** What is the "sum insured" in a non-life policy?
> **A:** The amount specified in the policy = the insurer's **maximum liability** for claims in the policy period. The insurer may set a minimum and maximum.

**Q3.** What is the typical term of a non-life policy?
> **A:** Usually **1 year**. For health and two-wheeler policies it can be **2 or 3 years**.

**Q4.** What factors determine a non-life premium?
> **A:** The **sum insured** and the **assessed risk** — e.g., age/gender/health history for medical; engine cubic capacity, place of registration, vehicle age for motor.

**Q5.** What is the difference between Restore and Recharge benefits?
> **A:** **Restore** refills the sum insured to 100% on **partial OR full** exhaustion. **Recharge** refills to 100% **only when** the sum insured is reduced due to a claim.

**Q6.** What is a No Claim Bonus (NCB)?
> **A:** Lower premiums (or extra bonus cover) enjoyed in later years for each **claim-free year**.

**Q7.** What does property insurance typically NOT cover?
> **A:** **Wilful destruction** and **normal wear and tear**. Valuables like jewellery/art need a separate or add-on policy.

**Q8.** What is an "in-patient" in health insurance?
> **A:** A patient **admitted to hospital** for treatment requiring at least **overnight or 24 hours** of stay.

**Q9.** What is domiciliary treatment?
> **A:** Treatment given **at home** when the patient is not in a condition to be moved to the hospital.

**Q10.** What is a family floater policy?
> **A:** One policy that covers **multiple family members** under a shared sum insured.

**Q11.** What must be true to benefit from health policy portability?
> **A:** The **previous policy must have been maintained without a break**. Then no-claim bonus and time-bound exclusion credits transfer to the new insurer.

**Q12.** Which part of motor insurance is compulsory?
> **A:** **Third-party liability** insurance — no vehicle can be used in a public place without it. Own-damage/theft cover is optional.

**Q13.** What are the two things motor third-party cover indemnifies?
> **A:** (i) **Death or bodily injury** to any person, and (ii) **damage to others' property** (not the insured's own).

**Q14.** What is the maximum car No Claim Bonus and after how long?
> **A:** Up to **50%** of the own-damage premium after **5 claim-free years**.

**Q15.** Does the car NCB belong to the car or the owner?
> **A:** To the **person (owner)**, not the car. A buyer of your old car gets no NCB; you transfer your NCB to your **new car**.

**Q16.** What three types of disablement does personal accident insurance cover?
> **A:** **Permanent total**, **permanent partial**, and **temporary total** disablement (from accidents by external, violent and visible means).

**Q17.** How does critical illness insurance differ from life insurance?
> **A:** Critical illness pays a **lump sum on diagnosis** of a specified disease (after **30-day** survival) and pays **nothing on death**; life insurance pays on death.

**Q18.** Why is critical illness cover often inadequate?
> **A:** People treat it as a small top-up, but it should be sized like **life insurance (income replacement)**. Large covers come only as standalone policies or **accelerated sum insured riders** on life policies.

**Q19.** What does liability insurance NOT cover?
> **A:** **Intentional damage** or damages from **criminal activities**. (It covers legal defence costs and damages for negligence or no-fault statutory liability.)

**Q20.** Give the most common example of liability insurance.
> **A:** **Professional indemnity** plans taken by Doctors, Lawyers, Investment Advisers.

**Q21.** What does fidelity insurance cover?
> **A:** Losses an **employer** suffers from an employee's **forgery, fraud or dishonesty** (money or goods). Used where employees handle large cash/valuables.

**Q22.** What does Directors & Officers (D&O) liability insurance cover?
> **A:** Directors/managers against **personal liability and defence costs** from claims alleging a **wrongful act** in their company duties.

**Q23.** Who is the beneficiary in a Keyman insurance policy?
> **A:** The **company** — it buys life cover on a key executive, pays the premiums, and receives the payout.

**Q24.** Why do multiple indemnity (health) policies make little sense?
> **A:** The **total claim can't exceed the combined sum insured**, and you must deal with multiple insurers at claim time.

**Q25.** When you hold an employer health policy plus your own, when does the contribution clause apply?
> **A:** **Not** if the chosen policy's sum insured covers the claim. It applies **only if the claim exceeds** the chosen policy's sum insured and you draw on another policy.

**Q26.** Which policy should you usually claim from first when you have employer + personal cover?
> **A:** The **employer policy first**.

**Q27.** Do multiple defined-benefit policies all pay?
> **A:** **Yes** — life, critical illness, and accidental death/disability policies all pay regardless of how many you hold (no contribution).

**Q28.** What is the core difference between a Top-up and a Super Top-up plan?
> **A:** **Top-up:** the threshold (deductible) applies to **each individual claim**. **Super Top-up:** the threshold applies to the **aggregate of all claims** in the year.

**Q29.** With a Rs. 5 lakh threshold and Rs. 15 lakh of claims (Rs. 3 + 6 + 2 + 4 lakh), how much does a Top-up vs Super Top-up pay (with a Rs. 5 lakh base)?
> **A:** **Top-up:** Base Rs. 5 lakh + Top-up Rs. 1 lakh = **Rs. 6 lakh**. **Super Top-up:** Base Rs. 5 lakh + Super Top-up Rs. 10 lakh = **Rs. 15 lakh**. (Small claims below the per-claim threshold get nothing under Top-up.)

**Q30.** Why keep the base and top-up plans with the same company?
> **A:** Simpler claims — you deal with one insurer when claims exceed the threshold. For super top-up, also inform the insurer of non-payable claims so it can **mark used threshold levels**.

**Q31.** Health insurance vs critical illness — which is indemnity and which is defined benefit?
> **A:** Health = **indemnity** (reimburses actual expenses); Critical illness = **defined benefit** (fixed lump sum).

**Q32.** Are online non-life policies usually cheaper or more expensive than offline?
> **A:** Often **cheaper** — many insurers charge lower premiums for policies bought online, and some offer "web only" policies.

**Q33.** Does a health (indemnity) policy normally cover hospitalisation outside India?
> **A:** **No** — health policies normally cover expenses **in India only**, because verifying foreign expenses is difficult. The exception is **overseas travel policies**.

**Q34.** Why do defined-benefit policies pay worldwide but indemnity health policies don't?
> **A:** Defined-benefit events (death, critical illness, accident) are easy to **verify anywhere**. Indemnity health needs **ascertaining actual expenses**, which insurers can't easily do abroad.

**Q35.** What is the LRS limit for buying foreign general insurance, and who commonly uses it?
> **A:** **USD 2,50,000 per annum**. Common for Indian **students going abroad** buying the insurer recommended by their university.

**Q36.** For students going overseas, are Indian or foreign policies usually cheaper?
> **A:** Other things being equal, **Indian policies tend to be cheaper** than similar foreign-company policies — but it depends on the country/university requirements.

**Q37.** What is day-care treatment in health insurance?
> **A:** Procedures that require hospitalisation but, due to technology, let the patient be **released the same day** — now covered by many policies.

**Q38.** What does "sub-limit" mean in health insurance?
> **A:** A **cap on a specific expense head** (e.g., room cost, consultation fees) within the overall sum insured.

**Q39.** Under what type of law can liability arise on a "no-fault basis"?
> **A:** Under **statutory law** — liability even when there is **no negligence**. (Common-law liability arises from negligence.)

**Q40.** In health insurance, how does the premium behave as cover increases?
> **A:** The **premium per extra lakh drops sharply** and becomes almost negligible after a point — a Rs. 100-lakh policy may cost only a few thousand rupees more than a Rs. 50-lakh one.
