# Chapter 18: Risk Profiling for Investors — Flashcards

> **How to use:** Cover the answer, recall it out loud, then check.

**Q1.** Define risk appetite (willingness).
> **A:** How much risk a person *wants* to take — driven by their **psychology** (attitude to volatility and losses), more than their finances.

**Q2.** Define risk capacity (ability).
> **A:** How much risk a person *can financially afford* to take, based on resources — income, capital base, dependents, loans.

**Q3.** Define risk tolerance.
> **A:** The level of risk a client is **willing to accept**, typically measured by a **questionnaire** that estimates both ability and willingness; responses become a score.

**Q4.** In the coin-toss example (pay Rs.1,00,000, win Rs.2,10,000 if correct), what is the expected payoff?
> **A:** Expected win = 50% × 1,10,000 = Rs. 55,000; expected loss = 50% × 1,00,000 = Rs. 50,000; **net expected payoff = Rs. 5,000 (positive)**. Yet most refuse due to fear of losing Rs. 1,00,000.

**Q5.** What does the coin-toss example teach?
> **A:** Willingness to take risk is **psychological**, not just statistical. People need a much higher payoff before the fear of loss is overcome.

**Q6.** Two things (besides willingness) that decide if risk is actually taken?
> **A:** **Resources** (more resources → more likely) and **need** (more important the payoff → more likely).

**Q7.** How do earning members vs dependent members affect risk appetite?
> **A:** More **earning members** → higher appetite. More **dependents** → lower appetite.

**Q8.** How do age and life expectancy affect risk appetite?
> **A:** **Lower age** → higher risk possible. **Longer life expectancy** → higher appetite.

**Q9.** How do employability, nature of job and capital base affect appetite?
> **A:** Well-qualified/multi-skilled → more risk; **steady job** → more risk; **higher capital base** → can absorb losses → more risk.

**Q10.** How does regularity of income affect appetite?
> **A:** **Regular income** → can take more risk than someone with unpredictable income.

**Q11.** Name the three risk-profile grades and one trait of each.
> **A:** **Conservative** (dislikes risk, prefers safe instruments); **Moderate** (some experience, risks part of assets); **Aggressive** (experienced, risks a significant portion).

**Q12.** Why can online risk-profiling tools be unreliable?
> **A:** Investors may try to **"guess the right answer"** (there is none); the tool only works if the investor is **truthful**. Tools also vary in robustness.

**Q13.** Under SEBI Reg 16, what information must the IA obtain?
> **A:** Age; investment objectives & time frames; resources (income, existing investments, innate ability to take risk, loans).

**Q14.** What must SEBI-compliant risk questionnaires NOT do?
> **A:** Not be misleading, vague, in difficult/complex language, and must contain **no leading questions**; they must be fair and clear.

**Q15.** When must risk profiling be done, and what about free trials?
> **A:** **Before any investment advice.** **No free trial** can be offered without communicating the risk profile to the client and obtaining their **consent** on it.

**Q16.** When should a risk profile be updated?
> **A:** Periodically, and especially on **special events**: change in family composition, income/expenses, or assets/liabilities.

**Q17.** What is asset allocation, and why does it work?
> **A:** Dividing the portfolio among asset classes. It works because asset classes have **low/negative correlation** — a fall in one is cushioned by a rise in another.

**Q18.** Give the 2007–09 example of diversification.
> **A:** Equities fell in many countries but **gold rose**; an investor in both did better than one in equities alone.

**Q19.** What does research say about asset allocation vs stock selection?
> **A:** **Asset allocation and investment policy explain portfolio performance better** than selecting individual securities.

**Q20.** Define Strategic Asset Allocation.
> **A:** Allocation **aligned to financial goals** (required return, time horizon, risk profile). Long-term; changes only if fundamentals change; **does not** chase expected asset performance; rebalanced back to the original mix.

**Q21.** Define Tactical Asset Allocation.
> **A:** A **call on likely market behaviour** (e.g., going overweight equities expecting a rally). Suitable only for **seasoned investors with large surpluses**, applied to a limited portion.

**Q22.** Define Dynamic Asset Allocation.
> **A:** Uses **pre-defined models**; reallocation triggered by asset valuations or portfolio performance. **Removes the subjective element.**

**Q23.** Which asset allocation type "depends on the market"?
> **A:** **Tactical** asset allocation. (Strategic does not depend on the market.)

**Q24.** Match objective to investment: growth, income, liquidity, capital preservation.
> **A:** Growth → equity/equity funds, real estate, gold. Income → deposits, debt/debt funds, real estate. Liquidity → cash, bank deposits, short-term MF. Preservation → cash, bank deposits, ultra-short-term funds.

**Q25.** What single risk did Jayesh (only real estate) and Latika (only equity) share?
> **A:** **Concentration risk** — their whole portfolio is in one category, so it all falls together.

**Q26.** What is a model portfolio?
> **A:** The asset-allocation mix most appropriate for a given investor profile, which is then tweaked for the specific individual.

**Q27.** How does a suitable allocation change near retirement?
> **A:** Higher allocation to **safer** investments (debt), lower to equity, as time horizon shortens and ability to recover from losses falls.

**Q28.** How often should a portfolio be reviewed, and what four questions does review answer?
> **A:** At least **once a year**. Are goals still relevant? On target in time? Investments performing as expected? Do investments need changing?

**Q29.** What is rebalancing — and what is it NOT for?
> **A:** Modifying exposure to asset classes to stay **aligned to the investor's risk/return needs**. It is **NOT** for chasing short-term price movements.

**Q30.** What did Jayant do as his children's education goal neared?
> **A:** After 8 years in equity with the goal 4 years away, he **moved funds to less risky investments** to protect the accumulated amount from equity fluctuations.
