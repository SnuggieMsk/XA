**Q1.** In the coin-toss example, how much does the person pay for one chance to play the game?
A) Rs. 50,000  B) Rs. 1,00,000  C) Rs. 2,10,000  D) Rs. 5,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The person pays a stake of Rs. 1,00,000 for one chance to play. If they guess right they win Rs. 2,10,000 (option C is the prize, not the stake). Rs. 50,000 (A) is the expected loss amount, and Rs. 5,000 (D) is the total expected payoff — both are derived figures, not the cost to play.
</details>

**Q2.** In the coin-toss game, what is the prize the person wins if they guess correctly?
A) Rs. 1,10,000  B) Rs. 1,00,000  C) Rs. 2,10,000  D) Rs. 55,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — If the coin lands on the chosen side, the person wins Rs. 2,10,000. The net gain is Rs. 1,10,000 (A) = 2,10,000 − 1,00,000 stake. Rs. 1,00,000 (B) is the stake. Rs. 55,000 (D) is the expected win (50% × 1,10,000). Only C is the actual prize amount.
</details>

**Q3.** In the coin-toss example, what is the total expected payoff of playing the game?
A) Rs. 55,000  B) Rs. 50,000  C) Rs. 1,10,000  D) Rs. 5,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Expected win = 50% × 1,10,000 = Rs. 55,000; expected loss = 50% × 1,00,000 = Rs. 50,000. Net expected payoff = 55,000 − 50,000 = Rs. 5,000. So A is just the expected win, B is just the expected loss, and C is the gross gain if right. Only D combines them correctly into the net expected value.
</details>

**Q23.** Under SEBI Reg 16, the process to assess a client's willingness and ability to take risk must be:

**Q29.** An efficient asset allocation includes asset classes that have:
