# Chapter 18: Risk Profiling for Investors — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** "Willingness to take risk" is most influenced by a person's:
A) Income level  B) Psychology  C) Number of bank accounts  D) Tax bracket
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Willingness has more to do with the individual's **psychology** than their financial circumstances. Financial factors affect *ability/capacity*, a separate dimension.
</details>

**Q2.** In the coin-toss example (pay Rs.1,00,000; win Rs.2,10,000 if correct), the net expected payoff is:
A) Rs. 1,10,000  B) Rs. 5,000  C) Rs. 55,000  D) Rs. 50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Expected win 50%×1,10,000 = 55,000; expected loss 50%×1,00,000 = 50,000; net = **Rs. 5,000**. Despite being positive, most people refuse out of fear of losing the stake.
</details>

**Q3.** Which factor, increasing, would LOWER a person's risk appetite (all else equal)?
A) Number of earning members  B) Life expectancy  C) Number of dependent members  D) Capital base
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — More dependents reduces appetite. More earners, longer life expectancy, and higher capital all *raise* it.
</details>

**Q4.** A person with an unpredictable income stream, all else equal, can take:
A) More risk  B) Less risk  C) The same risk  D) Unlimited risk
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Regular income supports more risk; irregular income means less ability to absorb downturns.
</details>

**Q5.** An investor who is "new to risky instruments and prefers bank accounts" is classified as:
A) Aggressive  B) Moderate  C) Conservative  D) Tactical
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — That is the conservative grade. They may still put a small portion into risky assets for the long term.
</details>

**Q6.** A key limitation of website risk-profiling tools is that:
A) They are illegal  B) Investors may try to guess the "right" answer though none exists  C) They are too expensive  D) They always give an aggressive score
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — There is no right answer; the tool only works if the investor is truthful. Robustness also varies, so the adviser must use them judiciously.
</details>

**Q7.** Under SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulation 16, risk profiling must be done:
A) After giving advice  B) Only for aggressive clients  C) Before any investment advice is provided  D) Only once at onboarding, never updated
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Profiling must precede advice. No free trial without communicating the profile and obtaining consent; and it must be updated periodically/on life events.
</details>

**Q8.** SEBI requires risk-profiling questionnaires to be all of the following EXCEPT:
A) Fair and clear  B) Free of leading questions  C) Written in difficult, complex language  D) Fit for purpose
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Questionnaires must NOT use difficult/complex or vague language and must avoid leading questions.
</details>

**Q9.** Distributing a portfolio across asset classes that have low or negative correlation is called:
A) Stock selection  B) Efficient asset allocation  C) Tactical trading  D) Leverage
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Combining low/negative-correlation assets is efficient asset allocation; their returns don't rise and fall together.
</details>

**Q10.** Which asset allocation type depends on a call about likely market behaviour?
A) Strategic  B) Tactical  C) Dynamic  D) Passive
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Tactical (e.g., going overweight equities expecting a rally). Strategic is goal-aligned and does not chase the market; dynamic is model-driven.
</details>

**Q11.** Strategic asset allocation changes primarily when:
A) An asset class is expected to outperform  B) The investor's goals or fundamentals change  C) The market rises sharply  D) A new fund launches
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Strategic allocation is goal-aligned and changes only when fundamentals/goals change — not based on expected asset performance.
</details>

**Q12.** Dynamic asset allocation is best described as:
A) Subjective and intuition-based  B) Using pre-defined models with valuation/performance triggers, removing subjectivity  C) Only for retirees  D) Identical to strategic allocation
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Dynamic AA uses pre-defined models and triggers, removing the subjective element.
</details>

**Q13.** Which investment best meets the objective of "capital preservation"?
A) Mid-cap equity fund  B) Ultra-short-term debt fund  C) Sector fund  D) Real estate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Capital preservation → cash, bank deposits, ultra-short-term funds. Equity/real estate are growth-oriented and can fall.
</details>

**Q14.** Latika has put all her money in equities and finds the value keeps fluctuating. Her core problem is:
A) Too little risk  B) Concentration in one asset category  C) Too many funds  D) Excess liquidity
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Concentration risk: with everything in equity, the whole portfolio rises and falls together.
</details>

**Q15.** A portfolio of investments should be reviewed at least:
A) Once a month  B) Once a week  C) Once a year  D) Once every five years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Ideally at least once a year, as part of the financial planning process.
</details>

**Q16.** Jayant has accumulated his children's education funds in equity, with the goal now 4 years away. He should:
A) Add more equity for growth  B) Move funds to less risky investments to protect the accumulated amount  C) Stop saving  D) Take a tactical overweight in equity
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — As a goal nears, shift from growth (equity) to safety to avoid fluctuations damaging the corpus. This is rebalancing aligned to needs, not chasing prices.
</details>

**Q17.** Rebalancing should ideally be done to:
A) Profit from short-term price moves  B) Keep the portfolio aligned to the investor's risk/return needs  C) Maximise trading activity  D) Always increase equity
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Rebalancing realigns the portfolio to the investor's needs and situation, not to chase short-term movements.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**Scenario:** Ms. Neha, 29, is a software engineer with a stable, well-paying job and strong tech skills. She is single with **no dependents**, has a small car loan, and a healthy savings cushion. She has invested before, including in equity, and is comfortable with market ups and downs. She is saving for (i) a house down-payment in **3 years** and (ii) retirement in **30 years**. She fills out your risk questionnaire and asks you to design her allocation.

**Q1.** Classify Neha's risk profile from her circumstances and explain using ability vs willingness.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Aggressive.** *Ability (capacity):* high — young age, steady well-paying job, multi-skilled/employable, no dependents, savings cushion (only a small loan). *Willingness:* high — experienced with equity and comfortable with volatility. Both ability and willingness are high, so an aggressive grade fits.
</details>

**Q2.** Why must the two goals NOT use the same asset allocation?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The goals have very different **time horizons**. The **house down-payment (3 years)** is short-term — capital must be protected from volatility, so it needs safer assets (short-term debt, FDs, liquid). The **retirement (30 years)** goal is long-term — it can take high equity exposure to capture growth and ride out fluctuations. Allocation must match each goal's horizon and the ability/willingness to take risk.
</details>

**Q3.** Suggest a suitable allocation approach for each goal.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- **House (3 years):** Predominantly **short-term debt funds / FDs / liquid schemes** for capital preservation and liquidity; minimal-to-no equity because there isn't time to recover from a fall.
- **Retirement (30 years):** Predominantly **equity / diversified equity funds (via SIP)**, with some gold and debt for diversification — a growth-oriented, aggressive model portfolio.
</details>

**Q4.** Which type of asset allocation should form the backbone of her plan, and when (if ever) might tactical allocation be appropriate?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Strategic asset allocation** should be the backbone — aligned to her goals, time horizons and risk profile, rebalanced periodically. **Tactical** calls are suitable only for seasoned investors with large surpluses, applied to a limited portion; for Neha, at most a small slice once her core strategic plan and surplus are well established.
</details>

**Q5.** As the house goal approaches (say 6 months away) what should you advise, and how often should the plan be reviewed?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Ensure the house fund is fully in **safe, liquid assets** (shift any remaining equity to debt/liquid) so a market dip can't derail the down-payment — exactly like Jayant moving his children's education funds to safety as the goal neared. Review the overall plan **at least once a year** (and on any life event such as marriage, new dependents, or job change), checking goal relevance, on-track status, and performance.
</details>
