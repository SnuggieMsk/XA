# Chapter 14: Basics of Estate Planning — Questionnaire

## 📝 Multiple Choice Questions

**Q1.** Estate planning is best described as:
A) A plan only for wealthy people  B) The process of accumulating, conserving and distributing one's estate  C) A way to avoid paying any tax  D) Only about writing a Will
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Estate planning covers accumulation, conservation and distribution of the estate to maintain family financial security. (A) is the classic misconception — it's for everyone. (D) is too narrow; a Will is just one element.
</details>

**Q2.** Which of the following is NOT a category of an estate?
A) Gross Estate  B) Residue Estate  C) Estate Debt  D) Probate Estate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — The three categories are Gross Estate, Residue Estate and Estate Debt. "Probate Estate" is not one of the three named categories in the text.
</details>

**Q3.** "Dying intestate" means:
A) Dying with a registered Will  B) Dying without a valid Will  C) Dying in debt  D) Dying without heirs
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Intestate = no valid Will (or a Will incapable of taking effect). It can be total or partial.
</details>

**Q4.** For a Hindu who dies intestate, which law governs the distribution?
A) Indian Succession Act, 1925  B) Hindu Succession Act, 1956  C) Muslim Personal Law  D) Married Women's Property Act
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — For intestate succession of Hindus, the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (amended 2005) applies. The Indian Succession Act, 1925 governs a Hindu's *testamentary* (with-Will) succession.
</details>

**Q5.** Which of these is NOT a Class I heir under the Hindu Succession Act?
A) Widow  B) Mother  C) Father  D) Son
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The Father is a **Class II** heir (Entry I), not Class I. Son, daughter, widow and mother are Class I.
</details>

**Q6.** A person related to another by blood or adoption *wholly through males* is a/an:
A) Cognate  B) Agnate  C) Uterine relative  D) Sharer
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — That's the definition of an **agnate**. A cognate is related but NOT wholly through males.
</details>

**Q7.** Two people with the same mother but a different father are related by:
A) Full blood  B) Half blood  C) Uterine blood  D) Adoption
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Same mother, different father = **uterine** blood. (Same father, different mother = half blood; same both = full blood.) Uterine siblings are excluded from Class I & II.
</details>

**Q8.** Under Section 14 of the Hindu Succession Act, property possessed by a Hindu female is held by her as:
A) A limited owner  B) A trustee  C) A full (absolute) owner  D) A nominee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Section 14 makes her property her **absolute property**; she is the full owner, not a limited owner.
</details>

**Q9.** How much of his net assets can a Muslim dispose of through a Will (Wasiyat)?
A) The entire estate  B) Up to one-half  C) Up to one-third  D) Up to two-thirds
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A Muslim can will away only **1/3** of net assets; the remaining 2/3 must be available for the heirs.
</details>

**Q10.** Under Muslim law, a childless widow is entitled to what share of her deceased husband's property?
A) 1/8  B) 1/4  C) 1/2  D) 2/3
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A **childless** Muslim widow gets **1/4**; a widow with children/grandchildren gets **1/8**.
</details>

**Q11.** The per capita method of distribution is mainly used in:
A) Shia law  B) Sunni law  C) Hindu law  D) Christian law
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Per capita** (equal split among the number of heirs) is used in **Sunni** law; **per strip/stirpes** (by branch) is used in Shia law.
</details>

**Q12.** Under Muslim law, when a deceased has no legal heir, the property goes to the Government through:
A) Probate  B) Mutation  C) Escheat  D) Wasiyat
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — This is **escheat**.
</details>

**Q13.** A nominee in most financial assets is generally treated as:
A) The absolute owner  B) A custodian/trustee until the legal heir gets the asset  C) Irrelevant  D) The executor
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The nominee usually just holds the asset as a custodian; the legal heir is the real owner. (Exception: beneficial nominees under the Insurance Act.)
</details>

**Q14.** Under the Insurance Act, who becomes a "beneficial nominee" able to keep the policy proceeds?
A) Any friend  B) The executor  C) Parents, spouse or children  D) The state government
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — If a parent, spouse or child is the nominee, they become a **beneficial nominee** and can consume the money; other legal heirs cannot claim it.
</details>

**Q15.** The Married Women's Property Act, 1874 was enacted mainly to:
A) Allow women to vote  B) Protect a woman's property from her husband's creditors  C) Ban joint ownership  D) Replace the Indian Succession Act
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — It insulates a married woman's property from her husband's creditors and from court/tax attachments against him.
</details>

**Q16.** Mutation of property:
A) Is conclusive proof of ownership  B) Is a central government subject  C) Updates revenue records but is NOT proof of title  D) Replaces the need for a Will
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Mutation updates the owner's name in revenue records (mainly for tax). It is a **state** subject and is **not** proof of title — the title document is.
</details>

**Q17.** A Living Will is primarily addressed to:
A) The bank  B) Physicians/doctors  C) The income tax department  D) The executor
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A Living Will is an advance directive **for physicians** about end-of-life medical care; it does not deal with money distribution.
</details>

**Q18.** Which is true about a testamentary trust?
A) It avoids probate  B) It is created through a Will and goes through probate  C) It only works for Muslims  D) It cannot hold assets
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A testamentary trust is created *through a Will*, so it is subject to the rulings under the Will and goes through **probate**.
</details>

## 🧩 Case Study

**The Sharma & Khan families — planning ahead**

Mr. Sharma is a 55-year-old Hindu businessman. He is married with two children (one is a 15-year-old with special needs). He owns a house in Maharashtra, a flat in Gujarat, has bank deposits, a life insurance policy, and a car loan still running. He has **not** written a Will. His friend Mr. Khan (a Muslim) wants advice too — Mr. Khan wants to leave part of his wealth to a charity.

**Q1.** If Mr. Sharma dies today without a Will, how will his assets be distributed?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

He would die **intestate**. Since he's a Hindu, the **Hindu Succession Act, 1956** applies. His **Class I heirs** — widow, son, daughter, (and mother if alive) — would inherit, each taking one share under Section 10. The court process and a **succession certificate** would be needed, and there's a higher risk of disputes and delays than if he had planned.
</details>

**Q2.** What is the single most important reason Mr. Sharma should write a Will, given his family situation?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

His **15-year-old special-needs child**. The law of inheritance does not account for special situations. Through a Will he can appoint a **guardian**, set up arrangements (e.g. a trust) to manage assets for the child's lifetime care, and ensure the child is financially protected. He can also name a guardian for the minor and provide for the running car loan.
</details>

**Q3.** Mr. Sharma's car loan is still unpaid at death. Which category of his estate does this fall under, and who must it be settled from?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The car loan is part of his **Estate Debt**. Liabilities must be cleared from the estate before assets are distributed to heirs. Good estate planning (and ensuring funds/insurance are available) makes sure debts are repaid without burdening the family.
</details>

**Q4.** Mr. Khan wants to leave 1/2 of his wealth to charity through his Will. Can he?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**No — not freely.** Under Muslim law a person can dispose of only up to **one-third (1/3)** of net assets by Will (Wasiyat); the other **2/3** must go to the legal heirs. So a 1/2 bequest exceeds the limit and would generally require the heirs' consent for the excess.
</details>

**Q5.** Mr. Sharma's wife has her own salary income and savings. Mr. Sharma has heavy business creditors. How can the wife's property be protected, and how can he protect his family's insurance money from his creditors?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Under the **Married Women's Property Act, 1874**, the wife's wages, earnings, savings and investments are her **separate property**, protected from the husband's creditors. Additionally, if Mr. Sharma takes a life insurance policy on his own life **expressly for the benefit of his wife/children (an MWP Act policy)**, that policy becomes a **trust** for them — it is beyond his control, not reachable by his creditors, and not part of his estate.
</details>
