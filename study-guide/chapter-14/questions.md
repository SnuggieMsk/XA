## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Estate planning is best defined as a process that deals with which three activities relating to an estate?
A) Buying, selling and leasing  B) Accumulation, conservation and distribution  C) Auditing, taxing and registering  D) Insuring, mortgaging and gifting
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The workbook defines estate planning as "a process that deals with the accumulation, conservation and distribution of an estate." Its purpose is to enhance and maintain the financial security of individuals and their families.
- A) Buying/selling/leasing are ordinary property transactions, not the definition.
- C) Auditing/taxing/registering are administrative acts, not the core process.
- D) Insuring/mortgaging/gifting are individual tools, not the overall definition.
Think of it as: gather your wealth (accumulate), protect it (conserve), then pass it on (distribute).
</details>

**Q2.** A person who makes a Will is called the:
A) Executor  B) Testator  C) Nominee  D) Beneficiary
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The person who makes a Will is the testator.
- A) An executor is the person appointed to carry out the Will's instructions.
- C) A nominee merely holds an asset as custodian until it reaches the legal heir.
- D) A beneficiary is someone who benefits from the estate/trust.
Memory hook: "Testator = person who tests-their-wishes in writing."
</details>

**Q3.** Dying "intestate" means dying:
A) With a registered Will  B) Without a valid Will capable of taking effect  C) Without any property  D) After appointing an executor
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Under Section 30 of the Indian Succession Act, a person dies intestate regarding property for which they have made no testamentary disposition capable of taking effect.
- A) A registered Will is the opposite — that is testate.
- C) Having no property is irrelevant; intestacy is about absence of a valid Will.
- D) Appointing an executor happens inside a Will, so that is testate.
</details>

**Q4.** The biggest misconception about estate planning is that it is:
A) Only for the wealthy  B) Illegal for minors  C) Compulsory by law  D) Only about taxes
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The workbook repeatedly stresses that the common myth "estate planning is only for the rich" is false; anyone owning property who wants it transferred to specific heirs in a specific manner needs it.
- B) Estate planning is not illegal for anyone.
- C) It is not legally compulsory.
- D) It is far broader than taxes (guardianship, disability, liabilities, etc.).
</details>

**Q5.** Which Act, under Section 30, defines when a person is deemed to die intestate?
A) Hindu Succession Act, 1956  B) Indian Succession Act, 1925  C) Married Women's Property Act, 1874  D) Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Act, 1937
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 30 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 defines dying intestate.
- A) The Hindu Succession Act governs intestate succession for Hindus but does not contain the Section 30 definition.
- C) The MWP Act protects a woman's property from creditors.
- D) The Shariat Act applies to Muslim non-testamentary succession.
</details>

**Q6.** When a person dies intestate, the legal heirs must apply to the civil court for a:
A) Probate  B) Succession certificate  C) Power of attorney  D) Living will
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — When a person dies without a Will, legal heirs apply to the civil court for a succession certificate, issued per the succession laws applicable to the deceased.
- A) Probate is the court's certification of a Will — relevant when there IS a Will.
- C) A power of attorney is created during one's lifetime to manage finances.
- D) A living will is an advance medical directive.
</details>

**Q7.** The intestate succession law for Hindus, Buddhists, Jains and Sikhs is the:
A) Indian Succession Act, 1925  B) Hindu Succession Act, 1956  C) Shariat Act, 1937  D) Married Women's Property Act, 1874
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — For Hindus, Buddhists, Jains and Sikhs, intestate succession is governed by the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (as amended in 2005).
- A) The Indian Succession Act, 1925 governs their *testamentary* succession (and Christians' intestate succession).
- C) The Shariat Act applies to Muslims.
- D) The MWP Act protects women's property from creditors.
</details>

**Q8.** For Christians, which Act applies to BOTH testamentary and intestate succession?
A) Hindu Succession Act, 1956  B) Indian Succession Act, 1925  C) Shariat Act, 1937  D) Special Marriage Act
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — For Christians, the Indian Succession Act, 1925 applies to both testamentary and intestate succession.
- A) The Hindu Succession Act applies to Hindus' intestate succession only.
- C) The Shariat Act applies to Muslims.
- D) The Special Marriage Act is not the succession law referenced here.
</details>

**Q9.** For Muslims, the inheritance law on dying intestate is:
A) Found in the Hindu Succession Act  B) Found in the Indian Succession Act, 1925  C) Based on religious texts, differing for Shias and Sunnis  D) Decided by the civil court alone
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — For Muslims the law of inheritance is not provided in any legislation; it is based on religious texts and differs for Shias and Sunnis.
- A) and B) Those statutes do not govern Muslim intestate inheritance.
- D) The court does not invent the rules; it applies the religious law.
</details>

**Q10.** A trust created through a Will is called a:
A) Living trust  B) Revocable trust  C) Testamentary trust  D) Charitable trust
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A trust created through a Will is a testamentary trust; it is subject to the rulings of the Will and so goes through probate.
- A) A living trust is created during one's lifetime, not via a Will.
- B) "Revocable" describes whether it can be changed, not how it is created.
- D) A charitable trust is defined by its purpose, not by being created via a Will.
</details>

**Q11.** A legal arrangement designating someone to manage one's finances if one becomes incapacitated is a:
A) Living will  B) Power of attorney  C) Codicil  D) Nomination
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A power of attorney lets an individual designate someone to manage finances if incapacitated or unavailable; NRIs commonly use it to appoint a manager in India.
- A) A living will addresses medical/end-of-life decisions.
- C) A codicil is an amendment to a Will.
- D) A nomination appoints a custodian for a specific financial asset.
</details>

**Q12.** A "living will" is an advance directive written primarily for:
A) The civil court  B) Physicians  C) Tax authorities  D) The executor
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A living will is an advance directive written primarily for physicians, stating one's wishes for end-of-life situations (terminally ill, coma, late-stage dementia) when one cannot communicate.
- A) It is not a court document.
- C) It has nothing to do with tax.
- D) The executor administers the regular Will, not the living will.
</details>

**Q13.** The rights of a nominee are generally limited to:
A) Permanent ownership of the asset  B) Holding the asset as custodian until transferred to the legal heir  C) Selling the asset immediately  D) Deciding who the legal heirs are
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A nominee's rights are limited to holding the asset as a custodian until it is transferred to the rightful legal heirs.
- A) A nominee is usually not the permanent owner.
- C) A nominee cannot freely sell as owner.
- D) A nominee does not decide who the legal heirs are; the law/Will does.
</details>

**Q14.** Under the new Insurance Act, which nominees automatically become "beneficial nominees" who can keep the money?
A) Any friend  B) Parents, spouse and children  C) Only the spouse  D) Brothers and sisters
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Under the new Insurance Act, if Parents, Spouse or Children are the nominee in the policy, they automatically become beneficial nominees and can consume (keep) the money too.
- A) A friend does not get this automatic beneficial status.
- C) Not only the spouse — parents and children are included.
- D) Siblings are not listed for this automatic benefit.
</details>

**Q15.** Which of the following is part of the "Gross Estate"?
A) Clothes and furniture at home  B) Housing loan outstanding  C) Real estate, bank accounts, investments and life insurance  D) Credit card dues
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The gross estate comprises items determining one's net worth: real estate, bank accounts, investments, businesses, retirement accounts (like NPS) and life insurance.
- A) Clothes and furniture are residue estate.
- B) A housing loan is estate debt.
- D) Credit card dues are estate debt.
</details>

**Q16.** Personal items like a car, jewellery, furniture and clothes, plus anything not mentioned in the Will, form part of the:
A) Gross estate  B) Residue estate  C) Estate debt  D) Trust corpus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The residue estate is the personal estate: car, jewellery, furniture, clothes, and any investment not mentioned in the Will or allocated to a trust, plus outstanding payments owed at death.
- A) Gross estate is the net-worth items.
- C) Estate debt is what one owes others.
- D) Trust corpus is property held in a trust.
</details>

**Q17.** Housing loans, car loans, credit card dues and tax liabilities form part of the:
A) Gross estate  B) Residue estate  C) Estate debt  D) Nomination
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Estate debt includes all debts and obligations owed to others: housing loan, car loan, credit card payments, business dues, tax liabilities and legal cases.
- A) and B) Those are asset categories, not debts.
- D) A nomination is not an estate category.
</details>

**Q18.** The two bare-minimum components of an estate plan are a last will and testament and a:
A) Trust  B) Power of attorney  C) Living will  D) Nomination form
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — At a bare minimum an estate plan should have (i) a last will and testament and (ii) a living will.
- A), B), D) Trust, power of attorney and nomination are added based on situation; they are not the stated bare minimum.
</details>

**Q19.** Under the Hindu Succession Act, the heirs of a Hindu male are broadly of how many types?
A) Two  B) Three  C) Four  D) Five
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The heirs of a Hindu male are broadly four types: Class I heirs, Class II heirs, Agnates and Cognates.
- A), B), D) These miscount the categories.
</details>

**Q20.** Which of the following is a Class I heir of a Hindu male?
A) Father  B) Brother  C) Widow  D) Father's father
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The widow is a Class I heir. Class I includes son, daughter, widow, mother and various descendants of pre-deceased children.
- A) Father is a Class II heir (Entry I), NOT Class I — a classic exam trap.
- B) Brother is a Class II heir.
- D) Father's father is a Class II heir.
</details>

**Q21.** Under the Hindu Succession Act, the father of a deceased Hindu male is:
A) A Class I heir  B) A Class II heir  C) An agnate only  D) Excluded entirely
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The father is a Class II heir (Entry I of Class II), not a Class I heir. This is a frequently tested point.
- A) The mother is Class I, but the father is not.
- C) He is specifically named in Class II, not merely an agnate.
- D) He is certainly not excluded.
</details>

**Q22.** A person related to another wholly through males (by blood or adoption) is called a/an:
A) Cognate  B) Agnate  C) Sharer  D) Residuary
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A person is an agnate if the two are related by blood or adoption wholly through males.
- A) A cognate is related but NOT wholly through males.
- C) and D) Sharer and residuary are Muslim-law heir categories.
Memory hook: "Agnate = All-males in the chain."
</details>

**Q23.** A person related to another by blood or adoption but NOT wholly through males is a/an:
A) Agnate  B) Cognate  C) Full-blood relative  D) Uterine relative
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A cognate is related by blood or adoption but not wholly through males (e.g., through a mother somewhere in the chain).
- A) An agnate is wholly through males.
- C) Full-blood means same father and mother.
- D) Uterine means same mother, different father.
</details>

**Q24.** Two persons descended from a common ancestor by the same wife (same father AND same mother) are related by:
A) Half blood  B) Uterine blood  C) Full blood  D) Adoption
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Full-blood relationship means both parents are the same.
- A) Half blood = same father, different mother.
- B) Uterine blood = same mother, different father.
- D) Adoption is a separate relationship category (treated as full blood under the Act).
</details>

**Q25.** Two persons with the same father but a different mother are related by:
A) Full blood  B) Half blood  C) Uterine blood  D) Cognate blood
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Half-blood relationship means descended from a common ancestor but by different wives — same father, different mother.
- A) Full blood = both parents same.
- C) Uterine blood = same mother, different father.
- D) "Cognate blood" is not a blood-relationship category.
</details>

**Q26.** Two persons with the same mother but a different father are related by:
A) Full blood  B) Half blood  C) Uterine blood  D) Agnate blood
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Uterine-blood relationship means descended from a common ancestress but by different husbands — same mother, different father. Uterine brothers/sisters are excluded from Class I and Class II heirs.
- A) Full blood = both parents same.
- B) Half blood = same father, different mother.
- D) "Agnate blood" is not a category here.
</details>

**Q27.** Under the Hindu Succession Act, an adopted child is treated as equivalent to a relation of:
A) Half blood  B) Uterine blood  C) Full blood  D) Cognate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — An adopted child is, for all intents and purposes, like a natural-born child and is treated as equivalent to a relation of full blood.
- A), B), D) These are lesser/different relationships not applied to adopted children.
</details>

**Q28.** Under the Hindu Succession Act, an illegitimate child's relationship is recognised with:
A) The father only  B) The mother (and through her, to each other)  C) Both parents equally  D) Neither parent
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Under the Hindu Succession Act, illegitimate relationship with the father is NOT recognised, but it is recognised with the mother, and through their mother the children are related to each other.
- A) Relationship with the father is specifically not recognised.
- C) Not both equally.
- D) The relationship with the mother IS recognised.
</details>

**Q29.** Which committee, reporting in 1948, recommended that a woman's property be her absolute property?
A) Law Commission  B) Rau Committee  C) Sachar Committee  D) Mandal Committee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The Rau Committee on Hindu Law (1948) recommended that all properties held by a woman be her absolute properties. These recommendations were substantially incorporated, culminating in Section 14 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956.
- A), C), D) These committees are not the one referenced in the workbook.
</details>

**Q30.** Section 14 of the Hindu Succession Act provides that property possessed by a Hindu female is held by her as:
A) A limited owner  B) A full owner  C) A trustee  D) A nominee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Section 14 says any property possessed by a Hindu female shall be held by her as full owner, not as a limited owner.
- A) "Limited owner" is exactly what Section 14 abolished.
- C), D) Trustee and nominee are different roles, not what Section 14 confers.
</details>

**Q31.** In Muslim law, a Will is referred to as:
A) Wasiyat  B) Ijma  C) Qiya  D) Sunna
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — In Muslim law, a Will is called Wasiyat. Any person aged 15+ of sound mind can make one.
- B) Ijma is consensus of learned community members (a source of law).
- C) Qiya is deduction by analogy (a source of law).
- D) Sunna is the practice of the Prophet (a source of law).
</details>

**Q32.** Under Muslim law, the maximum portion of net assets a person can dispose of by Will is:
A) One-half  B) One-third  C) Two-thirds  D) The entire estate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Muslim law allows disposing of no more than one-third of net assets by Will; the remaining two-thirds must be available for distribution among the heirs.
- A), C), D) These exceed the one-third limit.
</details>

**Q33.** Under Muslim law, when a deceased Muslim has no legal heir, the property goes to the Government through the process of:
A) Probate  B) Escheat  C) Mutation  D) Wasiyat
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — When a deceased Muslim has no legal heir, the Government inherits the property through the process of escheat.
- A) Probate certifies a Will.
- C) Mutation updates revenue records.
- D) Wasiyat is a Muslim Will.
</details>

**Q34.** The Married Women's Property Act, 1874 was primarily enacted to protect a woman's property against:
A) Her own relatives  B) Creditors (and court/tax attachments against the husband)  C) The state government  D) Her children
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The MWP Act, 1874 protects a woman's property by insulating it from creditors and from court or income-tax attachments run up by the husband.
- A), C), D) These are not the protective purpose stated in the Act.
</details>

**Q35.** Mutation is the process of:
A) Certifying a Will in court  B) Transferring property from one person's name to another in revenue records  C) Creating a trust  D) Appointing a nominee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Mutation transfers a property from one person's name to another by altering/substituting the name in revenue records showing right of title.
- A) That is probate.
- C) That is forming a trust.
- D) That is nomination.
Note: mutation is NOT proof of title — its sole objective is to collect revenue.
</details>
