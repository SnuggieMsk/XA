## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Estate planning is best defined as a process that deals with which three activities relating to an estate?
A) Buying, selling and leasing  B) Accumulation, conservation and distribution  C) Auditing, taxing and registering  D) Insuring, mortgaging and gifting
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The workbook defines estate planning as "a process that deals with the accumulation, conservation and distribution of an estate." Its purpose is to enhance and maintain the financial security of individuals and their families.
- A) Buying/selling/leasing are ordinary property transactions, not the definition.
- C) Auditing/taxing/registering are administrative acts, not the core process.
- D) Insuring/mortgaging/gifting are individual tools, not the overall definition.
Think of it as: gather your wealth (accumulate), protect it (conserve), then pass it on (distribute).
</details>

**Q2.** A person who makes a Will is called the:
A) Executor  B) Testator  C) Nominee  D) Beneficiary
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The person who makes a Will is the testator.
- A) An executor is the person appointed to carry out the Will's instructions.
- C) A nominee merely holds an asset as custodian until it reaches the legal heir.
- D) A beneficiary is someone who benefits from the estate/trust.
Memory hook: "Testator = person who tests-their-wishes in writing."
</details>

**Q3.** Dying "intestate" means dying:
A) With a registered Will  B) Without a valid Will capable of taking effect  C) Without any property  D) After appointing an executor
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

- A) A registered Will is the opposite — that is testate.
- C) Having no property is irrelevant; intestacy is about absence of a valid Will.
- D) Appointing an executor happens inside a Will, so that is testate.
</details>

- D) It is far broader than taxes (guardianship, disability, liabilities, etc.).

**Q47.** Under the Hindu Succession Act, when do agnates inherit?

- D) Intestate succession does not involve a Will.

**Q48.** Among Class II heirs, how is priority decided between different entries?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

- B) The opposite — earlier beats later.
- D) It is rule-based, not random.
</details>

**Q49.** A Hindu male dies intestate. He has no Class I heirs but is survived by his father AND a brother. Who inherits?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

- A) They are in different entries, so they do not share equally.
- C) The brother is in a later entry and is excluded.
- D) The Government does not inherit while Class II heirs exist (escheat is a Muslim-law concept here anyway).
</details>
