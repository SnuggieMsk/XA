# Chapter 14: Basics of Estate Planning — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check.

**Q1.** What is estate planning?
> **A:** The process of **accumulating, conserving, and distributing** your estate so your family's financial security is maintained after your death or incapacity. It decides who gets your property and how.

**Q2.** Is estate planning only for the wealthy?
> **A:** No — that's a misconception. It's for **anyone** who owns property and wants it to pass to specific people in a specific way. Even people with minor children need it (to appoint a guardian).

**Q3.** How is estate planning different from succession planning?
> **A:** Estate planning is about your **personal assets** and works mainly **after death/incapacity**; succession planning is about handing your **business** to the next generation **within your lifetime**.

**Q4.** What are the three categories of an estate?
> **A:** (1) **Gross Estate** (net-worth items: real estate, bank, investments, insurance), (2) **Residue Estate** (personal items + anything not in the Will + payments owed to you), (3) **Estate Debt** (loans, dues, taxes, legal liabilities).

**Q5.** What does "dying intestate" mean?
> **A:** Dying **without a valid Will** (or with a Will that can't legally take effect). Can be **total** or **partial**. (Indian Succession Act, Section 30.)

**Q6.** When someone dies intestate, what decides who inherits?
> **A:** The **law of inheritance applicable to their religion** — Hindu Succession Act 1956 for Hindus/Buddhists/Jains/Sikhs; Indian Succession Act 1925 for Christians/Parsis/Jews; religious texts for Muslims (differ for Shia/Sunni).

**Q7.** What is a succession certificate and who issues it?
> **A:** A document the legal heirs obtain from a **civil court** when a person dies intestate, authorising them to represent the deceased and collect debts/securities.

**Q8.** Name the five elements of an estate plan.
> **A:** Will, Trust, Power of Attorney, Living Will, and Nomination/Beneficiary designations. (Bare minimum = a Will + a Living Will.)

**Q9.** What is a testamentary trust?
> **A:** A trust created **through a Will**. Because it's part of the Will, it must go through **probate** (court validation).

**Q10.** What does a Power of Attorney (PoA) do?
> **A:** Lets you appoint someone to **manage your finances** if you become incapacitated. Commonly used by NRIs to have someone in India manage their assets.

**Q11.** What is a Living Will?
> **A:** An **advance directive for doctors** stating your wishes for end-of-life situations (coma, terminal illness, dementia) when you can't communicate. It's about **medical** decisions, not money.

**Q12.** Nominee vs Legal Heir — what's the difference?
> **A:** A **nominee** usually just **holds/receives** the asset as a custodian until it reaches the **legal heir**, who is the real owner under succession law.

**Q13.** When can a nominee actually keep the money?
> **A:** Under the **Insurance Act**, if a **parent, spouse, or child** is the nominee in a life policy, they become a **Beneficial Nominee** and can keep and use the proceeds — other legal heirs can't claim it.

**Q14.** Which law applies to a Hindu's *testamentary* succession vs *intestate* succession?
> **A:** Testamentary → **Indian Succession Act, 1925**; Intestate → **Hindu Succession Act, 1956** (amended 2005). The difference exists because of separate HUF laws.

**Q15.** Which law governs Christians for both testamentary and intestate succession?
> **A:** The **Indian Succession Act, 1925** — for both.

**Q16.** List the four types of heirs for a Hindu male, in priority order.
> **A:** **Class I → Class II → Agnates → Cognates.**

**Q17.** Is the father a Class I heir under the Hindu Succession Act?
> **A:** **No.** Father is a **Class II** heir (Entry I). Class I includes son, daughter, widow, and mother.

**Q18.** Define agnate and cognate.
> **A:** **Agnate** = related by blood/adoption **wholly through males**. **Cognate** = related by blood/adoption but **NOT wholly through males**.

**Q19.** Full blood vs half blood vs uterine blood?
> **A:** **Full** = same father + same mother. **Half** = same father, different mother. **Uterine** = same mother, different father (uterine siblings are **excluded** from Class I & II).

**Q20.** Under Section 10, how are shares split when a Hindu male leaves a widow, 2 sons, and his mother?
> **A:** Widow (1) + son (1) + son (1) + mother (1) = **4 equal shares**, so each gets **1/4**.

**Q21.** What does Section 14 of the Hindu Succession Act say about a Hindu female's property?
> **A:** Any property she possesses is her **absolute property** — she is a **full owner**, not a limited owner.

**Q22.** What are the four sources of Muslim succession law?
> **A:** The **Quran**, the **Sunna** (Prophet's practice), the **Ijma** (consensus of learned men), and the **Qiya** (deductions by analogy).

**Q23.** Sharers vs Residuary in Muslim law?
> **A:** **Sharers** get a **fixed share**; **Residuary** take whatever is **left over** after the sharers.

**Q24.** What share does a sole daughter take under Muslim law? And multiple daughters?
> **A:** A sole daughter = **1/2**; more than one daughter together = **2/3**. But if sons are also present, daughters become **residuary** and each son gets **double** of each daughter.

**Q25.** How much of his property can a Muslim give away by Will (Wasiyat)?
> **A:** Only up to **one-third (1/3)** of net assets; the other **2/3** must go to the heirs. (Maker must be **15+** and of sound mind; can be written, oral, or by gesture.)

**Q26.** What share does a childless Muslim widow get vs one with children?
> **A:** Childless = **1/4**; with children/grandchildren = **1/8** of the husband's property.

**Q27.** Per capita vs per strip distribution in Muslim law?
> **A:** **Per capita** (Sunni) = estate split equally among the **number** of heirs. **Per strip/stirpes** (Shia) = split by **branch**.

**Q28.** Does Muslim law recognise birthright in ancestral property?
> **A:** **No.** Rights are **fixed on the date of the ancestor's death**, and only if the heir survives the ancestor.

**Q29.** What is "escheat" under Muslim law?
> **A:** When a deceased Muslim has **no legal heir**, the **Government** inherits the property.

**Q30.** What does the Married Women's Property Act, 1874 protect?
> **A:** It insulates a married woman's property (wages, earnings, savings, insurance) from her **husband's creditors** and from court/tax attachments against the husband.

**Q31.** What's special about an MWP Act insurance policy taken by a husband?
> **A:** A policy on his own life expressed for the benefit of his wife/children becomes a **trust** for them — not controlled by him, not reachable by his creditors, not part of his estate.

**Q32.** What is mutation?
> **A:** The process of changing the **owner's name in government revenue records** when property transfers. Its main purpose is **property-tax collection**.

**Q33.** Is mutation proof of ownership/title?
> **A:** **No.** Revenue proceedings aren't judicial; **title** (e.g. sale deed/Will) proves ownership, not the mutation entry. But it stops one co-owner from claiming the whole alone.

**Q34.** Mr. XYZ (Hindu) dies intestate, unmarried, with 1 brother and 2 sisters. Who inherits?
> **A:** No Class I heirs exist, so we go to **Class II** — the brother and two sisters inherit **equally**.
