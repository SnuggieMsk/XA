**Q4.** In the King's employees example, the additional premium per employee was Rs. 8,000, which equals what percentage of the annual salary of Rs. 2,00,000?
A) 2%  B) 4%  C) 8%  D) 10%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Formula: percentage = (premium ÷ salary) × 100 = (8,000 ÷ 2,00,000) × 100 = 4%. The terms: premium = Rs. 8,000, salary = Rs. 2,00,000. Intuition: a small slice of pay buys protection for the whole family.
- A), C), D) Wrong: only 8,000 ÷ 2,00,000 = 0.04 = 4%.
</details>

**Q36.** A merchant insures the inventory in his own warehouse and also tries to insure his competitor's inventory. Which one is invalid and why?

- D) Wrong: his own inventory clearly qualifies.

</details>

- **Health Policy X** with Insurer A: sum insured Rs. 4,00,000, with a 10% co-pay.
- **Health Policy Y** with Insurer B: sum insured Rs. 6,00,000, with a compulsory deductible of Rs. 20,000 and no co-pay.
- He is also considering a **ULIP** that an agent is pushing, and he wants to know whether he should take a 30-year or 40-year term plan. A 30-year term plan (to age 60) costs Rs. 14,000/year and a 40-year term plan (to age 70) costs Rs. 16,500/year.

Work through the following sub-questions.

<details><summary>Q-CS1. Anand is hospitalised with a bill of Rs. 3,00,000 and chooses to claim from Policy X (co-pay 10%). How much does Insurer A pay, and does contribution apply?</summary>

**Worked answer:**
- Formula for co-pay: insured's share = co-pay % × claim = 10% × 3,00,000 = Rs. 30,000.
- Insurer A pays = claim − share = 3,00,000 − 30,000 = **Rs. 2,70,000**.
- Does contribution apply? No. The claim (Rs. 3,00,000) is **less than** Policy X's sum insured (Rs. 4,00,000). Per IRDAI health rules, when the chosen policy's sum insured is enough, the chosen insurer **cannot force contribution** from the other insurer. Anand simply bears his Rs. 30,000 co-pay; Insurer B is not involved.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS2. A year later Anand has a major hospitalisation of Rs. 8,00,000. He claims Rs. 4,00,000 (full sum insured) from Policy X. How does he recover the balance, and what principle governs the settlement?</summary>

**Worked answer:**
- Policy X pays its full sum insured = Rs. 4,00,000 (ignoring co-pay detail here, the cover caps at Rs. 4,00,000).
- Balance still needed = 8,00,000 − 4,00,000 = **Rs. 4,00,000**.
- Because the claim **exceeds the chosen policy's sum insured**, Anand can bring in **Policy Y** and the claim is settled by applying the **Contribution** provisions. He claims the remaining Rs. 4,00,000 from Insurer B (Policy Y sum insured Rs. 6,00,000 comfortably covers it, after its Rs. 20,000 compulsory deductible — so Insurer B pays 4,00,000 − 20,000 = Rs. 3,80,000, and Anand bears the Rs. 20,000 deductible).
- **Principle:** Contribution applies only to **indemnity** (health hospitalisation) policies because the loss is ascertainable and total claims cannot exceed the actual loss of Rs. 8,00,000.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS3. Anand failed to disclose a known diabetic condition when buying Policy X 18 months ago. At this large claim, the insurer investigates. What can happen, and what principle is involved?</summary>

**Worked answer:**
- The relevant principle is **Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei)**: Anand was obliged to disclose all material information, and diabetes (a chronic condition affecting risk) is clearly material.
- Because he suppressed it, he acted in **breach of good faith**, and the insurer "has the right to **reject the claim entirely**."
- Note: Section 45's 3-year non-contestability protects only **life** insurance after 3 years; here it is a health policy and the claim is within the **5-year moratorium**, during which claims can still be contested for **proven fraud**. Lesson: when in doubt, disclose — a slightly higher premium is far better than a rejected claim.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS4. Anand has dependents. The agent pushes a ULIP with a small death cover but attractive projected returns. As his adviser, how do you evaluate it, and what do you advise?</summary>

**Worked answer:**
- Method (from the workbook): **decouple** the insurance and investment elements first.
- **Check risk-cover adequacy.** Anand earns Rs. 12,00,000/year and is the sole earner with a wife and child — he needs substantial income-replacement life cover. A ULIP with a *small* death cover fails this primary purpose.
- Therefore the product **should be eliminated** on protection grounds, regardless of how good the returns look. Only after adequate cover is secured should investment returns be judged on their own merits and compared with other investment products.
- **Advice:** Buy a pure **term life insurance** plan for adequate income replacement (protection first — the cricket analogy: gear before bat), and invest separately in suitable investment products. Also, the agent must do a **suitability analysis** before selling any investment-cum-insurance product, and can skip it only with Anand's **written consent**.
</details>

<details><summary>Q-CS5. Anand must choose between the 30-year and 40-year term plans. Compute the extra cost of the longer plan and advise whether the option is worth it.</summary>

**Worked answer:**
- Formula: extra annual premium = longer-term premium − shorter-term premium = 16,500 − 14,000 = **Rs. 2,500 per year**.
- What the extra Rs. 2,500/year buys: the **option to keep life cover beyond his earning years** (from age 60 to age 70).
- **Reasoning/advice:** An income-replacement policy is primarily needed during the **earning phase** (typically up to retirement at 60). Strictly, cover after retirement may be less essential because dependents are usually self-supporting and goals are met by then. However, paying a modest extra Rs. 2,500/year preserves flexibility to extend cover if circumstances change. Since Anand is the sole earner with a young child, locking in the longer option early — while premiums are low at age 30 — is reasonable, provided it does not strain affordability. The decision hinges on whether he values that flexibility at Rs. 2,500/year.
</details>
